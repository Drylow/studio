Back at Cloud Ruler Temple, Martin can receive the Great Sigil Stone alongside the materials already gathered. The long work of studying the enemy's book has reached the point where he can act on its instructions. The Hero no longer has to search Cyrodiil for another component before the passage can be attempted. That change is substantial for Martin as well as for the traveler. His days of interpreting the Xarxes and sending the Hero after difficult objects can give way to the ritual those efforts were meant to support.


Martin opens the portal at Cloud Ruler Temple, and the Hero steps through into Mankar Camoran's Paradise. The passage exists because the gathered materials have made Martin's reading of the Mysterium Xarxes usable. Yet the destination initially looks unlike the devastated ground around an Oblivion Gate. The Hero arrives among gardens and living plants, in a realm whose appearance seems to fit the promise in its name. Mankar's voice addresses the traveler there. The beauty belongs to a place he has made for his followers, and those followers are present to explain what living in it means.

The people encountered in the garden are the Ascended Immortals, Mythic Dawn followers who have reached the existence promised by their cult. Their bodies don't provide an escape from suffering. When they die in Paradise, they return, and the Daedra can torment them again. The garden holds people who have received immortality under conditions that make it a continuing punishment. The Hero can learn this through conversation with them. Their accounts give the realm a daily reality beyond its pleasant appearance, one shaped by repeated harm and the inability to leave through death.

Mankar gives that suffering a place within his teaching. He presents the realm and its trials through the beliefs that led the Mythic Dawn to serve Mehrunes Dagon. His explanations remain his own doctrine, including his account of Tamriel's place among the realms of Oblivion. The Hero is hearing the founder justify the world he governs while walking among its inhabitants. The followers' situation supplies a concrete way to understand the consequences of his promises: they have reached his Paradise, and they remain subject to the Daedra within it.

The Hero's immediate concern is finding a route to Mankar and the Amulet. The garden doesn't offer free passage to every part of the realm. Kathutet, a Dremora encountered there, stands between the traveler and the route onward. He can offer assistance in exchange for freeing Anaxes, another Dremora imprisoned by the people of Paradise. The Hero can follow that bargain or confront Kathutet instead. The route therefore allows a conditional encounter rather than one required act of cooperation with a Daedra. Either approach leads toward the means of entering the Forbidden Grotto.

That means is the Bands of the Chosen. Wearing them allows passage into the grotto, but it also places the Hero under another of the realm's restrictions. Once inside, the bands prevent an ordinary departure until someone can remove them. Their name belongs to the cult's language of being selected and elevated, while their effect is experienced as confinement. The Hero has needed them to cross a boundary and then has to find a way to be rid of them. Paradise's divisions are enforced through objects and passages as well as through its Daedric inhabitants.

The Forbidden Grotto exposes the suffering that the garden's appearance could conceal from a newly arrived visitor. Here, the punishment of Mankar's followers is part of the place the Hero must cross. Lava and Daedric torment occupy the route onward. The inhabitants haven't arrived at a settled reward after their service in Tamriel. They remain available for punishment within the realm their leader has created. For the Hero, that suffering is encountered during the practical work of reaching the Amulet, among people whose allegiance helped make the Crisis possible outside Paradise.

Eldamil meets the Hero in the grotto and offers help. He was a Mythic Dawn priest, and his account connects him directly to the attack on Kvatch. His presence brings one of the invasion's servants into the same conversation as its consequences. Eldamil says he has recognized the falsehood of Mankar's promises and wants the Hero to destroy him. He can't undo what happened at Kvatch through that admission. He can, however, assist the person now trying to reach the man whose realm holds him, and whose defeat offers an end to their captivity.

Accepting Eldamil's aid gives the Hero a guide through the grotto's arrangements. He works within the situation there to help the traveler continue, and he can remove the Bands of the Chosen. That removal restores the possibility of leaving the restricted area. His contribution is specific and useful: the Hero entered wearing an object that barred departure, and Eldamil can undo that obstacle. His change of allegiance is expressed through those actions. The journey doesn't require treating his remorse as a complete account of his past, only recognizing the assistance he provides now.

Eldamil can also accompany the Hero toward Mankar. Like the other inhabitants, he belongs to Paradise's cycle of death and return, so his help takes place under conditions different from those of an ordinary companion in Cyrodiil. The Hero is a visitor trying to get out with the Amulet; Eldamil is already caught within Mankar's creation. Their purposes meet because Mankar's defeat offers a way to end the realm that confines its inhabitants. The route through the grotto thus gains help from someone whose promised reward has brought him into the same suffering as the followers around him.

Beyond the grotto, the way leads to Carac Agaialor, where Mankar waits. Ruma and Raven Camoran are there as well, beside the leader whose teaching they have served. The journey has moved through the garden, its restrictions, and the suffering of its inhabitants to reach the household at the center of the realm. Mankar still possesses the Amulet of Kings. The object that Martin needs is finally within the Hero's reach, after the cult's sanctuary in Tamriel and the refuge Mankar created beyond it have both been penetrated.

Defeating Mankar allows the Hero to recover the Amulet. His death also brings about the collapse of Paradise, ending the realm through which the traveler has just passed. There is no further search for another keeper or another hidden chamber before the stolen object can be taken home. The destination Martin constructed the portal to reach has yielded what the journey required. The Hero returns to Cloud Ruler Temple with the Amulet, leaving Mankar's collapsing creation behind and bringing the long search back to the heir who has been waiting for it.

Martin can now wear the Amulet of Kings, confirming his place in the bloodline in a way the object itself makes visible. The book, the ritual components, and the crossing into Paradise have achieved their practical purpose. He has the heirloom needed for the ceremony in the Imperial City. For the people encountered in Paradise, Mankar's defeat has ended the realm that sustained their punishment. For Martin, the recovered Amulet makes departure possible. The work at Cloud Ruler Temple gives way to the journey toward the Temple of the One and the Dragonfires.


Martin reaches the Imperial City with the Amulet of Kings, ready to take the step that the refuge at Cloud Ruler Temple couldn't provide. At the Imperial Palace, High Chancellor Ocato recognizes his claim. The heir has come before the Empire's government carrying the object associated with his bloodline, and the way to the Temple of the One appears open. For a moment, the journey has reached a recognizable public process. Martin can be acknowledged, escorted to the temple, and brought to the Dragonfires that the search for him was intended to restore.

That process is interrupted by news of an attack on the city. The Daedric invasion has reached the capital before Martin can complete the ceremony. Ocato's recognition still matters, but it can't keep the streets clear or hold the enemy outside the walls. Martin and the Hero have to leave the palace under conditions very different from a ceremonial escort. The heir is carrying the recovered Amulet toward its intended destination while fighting breaks out around the route. The final distance to the temple has become part of the invasion itself.

The Imperial City's scale doesn't make that distance safe. Guards and Daedra are fighting within the capital, and the journey passes through a place whose institutions are now exposed to the same crisis that has struck the counties. Martin's arrival hasn't caused the open gates to disappear. He still has to reach the Dragonfires and act. The Hero remains beside him as an escort, with Ocato drawn into the emergency as well. The people who had been arranging the succession are now trying to get the recognized heir through an invaded city.

Then Mehrunes Dagon himself appears in the Temple District. His presence changes the situation beyond another group of Daedra in the street. Martin understands that the old barrier has already been breached to the point that the Prince can stand bodily in Tamriel. The ceremony they intended to perform can't repair the situation in the way he had expected. The Hero has recovered the Amulet and brought its bearer to the capital, but Dagon's arrival has overtaken the ordinary use of those achievements before the Dragonfires can be lit.

Dagon's enormous form dominates the district around the Temple of the One. The immediate need is still to reach the temple, but Martin now has to consider what can be done with the Amulet under these altered conditions. He doesn't send the Hero away to gather a replacement object or search for another heir. Everything available to him has arrived here: the Amulet, his own bloodline, and the knowledge he has brought to this final journey. The decision he reaches belongs to this emergency, with the Prince already inside the world.

Martin asks the Hero to get him into the temple. There is still a practical task between his decision and whatever he intends to attempt. The building that was meant to receive an Emperor for the Dragonfires becomes the place where he will confront the invasion by another means. He doesn't give the Hero a long explanation of a new ritual. The journey continues into the temple with an immediate purpose, while Dagon's presence outside makes clear how little shelter the capital's sacred center can provide against the attack now underway.

Inside, Martin breaks the Amulet of Kings and gives his own life. He becomes the avatar of Akatosh, taking the form of a great dragon that confronts Mehrunes Dagon. The object carried through so much of the Crisis is destroyed in the act. There will be no later ceremony in which Martin wears it before a restored court. Its power is spent here, through the last heir who brought it to the temple. 

The dragon battles Dagon and banishes him from Tamriel. With that defeat, the invasion ends and the Oblivion Gates close. Martin's sacrifice achieves the protection that his journey to the Dragonfires was meant to restore, under circumstances that required him to abandon the expected ceremony. The Temple of the One is left broken by the encounter. Where the living dragon stood, a stone dragon remains. The Hero has survived to see the result, but Martin is gone, and the Amulet that could identify and serve a Septim Emperor has been shattered.

Martin's final words explain the ending in terms of his sacrifice and the protection of Tamriel. They also leave the future of the Empire to those who remain. His death ends the Septim line. The old arrangement had depended on a ruler of that blood, the Amulet, and the Dragonfires; the people now standing in the damaged capital have none of those in the form they expected. The Crisis has ended without delivering a living Septim Emperor to a throne. Martin's recognized claim has lasted only long enough for him to use it in saving the city and the world beyond it.

Ocato meets the Hero afterward and acknowledges the magnitude of what has happened. He can speak for the Empire's surviving government, but he can't crown Martin after his death or produce another Septim successor. The Hero receives recognition as Champion of Cyrodiil, a title attached to the service that brought the effort this far. That honor doesn't make the Champion the new Emperor. The distinction leaves the political consequence intact: imperial government continues through the people and institutions still present, while the dynasty around which the old succession had been arranged is finished.

The promised Imperial Dragon Armor gives the epilogue a smaller, concrete task. After it has been made, the Champion can collect it from the Imperial Legion Armory. The armor is an official reward for the Hero's service, something wearable that belongs to life after the invasion. Its existence allows for an ordinary continuation beyond the temple's destruction: craftsmen complete a commission, the Legion holds it, and the person who carried the Amulet can come to receive it. No assumed guild career or further adventure is needed to give that acknowledgment its place.

At the Temple of the One, the dragon statue remains as the visible consequence of Martin's last decision. The building holds the form left by the avatar that defeated Dagon, rather than the scene of a completed coronation. People can stand before it in a capital no longer under that invasion, beneath an imperial government that has survived without its Septim Emperor. Martin doesn't return to Cloud Ruler Temple or take his place in the palace. The Amulet is gone, the gates are closed, and the stone dragon stands where he gave his life.

A Tarnished follows the light of grace toward the Erdtree, while the heirs of its shattered order hold pieces they haven't managed to reunite. The tree is still standing. Its golden branches reach over a country where the war for succession has left no lord able to settle it. We'll follow that broken succession through the people who inherit it, then enter the Land of Shadow, where the history behind their inheritance becomes harder to separate from what they've tried to leave behind.

This telling includes full spoilers for Elden Ring and the released story of Shadow of the Erdtree, including their endings. Some events are revealed directly. Others reach us through people who have their own reasons for explaining the past as they do. Here, at the beginning, the situation is plain enough: the Elden Ring has been shattered, Queen Marika is missing, and the demigods who claimed its fragments have fought without producing a victorious successor. The Tarnished returns to a struggle that has already exhausted its first claimants.

The opening places this returning figure among people called back after death. Its summons reaches figures as different as Hoarah Loux, Goldmask, Fia, the Dung Eater, and Gideon Ofnir. The character we follow receives no comparable reputation in that introduction. They're another Tarnished, initially described as having no renown, entering a country where distinguished names already belong to someone else. That gives the journey an unusual starting point. The invitation to seek lordship reaches a person who doesn't arrive with a demigod's ancestry or an army waiting to receive them.

The first playable arrival is at the Chapel of Anticipation, a place whose name suits the uncertain beginning. A Grafted Scion confronts the newcomer, and the route eventually brings them to the Stranded Graveyard. Before the Tarnished has met a ruler or heard a full account of the war, they've already encountered something dangerous enough to interrupt their arrival. Elden Ring lets the country introduce itself through that experience. Whatever the summons promises, coming back hasn't made the traveler safe, and being called doesn't mean being prepared.

When the Tarnished emerges into Limgrave, the Erdtree gives the journey a visible destination. It's enormous enough to remain part of the view across regions that otherwise look and feel quite different. At a site of grace, golden light offers a more immediate direction. These traces point onward, and the traveler can follow them without yet knowing the people at the end of the road. The distant tree and the nearby grace work together. One gives the journey its broad shape; the other directs the next stretch of travel.

White Mask Varre is among the first people available to explain the newcomer's position. He recognizes the Tarnished and remarks on their lack of a maiden, connecting that absence with the practical difficulty of pursuing their calling. He also points them toward Stormveil Castle and its ruler, Godrick. His manner makes this sound like a settled route through the world, though his advice comes from a stranger with purposes of his own. For the returning Tarnished, even the first explanation of how to proceed arrives through someone they have little reason to understand.

Limgrave doesn't suspend its ordinary dangers while that explanation takes place. A Tree Sentinel patrols near the beginning of the journey, close enough to show how little protection the newcomer's status provides. Farther along, soldiers occupy the Gatefront Ruins, beneath the route toward Stormveil. Their presence gives the approach to the castle a practical shape. The Tarnished may have been summoned toward a throne, but the road passes through territory where armed people already guard the way. A distant claim to lordship carries little weight with those standing on it.

There are places to stop within that country, too. At the Church of Elleh, Merchant Kale sells supplies and speaks with the traveler among the remains of a church. His presence introduces a life that continues around the larger disaster. Trade hasn't disappeared because the demigods failed to settle their war. Someone still needs goods, and someone still makes a living providing them. The ruined building and the merchant occupying it belong to the same present, where damaged institutions leave spaces that people use as best they can.

Melina gives the Tarnished a more personal arrangement. When she appears at a site of grace, she offers to take on the role of a maiden and turn runes into strength. In exchange, she asks to be taken to the foot of the Erdtree. She also entrusts the traveler with Torrent, the spectral steed who becomes their companion across the country. Her offer answers an immediate difficulty raised by Varre. The Tarnished now has someone able to help them grow stronger, and that help carries a destination shared by both travelers.

Their agreement doesn't begin with complete familiarity. Melina has her own purpose at the Erdtree, while the Tarnished is pursuing the path opened by grace. For the moment, those routes coincide. This is how the journey starts acquiring human connections: through an offer, a need, and a stretch of road traveled together. Torrent also changes the scale of that road. Limgrave's slopes, ruins, and open ground become part of a country the traveler can cross, even while castles and guarded approaches continue to interrupt the way.

Stormveil rises ahead as the first great seat of a claimant. Godrick holds a Great Rune, one of the fragments claimed after the Elden Ring was shattered. That makes the castle relevant to the summons, rather than merely an obstacle on a northern road. The Tarnished has entered a country whose would-be rulers possess parts of the thing they're being called to restore. Grace can point toward those rulers, and Melina can help the traveler reach them. The succession itself remains unsettled, with a returning outsider now approaching the gates.


Inside the Erdtree, the Elden Ring appears within Marika's broken body. Its lines overlap in a golden pattern, a structure the opening of Elden Ring has already shown being struck with a hammer. There's no band to slip onto a finger. The word ring names something that can be housed in a god, broken into great runes, and eventually repaired. Anyone seeking to become Elden Lord is approaching a damaged arrangement of the world itself, with living people still trying to exist under what remains of its rules.

Marika is the vessel of that arrangement. Enia, the Finger Reader at Roundtable Hold, explains her position when she speaks about the imprisoned queen. Marika carries the Elden Ring and is a god, while her consort holds the title of Elden Lord. Those roles belong together, though they aren't interchangeable. Becoming lord doesn't mean that the Tarnished will carry the Ring in the same way Marika does. The throne places a ruler beside the divine vessel, within an order that already has its own structure.

The Erdtree makes that structure visible across the Lands Between. Its golden branches rise above Leyndell, and its image appears in sacred seals, clothing, and places of worship. Travelers can approach it from distant regions long before they can reach its entrance. In Leyndell, the capital gathers around the tree, with sanctuaries, defenses, and the royal seat concentrated beneath it. For someone arriving from Limgrave, the Erdtree gives the promise of a single center to a country whose roads and castles have fallen into competing hands.

That central place grew around a tree associated with tangible blessings. The Blessed Dew Talisman describes an earlier age when blessings fell from the Erdtree like tears, and it gradually restores the wearer's health. Other descriptions remember a period of abundance when people received the tree's bounty. Sacred tears, found in churches, preserve another connection between devotion and bodily restoration. A blessing in this world can enter someone's everyday life through healing. The Erdtree's place in worship has that material history behind it, even where its abundance has become something people remember.

The Golden Order is the particular religious and governing arrangement associated with Marika's rule. The Elden Ring can sustain an order, while the Golden Order gives that order a recognizable form, with teachings, holy practices, and accepted distinctions between beings. Brother Corhyn teaches incantations and speaks as someone committed to those teachings. He regards Marika as the one true god. His certainty gives a human scale to something otherwise difficult to picture: a traveling cleric can understand his whole vocation through the place Marika occupies in the world.

At the Church of Vows, Miriel offers a gentler example of how such teachings can be applied. The great tortoise preserves the history of a church associated with the union of the Erdtree and the moon. He accepts prayerbooks that other religious teachers may find troubling, and his understanding of religious learning leaves room for things to be brought together. Corhyn and Miriel can teach the same traveler, yet they don't approach unfamiliar beliefs with the same attitude. The Lands Between contains differences within its religious life, even beneath that enormous golden tree.

Golden Order fundamentalism also involves study. Its incantations draw on intelligence as well as faith, and their descriptions name the principles of regression and causality. Regression concerns the pull of things toward convergence. Causality concerns the connections through which things affect one another. These are principles that a scholar can investigate through sacred practice. The Golden Order Seal, which strengthens fundamentalist incantations, describes that scholarship as a pursuit carried out alongside faith. For its practitioners, understanding how existence holds together is part of worship, and learning can have a direct effect on the miracles they perform.

Goldmask takes that pursuit into the open country. He stands in contemplation, directing his attention toward the Erdtree, while Corhyn attempts to follow and interpret his work. If the Tarnished pursues their story, Corhyn treats Goldmask as a master whose calculations deserve study. Their journey gives the Golden Order a life beyond sermons and royal commands. Someone can devote himself to examining it, become troubled by a difficulty within it, and continue searching for an answer. The details of where that search leads belong to their own story, but the occupation itself matters here.

The Ring's great runes bring this vast arrangement back to individual bodies. The demigods possess fragments after the Shattering, and recovered great runes can grant blessings to the Tarnished. Godrick's Great Rune is described as an anchor ring at the center of the Elden Ring. Once restored and activated, it raises the Tarnished's attributes. The fragment therefore has both a place within the larger pattern and a perceptible effect on its bearer. The demigods' inheritance includes pieces of something whose power still works, even though the whole has been damaged.

Lesser runes circulate through the same world. The Tarnished gathers them and, with Melina's help, turns them into strength. Golden Rune descriptions connect these remnants to the grace that once dwelled in the eyes of the inhabitants of the Lands Between. Runes can be carried, lost, recovered, and exchanged. Their presence makes golden blessing part of travel and survival, alongside the grander struggle over the Elden Ring. A ruler's claim and a wanderer's growing strength both draw on powers that reach into the condition of living beings.

That's why repairing the Elden Ring is such a substantial undertaking. Marika remains its vessel, the Erdtree remains the great center toward which the Tarnished travels, and the Golden Order supplies the teachings people have lived under. Yet the broken Ring can be approached as something capable of a different arrangement. The immediate task of gathering great runes belongs within that larger possibility. Each recovered fragment brings the Tarnished closer to a place where a damaged order of life can be given a working form again.


Deep within the storm at Farum Azula, Dragonlord Placidusax waits with his remaining heads raised toward an absent god. The Tarnished can reach him by lying down in a place among the ruins, where the surrounding destruction draws back and the ancient arena takes shape. His remembrance identifies him as an Elden Lord from the age before the Erdtree. That places a holder of the title far earlier than the royal household whose descendants now contest the Lands Between. Lordship already had a history before the familiar golden dynasty.

Placidusax's remembrance also says that his god fled, and that he continued waiting for its return. It doesn't name that god. What remains is a particular arrangement: an ancient lord, a departed divine partner, and a vigil that outlasts the world around him. His posture gives that account a physical presence. When the Tarnished arrives, Placidusax is still suspended at the center of his enclosure, withdrawn into the storm. The broken succession of the present has an older counterpart here, though his waiting belongs to a different age.

Farum Azula preserves other traces of that age in its architecture and inhabitants. Ancient dragons occupy the ruins, and beastmen remain within its halls. Dragon bodies are embedded in the masonry, while a relief in Maliketh's arena depicts an elaborate form of the Elden Ring. The image has a different arrangement from the familiar emblem associated with Marika's order. Together with Placidusax's title, it suggests that the Ring's history reaches beyond the institutions the Tarnished first encounters. The Golden Order occupies part of that history, with older forms still visible in stone.

The ancient dragons themselves carry the material of their long survival. The Dragoncrest Shield talismans describe gravel stone scales as the source of the ancient dragons' protection, and connect those dragons with the prehistoric age before the Erdtree. Their bodies have a stony surface, and their lightning is red. When Lansseax takes flight or Fortissax raises lightning within Godwyn's dream, that older kind of dragon remains recognizable. The Tarnished encounters creatures whose nature was formed before the Erdtree became the great reference point for the world's chronology.

The beastmen complicate any picture of this distant past as an age inhabited only by dragons. The Cinquedea's description connects the five fingers of its design with intelligence granted to beasts long ago. In Farum Azula, beastmen wield weapons and occupy a built environment alongside the remains of ancient dragons. Their history includes thought and craft. The ruins preserve a society that had its own distinctions and capacities, even though the traveler arrives when its buildings are suspended over an abyss and much of its life has become hostile.

Elsewhere, the older history of living bodies gathers around the Crucible. The Aspects of the Crucible incantations describe it as the primordial form of the Erdtree, when life was blended together. This makes the Crucible part of the tree's own past. The immense golden tree emerged from an earlier condition in which the divisions between forms of life were less settled. Horns, tails, and other bodily growths belong to that account, and the game allows the Tarnished to encounter them in both sacred practices and living creatures.

Crucible Knights give those descriptions a clear physical form. A knight can manifest a tail and sweep it across the ground, or produce a horn for a charging attack. Another aspect allows its user to breathe fire through a throat pouch. These abilities briefly add animal features to an armored human shape. The knights preserve practices associated with the Crucible through their incantations, so the Tarnished doesn't have to imagine what a blended inheritance might mean. It appears in bodies that can still call upon those features.

Their armor carries the same history. The Crucible armor descriptions connect its reddish gold with the primordial matter from which the Erdtree developed. That color belongs to an earlier sacred condition, before the bright gold that dominates the present order. The armor also identifies the Crucible Knights with Godfrey, the first Elden Lord of Marika's age. His service therefore included warriors whose powers reached into the tree's ancient beginnings. The transition into Erdtree rule left older practices within the household of its first lord.

Later attitudes toward bodily differences were much harsher. The Crucible Knot, Feather, and Scale talismans describe features that grew on human bodies and were once treated as signs of the divine. Their descriptions then trace a change in judgment: civilization came to regard such traits as impurities. A growth could remain physically the same while its place in society changed. Someone bearing it might once have been honored, then belong to a category that later generations found shameful. That change helps explain the distance between the Crucible's sacred past and the treatment of its surviving traces.

The Misbegotten live with a version of that reversal. The Winged Misbegotten Ashes describe their kind as punished for contact with the Crucible and subjected to lifelong servitude. At Castle Morne, the Tarnished finds a rebellion by servants against their human masters. Edgar, the castle's castellan, remains concerned with his duty and the recovery of its treasured sword, while his daughter Irina has fled the violence. The castle's disaster has its own immediate circumstances, but the Misbegotten's place there belongs to a longer history of beings forced into service.

Placidusax and the Crucible broaden the past in different ways. The Dragonlord preserves a form of sacred lordship older than the Erdtree, while Crucible descriptions preserve the tree's beginnings in blended life. Their exact relationship isn't laid out as a complete sequence. What the Tarnished can find is already substantial: an ancient lord still waiting, beastmen with an inheritance of intelligence, knights who manifest ancestral features, and servants condemned for traits once considered holy. By Marika's age, the Lands Between already contained traditions to inherit and living beings whose place within those traditions had changed.


At the First Church of Marika, in the Mountaintops of the Giants, Melina can repeat words that she identifies as Marika's own. They address Godfrey and his warriors before battle. Marika calls on them to put the giants to the sword and confine their flame, so that the Erdtree's age can begin. The church stands within the region where that war left its most visible remains. By the time the Tarnished arrives, the giants have been defeated, yet the flame their conquerors feared still burns above the mountains.

Marika's words place Godfrey beside her at the beginning of this expansion. She's the divine ruler associated with the Erdtree, and he's the lord who leads her battles. His title as first Elden Lord belongs to that royal order. The remembrance of Dragonlord Placidusax describes an Elden Lord from an age before the Erdtree, so Godfrey's title shouldn't erase the rulers who preceded him. His distinction concerns the reign taking shape around Marika, with its own dynasty, sacred center, and enemies.

Godfrey's equipment preserves something of the warrior beneath that office. His axe is associated with his vow to conduct himself as a lord, while his armor explains the presence of Serosh, the beast upon his back. Godfrey took Serosh with him to suppress the relentless appetite for battle that belonged to his earlier identity, Hoarah Loux. Becoming Marika's consort required him to govern that part of himself. The king who fought for the Erdtree still carried the strength and temperament of a warrior, and his royal bearing required effort.

The war in the mountains gave that strength a particular purpose. The Fire Giants tended a flame capable of burning the Erdtree, and Marika's order couldn't safely grow while that threat remained beyond its reach. The Mountaintops still contain the enormous bodies of fallen giants, with weapons driven through them. Their presence gives the campaign a scale that a royal title alone can't convey. Godfrey's victories involved peoples whose bodies, practices, and places of worship differed greatly from those gathered beneath the golden tree.

Some of the clearest accounts of this campaign survive in the equipment of the Zamor warriors. Their curved swords and armor describe them as ancient enemies of the Fire Giants who fought on the Erdtree's side during the war. Their curved blades have been shaped by long conflict, and their warriors are associated with frost. Marika's armies therefore included allies with an existing grievance against the giants. For the Zamor, the campaign continued an older struggle, even as its outcome helped establish a new ruler's age.

The trolls also have a place in this history. The Troll's Hammer identifies them as descendants of the giants and explains that the ceremonial work of smithing, once associated with giants, continued among them. The weapon's description says that trolls distanced themselves from the giants during their war against the Erdtree. A sword monument in the mountains remembers the trolls' treachery alongside the giants' defeat. Taken together, these remnants describe a war in which kinship didn't keep everyone on the same side. The giants lost support from beings related to them.

The defense of the Erdtree also developed a lasting suspicion of fire. The Fire Monk armor describes an order of guardians appointed to watch over the giants' flame. Its members eventually became fascinated by the very power they guarded. Their robes and incantations preserve that uneasy service long after the campaign itself. In the Lands Between, someone can begin by keeping a forbidden force contained and come to revere it through prolonged exposure. Marika's victory created work that later generations still had to perform.

The final surviving Fire Giant bears an even more direct consequence. His remembrance explains that Marika found the flame could never be extinguished. She cursed him to remain at his forge and tend it for eternity. That account changes the meaning of the battlefield around him. The giants were defeated, but their conqueror couldn't remove the power at the center of their worship. The survivor was left with a task imposed by the ruler who had destroyed his people, and the Erdtree's age continued with its feared flame still alight.

The Giant's Red Braid adds another part of the giants' religious life. Its description links their red hair to their kind and notes Radagon's hatred of his own red locks, presenting the connection as a possible curse. More directly, the incantation Flame of the Fell God recalls a divine power associated with the giants' fire. The Fire Giant's remembrance says their fell god still lurks within him. The danger in the mountains has a surviving bearer as well as a surviving forge, despite the devastation of the war.

Godfrey's campaigns extended beyond this mountain battlefield. The description of his armor names the war against the giants and his defeat of the Storm Lord, whom he faced alone. It also places the end of his campaigns where his last worthy opponent fell. Those brief accounts leave much of his military life outside the record, but they describe a lord whose reign was measured through successive victories. Marika's rule spread through places where other powers had already established themselves, and Godfrey confronted rulers who could resist that expansion.

A sword monument near the Smoldering Church remembers the boundary of Lord Godfrey's march and identifies him with the Golden Lineage. The landscape therefore preserves his advance through both ruined places and declarations of royal achievement. His descendants would inherit a family identity built around the first lord's reputation. Godfrey had personally led the wars attached to his name; later members of that lineage would have to live with comparisons to an ancestor remembered for overcoming formidable enemies.

Marika's early reign emerges through these scattered remains as an undertaking that required armies, allies, and continuing guardianship. Melina's church echo gives the campaign its declared purpose: defeat the giants, contain their flame, and begin the Erdtree's age. The forge shows how that purpose was carried out only in part. Godfrey helped secure a world in which Marika could reign, while the Fire Giant remained above it, bound to tend a flame that her victory hadn't extinguished.


The gold faded from Godfrey's eyes when his last worthy enemy fell. The description of his armor places that loss at the end of his career as a conqueror, after the defeat of the Fire Giants and the Storm Lord. Godfrey had fought as Marika's first Elden Lord, and his warriors had helped establish her reign. Yet victory didn't secure their place beside the Erdtree. When the fighting that had defined his service was over, Godfrey lost the visible blessing that marked his belonging.

Melina preserves another part of that departure. At the Third Church of Marika in Limgrave, she can repeat words that Marika once spoke to Godfrey and his warriors. In this account, Marika announces that she's taking away their grace. Their eyes will grow dim, and they must leave the Lands Between. The armor gives us the moment as it was remembered in Godfrey's life, while Marika's words identify an act of banishment. The queen herself sends her lord and his fighting company beyond the land they'd served.

That removal gives the Tarnished their name. Grace has a visible association with gold in the eyes, so losing its hue leaves a bodily sign of separation from the Erdtree's blessing. Godfrey becomes the first Tarnished, and the people who depart with him share his condition. In the later age, the name also belongs to their descendants. The character who returns at the beginning of the game therefore inherits a history that began with Godfrey's exile, whether or not that character personally marched beside him.

Marika's instruction extends beyond departure. She tells the warriors to fight in a distant land, where they'll live and eventually die. It's a severe future to set before soldiers who have already won her wars. They aren't being given another province within her realm or a peaceful retirement under the tree. Their continued lives are to unfold outside it, through battles that will no longer be fought in the Lands Between. Godfrey leaves with followers whose shared identity now includes the loss of the blessing they once possessed.

At the Church of Pilgrimage on the Weeping Peninsula, another echo supplies the promise attached to that sentence. Marika says that after their deaths she'll return what she has taken. They'll come back to the Lands Between, wage war, and lay claim to the Elden Ring. She addresses them as warriors who must grow strong through death. Taken together, the church echoes describe exile with an intended return. Departure, struggle abroad, death, and renewed grace all belong to the future Marika sets out for them.

This doesn't mean Godfrey and his followers left because the Elden Ring had already been shattered. Their banishment belongs to an earlier period, when Marika's order still had its reigning goddess and a history of successful conquest. The later war among the demigods gives the returning Tarnished the fractured country encountered in the game. Keeping those events apart matters for Godfrey's story. He lost his place after serving Marika's victories, long before the summons brought Tarnished back to seek lordship among the ruins of her family.

The departure is remembered as the Long March, and objects left behind give it a more ordinary human scale. The Rusted Anchor, found on the Weeping Peninsula, comes from a ship associated with the Tarnished leaving the Lands Between. Its description recalls a vessel reportedly left behind as the others departed. An anchor is a practical remnant of a journey by sea, far removed from the language of divine blessing. Godfrey's followers had to leave a physical country, with shores and ships between their old service and the lives awaiting them elsewhere.

Godfrey's axe carries the history farther. Its description associates its damage with the Long March and identifies the weapon with his vow to conduct himself as a lord. He doesn't cease to have a lord's history because Marika withdraws his grace. The weapon accompanies a man whose position has changed, while the demands he places on himself remain attached to it. In exile, Godfrey is still the former Elden Lord, carrying the record of that office into circumstances where the Erdtree's blessing no longer sustains his standing.

The Long March also reaches the history of the Tarnished who never knew Godfrey's court. The description of the Hero's armor connects its wearers to the badlands and to descendants of Godfrey. That connection helps explain why a returned Tarnished can arrive with a background formed beyond the Lands Between. The exiles and their descendants haven't spent the intervening age waiting together at Marika's door. They've belonged to other lands and acquired lives of their own. Their shared name survives across that distance, even where their upbringing and experience differ.

When the opening summons names the dead who will rise, it includes Hoarah Loux, identified as a chieftain of the badlands. This is Godfrey under the name associated with his life as a warrior. The same summons reaches figures as different as the scholar Gideon Ofnir and the deathbed companion Fia, before reaching the Tarnished character. They aren't introduced as a company that has remained intact since the exile. The return gathers people with separate histories under a renewed call of grace, and each arrives with concerns formed through those histories.

The returning Tarnished also don't all possess the explanation Melina can recover at Marika's churches. The description of Roderika's crimson hood says she never saw the guidance of grace. At Roundtable Hold, other Tarnished speak of having lost sight of it. Being Tarnished doesn't supply a complete account of Marika's intentions, or even the same continuing experience of her summons. The player can piece together the promised return through the echoes, while the people gathered around that return remain unevenly informed. Godfrey's exile has ended in a call his scattered heirs must interpret within their own lives.


At the Church of Vows in Liurnia, Miriel remembers Radagon as a champion who arrived at the head of a golden host. He came into a country defended by sorcerers, where the royal house of Caria and the Academy of Raya Lucaria had their own traditions and sources of strength. The Erdtree's armies couldn't assume that victory here would follow the pattern of earlier conquests. Liurnia resisted, and Radagon's place in its history began with the fighting that brought him into conflict with Rennala.

Rennala was a queen with authority rooted in her mastery of sorcery. The description of her full moon spell recalls how she encountered that moon in her youth and later used its power to captivate the academy. Her remembrance describes a champion who led the academy and established the house of Caria as royalty. When Radagon encountered her, he faced someone whose standing had been earned among sorcerers as well as inherited by the household that followed her.

The sword monuments recording the Liurnian wars preserve the difficulty of that encounter. One remembers Radagon's red-haired presence in battle. Another describes a struggle between gold and the moon in which neither side won. That failure to secure a decisive victory matters because the settlement that followed allowed both traditions to continue. Rennala's sorcery wasn't erased from Liurnia, and Caria retained its royal identity. Radagon's campaign ended with an agreement that brought him into the very family his army had opposed.

Miriel places the change at the Church of Vows. According to his account, Radagon cleansed himself with celestial dew, put aside his aggression, and married Rennala. The church preserves that marriage as a reconciliation between the Erdtree and the moon. Its association with absolution gives the account a concrete setting: this is a place where damaged bonds can be repaired. Radagon and Rennala's union made such a repair part of the history of two powers that had recently fought each other.

Marriage also brought Radagon into a form of learning different from the faith associated with his arrival. The description of Radagon's Icon says that, as Rennala's husband, he studied sorcery. Later, as Marika's husband, he studied incantations, seeking completeness through both. During his time in Liurnia, then, he participated in the intellectual life of the household he had joined. Rennala's world offered knowledge he pursued, and the marriage placed a champion of the golden host beside a queen whose authority rested on the stars and moon.

A small surviving object makes that shared life easier to picture. The golden sewing needle, kept at the Church of Vows with the golden tailoring tools, was used to alter demigod attire. Its description associates it with Radagon's marriage to Rennala. Radagon's connection with tailoring receives another acknowledgment through Boc, who can hear that Radagon himself practiced the craft. These details give the husband and royal champion an occupation beyond warfare. The needle remains in the church where his marriage is remembered, after the household it belonged to has separated.

Rennala and Radagon had three children: Radahn, Rykard, and Ranni. Their parentage joined the Carian royal family to the red-haired champion who had entered Liurnia with an invading army. Their later paths would lead them far apart, but they shared this beginning in a household where sorcery and the Erdtree's traditions had been brought together. Ranni's connection with the moon continued through her mother. Radahn carried his father's red hair with pride, even while finding a different military hero to admire.

The description of Radahn's armor makes that combination explicit. He valued the red mane inherited from Radagon, while the lion imagery of his armor honored Godfrey, the first Elden Lord. Within one son's presentation, the two men occupied different places. Radagon supplied a visible family resemblance. Godfrey supplied an example of lordly strength. Radahn's admiration didn't require him to set his father aside, and his armor preserves both attachments. The children of the Liurnian marriage grew up with connections that reached beyond either parent's immediate household.

Radagon eventually left Rennala and returned to the capital. Miriel connects his departure with Godfrey's removal from the Lands Between and Radagon's elevation as Marika's consort and second Elden Lord. For the Carian household, this was a husband and father leaving the family formed through reconciliation. For the ruling dynasty around the Erdtree, it brought a new lord beside Marika. Radagon and Rennala's children subsequently became demigod stepchildren through his union with the goddess, as the description of Rykard's Great Rune explains.

Rennala was left with a gift that continued to occupy her long after Radagon's departure. He had given her an amber egg containing the Great Rune of the Unborn. In the academy's grand library, she holds that egg close and uses its power in her work of rebirth. The gift survived the marriage, and it became central to her secluded life. Anyone reaching her there encounters a queen absorbed in the means of making life anew, surrounded by scholars whose own rebirth has left them frail and short-lived.

The academy's response deepened the damage. Rennala's headpiece describes how its members discovered that she was no longer a champion after her heart broke with Radagon's departure. They turned against her, and the Carian royal family's authority fell away. The woman who had once captivated the academy could no longer command its allegiance. Her distress therefore had consequences beyond the private loss of a husband. The institution she had led withdrew its recognition when she could no longer sustain the strength on which her position had depended.

Miriel speaks of the separation with sadness and puzzlement. His account leaves Rennala's abandonment difficult to reconcile with the vows made in his church. What remains in Liurnia is a scattered household: children pursuing their own lives, a golden needle preserved at the wedding site, and Rennala in the library with Radagon's parting gift. The marriage had allowed an inconclusive war to end in reconciliation. After Radagon became Elden Lord, Rennala continued to hold the egg he had left her, within an academy that had ceased to follow her.


The Regal Omen Bairn depicts a royal child whose horns were allowed to remain. Its description explains a practice that separates the children of ordinary families from those born to royalty. Omen infants commonly had their horns cut away, and most died from the procedure. Royal Omen children escaped that treatment, but they were hidden underground for the rest of their lives. Morgott has servants who can pursue champions elsewhere in the Lands Between. To a Tarnished traveling toward the Erdtree, the king's resistance can therefore arrive along the road, before the city's walls or his hidden royal name have come into view.

Leyndell also preserves the record of a more direct defense. A sword monument outside the capital credits Margit with heaping up the bodies of champions during the Second Defense of Leyndell. An opening illustration appears to show Morgott bearing down on Radahn, suggesting a direct confrontation with one of his strongest relatives. These details give substance to his later condemnation of the demigods. He hasn't spent the whole Shattering sheltered behind a title. He's fought for the capital while the other heirs pursued their own claims.

Within Leyndell, his public reputation takes a different form. Gideon describes Morgott as the ruler who holds the capital and protects the Erdtree, while acknowledging how little is known about him. The Veiled Monarch can be recognized through the city he governs and the defenses he maintains without exposing the body beneath that title. It's an arrangement suited to a ruler whose ancestry grants him a claim that his appearance would complicate. Morgott can occupy the royal seat, though he remains concealed from the society around it.

When the Tarnished reaches the thrones, Morgott no longer maintains that separation. He appears in person and speaks as the king. Godrick, the twins Miquella and Malenia, Radahn, Rykard, and Ranni all come under his judgment. His anger gathers the scattered family into one accusation of betrayal. He then turns that judgment toward the newcomer. A Tarnished seeking lordship is, to Morgott, another intruder approaching something sacred. Whatever grace has brought this traveler back, Morgott won't accept their ambition as sufficient reason to surrender his guardianship.

He breaks the outer covering of his staff and reveals the curved blade inside. Its description explains that the sword was formed from the accursed blood he had put aside and sealed away. The weapon therefore carries something Morgott has tried to suppress within himself. Alongside it, he continues to wield golden conjured weapons. His defense of the Erdtree draws on both his devotion and the inheritance he despises. The battle makes that conflict visible through the things he actually brings against the Tarnished.

During the confrontation, his curse spills across the royal ground. Morgott responds with shame and anger at having stained the place with it. Even while fighting for his life and his throne, he treats his own body as a source of defilement. That reaction fits the secrecy surrounding the royal Omens: the king has carried the judgment imposed on his kind into his understanding of himself. His authority hasn't freed him from it. Nor does his presence above ground mean that the Omens confined beneath Leyndell have been welcomed into the city.

After his defeat, Morgott's body loses its prominent Omen features, leaving him diminished on the ground. Much later, when Godfrey returns, he holds his fallen son before facing the Tarnished. The moment gives Morgott a place in his father's arms after a life spent largely concealed. It also places the defender of Leyndell within the family he accused and tried to preserve. Whatever distance separated them, Godfrey recognizes him.

Morgott's remembrance says that he loved the Erdtree despite receiving no love in return. His actions give that statement its weight: he guards Stormveil's approach, commands riders on the roads, defends Leyndell, and finally exposes his hidden identity at the thrones. He dies having resisted the person seeking to replace the order he served. Beneath the capital, the Shunning-Grounds remain, with their prisoners still below the streets of his kingdom.


The entrance to the Erdtree is closed by thorns. After Morgott's defeat, the Tarnished can climb toward the opening behind his throne, close enough to find that the way inside is impassable. The tree has dominated the journey across the Lands Between. Its light reaches far beyond Leyndell, and its immense trunk rises above the royal sanctuary. Yet arriving at its entrance changes nothing about the barrier. The Tarnished has reached the place where lordship was supposed to become possible, and there's no passage through.

Morgott remains nearby, diminished after the battle. He tells the Tarnished that the Erdtree will admit no one and connects that refusal to the failure of their ambitions. His own long defense of Leyndell hasn't earned him entry either. There's bitterness in the situation without needing to make him its final authority. The Tarnished can approach the entrance and discover the obstruction directly. Whatever Morgott believes about the fate of those who seek the throne, the thorns are there, and defeating their defender hasn't removed them.

This matters because the campaign has had a fairly recognizable destination. Gather Great Runes, reach the capital, stand before the Erdtree, and become Elden Lord. The Two Fingers at Roundtable Hold have encouraged that undertaking through Enia, their interpreter. Access to Leyndell has required proof that the Tarnished can advance beyond the other claimants. Now the royal capital has been crossed and its king overcome. The tree itself blocks the final approach, leaving the Tarnished with an obstruction that victory over Morgott hasn't removed.

When the Tarnished rests at the site of grace after finding the thorns, Melina appears. She recognizes the obstruction and offers a route forward. Beyond the capital lie the Mountaintops of the Giants, where a flame can burn the Erdtree's thorns. Her proposal gives the journey a new destination, the Forge of the Giants. Reaching the tree has brought her closer to the purpose she has been seeking, though she still needs the Tarnished to travel with her. Their agreement can continue beyond the point that once seemed its natural conclusion.

Melina gives the Tarnished the Rold Medallion. It's a practical answer to a practical difficulty, since the Grand Lift of Rold provides access to the mountaintops. She also marks the route on the map. The next stage therefore begins with something more usable than a general instruction to persevere. There's a lift to reach, a medallion that will operate it, and a distant forge associated with the fire she needs. The path turns away from the Erdtree even while the blocked entrance remains the reason for taking it.

At Roundtable Hold, Enia receives news that the guidance given so far hasn't accounted for what happened. The Two Fingers have become motionless, raised toward the heavens. She explains that they're consulting the Greater Will and that this consultation may take an immense span of time. For the Tarnished, waiting offers no workable continuation of the journey. The interpreter who previously conveyed their direction can describe their silence, but she can't supply an immediate answer from them about the sealed entrance.

Asking Enia about burning the thorns brings out the danger in Melina's proposal. Enia identifies burning the Erdtree as the gravest of sins. The fire of the Giants belongs to a history of hostility toward the tree, so seeking it means approaching something the established order has treated as forbidden. A Tarnished following the Fingers' earlier direction has now reached a point where further progress involves an act their interpreter describes in those terms. The obstacle can't be solved by carrying out the original instruction with greater force.

Enia nevertheless doesn't insist that the Tarnished remain beside the silent Fingers. She acknowledges how far events have departed from the expectations that guided their return. Her response allows room for the Tarnished's judgment while the Fingers remain occupied with their consultation. It's an unusual exchange because she continues to explain the old prohibitions without making them the end of the conversation. She can name the severity of burning the tree and still discuss what such an undertaking would require.

Her explanation also gives the flame a human cost. Enia says that kindling is needed, and describes a person suited to that role through a vision of fire. Melina has offered to guide the journey, but the conversation at the Hold makes clear that reaching a forge won't amount to collecting an ordinary tool. Someone has to take part in what happens there. For now, the Tarnished has a destination and an accompanying purpose whose full demands haven't yet been carried out. The consequences belong ahead on the road.

The road leaves Leyndell through its eastern outskirts and crosses the Forbidden Lands toward the Grand Lift of Rold.

Grace still guides the Tarnished away from the closed Erdtree, while the Fingers remain silent about the forbidden act now needed to reach it.


At the Forge of the Giants, Melina can finally carry out the purpose that brought her to the Mountaintops. The Tarnished has defeated the Fire Giant, but the immense forge still requires someone capable of using its flame. Melina has spoken of being burned and bodiless, and of seeking the purpose her mother gave her inside the Erdtree. Here, on the ordinary route, she understands what she's come to do. She asks the Tarnished to commit to the journey ahead, then gives herself to the fire that will burn the tree.

Her decision follows a disagreement that's accompanied the ascent. Melina wants the world to continue, even with its suffering and disorder. When the possibility of the Frenzied Flame arises, she argues that births still happen and that life can still contain something worth preserving. Her willingness to burn belongs to that hope. She intends to open a way through the obstacle around the Erdtree so that someone can reach the Elden Ring. Destroying everything would defeat the purpose for which she's traveled beside the Tarnished.

There's a conditional route in which she never takes her place at the forge. Beneath Leyndell, the Three Fingers can embrace the Tarnished and leave them carrying the Frenzied Flame. If that happens before the journey to the forge is completed, Melina leaves. She's warned against accepting it, and she refuses to accompany its bearer any farther. The Tarnished then becomes the kindling for the burning. Melina survives this particular sacrifice, though saving her in this way also brings the possibility of the destruction she opposed.

Under the ordinary course of events, Melina's departure leaves the Tarnished alone at a different kind of threshold. The Erdtree begins to burn, and the journey continues in Crumbling Farum Azula. The game carries the Tarnished there without giving a detailed account of the passage. What awaits is a ruined place of suspended masonry, ancient dragons and beastmen, with a great storm moving through its broken architecture. The route toward the throne has passed beyond the roads and fortresses of the Lands Between into a sanctuary whose inhabitants preserve a much older religious world.

Beyond the Godskin Duo waits Maliketh, still guarding the Destined Death that Marika excluded from her order. The Tarnished's route now brings them against its appointed keeper.

The Tarnished first faces him as the Beast Clergyman. For those who've brought Deathroot to Gurranq at the Bestial Sanctum, the encounter can carry recognition, and his words change accordingly. The clergyman's hunger for Deathroot and Maliketh's custody of the stolen rune belong to the same wounded history. Death has spread through the roots in a corrupted form while its appointed keeper remains burdened by what escaped him. In Farum Azula, that keeper stands directly across the path the Tarnished must follow.

When the battle changes, the Beast Clergyman reveals Maliketh and draws the power concealed within him. The Black Blade carries Destined Death, and his attacks make that custody visible. This is the same power that made the demigods fear him. Defeating Maliketh breaks the arrangement by which the Rune of Death has been kept sealed away. The fire at the Erdtree and the release of Death now belong to the same advance toward its interior.

After Maliketh falls, the Tarnished returns to a transformed Leyndell. Ash has covered the capital. Streets and approaches that once organized movement through the royal city are buried, and the burning Erdtree dominates what remains. The change gives the journey a physical consequence beyond the loss of individual guardians. Reaching the Ring has required setting the sacred tree alight and releasing the force that its order had confined. The royal capital cannot remain untouched while the Tarnished breaks through the conditions that kept the throne inaccessible.

Sir Gideon Ofnir waits within the ashen city. He knows enough about the Tarnished's progress to oppose its completion, and he now claims that Marika intends an endless struggle rather than a successful ascent. That's Gideon's conclusion, delivered as he tries to stop the person he once supplied with information. His presence makes the remaining approach more personal. The gathering of ambitions at Roundtable Hold has narrowed into a confrontation between Tarnished who disagree about whether anyone should finish what grace began. Beyond him, the path through the ash still leads toward the Erdtree, now burning with Death released into the world.


Godfrey waits near the entrance to the Erdtree, holding Morgott's body before the confrontation begins. The first Elden Lord has returned to the capital after the long exile that made him and his warriors Tarnished. His arrival puts another claimant at the end of the same journey. The guidance that brought the player toward the throne has also brought back a man who once occupied it. Morgott passes from his arms, and Godfrey turns to face the Tarnished who has reached the place his son defended.

Godfrey initially fights with Serosh at his shoulder, the beast who restrains his appetite for battle. When that restraint is cast aside, he returns to the name Hoarah Loux and fights as a warrior without the bearing of a ceremonial king. The change recalls the distance between the conqueror and the lord Marika made of him. Defeating him settles the immediate rivalry over access to the Erdtree. The returning founder of the Golden Lineage won't be the one to enter and claim the Ring.

Inside, Marika hangs suspended and broken. Her body falls and changes into Radagon, who takes up the hammer associated with both the shattering and the attempted repair of the Elden Ring. The revelation that Radagon is Marika now has a physical presence before the Tarnished. Whatever their earlier history involved, this final encounter places them within the same damaged body. Radagon still stands between the challenger and the possibility of remaking the order whose fragments are visible within him.

After Radagon's defeat, the Elden Beast appears. Its remembrance calls it the vassal beast of the Greater Will and the living incarnation of the concept of order. It takes Radagon's body and fashions a sword from him. The last opponent therefore reaches beyond a contest among demigods or returning lords. The Tarnished faces the being associated with the Ring itself. Only after that encounter ends does the broken body of Marika become the point around which the available conclusions gather.

The simplest conclusion is the Age of Fracture. If the Tarnished mends the Elden Ring without adding a special mending rune, they become Elden Lord under that name. The ending gives the realm a lord and restores the Ring, but it doesn't describe a thorough removal of every cruelty or ruin encountered along the way. Its name leaves the condition of this inheritance in view. A shattered order has been made whole enough to continue, with the Tarnished seated as the new ruler.

Fia's alternative requires the Mending Rune of the Death-Prince. Her quest brings together the cursemarks associated with Godwyn and Ranni, and she conceives the rune through her union with the Prince of Death. If the Tarnished uses it, the resulting Age of the Duskborn incorporates the principle of life within death into the order. Those Who Live in Death have been hunted as a violation of the Golden Order. Fia's rune changes the law that makes their existence an offense, giving her cause a place within the Ring itself.

Goldmask reaches a different diagnosis through his study of the Golden Order. His work, pursued with Brother Corhyn nearby for much of the journey, produces the Mending Rune of Perfect Order. Its description identifies the instability of gods with humanlike hearts as the flaw it would address. If used, the rune brings the Age of Order. Goldmask's solution seeks to prevent divine fickleness from disturbing the system. The player isn't shown a detailed account of everyday life afterward, but the proposed correction is specific: the order should no longer depend on such changeable gods.

The Dung Eater also produces a mending rune, provided the Tarnished follows his quest through its grim requirements. His Seedbed Curses prevent the souls of his victims from returning to the Erdtree. The Mending Rune of the Fell Curse extends that defilement into the order, and its use leads to the Blessing of Despair. The description imagines a world in which everyone is cursed, so that the curse ceases to single out particular people. His answer to exclusion spreads the condition he inflicts rather than relieving the suffering of those already marked by it.

Ranni's ending proceeds through a separate invitation. If her quest has been completed, the Tarnished can summon her instead of repairing Marika with a mending rune. Ranni takes Marika's place as the divine figure of a new age, with the Tarnished as her consort. In her account of the coming Age of Stars, she intends to take her order far from the people who would otherwise live directly beneath it. Her departure with the Tarnished places the new divine pair at a distance, rather than seating them together in Leyndell.

The Frenzied Flame leads to a conclusion that abandons preservation. Its adherents seek to melt away the divisions from which birth and suffering arose. If the Tarnished remains its inheritor and chooses that ending, the world is consumed by the flame of frenzy. Where Melina survives the earlier journey, the ending returns to her amid the aftermath, and she promises to pursue the Lord of Frenzied Flame with Destined Death. Her opposition survives the success of the path she begged the Tarnished to reject.

These conclusions depend on what the player has done and what they select at the end. Acquiring several mending runes doesn't mean using all of them together, and completing Ranni's quest doesn't force the Age of Stars. The Frenzied Flame imposes its own restriction unless it's removed through the demanding use of Miquella's Needle. The released game leaves the outcome conditional. One Tarnished may take the throne, another may leave with Ranni, and another may burn the world. At Marika's broken body, the preparations made throughout the journey become a particular ending.


At the roots of the Haligtree, Malenia waits beside a hollow where her brother should have been. Miquella's absence has shaped the entire settlement above her. His tree still shelters soldiers and inhabitants, and Elphael still surrounds its lower reaches, but the demigod whose promise drew them there is gone. Malenia identifies herself through her devotion to him. Even after the catastrophe in Caelid, she remains his defender, resting within a refuge whose founder can no longer be found among its roots.

Reaching that refuge requires a departure from the ordinary road through the Lands Between. The Haligtree Secret Medallion opens the way to the Consecrated Snowfield, and Ordina conceals the passage onward. The Albinauric woman Latenna knows the importance of this route. If the Tarnished accepts her request, she travels with them as a spirit toward the place where she hopes to find a future for her people. Her journey makes Miquella's promise concrete. For the Albinaurics, the northern refuge is a destination worth crossing a hostile country to reach.

The medallion's history also shows how vulnerable that hope has become. Albus hides with part of it after the attack on the Village of the Albinaurics. Ensha later attacks the Tarnished at Roundtable Hold once that half has been obtained, and Gideon acknowledges Ensha's involvement. Information about the Haligtree is valuable enough to draw violence far from the tree itself. Those seeking sanctuary have to preserve a concealed route while people connected to the struggle for lordship try to acquire it.

Miquella cultivated the Haligtree with his own blood. The Haligtree Knight armor describes that effort and says the tree ultimately failed to become an Erdtree. This leaves a substantial achievement alongside a failed transformation. A great living structure exists, with a settlement built around it, though it never became the replacement Miquella sought. Its branches carry the Tarnished down toward Elphael, where the architecture and defenses suggest an established community. The rot spreading through the lower reaches shows how far that community remains from the protection its founder hoped to provide.

Miquella had already tried another approach to healing Malenia. The description of Radagon's Rings of Light explains that he abandoned Golden Order fundamentalism because it could do nothing to treat her scarlet rot. Unalloyed gold grew out of that search. The needles associated with him can resist the interference of outer gods, and Millicent's story shows both the help such a needle can offer and the limits of imposing a purpose on another person. Miquella's work began with particular afflictions that the established order couldn't resolve.

Within Elphael, the soldiers' equipment preserves a harsher expression of loyalty. Haligtree soldier ashes describe warriors who awaited Miquella's return and hoped their final flash would guide him home. Some soldiers encountered there turn themselves into explosions. Their willingness to die exists beside the original promise of shelter. Miquella's absence hasn't dissolved devotion to him. It has left followers holding to the expectation of his return while the place around them suffers, and while Malenia remains below, waiting for the brother whose cure she still lacks.

Far away in Mohgwyn Palace, another sacred structure holds Miquella's cocoon. An arm extends from it, and Mohg addresses the figure within as the center of his coming dynasty. Mohg wants to raise Miquella to full godhood and become his consort. The remembrance of the Lord of Blood describes his attempts to share his bloody bedchamber with Miquella and says he received no response. In the base game, the contrast is stark: the Haligtree has lost its founder, while Mohg possesses his body and claims a future through him.

Mohg's own religious commitments matter here. He's an Omen born into the royal family, and his contact with the Formless Mother has given his accursed blood a sacred meaning within his new dynasty. His palace is filled with blood worship, and his followers pursue the establishment of his coming reign. Miquella's Empyrean status makes him essential to Mohg's ambition. Possessing a powerful demigod isn't enough by itself. Mohg needs the god whose consort he intends to become, and the silent cocoon gives him no visible fulfillment of that plan.

Shadow of the Erdtree changes how this situation can be understood. Sir Ansbach, once a servant of Mohg, tells the Tarnished that he challenged Miquella in an attempt to free his lord from Miquella's enchantment. He was then enchanted himself. His account establishes that Mohg's involvement can't be treated solely as the independent scheme it first appears to be. Miquella's ability to compel affection has reached into the household that supposedly held him captive, and into the man who tried to resist him on Mohg's behalf.

That revelation doesn't provide a complete sequence for the removal from the Haligtree, or settle when every intention took shape. It does change the position of the absent demigod. Miquella has acted upon Mohg and Ansbach, and he's used Mohg in reaching the Land of Shadow. The cocoon therefore belongs to a passage Miquella pursued as well as to Mohg's failed dynasty. Ansbach's testimony gives Mohg a different place in that history without turning the entire earlier story into a fully explained plan.

The Tarnished can leave Malenia undefeated and still reach the cocoon after overcoming Mohg. The expansion's entrance doesn't require resolving the fate of the Haligtree or delivering its inhabitants the return they await. This separation leaves the refuge's sorrow intact. At the palace, Miquella's discarded flesh remains within reach, while his path continues elsewhere. The brother Malenia waits for has gone beyond the tree he nourished, carrying his effort to establish a gentler age into a concealed land.


Leda stands beside the cocoon in Mohgwyn Palace and invites the Tarnished to follow Miquella. Mohg has been defeated, and Radahn's defeat is also required for this passage to open. The entrance is the withered arm extending from the cocoon. Touching it carries the Tarnished into the Land of Shadow, where Miquella has already traveled. Leda recognizes the Tarnished as another person drawn toward him, though the player arrives without sharing the histories that have brought his other followers to this point.

The first open country is the Gravesite Plain. Spectral gravestones cover stretches of the land, and the dark Scadutree rises in the distance with a form unlike the Erdtree the Tarnished has left behind. A vast hanging veil accompanies that view. The place has its own fortresses, settlements and sacred ruins, and its inhabitants continue lives shaped by conflicts that aren't immediately familiar. Crossing from the cocoon brings the Tarnished into a country with a history of its own, where Miquella's recent passage runs through older wounds.

Miquella's crosses give that passage a visible form. Near the Three-Path Cross, a golden mark stands where he has abandoned a part of himself. The inscription identifies flesh left behind at the beginning of his journey through this land. Further crosses record other relinquishments, including bodily parts and qualities connected to his inner life. They make his absence unusually traceable. The Tarnished can't walk beside him, but can stop where he stopped and read what he chose to surrender there.

Scadutree Fragments found near some crosses belong to the local practice of blessing. The inhabitants gather fragments shed by the Scadutree, and the Tarnished can receive their aid within this land. Revered Spirit Ashes provide a related blessing for summoned spirits and the spectral steed. These customs give the journey a local dependence. The strength brought from the Lands Between remains part of the Tarnished's history, yet survival here also draws on objects venerated by people who've lived beneath the shadowed tree.

At the Three-Path Cross, Redmane Freyja and Hornsent already have different reasons for following the same trail. Freyja served Radahn, and she credits Miquella with saving her after she was afflicted by scarlet rot in Aeonia. Her loyalty grows from that rescue and her respect for the warrior she served. Hornsent carries the grievance of a people devastated by Messmer's crusade. He looks to Miquella's promised age for a future in which the suffering of his people can finally be answered.

Their proximity doesn't erase that difference. Freyja can speak with admiration of Radahn and with gratitude toward Miquella, while Hornsent speaks bitterly of the Erdtree's people. A Tarnished associated with grace arrives among them carrying an identity that Hornsent has reason to distrust. Miquella's following has brought these travelers into one small gathering without making their pasts interchangeable. What joins them is an expectation about the person ahead of them, and that expectation has room for very different hopes.

Near the Main Gate Cross, Ansbach and Moore present another pairing. Ansbach was a Pureblood Knight in Mohg's service. He's an older warrior who now spends his efforts gathering information, and he doesn't pretend to understand Miquella's purpose already. Moore supplies the group and speaks with affection of the forager brood. These foragers are members of the kindred of rot who provide ingredients and supplies. Moore's place among them gives the traveling company a network of care outside the human loyalties most immediately visible at the crosses.

Ansbach asks for information about the crosses because the things Miquella abandons may explain where his journey is going. His method is practical: find the marks, learn what was left, and compare those discoveries with the path ahead. The act of following thus includes a growing record of loss. Miquella is a demigod known for offering healing and refuge, but his progress through this land requires him to surrender pieces of the person his followers remember. Ansbach has reason to examine what that process means.

At Pillar Path Cross, Thiollier's concerns turn toward Saint Trina. He's skilled with poisons and reluctant to present himself as a strong fighter. His devotion gives him a focus different from Leda's direct commitment to Miquella. Saint Trina is already associated with sleep in the Lands Between, through lilies, weapons and remedies bearing her name. Here, Thiollier has come in search of her. His presence means that even within Miquella's company, someone can be listening for another figure and measuring the journey by another hoped-for meeting.

Leda herself is a Needle Knight devoted to Miquella. Her confidence gives the scattered travelers an apparent center, and her messages help direct the Tarnished along his route. Yet the company isn't an army traveling in formation. Its members occupy different crosses and investigate different places. Some need information, some offer supplies, and others speak of a debt or grievance they expect Miquella to resolve. The Tarnished becomes acquainted with them through these ordinary exchanges before understanding how their commitments fit together.

The crosses also draw the journey toward places that belong to the land's earlier religious life. Belurat rises beyond the settlement outside its gate, with towers and sculpted beasts that carry traditions distinct from Leyndell's. Castle Ensis holds another defended route deeper into the country. Miquella hasn't made an empty road for his followers. His marks pass through territories where old communities and armed custodians remain. Moving from one cross to another therefore means encountering the people whose country has become the setting for his attempt at godhood.


Shaman Village lies beyond the Hinterland, reached through a concealed passage from Shadow Keep. It's a small settlement compared with the fortresses and sacred cities elsewhere in the Land of Shadow. A golden tree grows there, and the Minor Erdtree incantation preserves the reason for its presence. Marika bathed the village of her home in gold, knowing there was nobody left to heal. The description gives the empty place a direct connection to the woman later worshipped as the Eternal Queen.

The spell is described as Marika's secret incantation, and its gold belongs to a kindness without order. That wording places this gift apart from the elaborate religious system associated with her later rule. Here, the golden light was directed toward an abandoned home. The act didn't restore its people. Marika knew they were gone when she left the blessing. The village therefore preserves an action with no surviving recipients, carried out by someone who would eventually stand at the center of a world-spanning faith.

The Golden Braid provides another personal trace. It's a cut lock of Marika's hair, left as an offering to the Grandmother. Its description asks what she prayed for, wished for or confessed, then leaves those questions unanswered. It also says she never returned home again. The object supplies a departure and an offering without supplying the words that accompanied them. The braid and the empty village already make her separation from home concrete.

Elsewhere, Bonny Village exposes a practice that helps explain what happened to people called shamans. The settlement is connected to the production of living jars, and the nearby gaols contain the results. Jar potentates work with great pots and with human bodies. The places associated with them include implements and remains that make the process visible without requiring an account of each victim. This was organized work, sustained through tools, places of confinement and a religious language that could describe the intended result as sainthood.

The Tooth Whip is particularly direct. Its description explains that the teeth embedded in it cause wounds that fester, and that shaman flesh was said to meld harmoniously with the flesh of others. It calls their resulting treatment a cruel fate. This connects the shamans' bodies to the jar process through a particular quality attributed to their flesh. They were useful to the people making the jars because they could bind with other flesh. That usefulness became a reason to subject them to suffering.

A spirit in Bonny Village addresses a shaman and insists that being placed in a jar is the prospect available to them. The speaker treats this as an opportunity to become a saint and presents the shaman's assigned role as something natural. The words belong to the persecuting society's explanation of its practice. Alongside the Tooth Whip, they show how an act of confinement and bodily violation could be spoken of as improvement. The victim's place had already been decided by the people holding the tools.

Bonny Village and its nearby gaols preserve the work of the jar makers. The Tooth Whip describes why shaman flesh was sought for that work, while the jars and places of confinement show the human cost of pursuing sainthood through other people's bodies.

Marika's home, the reference to shamans, and the descriptions of their treatment connect her people with this persecution. Taken together, they suggest a background for her later hostility toward the hornsent, though the game doesn't give a complete chronology of her escape or ascent. What it does give is enough to change the setting of her rise. Before the royal family, the Erdtree's conquests and the divine court, there was a home whose population was gone and a practice that assigned its people to jars.

The hornsent civilization can't be reduced to the work at Bonny Village. Belurat contains sculpted guardians, sacred architecture and the traditions of the divine beast. Horns and other signs of the Crucible have religious value there. Spiral imagery accompanies an aspiration toward divinity, visible in the forms built into the land's sacred places. This wider setting matters because the persecution of shamans belonged to a society with a developed account of holiness. The jar makers' actions existed within that religious world rather than outside all claims to sacred purpose.

The jars also complicate a familiar sight from the Lands Between. Iron Fist Alexander travels as a warrior jar, seeks worthy battles and speaks with the Tarnished as a companion. His existence doesn't make Bonny's practices gentle, and Bonny's practices don't erase his individual life. In the Land of Shadow, broken pots reveal another history attached to the same broad form. A vessel that can be a speaking traveler elsewhere is here bound to confinement, forced melding and the treatment of particular people as material.

The later crusade against the hornsent devastated communities far beyond the jar makers themselves. Hornsent survivors remember destroyed homes and families, and they associate that suffering with Marika's Erdtree. The village offers a reason to consider how revenge entered this history, but it doesn't make the persecution of one population a justification for burning another. Both histories remain present in the land. Bonny retains the machinery of the shamans' suffering, while the ruined hornsent settlements retain the consequences of the violence directed against them.

Returning to Shaman Village after those places gives its small golden tree a particular sadness. Marika's gift could heal someone standing within its light, yet the description says she knew her people were beyond that help. The braid remains an offering made before a final departure. These are the surviving actions attached to her home: she left gold, she left part of her hair, and she didn't return.

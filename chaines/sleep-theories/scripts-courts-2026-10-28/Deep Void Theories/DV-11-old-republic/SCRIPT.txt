Coruscant is under attack while Republic delegates are trying to negotiate peace with the Sith Empire. The Jedi Temple burns, and the government on the capital finds itself bargaining with an enemy that has already reached its home. The settlement becomes the Treaty of Coruscant. We'll begin with that damaged capital, then follow the older history behind it: how scattered worlds formed a Republic, how the Jedi became its protectors, and how successive wars changed the civilization both were trying to preserve.

This history follows Star Wars Legends, the older continuity, with spoilers for Dawn of the Jedi, Tales of the Jedi, both Knights of the Old Republic games, The Old Republic's core stories and major later expansions, and the Darth Bane trilogy. In the period surrounding The Old Republic, Coruscant is already the seat of an ancient government. Its senators represent worlds separated by immense distances. Its survival depends on ships being able to cross those distances, carrying officials, supplies and military help where they're needed.

A ship leaving Coruscant needs a usable course to its destination. Having a hyperdrive doesn't make every direction safe. Stars, planets and other masses affect navigation, and a journey depends on calculations that carry a vessel past those hazards. A known hyperspace route gives travelers a course that others have already surveyed and used. As those courses connect inhabited systems, a world can exchange goods, receive visitors and maintain regular contact with governments far beyond its own sun. That regular passage helps explain how separate planetary societies begin to form a Republic.

In the early Republic history of Star Wars Legends, worlds of the galactic Core build a shared government around connections like these. Coruscant becomes its capital, while Corellia and other early member worlds retain their own identities and local affairs. The early chronology is sparse compared with the wars recorded in the games and comics. Its broad development is clear enough, though: travel makes sustained diplomacy possible, and diplomacy gives connected worlds a way to settle common business without treating every arriving ship as the representative of a foreign power.

Coruscant's position gives those journeys a political destination. The planet becomes the place where member worlds send representatives and where the Senate conducts the Republic's business. The office of Supreme Chancellor provides leadership within that larger government. These institutions allow disputes and shared concerns to be addressed above the planetary level. Membership doesn't erase a world's own government. Across the Republic's history, planets continue to have rulers, councils and local laws, while their representatives participate in decisions that affect the wider community.

Alderaan's royal tradition shows how a world's local institutions can persist through its relationship with the Republic. In The Old Republic's class-story era, the struggle among its noble houses also affects its ties to the competing galactic powers. That later situation gives some substance to an institution that can otherwise sound remote. A senator's seat belongs to a world with internal politics, influential families and arguments of its own. Decisions made on Coruscant have to operate through those existing societies, whose disagreements don't disappear when they join a larger government.

Knights of the Old Republic also shows why a connected galaxy can't be treated as a single political territory. Manaan's government regulates access to its kolto and maintains neutrality between the Republic and the Sith. Republic personnel can travel there and negotiate, yet they still have to deal with the Selkath authorities. A route makes Manaan accessible without giving Coruscant the right to command it. Throughout Republic history, inhabited space includes member worlds, independent governments and regions where other powers hold influence. Travel crosses those boundaries more readily than institutions do.

The Republic's survival doesn't mean uninterrupted peace. The same routes carry merchants and military forces, and worlds connected by trade can later find themselves on opposing sides.

The early Republic takes shape through the continuing work of keeping worlds in contact. Ships carry representatives to Coruscant, charts guide them along established courses, and the Senate gives their governments a place to conduct shared affairs. Local societies remain recognizable within that arrangement. Alderaan doesn't have to become Coruscant to participate, and Corellia's shipyards remain part of a world with its own interests. Across the distances between them, regular journeys make a common government workable. A decision reached in the capital can travel outward aboard a ship and become business for another world.

The defeated Dark Jedi were sent away from the territory they'd fought to change. After the Hundred-Year Darkness, the survivors of their struggle with the Jedi departed into exile and eventually reached Korriban. There they encountered a people who already had their own rulers, religious practices and command of the dark side. These were the Sith. The name belonged to a people before it became the title carried by generations of dark side masters, and the newcomers' arrival began the long process that joined those meanings together.

The conflict behind that journey had begun within the Jedi. In the older Legends history, the dissidents pursued uses of the Force that the Order rejected, including dark side experiments involving living creatures. Their ambitions brought them into a prolonged war with the Jedi they'd separated from. The name Hundred-Year Darkness describes that extended struggle, rather than the whole history of disagreement within the Order. By its end, the defeated faction had lost its attempt to prevail in the world of the Republic. Exile carried the surviving participants beyond that familiar setting.

Their defeat at Corbos brought the war to its close. 

Korriban wasn't an empty refuge waiting for settlers. The Sith had a civilization of their own, and their use of the Force hadn't begun with instruction from the exiles. Their traditions included sorcery and a social order in which strength in the dark side could carry religious and political weight. For the newcomers, that created an opening. They'd arrived after losing a war, but among the Sith they could present powers and knowledge that helped them claim a position above the people who received them.

The exiles gained ascendancy over the native Sith and became their rulers. The newcomers were treated as beings of extraordinary power, and their conquest established a lasting hierarchy. Ajunta Pall is remembered among the first Dark Lords associated with this foundation. The title attached a ruling outsider to the Sith people beneath him. A former Jedi could therefore become a Lord of the Sith without having been born a member of the Sith species, a distinction that later uses of the name can easily obscure.

That change didn't happen through a simple replacement of the local population. The exiles and the Sith became connected through intermixture as well as domination, and their descendants inherited elements of both communities. Across generations, ancestry and teaching became difficult to separate. A ruler might belong to the population descended from the native Sith while also inheriting the traditions of the fallen Jedi. The emerging Sith civilization carried both histories.

The physical Sith remained recognizable within that civilization. In The Golden Age of the Sith, red-skinned figures with distinctive facial features belong to the society that has developed from this encounter. Yet appearance alone doesn't explain someone's place within its ruling order. Sith identity has acquired associations with descent, service and knowledge of the dark side. When later stories call a human dark side adept a Sith, they're using a meaning that this early history helped make possible. The word has traveled beyond its original use for a people.

Korriban's tombs give that ruling tradition a durable presence. The Valley of the Dark Lords contains monuments to figures whose standing survives their deaths, and the burial places remain important to those who seek ancient Sith knowledge. A tomb can hold dangerous objects and teachings as well as the remains of a ruler. Within this culture, approaching the dead can become part of a living adept's pursuit of power. The authority claimed by the first exiles consequently outlasts the people who established it.

Knights of the Old Republic returns to that inheritance through Ajunta Pall's tomb. The player character encounters Pall's spirit, who speaks about his fall and the ruin associated with his former companions. His account is the testimony of a dead participant looking back on his own life. The encounter allows different responses, including an attempt to help him turn away from the darkness. Those player choices vary, but the setting consistently places an ancient fallen Jedi within the burial ground of the Sith.

Pall's presence also gives the early schism a personal consequence that the broad account of conquest can conceal. The exile who became a revered lord remains attached to the place where his power was commemorated. In the game, his recollection contains regret rather than the confidence his title might suggest. The monument and the person encountered inside it give different impressions of the same career. Someone approaching the tomb for an ancient weapon meets a figure troubled by what he became.

Beyond Korriban, the Sith realm developed apart from the Republic. The exiles' defeat had removed them from the institutions and territories where their rebellion began, while their success among the Sith gave their successors a separate society to inherit. The Republic didn't absorb this community, and the Jedi didn't oversee its teaching. Over time, that separation allowed a dark side tradition descended partly from Jedi dissidents to become established across an unfamiliar region, with its own rulers and competing claims to rank.

By the era depicted in The Golden Age of the Sith, those beginnings lie behind an established empire. Its lords inherit a civilization that neither the original exiles nor the native Sith had possessed before their encounter. The Hundred-Year Darkness ended with the rebels expelled from the Republic's world, but their departure carried their teachings to Korriban. There, conquest and generations of shared descent gave the surviving tradition a new name and a home among the Sith.


Gav and Jori Daragon reach Korriban while the Sith lords are gathered for the funeral of Marka Ragnos. Their ship has crossed into a region unfamiliar to the Republic, and its arrival interrupts a dispute already forming around the dead ruler's succession. Naga Sadow and Ludo Kressh both seek leadership. The strangers give that rivalry a new subject. Someone has found a passage into Sith territory, which means the empire's long separation from the Republic may be ending whether its rulers welcome the contact or fear it.

In the Star Wars Legends comics The Golden Age of the Sith and The Fall of the Sith Empire, the Daragons are hyperspace explorers whose search for usable routes has brought them into danger. A successful discovery could make their voyages worthwhile, but an unknown destination can also leave them without protection or a way to explain themselves. On Korriban, they encounter rulers who treat their arrival as a matter of imperial survival. Gav and Jori have reached an inhabited civilization, yet neither sibling can negotiate with it as an equal.

Kressh regards the outsiders as a threat and wants them killed. Sadow sees an opportunity to reach the worlds beyond the empire. Their disagreement grows out of different expectations about what contact will bring. Kressh fears the destruction of the Sith way of life through dealings with the Republic. Sadow believes the Republic can be conquered. The siblings' ship gives his ambition a practical direction, because its journey demonstrates that the distance between the two societies can be crossed.

Sadow doesn't wait for the other lords to agree. He arranges an attack that appears to involve enemies from the Republic, then uses the disturbance to remove the prisoners from captivity. The deception makes his warnings about an outside enemy seem more urgent. It also places Gav and Jori in his hands. They understand that he has saved them from execution, while he has created circumstances in which their gratitude and uncertainty can help him. The argument over succession now includes an apparent emergency that Sadow himself has manufactured.

The siblings are then drawn along different paths. Sadow begins teaching Gav to use the Force, giving him attention and instruction at a moment when he's stranded among strangers. Jori remains concerned with getting away and returning home. Sadow allows her escape to become useful to him by tracking her ship's passage back toward Republic space. Her flight carries a warning to the people she knows, but it also supplies the Sith with a route for their invasion. The same passage can carry an explorer home and bring a fleet after her.

Back in the Koros system, Jori has difficulty persuading others of what she's encountered. Her background and the trouble surrounding the Daragons' earlier voyages don't make her an obvious authority on an unknown empire. Empress Teta nevertheless takes the warning seriously, alongside the Jedi Odan-Urr, whose visions have already disturbed him. Teta has been fighting to unite the Koros worlds. She now has to consider an enemy beyond that local struggle, arriving by a route that her own people have only just discovered.

Sadow assembles his invasion while his rivalry with Kressh continues. He has gained the initiative through deception and military action, but the lords haven't become a settled community behind him. That matters because he is committing Sith forces to distant targets while leaving enemies within his own society. His decision brings the Great Hyperspace War into Republic territory. For those facing the attack, the danger arrives as ships and soldiers. They have little opportunity to untangle the succession dispute that helped send those forces toward them.

The invasion reaches worlds including Coruscant and Koros Major. On Coruscant, the Republic and its Jedi defenders face Sith troops and the creatures fighting alongside them. The capital's importance doesn't spare it from direct assault. Sadow also expands the apparent size of his forces through Sith illusion, projecting images that make the attacks seem more overwhelming. From his meditation sphere, he can influence battles far from his own position. Republic defenders are therefore fighting actual invading forces while also confronting appearances sustained by a distant Sith lord.

Gav's part in the campaign brings the war back to people he recognizes. On Koros Major, he encounters Aarrba, the Hutt who had helped the Daragons during their earlier difficulties. Sith warriors kill Aarrba, and Jori finds her brother amid the consequences of an invasion he has helped bring home. Gav's instruction under Sadow has placed him among the attackers. Whatever hopes he attached to that instruction, he now has to confront the suffering caused by the forces traveling under his new master's command.

Gav turns against Sadow and attacks his meditation sphere. The interruption breaks Sadow's concentration, and the illusions supporting the invasion disappear. The defenders can see that the attacking strength is smaller than it appeared. Their resistance has already mattered, and this disruption gives them a further opening. On the Republic side, Teta's forces help drive the invaders back. Sadow's method had allowed one Sith adept to enlarge the reach of his fleet, but Gav's attack exposes how much of that advantage depended on his continuing concentration.

The retreat doesn't bring Sadow back to a secure throne. Kressh has survived their earlier conflict and confronts him when he returns to Sith space. Sadow must fight another Sith fleet after the failure of his Republic campaign. He defeats Kressh, only to face Republic forces pursuing the retreating invaders. The competing claims that surrounded Ragnos's funeral have consumed ships and followers throughout the war. By the time the enemy reaches Sith territory, Sadow has spent strength both on conquest abroad and on rivals at home.

Sadow eventually escapes to Yavin Four with Massassi followers, leaving the imperial struggle behind him. Jori survives to carry the Daragons' story forward, while Gav dies after turning against the master who recruited him. The invasion fails, but the Republic's pursuit carries the fighting into Sith territory along the connection the Daragons opened.

Ulic Qel-Droma went to the Krath intending to destroy their movement from inside. He believed he could enter their service, learn how their leaders worked, and bring them down before they carried their war farther into the Republic. It was a dangerous undertaking for a Jedi already grieving his teacher. Arca Jeth had died during the attack on the Jedi gathering at Deneba, and Ulic's response carried the urgency of that loss. He wanted to reach the people responsible. His companions couldn't persuade him that entering their stronghold would leave him dangerously exposed.

The Krath already control territory and command armed followers. Their leaders, Satal Keto and Aleema Keto, belong to the ruling family of the Empress Teta system. Their interest in forbidden teachings has grown into a movement capable of overthrowing the existing order. Ulic is approaching people who have seized the means to govern and wage war. To get close enough to hurt them, he has to place himself within the institutions they've taken over.

Satal answers Ulic's arrival with imprisonment and torture. The Krath also subject him to a Sith poison that helps turn his emotions toward the dark side. This gives his time among them a bodily cost as well as an emotional one. He's already angry over Arca's death, and his captors work upon that anger while he can't freely leave. Ulic had entered the system believing that his Jedi training would let him withstand the danger. The people holding him have their own methods for changing the man who came to deceive them.

His path then meets Exar Kun's. Kun comes to the Empress Teta system prepared to eliminate a rival, and the two fallen Jedi fight. They've reached this encounter through different experiences. Kun deliberately sought forbidden teachers and ancient Sith sites. Ulic entered an active enemy government with a plan to defeat it. By the time they face each other, both are drawing on the dark side. Their shared Jedi background makes their confrontation especially consequential. Each has training that once belonged in the Republic's defense.

Their duel is interrupted by the appearance of ancient Sith spirits associated with their amulets. Marka Ragnos names Kun the Dark Lord of the Sith and places Ulic beside him as his apprentice. The encounter gives their rivalry a direction in which they can cooperate. Ulic brings his place among the Krath, while Kun brings the power and followers he's gathered. Ulic's infiltration has ended with him accepting a Sith allegiance alongside another fallen Jedi. Aleema's government and Kun's growing following can now contribute to a common war against the Republic Ulic originally meant to protect.


Exar Kun brought the war into the places where Jedi expected to find their own students. He gathered younger members of the Order around him and used a Sith holocron to draw them under the influence of ancient spirits. These recruits could travel among Jedi who knew them, approach their teachers, and carry the conflict into communities that hadn't yet faced a Sith fleet. The attacks that followed cost the Order experienced masters at the moment the Republic needed them. On distant worlds, a familiar apprentice could arrive carrying an allegiance their teacher hadn't recognized.

Crado and Sylvar were pulled onto opposite sides of this division. Crado followed Kun, while Sylvar resisted the Sith and grew angry at what they were doing to the Jedi. Their attachment made the war painfully immediate. Kun's followers weren't strangers arriving from some unknown civilization. They included people who'd trained alongside the Jedi now opposing them. The killings of masters also broke the ordinary passage of teaching between generations. Surviving students had to face the possibility that companions they'd trusted had helped destroy the people who'd raised them within the Order.

Ulic had defeated Mandalore in single combat and gained his allegiance, bringing Mandalorian warriors into the Sith coalition. With their support and the strength of the Krath, he carried the campaign toward the Republic's capital. His attack on Coruscant put the fighting close to the Senate itself. Yet the coalition behind him had its own rivalries. Aleema betrayed Ulic during the assault, and he was captured. A commander who'd helped direct the war now faced judgment in the city he'd attacked. The Republic had an opportunity to hold him accountable, though his capture left Exar Kun free to act elsewhere.

Kun entered the Senate during Ulic's trial and used the Force to immobilize those gathered there. Vodo-Siosk Baas, his former teacher, confronted him. Vodo had known Kun as a gifted student whose aggression and appetite for forbidden knowledge had grown beyond his instruction. Now he faced him in the Republic's political center, where the consequences of that earlier failure surrounded them. Kun killed Vodo and freed Ulic. The rescue denied the Republic its prisoner and cost the Jedi another teacher, while demonstrating how far Kun could reach beyond his base on Yavin.

After Aleema's betrayal and Ulic's rescue, she was sent with Crado to attack the Republic fleet using Naga Sadow's ancient Sith battleship. The vessel carried a weapon capable of disturbing stars, a surviving piece of Sith warfare that could endanger whole inhabited systems. Aleema used it in the Cron Cluster. The resulting stellar destruction overwhelmed her own force as well. Crado's commitment to Kun brought him into that catastrophe, far beyond the training grounds where Sylvar had known him.

The disaster spread toward Ossus, where the Jedi had gathered knowledge across generations. Its approaching destruction forced an evacuation. Jedi worked to save lives and carry away what they could from their libraries, while the Sith treated the emergency as an opportunity. Kun and Ulic came to seize Jedi treasures as their enemies struggled to remove them. Ossus had been a place where students could study the accumulated work of the Order. Now the same collections had to be moved under pressure, with a hostile force arriving before the evacuation was complete.

Master Ood Bnar remained with a cache of lightsabers. As a Neti, he could take a rooted, tree-like form, and he used that transformation to shelter the weapons from Kun. The encounter gives the loss of Ossus a concrete scale. There were people leaving, records being rescued, and objects that a guardian chose to protect in place. Kun couldn't take everything he wanted. Even so, the evacuation scattered a community whose teaching had depended on a shared home, and the damage to that home would outlast the armies fighting around it.

Cay Qel-Droma confronted his brother on Ossus. He still hoped to bring Ulic back, despite everything Ulic had done. Their meeting carried the history of their training under Arca Jeth and Cay's repeated refusal to abandon him. Ulic answered that effort with a duel and killed Cay. The violence ended the possibility Cay had kept open through the earlier confrontations. Ulic then recognized what he'd done, and the conviction that had carried him through the campaign collapsed in grief beside his brother.

Nomi Sunrider witnessed Cay's death. She turned her Force abilities against Ulic and severed his connection to the Force. For someone who'd lived as a Jedi and then fought as a Sith commander, the change reached through almost every part of his experience. He could no longer draw on the power he'd used in battle or feel the surrounding life as he'd once felt it. Nomi had also known him before his fall. Her action came out of an encounter between people whose affection and shared past had survived into a war that was destroying them.

Ulic then helped the Jedi reach Exar Kun on Yavin Four. His knowledge of Kun's refuge gave the surviving Jedi a destination for their gathered strength. On Yavin, Kun had built his power among the Massassi and the temples they'd raised. Faced with the Jedi approaching, he prepared a ritual that consumed the lives of his Massassi followers so he could leave his body behind. The people who'd sustained his rule paid for his attempted escape. His physical defeat therefore didn't bring an ordinary surrender, or a prisoner the Republic could put on trial.

The Jedi joined their power against Yavin, and the confrontation devastated the jungle around Kun's temples. His spirit survived, bound to the ruins. The Sith campaign had lost its leaders in the field: Ulic was helping those he'd fought, and Kun could no longer lead fleets or recruit openly. Elsewhere, the Mandalorian assault on Onderon failed, and Mandalore's retreat to Dxun ended in his death. The alliances that had carried the offensive across Republic space came apart. For the survivors on Ossus, victory arrived amid evacuation, bereavement, and the work of carrying their Order's remaining knowledge somewhere it could be taught again.

Revan called for Jedi to join the Republic's defense while the Council still counseled restraint. Malak joined him, and other Jedi followed them into the Mandalorian Wars without the Order's approval. For those volunteers, the Republic's need was already visible in the fighting. Its soldiers were facing an expanding enemy, and Jedi training offered abilities that could help them. Revan gave that conviction a practical direction. A Jedi who disagreed with the Council now had someone to follow, and a war in which to serve.

The disagreement ran through an Order whose members shared much of the same education. Revan and Malak had learned to protect others, to recognize suffering, and to accept responsibilities beyond their own lives. Those teachings helped make intervention persuasive. Remaining apart from the fighting could feel difficult to reconcile with them. The Council, however, also expected Jedi to examine consequences that weren't immediately apparent. The dispute concerned how those obligations should guide action when the Republic was under attack, and whether an individual Jedi could decide that the Council had waited long enough.

In Knights of the Old Republic, Bastila describes Revan's departure as defiance of the Council. Her account carries the perspective of a Jedi who accepts the Order's authority and regards its warnings seriously. The volunteers had broken with a decision made by their own teachers. Bastila's concern makes sense within an institution that gives its members dangerous abilities and expects discipline in their use. A Jedi entering a war would face decisions about killing, obedience, and the lives of soldiers. The Council couldn't regard that commitment as an ordinary difference of opinion.

Revan's appeal reached Jedi who thought those risks had to be accepted. The Republic was already fighting, so refusing to participate didn't remove the danger faced by its people. It left the fighting to others. In the second game, the Jedi Exile's past gives that argument a particular face. The Exile had followed Revan to war and served as a general. Before exile became the condition of that character's life, there had been a decision to leave the Order's protection and accept responsibility within a military campaign.

The Exile's conversations allow the player to express different reasons for that decision. Compassion for people under attack can be central, while other responses give the past a different emphasis. Those are local player choices, rather than a single fixed confession. Across those variations, the shared history remains that the Exile went when the Council wouldn't commit the Order. That matters because Revan's movement contained individual Jedi with their own reasons for joining. Following the same leader didn't require every volunteer to have reached the battlefield through exactly the same reasoning.

Republic soldiers had another reason to welcome them. Carth Onasi remembers the Mandalorian Wars as a veteran, and he credits Revan with helping turn the Republic's military fortunes. His account explains why Revan's reputation couldn't be contained within Jedi debates. Soldiers judged the arrival of the volunteers through the campaign they were trying to survive. They needed leadership that could answer Mandalorian attacks. A Council warning about dangers beyond the immediate war had to compete with the experience of serving under commanders who seemed capable of meeting the enemy.

Canderous Ordo supplies a view from the opposing side. He remembers Revan as a formidable commander whose methods challenged the Mandalorians. His admiration belongs to a warrior who valued a capable opponent, so it has a different basis from Carth's gratitude. Together, their accounts help explain the standing Revan acquired during the war. Republic service and Mandalorian resistance both brought people into contact with his military ability. The Council could withhold its approval, but it couldn't prevent that reputation from growing among the people doing the fighting.

Malak was beside Revan in this break with the Council. The games remember them together as the Jedi who committed themselves to the Republic's cause and drew others after them. Revan receives most of the attention in later recollections, especially when characters discuss command. Malak's participation still matters to the division within the Order. The volunteers were following leading Jedi who'd publicly taken a different course from their teachers. Their departure made disagreement something other Jedi could act upon, rather than something they could only discuss among themselves.

The Council's reluctance also needs its own setting. The Jedi remembered earlier wars in which members of their Order had fallen to the dark side and carried destruction into the Republic. Exar Kun and Ulic Qel-Droma belonged to that history. The possibility that another conflict might draw Jedi into similar dangers wasn't remote to their teachers. In Knights of the Old Republic II, accounts of the Council's position include concern about a threat behind the Mandalorian fighting. They feared that committing themselves without understanding it could expose the Order to a wider danger.

That concern didn't give the Council an easy answer for someone facing a Mandalorian assault. Revan could direct a willing Jedi toward service with Republic forces. The Council asked that Jedi to accept restraint while the fighting continued. This difference helps explain the movement's appeal without requiring the Council's members to be indifferent to suffering. Their responsibilities included preserving Jedi who might be needed against a danger they couldn't yet clearly identify. Revan's followers believed the existing emergency already required those Jedi, and acted on that judgment.

The recording of the Exile's hearing in the second game gives the division a formal setting. Having returned to face the Council, the Exile stands before Jedi Masters and answers for going to war. The player can shape the responses, including how firmly the character defends that service. Even within the Order's chamber, the argument remains attached to lives outside it. The Council judges a Jedi's departure from its direction; the returning general can answer in terms of people who needed help. Neither side can erase what the other has been asked to bear.


Bao-Dur built the Mass Shadow Generator, and at Malachor V the general who would become the Jedi Exile gave the order to use it. The weapon caught ships and combatants in the destruction around the planet, striking Republic forces as well as Mandalorians. It brought the final battle of the Mandalorian Wars to a close at a cost that followed the survivors long after the fighting stopped. For Bao-Dur and the Exile, their later reunion would carry the weight of that shared act.

Malachor was the place where Revan brought the war to its conclusion. The Mandalorians had pushed the Republic through a campaign that drew soldiers and Jedi far from the worlds they were defending. By the final engagement, Revan commanded a force capable of defeating them. He also faced Mandalore, the leader whose authority held their campaigns together. Canderous later recounts Revan's victory over Mandalore in single combat. That defeat removed their leader while the battle around Malachor shattered the forces that had followed him.

The Mass Shadow Generator made that military defeat devastating. Bao-Dur's machine exploited the gravitational conditions at Malachor, dragging vessels into the catastrophe. The ruined planet encountered in Knights of the Old Republic II is the lasting physical result. Its broken surface and the wreckage around it belong to a battlefield that was never restored to anything resembling an ordinary inhabited world. The war's final engagement left a place where even reaching the ground means entering the remains of its destruction.

Bao-Dur had joined the Republic's war effort after the Mandalorians attacked his homeworld, Iridonia. His engineering skill gave the Republic the weapon used at Malachor, but his reasons for serving weren't detached from the invasion. When he speaks about the war, his anger toward the Mandalorians is part of the account. Defeating them had mattered to him personally. Afterward, he had to live with the means by which that defeat had been accomplished, including the Republic lives lost when his machine was activated.

His conversations with the Exile divide responsibility between people who knew each other during the fighting. Bao-Dur designed the device. The Exile gave the order. He doesn't treat the general as a stranger whose decision he can discuss without remembering his own part in it. He continues to address the Exile through their former military relationship, even after both have left that battlefield behind. Their familiarity makes Malachor an unfinished subject between companions who survived something they helped bring about.

For the Exile, the deaths at Malachor also arrived through the Force. The Jedi had formed bonds with others during the war, and the loss of those lives became unbearable. Knights of the Old Republic II connects the Exile's survival to a withdrawal from the Force at that moment. Cut off from the connection through which a Jedi ordinarily senses living beings, the Exile survived the destruction with an absence that remained for years. The general returned from the war unable to reach the Force as before.

That break happened before the Council's sentence of exile. The Masters' later examination of the returning Jedi belongs to the aftermath of an injury already sustained at Malachor. They encountered someone whose condition disturbed their understanding of what a Jedi could survive. The player can shape how the Exile speaks about wartime service and its consequences, but the central history remains: the order was given, the battlefield was destroyed, and the Exile came away severed from the Force.

Other Jedi who survived Revan's campaign carried a different experience away from the same war. They'd fought under his direction and remained with forces whose victories had strengthened his position. Malachor destroyed an enemy army, while the surviving Republic fleet still had a commander and a military organization. The Exile's departure wasn't the common ending for everyone who had followed Revan. Some returned from the battlefield within the force that had won, still attached to its leadership and its wartime habits.

Kreia later gives that survival a darker interpretation. She describes Revan as someone who understood how war could change Jedi and draw them away from their earlier loyalties. In her account, Malachor helped determine who could continue in his service. That's her reading of Revan's purpose, rather than a direct account from Revan himself. It does fit her sustained interest in the people the war left behind: Jedi accustomed to killing, soldiers devoted to a commander, and an Exile whose response separated them from the others.

Canderous remembers the defeat from the Mandalorian side. He respects Revan's ability even while describing the destruction of the cause he'd served. Mandalore's death and the loss at Malachor broke the campaign that had brought his people into Republic space. Surviving Mandalorians had to find lives beyond an army advancing under their own leader. Canderous eventually works as a mercenary on Taris. His account gives the defeat a personal continuation, through a veteran whose skills survived after the war that had given them a collective purpose.

Bao-Dur's later work on Telos gives another survivor a specific occupation after Malachor. He helps the effort to restore a world devastated during the conflicts that followed the Mandalorian Wars. His engineering is directed toward making damaged land habitable again. The work can't undo the use of the Mass Shadow Generator, and he doesn't speak as though it has removed his responsibility. It does give him something concrete to repair. When he meets the Exile there, he can help the former general again, this time amid an attempt to restore life.

The Mandalorian Wars ended with Revan victorious and the Mandalorian campaign broken. Malachor remained ruined. Bao-Dur survived with the knowledge of what his machine had done, while the Exile left with the Force beyond reach. Ships and soldiers continued under Revan's command, carrying the experience of the final battle with them. The surrender ended the fighting between those armies. The people who came away from Malachor still had to live with what they'd done there.


Admiral Saul Karath brought his knowledge of the Republic fleet into Sith service. He'd held senior command during the Mandalorian Wars, and officers who remained loyal to the Republic had known him as a colleague and superior. When he changed sides, the Republic lost an experienced commander to an enemy who understood its military from within. Revan and Malak returned with the same advantage on a much larger scale. In Knights of the Fallen Empire, Zakuul enters the war through the Eternal Empire and its fleet. Its forces attack Republic and Sith alike. The familiar division between those two sides no longer explains who can command the galaxy's major powers. Each now faces an enemy capable of imposing terms on it. The Eternal Fleet gives Zakuul a reach that neither government can comfortably resist, and both are reduced to paying tribute. Their governments survive, but they have to conduct their affairs beneath the threat of another empire's intervention.

Valkorion's household also becomes part of galactic politics. Arcann, Thexan and Vaylin are his children with Senya, a former Knight of Zakuul. Thexan dies at Arcann's hands before the Outlander's struggle against the Eternal Empire takes shape. Arcann's anger toward his father and his place within this family become immediate concerns for everyone living under Zakuulan rule. Senya eventually joins the resistance against her own children. Her involvement gives the opposition someone who knows the imperial family, while leaving her to face the personal cost of fighting it.

The player character enters this upheaval as the Outlander. During the encounter with Valkorion, the Emperor's physical body is killed, and his presence takes up residence within the Outlander's mind. Arcann seizes the throne, while the Outlander is imprisoned in carbonite. This removes the player from public events while Zakuul consolidates its position. When Lana Beniko helps free the Outlander, the Republic and Sith Empire are already living with the consequences of defeat. Returning to the war means dealing with a different political situation from the one the player left behind.

Lana's rescue leads toward an Alliance based on Odessen. Its membership crosses boundaries that once determined nearly every military allegiance in the conflict. Republic soldiers, Sith, smugglers and specialists can work toward the same campaign against Arcann. Theron Shan and Lana are especially useful examples of that arrangement. Their backgrounds lie on opposing sides of the old war, yet both help build the organization around the Outlander. Odessen gives these recruits a common headquarters away from either capital, where cooperation can become part of daily military work.

The Alliance has to assemble the means to challenge Zakuul while the Eternal Fleet remains a threat. Its campaign therefore involves recruitment, intelligence and finding weaknesses that conventional Republic or Sith forces haven't overcome. The Gravestone, an ancient warship capable of fighting the Eternal Fleet, gives the resistance an unusual military asset. Its importance helps explain why the Alliance can grow beyond a gathering of fugitives. It has leadership, a base and a means of confronting the fleet that made Zakuul's demands enforceable across the older powers' territories.

Arcann's reign eventually gives way to Vaylin's, bringing another member of Valkorion's family onto the Eternal Throne. The succession doesn't settle the conflict with the Alliance. Vaylin continues the struggle, and the danger posed by the imperial household remains bound up with Valkorion's treatment of his children. Arcann's later place in events depends on the player's decisions, including whether he survives and has an opportunity to change. Across those different paths, the broad historical movement remains the same: the Alliance challenges Zakuul while its ruling family fractures around the throne.

Throughout this campaign, Valkorion speaks to the Outlander from within. He offers power and advice, and the player must continue acting while carrying the presence of the former Emperor. His apparent assistance belongs to a larger attempt to possess the Outlander's body. In Knights of the Eternal Throne, that attempt becomes the central confrontation. The fight reaches into the Outlander's mind, where Valkorion tries to make his domination complete. The Outlander defeats him there, with members of his family participating in the struggle against him.

Victory leaves the Alliance in possession of the Eternal Throne and the power associated with the fleet. For a time, Odessen is the center of an organization whose reach exceeds what its beginnings might have suggested. The Outlander can approach that position as a ruler or as a protector, according to player decisions. Either way, the Republic and Sith Empire still exist. Their officers and citizens haven't all transferred their loyalties to the Alliance, and cooperation against Zakuul hasn't erased the governments, military traditions or territorial interests that divided them.

The Alliance's command of the Eternal Fleet also fails to provide a lasting settlement. During the events surrounding Iokath and the later struggle involving Zildrog, the machinery behind its military advantage becomes vulnerable, and the fleet is destroyed. That loss changes what Odessen can demand and what it needs from potential allies. The Alliance continues, but it can no longer rely on overwhelming fleet strength to keep the older powers beneath it. The Republic and Sith Empire recover room to pursue their own campaigns.

Onslaught returns the Republic and Empire to open war. Fighting over places such as Onderon and Corellia again makes their competing military needs central to the story, with the Alliance drawn into those alignments. Later, Echoes of Oblivion brings another confrontation with an Emperor remnant threatening Satele Shan and her students. The struggle takes place within their connected minds and includes help from people drawn from the Emperor's past. His persistence reaches beyond the fall of Zakuul, even as the Republic-Sith conflict continues around the people confronting him.


Much later, in the age of the New Sith Wars, the man who will become Darth Bane works in the cortosis mines on Apatros before he ever enters a Sith academy. The mining company keeps its workers dependent through debt, and the war reaches them through visiting soldiers and the demand for valuable ore. His name is Dessel, and his route into the Sith begins with a killing and a need to escape. By this stage of the Republic's history, armies can draw recruits from people whose most immediate concern is getting away from the life they've already got.

A long stretch of Star Wars Legends history separates Dessel's lifetime from the Republic and Empire of the earlier class stories. The New Sith Wars belong to a later age, with different leaders, different territories, and repeated changes in the fortunes of both sides. The centuries between weren't one uninterrupted campaign carried forward by the same Imperial government. Wars ended, Sith movements fractured, and new enemies emerged. When the fighting finally produces the Brotherhood of Darkness, the Republic has been dealing with recurring Sith challenges for generations.

The beginning of the New Sith Wars is associated with Darth Ruin, a former Jedi who gathers followers and establishes a new Sith movement. His break with the Jedi begins another period in which knowledge carried out of the Order helps create an enemy for it. Ruin's followers eventually turn against him, and his death leaves the conflict without its founding leader. Sith ambitions survive him. Across the wars that follow, different rulers try to gather enough strength to defeat the Republic while also defending their positions against other Sith.

That competition makes the later wars difficult to describe as a simple contest between two stable governments. Sith lords hold territory and command forces, but they also fight one another. A victory against the Republic doesn't settle who will inherit the conquered worlds or command the next offensive. The Republic, meanwhile, loses its reach across parts of the galaxy as prolonged warfare disrupts the connections between worlds. Keeping a government seated on Coruscant doesn't mean that every distant system can expect its fleets to arrive.

The Jedi become increasingly involved in military leadership during these struggles. Some take on the responsibilities of governing and defending territories, and Jedi commanders gather forces around their own leadership. For people living far from dependable Republic assistance, a Jedi able to organize a defense has immediate practical importance. That arrangement also changes the Order's daily work. Campaigns require commanders, recruits, and supplies, and Jedi who might otherwise have taught or mediated disputes spend their lives directing soldiers against Sith armies.

Lord Kaan attempts to overcome the divisions among the Sith by gathering them into the Brotherhood of Darkness. He brings rival leaders into an alliance that recognizes them as equals, with Kaan directing their common war effort. The arrangement gives Sith who have spent their lives competing for supremacy a place in the same military enterprise. Kaan's personal influence helps hold them together. His persuasion also draws on the Force, so the apparent agreement among his followers owes something to his ability to press upon their minds.

The Brotherhood trains new Sith at academies, including the one on Korriban where Dessel eventually arrives. Before that promotion, he serves as an ordinary soldier in the Brotherhood's forces. In the Gloom Walkers, his perception of danger and his effectiveness in combat help distinguish him from the recruits around him. The army gives him opportunities that the mines never offered, although it also expects obedience to officers whose decisions can get their soldiers killed. His abilities bring him to the attention of those who recognize his potential in the Force.

At Korriban, Bane studies under instructors who are preparing students to serve the Brotherhood. The academy includes practice with weapons, contests between apprentices, and instruction in the dark side. Bane's strength attracts attention, but his progress isn't steady. After killing another student during training, he struggles with guilt and loses confidence in his abilities. Sirak, a powerful rival at the academy, defeats him. For a time, the former miner who seemed unusually promising becomes someone the other students can dismiss.

Bane begins spending more time with the academy's archives, where the teachings of earlier Sith give him a way to question what Kaan's followers are building. The Brotherhood discourages the title Darth, treating it as a reminder of the rivalries that once divided the Sith. Bane sees value in the older insistence on individual supremacy. His disagreement develops through study and through his experience among the apprentices, where claims of fellowship coexist with humiliation, jealousy, and lethal competition. Kaan's arrangement starts to look to him like a settlement that conceals those ambitions.

Lord Hoth gathers the Army of Light to oppose the Brotherhood. His forces include Jedi who answer his call alongside the soldiers fighting with them, and the campaign places him under the strain of repeated losses and difficult decisions. His friendship with Lord Farfalla suffers as the commanders disagree over the conduct of the war. Both remain committed to defeating the Sith, yet that shared purpose doesn't spare them resentment. The Jedi army has its own exhausted leaders, and their disagreements affect how they work together in the field.

The campaigns converge on Ruusan, where Hoth and Kaan commit their forces to a succession of battles. The world becomes the focus of a struggle that draws Jedi away from other duties and Sith away from their separate holdings. Kaan needs the Brotherhood to maintain its unity under pressure. Hoth needs enough support to finish the campaign without losing the army he's assembled. Neither commander has an unlimited reserve of people who can replace the dead, and each battle leaves fewer experienced fighters available for the next one.

By the time Bane rejects the Brotherhood's teachings, both armies are concentrated around leaders carrying the burden of those losses. Kaan has brought competing Sith into a common force, and Hoth has gathered Jedi into a dedicated army. On Ruusan, those efforts have placed the opposing orders within reach of one another, committed to a battlefield neither leader is prepared to abandon.


Kaan gathers the Brotherhood inside a cavern on Ruusan, preparing a weapon that requires the Sith to join their strength in the Force. Outside, Lord Hoth's Army of Light has brought the campaign close to its end. Kaan still believes he can destroy the Jedi approaching him. Bane understands what the ritual will do to its users, and he's already arranged to be elsewhere when they complete it. The Brotherhood's remaining leaders are about to commit themselves to a weapon that will leave none of them alive.

In the Legends novels Darth Bane: Path of Destruction and Rule of Two, this ending grows out of Bane's rejection of Kaan's Brotherhood. Bane supplies Kaan with the knowledge needed to create the thought bomb, drawn from older Sith teachings. Kaan accepts it as a way out of military defeat. Bane intends the ritual to remove the Sith gathered under Kaan, whose shared leadership he considers a failure. Their disagreement has moved beyond the academy and its arguments. Bane is helping the Brotherhood destroy itself.

Before the final ritual, Bane has already demonstrated how dangerous their combined power can be. He leads the Sith in an attack through the Force that sets the forest ablaze. Maintaining it requires concentration, and the Brotherhood's leaders eventually break away to pursue the battle by more familiar means. To Bane, their inability to sustain the effort confirms his contempt for them. Kaan returns to a plan built around his assembled followers, even though those followers are frightened, exhausted, and increasingly close to defeat.

The thought bomb requires those same Sith to surrender themselves to a collective ritual. Its effects reach beyond the destruction of their bodies. The beings caught within it have their spirits trapped, leaving a lasting wound beneath Ruusan. Kaan approaches the ceremony with confidence in his own ability to direct it. Lord Qordis has already died at Bane's hands, and Githany, who recognizes the danger too late, cannot escape what Kaan has begun. The Brotherhood has gathered its surviving strength into a place from which there will be no recovery.

Hoth learns enough about the weapon to understand that confronting Kaan will cost him his life. He enters the cavern with Jedi who accept that danger, while Farfalla remains outside with the surviving forces. Hoth's decision brings his long pursuit of the Brotherhood to its end. When Kaan releases the thought bomb, the Jedi who have come to face him die along with the Sith. Farfalla survives to deal with the aftermath, but the commander around whom the Army of Light formed won't return from the cavern.

Bane survives because he stays beyond the weapon's reach. His survival gives him the opportunity to begin again without Kaan's followers, their ranks, or their claim to equality. He retains the title Darth and sets out the arrangement that will govern his new Sith line: a master who possesses power and an apprentice who seeks it. There will be no Brotherhood to recruit replacements or settle disputes. Bane expects the apprentice eventually to challenge the master, and he intends that struggle to strengthen the succession.

His reasoning comes from the rivalries he found among the Sith. Several weaker followers could cooperate to kill a stronger leader, then divide what remained among themselves. Bane believes that process allows Sith knowledge and ability to decline. By limiting the line to a master and an apprentice, he means to require a successor capable of overcoming the person who trained them. It's a demanding and dangerous design. The master has to teach someone who may become a lethal rival, while the apprentice has to survive long enough to learn.

On Ruusan, Bane finds the child who will become that apprentice. Zannah has been caught in a war brought to her world by armies far larger than anything she could resist. After Jedi kill her companion Laa, one of Ruusan's native bouncers, she lashes out through the Force and kills them. Bane recognizes the strength in her reaction. He takes her as his pupil, giving the new Sith line a future at the very place where the Brotherhood has ended.

Zannah's cousin Darovit complicates that departure. He wants to rescue her from Bane and tries to intervene, but Zannah maims his hand through the Force. Her action keeps Bane from killing him. Darovit remains alive on Ruusan, separated from the cousin he hoped to bring back. For Zannah, entering the Sith doesn't erase the people she knew before the war. It leaves her making a brutal decision about how to protect one of them while remaining beside the man who will train her.

Beyond Ruusan, the Republic begins reorganizing after the wars. Under Chancellor Tarsus Valorum, the Ruusan Reformation curtails the military arrangements that have grown around the conflict. The Republic steps away from the great standing forces of the war era, and the Jedi relinquish their military authority. Hoth's Army of Light belongs to the conflict that has just ended. Jedi service will continue within a Republic whose civilian government is reasserting its place, rather than through Jedi commanders maintaining armies and governing territories of their own.

Those changes answer problems the war has made visible. Jedi drawn into prolonged campaigns have become generals, and the demands of holding territory have carried them into political rule. Reform redirects the order toward its duties as peacekeepers under the Republic. Worlds emerging from the fighting can begin rebuilding without supplying another campaign against Kaan. With the Brotherhood destroyed, that seems an achievable settlement. Bane's survival, however, means the Sith danger has taken a form the Republic's victorious armies weren't organized to pursue.

Bane has no intention of immediately replacing Kaan's armies. He and Zannah will need secrecy, knowledge, and time to establish their succession. The Republic's recovery can proceed without a Sith fleet appearing over another member world, and the Jedi can return to responsibilities the war displaced. On Ruusan, Farfalla and the survivors are left with the dead and the damage of the final campaign. As the Republic rebuilds its institutions, Bane leaves the battlefield with Zannah, carrying the Sith teachings into the life of a master and a single apprentice.

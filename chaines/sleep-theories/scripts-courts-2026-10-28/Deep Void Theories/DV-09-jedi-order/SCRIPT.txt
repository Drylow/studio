Obi-Wan wins his duel, but the outcome offers him little relief. Anakin tries to leap past him from below, and Obi-Wan cuts off his remaining organic limbs. Anakin falls beside the lava and catches fire. Obi-Wan speaks of the hopes once placed in him and of his love for him, then takes Anakin's lightsaber and leaves. He returns to Padme's ship with the weapon of the apprentice he believes he's lost. Sidious later reaches Mustafar and finds Anakin alive, so the two confrontations leave both Sith still present.

Padme is taken to Polis Massa, where Obi-Wan, Yoda, and Bail gather beyond the Empire's immediate reach. The medical droid reports that it can't explain her decline despite finding her physically healthy. She gives birth to twins and names them Luke and Leia. Before she dies, she tells Obi-Wan that she believes there's still good in Anakin. That belief belongs to Padme at the end of her life. Obi-Wan has just returned from fighting him and has seen how far Anakin was willing to go.

The twins give the surviving Jedi a responsibility they can still carry out. Yoda says the children must be hidden and separated, with their safety tied to keeping the Sith from discovering them. There's no proposal to bring them into a replacement Temple or announce a new Council. Their parents' public lives have become dangerous connections. Anakin is now bound to Sidious, and Padme has died as the Republic disappears. Obi-Wan, Yoda, and Bail arrange homes where the children can grow up without openly carrying that history.

Bail offers to take Leia to Alderaan, where he and his wife will raise her as their daughter. Obi-Wan will take Luke to Anakin's family on Tatooine and watch over him there. These arrangements draw on people outside the Jedi's old teaching community. Bail can give Leia a family, while Owen and Beru Lars can receive Luke far from Coruscant. The children will live separately, and the adults involved will keep the circumstances of their birth concealed. Their protection depends on these particular households accepting them.

Before they part, Yoda gives Obi-Wan another task for his solitude. He says Qui-Gon has returned from beyond death and that he'll teach Obi-Wan how to commune with his former master. Obi-Wan's surprise brings a different possibility into their departure. His life on Tatooine will include watching over Luke, but Yoda also gives him something to learn. The Temple's teaching rooms are gone from his life. A connection with the teacher who first brought him into adulthood may still continue beyond them.

Leia arrives in Bail's arms on Alderaan. Obi-Wan carries Luke to Owen and Beru at their moisture farm, then leaves the child with them. Yoda goes into exile, and Obi-Wan remains on Tatooine. Neither has restored the Order they served. They have changed a dangerous signal, confronted the Sith, and placed two children in homes beyond the ruined Temple. Their work now continues at that smaller scale, with Leia held by her new parents and Luke in Beru's arms beside Owen.

Obi-Wan places Anakin Skywalker's lightsaber in Luke's hands on Tatooine before Luke knows what happened to its owner. In A New Hope, the weapon connects him to a father he's been told was a navigator on a spice freighter. Obi-Wan describes a Jedi Knight who fought beside him, then attributes Anakin's death to Darth Vader. Luke receives a family inheritance and an account of the Jedi together. The account leaves out the fact that Vader is his father, a silence that will shape his apprenticeship.

Luke doesn't enter a surviving school. His first teacher is an old man living beyond the farm where Owen and Beru have raised him. Obi-Wan explains the Force as an energy field created by living things, surrounding them and connecting the galaxy. For Luke, these ideas arrive alongside the possibility of leaving Tatooine. He initially refuses the journey to Alderaan because he has obligations at home. Only after discovering the destruction of the homestead and the deaths of his aunt and uncle does he commit to going.

Aboard the Millennium Falcon, Obi-Wan introduces Luke to perceiving through the Force with a training remote. Luke begins to respond without sight, but has little time to develop that experience before the Falcon reaches the Death Star.

Obi-Wan's death ends their time together before Luke has learned much about the Order itself. Luke sees Vader strike his teacher after Obi-Wan raises his blade and stops defending himself. Soon afterward, Obi-Wan's voice reaches him during his escape. At the Battle of Yavin, that voice urges him to rely on the Force. Luke turns off his targeting computer and fires the shot that destroys the station. His success gives the teaching enormous personal weight. He has acted on something his teacher can no longer explain face to face.

By The Empire Strikes Back, Luke has learned to draw his lightsaber to his hand with the Force, but he still needs instruction. Obi-Wan appears to him on Hoth and directs him to Yoda on Dagobah. Luke arrives expecting a great warrior. Instead, he grows irritated with the small stranger who interrupts his search, handles his belongings and questions his expectations. Yoda is examining the pupil before revealing himself. Luke's impatience is already part of the difficulty the teacher will have to address.

On Dagobah, Yoda challenges Luke's impatience and his assumptions about what the Force can do. Luke finally receives sustained instruction, although his education still depends on a single living teacher far from any school.

Yoda also directs him into the cave where Luke encounters a vision of Vader. Luke takes his weapons despite Yoda's advice, strikes the apparition and sees his own face inside the severed helmet. The experience places Luke's fear of Vader alongside the possibility of becoming like him. Later, visions of Han and Leia in pain pull his attention toward Bespin. Yoda warns that the future remains difficult to read. Luke nevertheless leaves before completing his training, convinced that his friends need him.

On Bespin, Vader defeats Luke and severs his hand. Luke's attempt to rescue his friends has brought him into a confrontation for which he isn't prepared. Then Vader tells him that he's his father and offers him a place at his side. Luke rejects the offer and falls away from him, eventually calling to Leia for help. The revelation leaves him with questions about Obi-Wan as well as Vader. His teacher's account of Anakin can no longer explain the man Luke has just faced.

When Luke returns to Dagobah in Return of the Jedi, Yoda is dying. He confirms that Vader is Luke's father and tells Luke that he must confront him. After Yoda's death, Obi-Wan explains his earlier account through the change Anakin underwent when he became Vader. Luke doesn't accept that his father is beyond recovery. He also learns that Leia is his sister. His inheritance now includes a family history his teachers withheld, and he has to decide how to face Vader with that knowledge.

Luke surrenders to Imperial forces on Endor because he believes he can reach his father. Speaking with Vader, he appeals to the person Anakin once was. Vader takes him to the Emperor, who has arranged for Luke to witness the danger facing the Rebel fleet. Sidious encourages his anger and invites him to attack. When Luke finally strikes toward the Emperor, Vader blocks the blow. Their duel places Luke between his wish to save his father and the rage Sidious is trying to cultivate.

Vader's discovery that Luke has a sister breaks Luke's restraint. When Vader threatens to turn Leia, Luke attacks with enough fury to drive him back and cut off his hand. Looking at the damaged machinery, Luke then looks at his own artificial hand. He stops. With Vader beaten and the Emperor urging him to finish the killing, Luke throws away his lightsaber and declares his allegiance to the Jedi. Sidious answers with Force lightning, and Luke is left calling to his father while Vader watches.

Anakin intervenes, lifting Sidious and casting him down the shaft, suffering fatal injuries as he saves Luke. Before dying, he asks to see his son without the mask. Luke's teachers gave him exercises, warnings and a path toward confronting Vader. Luke carried those teachings into the throne room while continuing to believe his father could respond to him. On Endor, after Anakin's funeral, Luke sees Obi-Wan and Yoda beside the restored figure of his father. Obi-Wan, Yoda, and Anakin now appear together through the Force, leaving Luke to carry their different legacies forward.


Luke enters Ben Solo's hut while his nephew is asleep. By Luke's later account, he's already sensed darkness during Ben's training, but what he finds that night frightens him more deeply. He looks into Ben's mind and sees the destruction of people and things he loves. For a brief moment, he ignites his lightsaber. The impulse passes, and shame follows. Ben wakes before Luke can explain. From the bed, he sees his teacher standing over him with a weapon drawn.

The Last Jedi approaches that encounter through accounts that change as Rey learns more. When Luke first describes the loss of his school, he says Ben turned against him, destroyed the Temple and took some of the students away. Luke places himself at the scene as someone who came to confront an apprentice whose darkness had grown. His own part remains partly concealed. Rey hears the story of a teacher who failed to recognize the danger soon enough, without yet hearing what Ben saw when he opened his eyes.

Before that night, the school had grown out of Luke's decision to teach a new generation. Leia entrusted Ben to him, hoping Luke could help her son. Ben was Luke's nephew, the child of Han Solo and Leia Organa, and the grandson of Anakin Skywalker. That family connection brought expectations into the training. Luke recalls believing his own reputation and assuming that his standing as a Jedi would help him succeed. He'd become a figure people looked toward, and he accepted responsibility for students whose futures he couldn't guarantee.

The Force Awakens first describes the school's collapse through the people left behind. Han tells Rey and Finn that Luke had been training a new generation when a pupil turned against him and destroyed what he'd built. Han says Luke blamed himself and walked away. At that point, Luke's absence is a problem stretching across the galaxy. Leia wants her brother found, while the First Order wants to prevent his return. The failed school has left no visible teaching community for Rey to approach.

For Han and Leia, Ben's fall also belongs to the history of their family. Leia believes Snoke drew their son toward the dark side, and she still hopes Han can reach him. Han approaches Ben on Starkiller Base with that hope, calling him back toward home. Ben kills him. By the time Rey reaches Luke, she's already seen how far Ben's actions have carried him from his parents. Her questions about Luke's school concern someone who has become dangerous, and someone whose family hasn't stopped trying to recover him.

Ben gives Rey a different account through their connection in the Force. He remembers waking to find Luke above him with an ignited lightsaber, and he understands the encounter as an attempt to murder him. In this version, Ben raises his own weapon to defend himself against his uncle. The detail changes Rey's understanding of the ruined school. Luke had allowed her to picture him confronting an apprentice who was already lost. Ben describes being threatened in his sleep by the person responsible for teaching him.

When Rey confronts Luke, he gives the fuller admission. He acknowledges the moment in which he considered killing Ben to prevent the suffering he'd foreseen. He also says the impulse vanished almost immediately. The film shows Ben responding with his lightsaber and bringing the hut down around them. There isn't a sustained duel in which Luke pursues his nephew. The disaster grows out of a brief, terrible action and Ben's response to it. Luke's regret arrives before he can repair what Ben has seen.

Luke's description of the vision matters because he reacts to suffering that hasn't yet happened. He believes he can see what Ben will bring about, and for an instant he treats that future as something he can stop with a blade. His earlier training had included warnings about the difficulty of reading the future. Here, the threatened people are those he loves, and that knowledge doesn't steady him quickly enough. The audience is given Luke's frightened experience of the vision, rather than an assurance that every part of it was inevitable.

When Luke emerges from the wreckage, his school is burning. In his account, Ben has vanished with a handful of students, while the others have been killed. The films leave the individual students largely unnamed, so the loss reaches us through Luke's account and the ruined training site. These were pupils he'd gathered and undertaken to teach. Their deaths and disappearance turn his private failure with Ben into the destruction of a community. Luke survives, but he no longer believes he should continue the work that brought them together.

On Ahch-To, Luke has withdrawn to the site of the first Jedi Temple. Rey arrives carrying his old lightsaber, expecting to find a teacher willing to return. He throws it away and refuses the role she offers him. His account of the Jedi's failures connects his own school to the earlier Order, whose members couldn't prevent Darth Sidious from rising. That history helps explain his refusal, though Rey challenges the conclusion he draws. She's facing an immediate struggle, and Luke's retreat leaves her seeking help from a man determined to withhold it.

When Yoda visits Luke on Ahch-To, he urges him to pass on what he has learned, including failure itself. Luke has treated Ben's fall as the end of his right to teach. Yoda asks him to consider what a student might learn from an honest account of where a teacher went wrong.

Luke doesn't rebuild his school before his death. On Crait, he faces Ben through a projection in the Force and apologizes for failing him, while giving the Resistance time to escape. Ben remains hostile, and their encounter doesn't restore the relationship broken at the hut. Luke's final act nevertheless helps people beyond the island where he'd withdrawn. His school has fallen, and its students can't be brought back. He returns to service carrying that loss, without claiming that he's repaired it.


While Kylo Ren concentrates on Luke outside the Resistance base on Crait, the people inside have time to find another way out. Poe recognizes that Luke's appearance has given them an opportunity, and the surviving Resistance members follow the crystal foxes through the tunnels. At the blocked exit, Rey lifts the fallen rocks with the Force. Luke's intervention and Rey's arrival meet in that escape. He holds the First Order's attention, and she opens the passage through which Leia and the others can leave.

The Millennium Falcon carries something else away with them. Aboard the ship are the ancient Jedi books Rey took from Ahch-To. Luke's withdrawal hasn't left those writings beyond anyone's reach, and the burning tree hasn't consumed them. They're now traveling with a young woman who still has questions about the Force and has received only a brief, difficult introduction to Jedi teaching. The books can accompany her wherever she goes. Reading them, though, will require the work of someone willing to put their lessons into practice.

The Last Jedi gives another example of a teaching reaching beyond its original setting. On Canto Bight, the stable children retell Luke's confrontation with the First Order. One of them draws a broom into his hand with the Force, then looks toward the stars. He hasn't entered a Jedi school, and the film doesn't give him a teacher. What has reached him is an account of someone standing against the forces that dominate his world. Luke's action has become a story that children can share even where the Resistance has little practical reach.

By The Rise of Skywalker, Rey has a teacher in Leia. At the Resistance base on Ajan Kloss, Leia oversees her training, and Rey practices both meditation and physical exercises. Her attempts aren't effortless. She struggles to connect with the Jedi who came before her, and troubling visions interrupt her concentration. This gives the surviving tradition a recognizable daily shape again. Someone practices, loses focus, tries to understand what happened, and returns to a teacher who expects her to continue. There isn't a restored Temple around them, but instruction is taking place.

Leia brings her own unfinished Jedi training to that relationship. On Ahch-To, Luke later tells Rey that Leia trained with him and surrendered her lightsaber after a vision concerning her son's death. The film shows the siblings practicing together. Leia's instruction of Rey therefore draws on something she learned and carried through the years, alongside her responsibilities in the Resistance. Luke is gone, yet his pupil can still teach another person. For Rey, that means the loss of one teacher hasn't ended the possibility of learning from someone who knew him.

The books also help Rey with a practical problem. Luke's notes describe his search for Exegol and point her toward Pasaana, where he had been looking for a Sith wayfinder. Rey follows that unfinished investigation with Finn, Poe, Chewbacca, and the droids. Written knowledge gives them somewhere to begin, though it doesn't spare them the dangers of the journey. Luke couldn't complete the search himself. By preserving his findings, he leaves Rey a route she can investigate with people whose abilities and experience differ from her own.

On Pasaana, Rey encounters a wounded serpent in the tunnels beneath the sinking fields. Rather than kill it to clear their path, she approaches and heals its injury through the Force. She explains to BB-8 that she transferred some of her own life energy to the creature. Once healed, it moves away and reveals an exit. The action belongs to the Jedi work of helping a living being in front of them. Rey and her companions need to escape, and attending to the serpent's wound allows everyone to move on.

Rey uses that ability again after fighting Kylo Ren on the wreckage of the second Death Star. When Leia reaches out to her son and Rey wounds him, Rey then heals the injury she has inflicted. Their encounter leaves her frightened by her own capacity for violence, and she retreats to Ahch-To intending to remain there. Luke appears to her and challenges that retreat. He also gives her Leia's lightsaber and raises his old X-wing from the water. His help leaves her with the means to travel, as well as a reason to return.

At Exegol, Rey finally hears the past Jedi she had struggled to reach during her training. Their voices address her while she lies injured before the Emperor. Among them are Obi-Wan, Yoda, Anakin, Ahsoka, Kanan, and Luke. They encourage her to rise and draw strength from the Jedi who came before. The help answers a difficulty introduced in her exercises with Leia. She couldn't summon that connection when she wanted it at the base. Here, in danger and exhaustion, she receives it.

Rey gets back to her feet and faces Palpatine with the lightsabers of Luke and Leia. She crosses the blades against his lightning and turns it back upon him, destroying him. The effort leaves her dead until Ben Solo reaches her and gives his life to restore hers. Rey's survival therefore includes help from people with very different histories: the Jedi whose voices reach her, the siblings whose weapons she carries, and the man who has returned from his allegiance to the dark side.

The films leave Rey with the Jedi writings and the experience of being taught by Luke and Leia. They don't show her opening a school or assembling a new Council. Her inheritance already has uses within the story: she can follow an unfinished investigation, heal an injured creature, and accept help when she can no longer stand alone. On Tatooine, she buries Luke's and Leia's lightsabers near the Lars homestead. She then reveals a lightsaber of her own, its blade yellow. The old weapons remain together beneath the sand, while Rey carries one she can use herself.

A Tarnished follows the light of grace toward the Erdtree, while the heirs of its shattered order hold pieces they haven't managed to reunite. The tree is still standing. Its golden branches reach over a country where the war for succession has left no lord able to settle it. We'll follow that broken succession through the people who inherit it, then enter the Land of Shadow, where the history behind their inheritance becomes harder to separate from what they've tried to leave behind.

This telling includes full spoilers for Elden Ring and the released story of Shadow of the Erdtree, including their endings. Some events are revealed directly. Others reach us through people who have their own reasons for explaining the past as they do. Here, at the beginning, the situation is plain enough: the Elden Ring has been shattered, Queen Marika is missing, and the demigods who claimed its fragments have fought without producing a victorious successor. The Tarnished returns to a struggle that has already exhausted its first claimants.

The opening places this returning figure among people called back after death. Its summons reaches figures as different as Hoarah Loux, Goldmask, Fia, the Dung Eater, and Gideon Ofnir. The character we follow receives no comparable reputation in that introduction. They're another Tarnished, initially described as having no renown, entering a country where distinguished names already belong to someone else. That gives the journey an unusual starting point. The invitation to seek lordship reaches a person who doesn't arrive with a demigod's ancestry or an army waiting to receive them.

White Mask Varre is among the first people available to explain the newcomer's position. He recognizes the Tarnished and remarks on their lack of a maiden, connecting that absence with the practical difficulty of pursuing their calling. He also points them toward Stormveil Castle and its ruler, Godrick. His manner makes this sound like a settled route through the world, though his advice comes from a stranger with purposes of his own. For the returning Tarnished, even the first explanation of how to proceed arrives through someone they have little reason to understand.

Melina gives the Tarnished a more personal arrangement. When she appears at a site of grace, she offers to take on the role of a maiden and turn runes into strength. In exchange, she asks to be taken to the foot of the Erdtree. She also entrusts the traveler with Torrent, the spectral steed who becomes their companion across the country. Her offer answers an immediate difficulty raised by Varre. The Tarnished now has someone able to help them grow stronger, and that help carries a destination shared by both travelers.

Stormveil rises ahead as the first great seat of a claimant. Godrick holds a Great Rune, one of the fragments claimed after the Elden Ring was shattered. That makes the castle relevant to the summons, rather than merely an obstacle on a northern road. The Tarnished has entered a country whose would-be rulers possess parts of the thing they're being called to restore. Grace can point toward those rulers, and Melina can help the traveler reach them. The succession itself remains unsettled, with a returning outsider now approaching the gates.


Inside the Erdtree, the Elden Ring appears within Marika's broken body. Its lines overlap in a golden pattern, a structure the opening of Elden Ring has already shown being struck with a hammer. There's no band to slip onto a finger. The word ring names something that can be housed in a god, broken into great runes, and eventually repaired. Anyone seeking to become Elden Lord is approaching a damaged arrangement of the world itself, with living people still trying to exist under what remains of its rules.

Marika is the vessel of that arrangement. Enia, the Finger Reader at Roundtable Hold, explains her position when she speaks about the imprisoned queen. Marika carries the Elden Ring and is a god, while her consort holds the title of Elden Lord. Those roles belong together, though they aren't interchangeable. Becoming lord doesn't mean that the Tarnished will carry the Ring in the same way Marika does. The throne places a ruler beside the divine vessel, within an order that already has its own structure.

The Golden Order is the particular religious and governing arrangement associated with Marika's rule. The Elden Ring can sustain an order, while the Golden Order gives that order a recognizable form, with teachings, holy practices, and accepted distinctions between beings. Brother Corhyn teaches incantations and speaks as someone committed to those teachings. He regards Marika as the one true god. His certainty gives a human scale to something otherwise difficult to picture: a traveling cleric can understand his whole vocation through the place Marika occupies in the world.

The Ring's great runes bring this vast arrangement back to individual bodies. The demigods possess fragments after the Shattering, and recovered great runes can grant blessings to the Tarnished. Godrick's Great Rune is described as an anchor ring at the center of the Elden Ring. Once restored and activated, it raises the Tarnished's attributes. The fragment therefore has both a place within the larger pattern and a perceptible effect on its bearer. The demigods' inheritance includes pieces of something whose power still works, even though the whole has been damaged.

Deep within the storm at Farum Azula, Dragonlord Placidusax waits with his remaining heads raised toward an absent god. The Tarnished can reach him by lying down in a place among the ruins, where the surrounding destruction draws back and the ancient arena takes shape. His remembrance identifies him as an Elden Lord from the age before the Erdtree. That places a holder of the title far earlier than the royal household whose descendants now contest the Lands Between. Lordship already had a history before the familiar golden dynasty.

Placidusax's remembrance also says that his god fled, and that he continued waiting for its return. It doesn't name that god. What remains is a particular arrangement: an ancient lord, a departed divine partner, and a vigil that outlasts the world around him. His posture gives that account a physical presence. When the Tarnished arrives, Placidusax is still suspended at the center of his enclosure, withdrawn into the storm. The broken succession of the present has an older counterpart here, though his waiting belongs to a different age.

Elsewhere, the older history of living bodies gathers around the Crucible. The Aspects of the Crucible incantations describe it as the primordial form of the Erdtree, when life was blended together. This makes the Crucible part of the tree's own past. The immense golden tree emerged from an earlier condition in which the divisions between forms of life were less settled. Horns, tails, and other bodily growths belong to that account, and the game allows the Tarnished to encounter them in both sacred practices and living creatures.

At the First Church of Marika, in the Mountaintops of the Giants, Melina can repeat words that she identifies as Marika's own. They address Godfrey and his warriors before battle. Marika calls on them to put the giants to the sword and confine their flame, so that the Erdtree's age can begin. The church stands within the region where that war left its most visible remains. By the time the Tarnished arrives, the giants have been defeated, yet the flame their conquerors feared still burns above the mountains.

Marika's words place Godfrey beside her at the beginning of this expansion. She's the divine ruler associated with the Erdtree, and he's the lord who leads her battles. His title as first Elden Lord belongs to that royal order. The remembrance of Dragonlord Placidusax describes an Elden Lord from an age before the Erdtree, so Godfrey's title shouldn't erase the rulers who preceded him. His distinction concerns the reign taking shape around Marika, with its own dynasty, sacred center, and enemies.

The war in the mountains gave that strength a particular purpose. The Fire Giants tended a flame capable of burning the Erdtree, and Marika's order couldn't safely grow while that threat remained beyond its reach. The Mountaintops still contain the enormous bodies of fallen giants, with weapons driven through them. Their presence gives the campaign a scale that a royal title alone can't convey. Godfrey's victories involved peoples whose bodies, practices, and places of worship differed greatly from those gathered beneath the golden tree.

The final surviving Fire Giant bears an even more direct consequence. His remembrance explains that Marika found the flame could never be extinguished. She cursed him to remain at his forge and tend it for eternity. That account changes the meaning of the battlefield around him. The giants were defeated, but their conqueror couldn't remove the power at the center of their worship. The survivor was left with a task imposed by the ruler who had destroyed his people, and the Erdtree's age continued with its feared flame still alight.

Godfrey's campaigns extended beyond this mountain battlefield. The description of his armor names the war against the giants and his defeat of the Storm Lord, whom he faced alone. It also places the end of his campaigns where his last worthy opponent fell. Those brief accounts leave much of his military life outside the record, but they describe a lord whose reign was measured through successive victories. Marika's rule spread through places where other powers had already established themselves, and Godfrey confronted rulers who could resist that expansion.

The gold faded from Godfrey's eyes when his last worthy enemy fell. The description of his armor places that loss at the end of his career as a conqueror, after the defeat of the Fire Giants and the Storm Lord. Godfrey had fought as Marika's first Elden Lord, and his warriors had helped establish her reign. Yet victory didn't secure their place beside the Erdtree. When the fighting that had defined his service was over, Godfrey lost the visible blessing that marked his belonging.

Melina preserves another part of that departure. At the Third Church of Marika in Limgrave, she can repeat words that Marika once spoke to Godfrey and his warriors. In this account, Marika announces that she's taking away their grace. Their eyes will grow dim, and they must leave the Lands Between. The armor gives us the moment as it was remembered in Godfrey's life, while Marika's words identify an act of banishment. The queen herself sends her lord and his fighting company beyond the land they'd served.

That removal gives the Tarnished their name. Grace has a visible association with gold in the eyes, so losing its hue leaves a bodily sign of separation from the Erdtree's blessing. Godfrey becomes the first Tarnished, and the people who depart with him share his condition. In the later age, the name also belongs to their descendants. The character who returns at the beginning of the game therefore inherits a history that began with Godfrey's exile, whether or not that character personally marched beside him.

Marika's instruction extends beyond departure. She tells the warriors to fight in a distant land, where they'll live and eventually die. It's a severe future to set before soldiers who have already won her wars. They aren't being given another province within her realm or a peaceful retirement under the tree. Their continued lives are to unfold outside it, through battles that will no longer be fought in the Lands Between. Godfrey leaves with followers whose shared identity now includes the loss of the blessing they once possessed.

This doesn't mean Godfrey and his followers left because the Elden Ring had already been shattered. Their banishment belongs to an earlier period, when Marika's order still had its reigning goddess and a history of successful conquest. The later war among the demigods gives the returning Tarnished the fractured country encountered in the game. Keeping those events apart matters for Godfrey's story. He lost his place after serving Marika's victories, long before the summons brought Tarnished back to seek lordship among the ruins of her family.

When the opening summons names the dead who will rise, it includes Hoarah Loux, identified as a chieftain of the badlands. This is Godfrey under the name associated with his life as a warrior. The same summons reaches figures as different as the scholar Gideon Ofnir and the deathbed companion Fia, before reaching the Tarnished character. They aren't introduced as a company that has remained intact since the exile. The return gathers people with separate histories under a renewed call of grace, and each arrives with concerns formed through those histories.

At the Church of Vows in Liurnia, Miriel remembers Radagon as a champion who arrived at the head of a golden host. He came into a country defended by sorcerers, where the royal house of Caria and the Academy of Raya Lucaria had their own traditions and sources of strength. The Erdtree's armies couldn't assume that victory here would follow the pattern of earlier conquests. Liurnia resisted, and Radagon's place in its history began with the fighting that brought him into conflict with Rennala.

Miriel places the change at the Church of Vows. According to his account, Radagon cleansed himself with celestial dew, put aside his aggression, and married Rennala. The church preserves that marriage as a reconciliation between the Erdtree and the moon. Its association with absolution gives the account a concrete setting: this is a place where damaged bonds can be repaired. Radagon and Rennala's union made such a repair part of the history of two powers that had recently fought each other.

Rennala and Radagon had three children: Radahn, Rykard, and Ranni. Their parentage joined the Carian royal family to the red-haired champion who had entered Liurnia with an invading army. Their later paths would lead them far apart, but they shared this beginning in a household where sorcery and the Erdtree's traditions had been brought together. Ranni's connection with the moon continued through her mother. Radahn carried his father's red hair with pride, even while finding a different military hero to admire.

Radagon eventually left Rennala and returned to the capital. Miriel connects his departure with Godfrey's removal from the Lands Between and Radagon's elevation as Marika's consort and second Elden Lord. For the Carian household, this was a husband and father leaving the family formed through reconciliation. For the ruling dynasty around the Erdtree, it brought a new lord beside Marika. Radagon and Rennala's children subsequently became demigod stepchildren through his union with the goddess, as the description of Rykard's Great Rune explains.

Rennala was left with a gift that continued to occupy her long after Radagon's departure. He had given her an amber egg containing the Great Rune of the Unborn. In the academy's grand library, she holds that egg close and uses its power in her work of rebirth. The gift survived the marriage, and it became central to her secluded life. Anyone reaching her there encounters a queen absorbed in the means of making life anew, surrounded by scholars whose own rebirth has left them frail and short-lived.

The academy's response deepened the damage. Rennala's headpiece describes how its members discovered that she was no longer a champion after her heart broke with Radagon's departure. They turned against her, and the Carian royal family's authority fell away. The woman who had once captivated the academy could no longer command its allegiance. Her distress therefore had consequences beyond the private loss of a husband. The institution she had led withdrew its recognition when she could no longer sustain the strength on which her position had depended.

The Regal Omen Bairn depicts a royal child whose horns were allowed to remain. Its description explains a practice that separates the children of ordinary families from those born to royalty. Omen infants commonly had their horns cut away, and most died from the procedure. Royal Omen children escaped that treatment, but they were hidden underground for the rest of their lives. Morgott and Mohg belonged to Marika and Godfrey's own family. Their birth placed them within the ruling dynasty, while their bodies gave that dynasty a reason to conceal them.

Godwyn belonged to the same line, and his remembered place in the capital was very different. He was Godwyn the Golden, a son whose deeds could become part of Leyndell's public history. During the ancient dragons' attack on the capital, he fought Fortissax and eventually befriended him. The description of the Death Lightning incantation recalls that victory and friendship. Godwyn's standing rested on more than descent. He had helped defend the city and formed a bond with an enemy powerful enough to threaten its walls.

Mohg carried the same parentage into a different religious allegiance. His remembrance describes an encounter with the Formless Mother deep underground, where his accursed blood erupted with fire. The Bloodboon incantation places that encounter within his confinement beneath the earth and says that he came to cherish the defilement into which he'd been born. A body treated as shameful by Marika's order could receive the attention of another power. Mohg's later ambitions have their own history, but this early encounter helps explain how the brothers' paths could diverge despite their shared treatment.

Marika's children with Radagon faced another set of conditions. Miquella and Malenia were twins, and Malenia's remembrance describes them as the children of a single god. It connects that birth with the afflictions each carried: Miquella remained eternally young, while Malenia harbored scarlet rot. They were also Empyreans, a status associated with the possibility of becoming a god. Within this household, even the children singled out for divine succession lived with conditions that their parents' standing hadn't prevented.

Miquella sought a remedy for his sister within Golden Order fundamentalism. The description of Radagon's Rings of Light explains that he abandoned it because it could do nothing to treat Malenia's rot. Before that departure, father and son had exchanged incantations: Miquella's Discus of Light was a gift to Radagon, and Radagon's Rings of Light was given in return. Their shared religious study had a family setting. Miquella's change of direction followed a concrete failure to help someone beside him, despite the learning available within his father's tradition.

Maliketh carries the part of Marika's order that she removed from the Elden Ring. The Rune of Death, also called Destined Death, has been entrusted to him, beyond the ordinary reach of the people living beneath the Erdtree. Enia describes it as a forbidden shadow taken away when the Golden Order was created. That places its confinement near the beginning of the order's own history. The familiar world of Marika's rule already rests on a decision about death, made before the wars that leave her descendants struggling over its remains.

That doesn't mean every creature beneath the Erdtree has become incapable of dying. Soldiers leave bodies on battlefields, graves fill burial grounds, and spirits remain after the lives they once inhabited. The Lands Between has practices for tending its dead, along with creatures that feed on remains. These things belong to the world as the Tarnished encounters it. Destined Death names a particular power within that world's divine arrangement. Treating its removal as a promise that nobody can ever die would make the burial places and the losses suffered by its inhabitants difficult to explain.

Maliketh's responsibility is more unusual than that of a soldier guarding a tomb. His remembrance describes him as Marika's shadowbound beast and says that she needed him as a vessel for Destined Death. His own body and weapon are bound up with its safekeeping. The same remembrance says that Marika betrayed him, without giving a complete account of that betrayal. What it makes plain is the narrow duty she placed upon him. He held the power she had excluded, and the safety of that arrangement depended on his ability to keep it.

His black blade explains why this custody mattered even to Marika's children. The weapon contains the power of the Rune of Death, and Maliketh's armor description recalls the fear he inspired in the demigods. Their place in the divine family didn't put them beyond his reach. Marika could elevate her descendants within an order that withheld Destined Death, while her shadow retained a means of threatening them. For a demigod, Maliketh was a danger with a direct connection to the goddess whose rule sustained the family.

The theft from Maliketh breaks into this arrangement at its most carefully guarded point. The description of his black blade states that a fragment of Death was stolen from him. A fragment was enough to carry the forbidden power beyond its appointed keeper. The rune hadn't been destroyed when it was removed from the Elden Ring. It remained accessible through Maliketh, which meant that someone able to steal a piece could put part of its power to use elsewhere. Marika's solution had left a dangerous object in the custody of a single guardian.

Maliketh changed how he kept the blade after that theft. Its description explains that he bound it within his own flesh so that Death couldn't be stolen again. The act gives his later appearance a particular weight. He bears the failure of his first custody inside his body, together with the thing he still has to protect. When the Tarnished eventually encounters him as Maliketh, the weapon emerges from that bodily confinement. His duty has become inseparable from the means he uses to fight.

The knives that killed Godwyn carried a stolen fragment of Destined Death. Their blades had been prepared through a rite, giving the assassins a power that ordinary weapons didn't possess. Godwyn belonged to Marika's divine household, yet that standing couldn't protect him from what had been taken from Maliketh. The Night of the Black Knives began with a breach in the keeping of death itself, and its victims suffered different parts of a death that should have joined body and soul together.

Ranni acknowledges her part when the Tarnished confronts her with what Rogier has discovered. She admits stealing a fragment of the Rune of Death and using a rite to make the knives capable of killing the demigods. Her confession places her directly within the preparations. She wasn't merely someone who benefited after Godwyn's murder. She helped provide the means by which it could happen. She also accepts responsibility for the dark plot, although she doesn't give the Tarnished a complete account of everyone involved or each person's purpose.

For Ranni, the plot concerned her own flesh as well. She explains that she was an Empyrean, chosen by her Two Fingers as a candidate to succeed Marika. That distinction brought expectations she rejected. Ranni wanted to escape the direction imposed upon her by the Fingers, and she says that she killed her Empyrean body to do so. Her royal birth and her possible divine future were carried in a body she deliberately abandoned. The doll that speaks to the Tarnished belongs to the life she continued after that act.

Godwyn's fate was paired with hers through the timing of their deaths. The Cursemark of Death explains that the mark should have formed a complete circle upon the first demigod to die. Instead, two demigods perished at the same time, and the circle divided into two half-wheels. Ranni died in flesh while Godwyn died in soul. Each suffered one part of the event. Ranni could continue without her original body, while Godwyn's body remained after the death of his soul. The killing therefore left neither victim in the ordinary condition of someone wholly dead.

The Black Knife Armor describes the assassins as women said to be Numen with close ties to Marika. Its muffling veil allowed them to move without their footsteps being heard. Those details place Godwyn's killers uncomfortably near the queen's own background. Their connection to Marika doesn't establish that she ordered the murder; the game leaves her precise involvement unresolved. What it does give us is an attack carried out by people with ties to the divine household, using power stolen from the guardian appointed to keep it sealed.

Rogier places the assassination before the shattering of the Elden Ring and describes it as a catalyst for the later catastrophe. He also admits that its full truth remains difficult to uncover. His investigation supplies an order of events: Godwyn was attacked while Marika's order still stood, before the wars over the broken Ring. The murder wasn't an attempt to seize a Great Rune in the conflict that followed. It injured the reigning family before that conflict began, introducing a death the existing arrangements had been meant to prevent.


Beneath the Erdtree, in Deeproot Depths, Godwyn's remains have grown into an enormous, distorted shape among the roots. The face still has recognizable features, though the body has changed far beyond the form of the golden prince. Fia can be found here if her journey reaches its end, beside the remains she calls sacred. This is where the consequences of Godwyn's death have gathered. His burial placed him within the roots of the tree, and the death associated with him has spread through that same underground system.

The description of Deathroot explains how this happened. After the first death of a demigod, the Rune of Death spread through the underground roots of the Greattree and emerged as deathroot across the Lands Between. Godwyn therefore has a presence far beyond the chamber containing his body. The roots connect his burial to places where the dead rise again. A piece of deathroot recovered from a catacomb belongs to the same continuing disaster as the vast remains beneath the capital.

Fia offers the persecuted dead a different kind of allegiance. At Roundtable Hold, she identifies herself as a deathbed companion, someone whose vocation involves receiving vitality from champions and lying with the remains of exalted nobles. Her embraces give the Tarnished a blessing while temporarily reducing their vitality. That exchange makes her calling tangible before her purpose concerning Godwyn becomes clear. Life can be received, held, and offered through her role, and she intends to bring that practice to the dead prince.

At Deeproot Depths, Fia's purpose becomes more specific. She seeks the other half of the deathly mark, the Cursemark recovered from Ranni's discarded body. Godwyn died in soul, while Ranni died in flesh, leaving the marks divided between them. If the Tarnished brings Ranni's Cursemark to Fia, she can unite what the assassination separated. Fia describes her intended result through the language of bearing a child with Godwyn. The object that ultimately appears is the Mending Rune of the Death-Prince, formed from the joined marks.

Fia's achievement doesn't return Godwyn to ordinary life or establish him as a conscious king ruling from beneath the roots. The rune's description concerns life within death becoming part of the order. If the Tarnished later uses it to mend the Elden Ring, Fia's work can shape the resulting age. Her answer to the hunters is therefore written into a possible future law of the world. Beside Godwyn's persistent remains, she produces a rune through which Those Who Live in Death may finally have a place.


Marika's hammer was made of stone, in the lands of the Numen outside the Lands Between. Its description identifies the same tool with two opposing acts: Marika used it to shatter the Elden Ring, and Radagon tried to repair what she'd broken. Fragments of runes lodged in the hammer when the Ring shattered. The damage remains visible in the object itself. By the time the Tarnished encounters it, this tool carries traces of both the queen's destruction and the effort to undo it.

For Marika, the act means damaging the sacred order housed within her own body. Enia explains at Roundtable Hold that Marika is the vessel of the Elden Ring. She's also its reigning god, the figure around whom the royal household and the Golden Order have been arranged. The hammer therefore reaches into something bound to her own existence. The fractures in her body belong to the same catastrophe as the broken Ring. The queen who had embodied the established order becomes the person responsible for its destruction.

Their shared involvement has a concealed history in Leyndell. At the Church of Vows, Miriel tells the Tarnished about a sculptor who made a statue of Radagon and discovered a secret during the work. The secret remained within the statue. Miriel's account gives an ordinary commission an unusual consequence: someone asked to reproduce the Elden Lord's appearance encountered something hidden about him. The statue survives in the capital, where the Tarnished can uncover what the sculptor learned, if they pursue the clue and have the means to reveal it.

Before that statue, the Law of Regression changes Radagon's image into Marika's. A message identifies Radagon as Marika. The incantation itself concerns the tendency of things to converge, and here its effect reveals an identity concealed beneath a public monument. A visitor to Leyndell could have passed the statue and seen the queen's consort. For a Tarnished who performs the spell, the same stone image takes on the queen's form. The relationship between the two figures now includes a bodily connection that their royal titles alone never disclosed.

Yet Marika's own remembered words address Radagon as someone she can oppose. In the Queen's Bedchamber, Melina can repeat an echo in which Marika calls him a devoted adherent of the Golden Order. She also speaks of him as her other self, while saying that he has yet to become her or become a god. She calls for them to be shattered together. These words leave their earlier condition unresolved; the statue doesn't supply a complete chronology of how their shared existence began. They do preserve disagreement within that existence.

Enia tells the Tarnished that Marika's imprisonment followed the shattering and describes it as punishment for a grave transgression. Marika is confined within the Erdtree, beyond the reach of those who continue to invoke her name. Her absence leaves the royal seat without an accessible queen, even though she remains the vessel of the Ring. The Tarnished can visit churches where her words survive through Melina, or stand before statues of her across the Lands Between. None of those encounters allows a direct conversation with the imprisoned ruler.

The opening account describes the demigods claiming the Great Runes and being drawn into war by the strength those fragments gave them. Each rune is a substantial part of the damaged Ring, and possession gives its bearer power as well as a claim upon the future. Yet the demigods don't assemble around a shared successor. Their armies fight, their territories become separated by hostile borders, and the throne remains beyond the reach of the challengers. The opening's judgment on the outcome is direct: the war produces no lord.

The opening identifies Malenia and Radahn as the mightiest remaining demigods, and their meeting at Aeonia becomes the war's last great confrontation. Neither can secure an ordinary victory over the other. Their struggle ends with Malenia's scarlet bloom, and Caelid suffers the consequences. The detailed aftermath belongs to the lives and places left there. Within the succession war, the immediate result is that even the two strongest contenders fail to produce a reigning lord. Military strength reaches its greatest concentration here, without resolving the contest that drew the armies together.

By the time the Tarnished returns, the great campaigns have subsided into defended holdings and lingering battles. Soldiers still guard roads for rulers whose wars never brought them the Elden Throne. Leyndell's walls held, Godrick survived his defeats, and Malenia was brought home, while the Great Runes remained scattered. These survivals explain the unfinished world the Tarnished enters. The demigods had enough strength to prevent one another's succession, and their exhausted armies could still keep a returning exile far from the throne.


Radahn and Malenia had been among the strongest demigods to fight in the Shattering. He carried the reputation of a great general, while she had never known defeat. Their meeting in Caelid brought neither a clean victory. During their confrontation, Malenia unleashed the scarlet bloom. When it did, the rot spread through Caelid and left Radahn afflicted. The battle's aftermath remains visible across the region, reaching well beyond the people who had gathered to fight it. Roads, settlements, and open country now lie within the rot's reach.

Radahn remained on the battlefield. By the time the Tarnished can attend the festival held in his name, he's wandering the Wailing Dunes below Redmane Castle. Jerren describes him as having lost his reason to the rot and feeding on the bodies left there. His Great Rune gives another part of the account. It burns in resistance to the scarlet rot, even as the affliction continues to consume him. The shard he won during the Shattering hasn't restored his mind or brought his campaign to a conclusion. He survives with it inside a condition his followers can no longer remedy.

At Redmane Castle, Jerren has arranged a gathering intended to end Radahn's suffering. The festival brings warriors together for a chance to confront the general in the dunes. Its language of celebration sits beside Jerren's plain account of what Radahn has become. For Jerren, a proper death in battle would fulfill an old agreement with his lord. The Eccentric's Hood describes their promise of honorable deaths to one another. His preparations therefore carry a personal obligation alongside the public invitation. He's gathering people strong enough to do what Radahn's own survival has made necessary.

If Radahn falls, his death releases the stars. A falling star strikes Limgrave, opening a vast crater and a route toward Nokron beneath the surface. The consequence reaches outside Caelid at once. For Blaidd and the Tarnished, that opening can advance the search being undertaken for Ranni. For Jerren, the general has received the death he sought to arrange. Caelid itself remains scarred by rot. The Redmanes' struggle against its spread continues, while the battlefield has finally lost the wandering lord whose strength had kept the heavens still.


Stormveil makes approaching its ruler difficult. At the gate, Gostoc warns the Tarnished against the main entrance and recommends a route through an opening in the walls. If the Tarnished insists on the gate, soldiers are ready beyond it with weapons trained on the approach. The side route passes through damaged parts of the fortress, with soldiers and warhawks occupying the way upward. Godrick has inherited a strong defensive position. Reaching him means passing through the people and creatures that keep that position secure.

Kenneth Haight offers a sharply different account of Limgrave's lord. If the Tarnished meets him near the ruins in eastern Limgrave, Kenneth speaks contemptuously of Godrick and describes him as unfit to rule. Kenneth has his own claim to noble standing and his own interest in recovering Fort Haight, so his judgment comes from a participant in Limgrave's affairs. Still, his search for a suitable ruler reveals that Godrick's possession of Stormveil hasn't persuaded every local noble to accept his fitness for the position.

Godrick's appearance brings the practice into full view. Extra limbs crowd his body, and he carries a great axe with a strength assembled through grafting. His title invokes gold, and his speech reaches back toward the great figures of his family. He addresses Godfrey as a forefather whose attention he wants to earn. The relationship matters to him as a measure of achievement. In front of a Tarnished challenger, he wants his accumulated strength to be worthy of the ancestor whose name supports his claim.

A dead dragon lies in the courtyard where Godrick fights. He addresses it as a kindred creature and speaks with admiration for its power. During the encounter, he cuts away one of his own arms and grafts the dragon's head onto himself. The head then breathes fire against his opponent. Grafting can give him abilities he didn't possess before. That visible result helps explain why he continues the practice, though the bodies required for it make Stormveil a place of fear for those brought within its walls.

If the Tarnished defeats him, Godrick leaves behind a Great Rune that can be restored at the Divine Tower of Limgrave. Its description places it at the center of the Elden Ring and identifies the earliest demigods with Godfrey and his descendants, the Golden Lineage. The inheritance Godrick guards is genuine. That makes his failure more telling. The Tarnished takes a fragment of the order from a man whose place in the family gave him access to something far greater than his own achievements.

Rennala holds an amber egg in the Grand Library of Raya Lucaria, while the scholars gathered beneath her struggle through their lessons in rebirth. She's still called Queen of the Full Moon, and the academy still occupies its great buildings above Liurnia. Yet the queen inside the library no longer directs the institution that once followed her. The Tarnished arrives seeking a Great Rune and finds a woman absorbed in caring for the strange lives around her. Her place in the broken succession is unusual, because she holds something the would-be Elden Lord needs without pursuing the throne herself.

After Radagon's departure broke Rennala's heart, the academy turned against her. She now remains confined within the institution she once led.

Radagon left Rennala the amber egg that she continues to cradle. Within it is the Great Rune of the Unborn. Unlike the runes taken from the demigods who fought over the shattered order, this one comes through a gift made before the Tarnished's arrival. Rennala herself isn't a demigod, despite being counted among the figures whose runes matter to the journey. Her royal title, her family, and the rune in her keeping place her alongside them in the Tarnished's search. They don't give her the same ambitions or the same condition as the rulers fighting elsewhere.

The first part of the encounter stays within that troubled library. Rennala floats above the scholars inside a golden barrier, while certain scholars below sustain her protection through their singing. When that protection breaks, she's brought down within reach of the Tarnished. The encounter makes her dependence on the room's inhabitants quite literal. These frail children protect the woman who keeps trying to remake them. Around them, the library still contains the books and furnishings of a great center of sorcery, though its queen's attention has narrowed to the egg and her children.

When the Tarnished overcomes that protection, Ranni intervenes through a spell. Her voice identifies Rennala as her mother, and the encounter changes to an expanse of water beneath a full moon. There, the Tarnished faces a powerful image of the Carian queen, capable of lunar sorcery and summoning spirit allies. This magical defense presents Rennala with the strength associated with her former standing. Ranni's intervention protects her mother at the moment when the woman in the library has become vulnerable. The battle's second phase belongs to that protection, rather than to a lasting recovery by Rennala herself.

Afterward, Rennala remains alive in the Grand Library, still holding the egg. The Tarnished receives the Great Rune of the Unborn and can return to speak with her. Her survival changes the meaning of victory here. Defeating the library's defenses and Ranni's spell allows the Tarnished to obtain the rune without removing the queen from her remaining home. Rennala doesn't resume leadership of the academy. She continues with the practice that occupied her before the intrusion, and the Tarnished can now become one of those who asks for it.

At Ranni's Rise, beyond Caria Manor, the Tarnished can enter the service of a princess who inhabits a doll. Ranni has already abandoned the body that marked her as a possible successor to Marika. She explains that she was an Empyrean, chosen by her own Two Fingers as a candidate for godhood. That distinction brought obligations she refused to accept. Her plan now requires something hidden beneath the earth, and the Tarnished can agree to help her retrieve it.

If the Tarnished defeats Radahn, the stars resume their course, and a falling star strikes Limgrave. Near the Mistwood, the impact leaves an opening into the earth. The journey toward Nokron now begins through that broken ground. Ranni's pursuit depends on the defeat of her own brother, even though the Tarnished may have entered the festival for other reasons. Radahn's fall changes access to an entire buried city, allowing the search that stalled beside the Siofra River to continue.

Within Nokron, the route leads through silver beings capable of imitating other forms and onward into Night's Sacred Ground. Beneath a great seated figure, the Tarnished finds the Fingerslayer Blade. Its description calls it evidence of the Eternal City's treason and says it can harm the Greater Will and its vassals. It also places a restriction on its use: those without a fate can't wield it. For Ranni, whose fate has begun moving again, this buried weapon offers a way to act against her Two Fingers.

When the Tarnished brings her the blade, Ranni accepts it and gives them the Carian Inverted Statue. That object opens a separate route through the Carian Study Hall toward the Divine Tower of Liurnia. Ranni then leaves her tower to continue her work. The exchange gives the Tarnished access to the remains of her discarded Empyrean body, while Ranni takes possession of the weapon she sought. Their cooperation has carried each of them to a place the other couldn't have reached alone.

The Tarnished can follow Ranni through a sending gate in Renna's Rise and into Ainsel River Main. There they find a miniature doll resembling her. If they persist in speaking to it at a site of grace, Ranni answers. The princess who received a servant in her tower now travels in a form that servant can carry. She asks the Tarnished to deal with the Baleful Shadows pursuing her, and their work together continues through the underground ruins toward Nokstella.

During this passage, Ranni explains more about the bond she rejected. She names herself alongside Miquella and Malenia as an Empyrean, and describes Blaidd as the shadow given to her. She also speaks with affection about Blaidd and Iji, who accepted her decision despite its consequences. Her account gives their service a history beyond the search for a blade. They've remained beside someone who chose to oppose the very power that designated her for a divine role.

Beyond Nokstella, the Tarnished encounters a Baleful Shadow in Blaidd's shape. Defeating it ends the immediate pursuit, and Ranni gives the Tarnished a key. This enemy isn't Blaidd himself. The key opens the chest beside Rennala in Raya Lucaria's library, where the Dark Moon Ring has been kept. Its description connects the ring to Ranni's intended consort. Continuing after her now carries the possibility of a closer commitment than the service first offered at her tower.

The route passes through the Lake of Rot and the Grand Cloister, then beyond Astel to the Moonlight Altar. Beneath the Cathedral of Manus Celes, the Tarnished finds Ranni with the slain remains of her Two Fingers. If they bring the ring, they can place it on her hand and become her promised consort. Ranni acknowledges that bond before departing. Her immediate struggle with the Fingers has reached its end, and the Tarnished has helped secure the independence she sought when she abandoned her flesh.

Tanith receives visitors at Volcano Manor beside an armored knight, within a house whose warmth comes from Mount Gelmir. She offers the Tarnished a place among the recusants, warriors who reject the Erdtree and the guidance of the Fingers. Joining means turning against the cause that brought the Tarnished back to the Lands Between. Tanith presents that refusal as a release from an unjust burden. Yet the first work she gives a recruit is a commission to kill another Tarnished. Before the manor asks anyone to meet its lord, it asks them to become useful to his rebellion.

Rykard had a place within the ruling family before he became the manor's hidden lord. He's a son of Radagon and Rennala, and his title of praetor survives in the equipment and history associated with him. The Gelmir Knight armor describes knights who followed him because they considered his ambitions worthy. Their loyalty changed when those ambitions deteriorated into greed. This matters because Tanith's present household grew out of a revolt that once commanded a different kind of allegiance. Soldiers could oppose the Erdtree under Rykard and still recoil from what their leader eventually chose.

Rykard's remembrance describes that choice directly. He fed himself to the great serpent, seeking a life that would allow him to grow and consume without end. His Great Rune gives the act another part of its meaning: he let the serpent devour him along with the rune he possessed. A demigod carrying a fragment of the broken Elden Ring had joined himself to a creature whose appetite could keep increasing. The lord whom Tanith praises now exists within that union. Her language of rebellion reaches toward the overthrow of the gods, while the serpent supplies a means that requires further consumption.

The Tarnished can reach Rykard through Tanith's favor after completing the manor's commissions, or by exploring the hidden route through the estate. The encounter begins with the God-Devouring Serpent. Rykard then reveals himself within the creature, wielding a blade that carries the remains of those it has consumed. His invitation to join him extends the manor's recruitment into its fullest and most terrible form. The champions gathered around his cause have become nourishment for the being expected to devour the gods.

If the Tarnished defeats Rykard, Tanith refuses to treat his death as final. She later appears beside his remains and begins consuming them herself, hoping to receive him into her body and allow him to live again. The woman who once managed letters, rewards, and introductions now attends to the serpent's remains with the same devotion. Volcano Manor's revolt has consumed the lord who led it, the champions drawn into him, and Tanith's life outside her service to him. Beside what remains of Rykard, she continues trying to sustain him.


Morgott keeps the thrones of the demigods before the Erdtree, though the people of Leyndell know their ruler as the Veiled Monarch. His royal identity conceals an Omen body, marked by horns and a tail. When the Tarnished reaches him, he stands among seats belonging to members of the divine family and names their absent occupants with contempt. He regards those relatives as traitors. The capital still has a king willing to defend it, and that king belongs to a class of people its own customs condemn.

The concealment matters because Leyndell's treatment of Omens is physical and lasting. The Omen Bairn descriptions distinguish between ordinary Omen infants, whose horns are removed in a procedure that often kills them, and royal children whose horns are left intact. Royal Omens are instead confined underground, hidden from public life. Beneath the capital, the Subterranean Shunning-Grounds contain Omens far from the avenues and sanctuaries above. Morgott's royal birth spared him one form of violence while leaving him subject to another. His eventual kingship doesn't erase the imprisonment that preceded it.

Before the Tarnished knows any of this, Morgott confronts them under another name. At the approach to Stormveil, he appears as Margit, the Fell Omen. He recognizes the newcomer's ambition and moves to stop it before it can reach the capital. His weapon looks like a rough staff, yet he can also summon golden weapons, including a dagger and a great hammer. The encounter brings the Erdtree's future defender into the Tarnished's path well outside Leyndell. Morgott is already acting against potential claimants before they can threaten his seat.

When the Tarnished reaches the thrones, Morgott no longer maintains that separation. He appears in person and speaks as the king. Godrick, the twins Miquella and Malenia, Radahn, Rykard, and Ranni all come under his judgment. His anger gathers the scattered family into one accusation of betrayal. He then turns that judgment toward the newcomer. A Tarnished seeking lordship is, to Morgott, another intruder approaching something sacred. Whatever grace has brought this traveler back, Morgott won't accept their ambition as sufficient reason to surrender his guardianship.

He breaks the outer covering of his staff and reveals the curved blade inside. Its description explains that the sword was formed from the accursed blood he had put aside and sealed away. The weapon therefore carries something Morgott has tried to suppress within himself. Alongside it, he continues to wield golden conjured weapons. His defense of the Erdtree draws on both his devotion and the inheritance he despises. The battle makes that conflict visible through the things he actually brings against the Tarnished.

After his defeat, Morgott's body loses its prominent Omen features, leaving him diminished on the ground. Much later, when Godfrey returns, he holds his fallen son before facing the Tarnished. The moment gives Morgott a place in his father's arms after a life spent largely concealed. It also places the defender of Leyndell within the family he accused and tried to preserve. Whatever distance separated them, Godfrey recognizes him.

Morgott's remembrance says that he loved the Erdtree despite receiving no love in return. His actions give that statement its weight: he guards Stormveil's approach, commands riders on the roads, defends Leyndell, and finally exposes his hidden identity at the thrones. He dies having resisted the person seeking to replace the order he served. Beneath the capital, the Shunning-Grounds remain, with their prisoners still below the streets of his kingdom.


The entrance to the Erdtree is closed by thorns. After Morgott's defeat, the Tarnished can climb toward the opening behind his throne, close enough to find that the way inside is impassable. The tree has dominated the journey across the Lands Between. Its light reaches far beyond Leyndell, and its immense trunk rises above the royal sanctuary. Yet arriving at its entrance changes nothing about the barrier. The Tarnished has reached the place where lordship was supposed to become possible, and there's no passage through.

When the Tarnished rests at the site of grace after finding the thorns, Melina appears. She recognizes the obstruction and offers a route forward. Beyond the capital lie the Mountaintops of the Giants, where a flame can burn the Erdtree's thorns. Her proposal gives the journey a new destination, the Forge of the Giants. Reaching the tree has brought her closer to the purpose she has been seeking, though she still needs the Tarnished to travel with her. Their agreement can continue beyond the point that once seemed its natural conclusion.

Melina gives the Tarnished the Rold Medallion. It's a practical answer to a practical difficulty, since the Grand Lift of Rold provides access to the mountaintops. She also marks the route on the map. The next stage therefore begins with something more usable than a general instruction to persevere. There's a lift to reach, a medallion that will operate it, and a distant forge associated with the fire she needs. The path turns away from the Erdtree even while the blocked entrance remains the reason for taking it.

At Roundtable Hold, Enia receives news that the guidance given so far hasn't accounted for what happened. The Two Fingers have become motionless, raised toward the heavens. She explains that they're consulting the Greater Will and that this consultation may take an immense span of time. For the Tarnished, waiting offers no workable continuation of the journey. The interpreter who previously conveyed their direction can describe their silence, but she can't supply an immediate answer from them about the sealed entrance.

Asking Enia about burning the thorns brings out the danger in Melina's proposal. Enia identifies burning the Erdtree as the gravest of sins. The fire of the Giants belongs to a history of hostility toward the tree, so seeking it means approaching something the established order has treated as forbidden. A Tarnished following the Fingers' earlier direction has now reached a point where further progress involves an act their interpreter describes in those terms. The obstacle can't be solved by carrying out the original instruction with greater force.

Her explanation also gives the flame a human cost. Enia says that kindling is needed, and describes a person suited to that role through a vision of fire. Melina has offered to guide the journey, but the conversation at the Hold makes clear that reaching a forge won't amount to collecting an ordinary tool. Someone has to take part in what happens there. For now, the Tarnished has a destination and an accompanying purpose whose full demands haven't yet been carried out. The consequences belong ahead on the road.

At the Forge of the Giants, Melina can finally carry out the purpose that brought her to the Mountaintops. The Tarnished has defeated the Fire Giant, but the immense forge still requires someone capable of using its flame. Melina has spoken of being burned and bodiless, and of seeking the purpose her mother gave her inside the Erdtree. Here, on the ordinary route, she understands what she's come to do. She asks the Tarnished to commit to the journey ahead, then gives herself to the fire that will burn the tree.

There's a conditional route in which she never takes her place at the forge. Beneath Leyndell, the Three Fingers can embrace the Tarnished and leave them carrying the Frenzied Flame. If that happens before the journey to the forge is completed, Melina leaves. She's warned against accepting it, and she refuses to accompany its bearer any farther. The Tarnished then becomes the kindling for the burning. Melina survives this particular sacrifice, though saving her in this way also brings the possibility of the destruction she opposed.

Under the ordinary course of events, Melina's departure leaves the Tarnished alone at a different kind of threshold. The Erdtree begins to burn, and the journey continues in Crumbling Farum Azula. The game carries the Tarnished there without giving a detailed account of the passage. What awaits is a ruined place of suspended masonry, ancient dragons and beastmen, with a great storm moving through its broken architecture. The route toward the throne has passed beyond the roads and fortresses of the Lands Between into a sanctuary whose inhabitants preserve a much older religious world.

Beyond the Godskin Duo waits Maliketh, still guarding the Destined Death that Marika excluded from her order. The Tarnished's route now brings them against its appointed keeper.

The Tarnished first faces him as the Beast Clergyman. For those who've brought Deathroot to Gurranq at the Bestial Sanctum, the encounter can carry recognition, and his words change accordingly. The clergyman's hunger for Deathroot and Maliketh's custody of the stolen rune belong to the same wounded history. Death has spread through the roots in a corrupted form while its appointed keeper remains burdened by what escaped him. In Farum Azula, that keeper stands directly across the path the Tarnished must follow.

When the battle changes, the Beast Clergyman reveals Maliketh and draws the power concealed within him. The Black Blade carries Destined Death, and his attacks make that custody visible. This is the same power that made the demigods fear him. Defeating Maliketh breaks the arrangement by which the Rune of Death has been kept sealed away. The fire at the Erdtree and the release of Death now belong to the same advance toward its interior.

After Maliketh falls, the Tarnished returns to a transformed Leyndell. Ash has covered the capital. Streets and approaches that once organized movement through the royal city are buried, and the burning Erdtree dominates what remains. The change gives the journey a physical consequence beyond the loss of individual guardians. Reaching the Ring has required setting the sacred tree alight and releasing the force that its order had confined. The royal capital cannot remain untouched while the Tarnished breaks through the conditions that kept the throne inaccessible.

Sir Gideon Ofnir waits within the ashen city. He knows enough about the Tarnished's progress to oppose its completion, and he now claims that Marika intends an endless struggle rather than a successful ascent. That's Gideon's conclusion, delivered as he tries to stop the person he once supplied with information. His presence makes the remaining approach more personal. The gathering of ambitions at Roundtable Hold has narrowed into a confrontation between Tarnished who disagree about whether anyone should finish what grace began. Beyond him, the path through the ash still leads toward the Erdtree, now burning with Death released into the world.


Godfrey waits near the entrance to the Erdtree, holding Morgott's body before the confrontation begins. The first Elden Lord has returned to the capital after the long exile that made him and his warriors Tarnished. His arrival puts another claimant at the end of the same journey. The guidance that brought the player toward the throne has also brought back a man who once occupied it. Morgott passes from his arms, and Godfrey turns to face the Tarnished who has reached the place his son defended.

Godfrey initially fights with Serosh at his shoulder, the beast who restrains his appetite for battle. When that restraint is cast aside, he returns to the name Hoarah Loux and fights as a warrior without the bearing of a ceremonial king. The change recalls the distance between the conqueror and the lord Marika made of him. Defeating him settles the immediate rivalry over access to the Erdtree. The returning founder of the Golden Lineage won't be the one to enter and claim the Ring.

Inside, Marika hangs suspended and broken. Her body falls and changes into Radagon, who takes up the hammer associated with both the shattering and the attempted repair of the Elden Ring. The revelation that Radagon is Marika now has a physical presence before the Tarnished. Whatever their earlier history involved, this final encounter places them within the same damaged body. Radagon still stands between the challenger and the possibility of remaking the order whose fragments are visible within him.

After Radagon's defeat, the Elden Beast appears. Its remembrance calls it the vassal beast of the Greater Will and the living incarnation of the concept of order. It takes Radagon's body and fashions a sword from him. The last opponent therefore reaches beyond a contest among demigods or returning lords. The Tarnished faces the being associated with the Ring itself. Only after that encounter ends does the broken body of Marika become the point around which the available conclusions gather.

The simplest conclusion is the Age of Fracture. If the Tarnished mends the Elden Ring without adding a special mending rune, they become Elden Lord under that name. The ending gives the realm a lord and restores the Ring, but it doesn't describe a thorough removal of every cruelty or ruin encountered along the way. Its name leaves the condition of this inheritance in view. A shattered order has been made whole enough to continue, with the Tarnished seated as the new ruler.

Fia's alternative requires the Mending Rune of the Death-Prince. Her quest brings together the cursemarks associated with Godwyn and Ranni, and she conceives the rune through her union with the Prince of Death. If the Tarnished uses it, the resulting Age of the Duskborn incorporates the principle of life within death into the order. Those Who Live in Death have been hunted as a violation of the Golden Order. Fia's rune changes the law that makes their existence an offense, giving her cause a place within the Ring itself.

Goldmask reaches a different diagnosis through his study of the Golden Order. His work, pursued with Brother Corhyn nearby for much of the journey, produces the Mending Rune of Perfect Order. Its description identifies the instability of gods with humanlike hearts as the flaw it would address. If used, the rune brings the Age of Order. Goldmask's solution seeks to prevent divine fickleness from disturbing the system. The player isn't shown a detailed account of everyday life afterward, but the proposed correction is specific: the order should no longer depend on such changeable gods.

The Dung Eater also produces a mending rune, provided the Tarnished follows his quest through its grim requirements. His Seedbed Curses prevent the souls of his victims from returning to the Erdtree. The Mending Rune of the Fell Curse extends that defilement into the order, and its use leads to the Blessing of Despair. The description imagines a world in which everyone is cursed, so that the curse ceases to single out particular people. His answer to exclusion spreads the condition he inflicts rather than relieving the suffering of those already marked by it.

Ranni's ending proceeds through a separate invitation. If her quest has been completed, the Tarnished can summon her instead of repairing Marika with a mending rune. Ranni takes Marika's place as the divine figure of a new age, with the Tarnished as her consort. In her account of the coming Age of Stars, she intends to take her order far from the people who would otherwise live directly beneath it. Her departure with the Tarnished places the new divine pair at a distance, rather than seating them together in Leyndell.

The Frenzied Flame leads to a conclusion that abandons preservation. Its adherents seek to melt away the divisions from which birth and suffering arose. If the Tarnished remains its inheritor and chooses that ending, the world is consumed by the flame of frenzy. Where Melina survives the earlier journey, the ending returns to her amid the aftermath, and she promises to pursue the Lord of Frenzied Flame with Destined Death. Her opposition survives the success of the path she begged the Tarnished to reject.

These conclusions depend on what the player has done and what they select at the end. Acquiring several mending runes doesn't mean using all of them together, and completing Ranni's quest doesn't force the Age of Stars. The Frenzied Flame imposes its own restriction unless it's removed through the demanding use of Miquella's Needle. The released game leaves the outcome conditional. One Tarnished may take the throne, another may leave with Ranni, and another may burn the world. At Marika's broken body, the preparations made throughout the journey become a particular ending.


At the roots of the Haligtree, Malenia waits beside a hollow where her brother should have been. Miquella's absence has shaped the entire settlement above her. His tree still shelters soldiers and inhabitants, and Elphael still surrounds its lower reaches, but the demigod whose promise drew them there is gone. Malenia identifies herself through her devotion to him. Even after the catastrophe in Caelid, she remains his defender, resting within a refuge whose founder can no longer be found among its roots.

Miquella cultivated the Haligtree with his own blood. The Haligtree Knight armor describes that effort and says the tree ultimately failed to become an Erdtree. This leaves a substantial achievement alongside a failed transformation. A great living structure exists, with a settlement built around it, though it never became the replacement Miquella sought. Its branches carry the Tarnished down toward Elphael, where the architecture and defenses suggest an established community. The rot spreading through the lower reaches shows how far that community remains from the protection its founder hoped to provide.

Far away in Mohgwyn Palace, another sacred structure holds Miquella's cocoon. An arm extends from it, and Mohg addresses the figure within as the center of his coming dynasty. Mohg wants to raise Miquella to full godhood and become his consort. The remembrance of the Lord of Blood describes his attempts to share his bloody bedchamber with Miquella and says he received no response. In the base game, the contrast is stark: the Haligtree has lost its founder, while Mohg possesses his body and claims a future through him.

Shadow of the Erdtree changes how this situation can be understood. Sir Ansbach, once a servant of Mohg, tells the Tarnished that he challenged Miquella in an attempt to free his lord from Miquella's enchantment. He was then enchanted himself. His account establishes that Mohg's involvement can't be treated solely as the independent scheme it first appears to be. Miquella's ability to compel affection has reached into the household that supposedly held him captive, and into the man who tried to resist him on Mohg's behalf.

That revelation doesn't provide a complete sequence for the removal from the Haligtree, or settle when every intention took shape. It does change the position of the absent demigod. Miquella has acted upon Mohg and Ansbach, and he's used Mohg in reaching the Land of Shadow. The cocoon therefore belongs to a passage Miquella pursued as well as to Mohg's failed dynasty. Ansbach's testimony gives Mohg a different place in that history without turning the entire earlier story into a fully explained plan.

The Tarnished can leave Malenia undefeated and still reach the cocoon after overcoming Mohg. The expansion's entrance doesn't require resolving the fate of the Haligtree or delivering its inhabitants the return they await. This separation leaves the refuge's sorrow intact. At the palace, Miquella's discarded flesh remains within reach, while his path continues elsewhere. The brother Malenia waits for has gone beyond the tree he nourished, carrying his effort to establish a gentler age into a concealed land.


Leda stands beside the cocoon in Mohgwyn Palace and invites the Tarnished to follow Miquella. Mohg has been defeated, and Radahn's defeat is also required for this passage to open. The entrance is the withered arm extending from the cocoon. Touching it carries the Tarnished into the Land of Shadow, where Miquella has already traveled. Leda recognizes the Tarnished as another person drawn toward him, though the player arrives without sharing the histories that have brought his other followers to this point.

Miquella's crosses give that passage a visible form. Near the Three-Path Cross, a golden mark stands where he has abandoned a part of himself. The inscription identifies flesh left behind at the beginning of his journey through this land. Further crosses record other relinquishments, including bodily parts and qualities connected to his inner life. They make his absence unusually traceable. The Tarnished can't walk beside him, but can stop where he stopped and read what he chose to surrender there.

At the Three-Path Cross, Redmane Freyja and Hornsent already have different reasons for following the same trail. Freyja served Radahn, and she credits Miquella with saving her after she was afflicted by scarlet rot in Aeonia. Her loyalty grows from that rescue and her respect for the warrior she served. Hornsent carries the grievance of a people devastated by Messmer's crusade. He looks to Miquella's promised age for a future in which the suffering of his people can finally be answered.

Near the Main Gate Cross, Ansbach and Moore present another pairing. Ansbach was a Pureblood Knight in Mohg's service. He's an older warrior who now spends his efforts gathering information, and he doesn't pretend to understand Miquella's purpose already. Moore supplies the group and speaks with affection of the forager brood. These foragers are members of the kindred of rot who provide ingredients and supplies. Moore's place among them gives the traveling company a network of care outside the human loyalties most immediately visible at the crosses.

At Pillar Path Cross, Thiollier's concerns turn toward Saint Trina. He's skilled with poisons and reluctant to present himself as a strong fighter. His devotion gives him a focus different from Leda's direct commitment to Miquella. Saint Trina is already associated with sleep in the Lands Between, through lilies, weapons and remedies bearing her name. Here, Thiollier has come in search of her. His presence means that even within Miquella's company, someone can be listening for another figure and measuring the journey by another hoped-for meeting.

Leda herself is a Needle Knight devoted to Miquella. Her confidence gives the scattered travelers an apparent center, and her messages help direct the Tarnished along his route. Yet the company isn't an army traveling in formation. Its members occupy different crosses and investigate different places. Some need information, some offer supplies, and others speak of a debt or grievance they expect Miquella to resolve. The Tarnished becomes acquainted with them through these ordinary exchanges before understanding how their commitments fit together.

Shaman Village lies beyond the Hinterland, reached through a concealed passage from Shadow Keep. It's a small settlement compared with the fortresses and sacred cities elsewhere in the Land of Shadow. A golden tree grows there, and the Minor Erdtree incantation preserves the reason for its presence. Marika bathed the village of her home in gold, knowing there was nobody left to heal. The description gives the empty place a direct connection to the woman later worshipped as the Eternal Queen.

The Golden Braid provides another personal trace. It's a cut lock of Marika's hair, left as an offering to the Grandmother. Its description asks what she prayed for, wished for or confessed, then leaves those questions unanswered. It also says she never returned home again. The object supplies a departure and an offering without supplying the words that accompanied them. The braid and the empty village already make her separation from home concrete.

Elsewhere, Bonny Village exposes a practice that helps explain what happened to people called shamans. The settlement is connected to the production of living jars, and the nearby gaols contain the results. Jar potentates work with great pots and with human bodies. The places associated with them include implements and remains that make the process visible without requiring an account of each victim. This was organized work, sustained through tools, places of confinement and a religious language that could describe the intended result as sainthood.

The Tooth Whip is particularly direct. Its description explains that the teeth embedded in it cause wounds that fester, and that shaman flesh was said to meld harmoniously with the flesh of others. It calls their resulting treatment a cruel fate. This connects the shamans' bodies to the jar process through a particular quality attributed to their flesh. They were useful to the people making the jars because they could bind with other flesh. That usefulness became a reason to subject them to suffering.

Marika's home, the reference to shamans, and the descriptions of their treatment connect her people with this persecution. Taken together, they suggest a background for her later hostility toward the hornsent, though the game doesn't give a complete chronology of her escape or ascent. What it does give is enough to change the setting of her rise. Before the royal family, the Erdtree's conquests and the divine court, there was a home whose population was gone and a practice that assigned its people to jars.

The later crusade against the hornsent devastated communities far beyond the jar makers themselves. Hornsent survivors remember destroyed homes and families, and they associate that suffering with Marika's Erdtree. The village offers a reason to consider how revenge entered this history, but it doesn't make the persecution of one population a justification for burning another. Both histories remain present in the land. Bonny retains the machinery of the shamans' suffering, while the ruined hornsent settlements retain the consequences of the violence directed against them.

Within Shadow Keep, Messmer's soldiers still occupy the fortress from which his crusade has spread across the Land of Shadow. The Storehouse contains preserved specimens and the work of scholars, while other parts of the keep hold soldiers, weapons and the traces of a long campaign. Beyond its walls, the damage reaches settlements whose inhabitants had no place in the order his army served. Messmer remains at the center of this military world, a son of Marika whose service has continued far from the Erdtree and the public history of her reign.

Messmer's armor describes the public burden placed upon that commander. His mother's wishes made him a focus for the resentment produced by the crusade. That arrangement allowed Marika's enemies to direct their hatred toward the son conducting the assault. His title, the Impaler, belongs to the punishment his forces inflicted, and the hornsent have ample reason to remember it. At the same time, the account attached to his equipment places his actions within service to Marika. The cruelty of the campaign and his obedience to his mother occupy the same history.

When the Tarnished reaches him, Messmer recognizes someone guided by the grace associated with Marika. That discovery troubles him. A Tarnished has arrived as a prospective lord, even though Messmer's task has involved condemning those denied the grace of gold. The visitor therefore carries a sign of his mother's favor into the place where he has been left. His challenge to the Tarnished comes out of that contradiction. The order he enforces has admitted someone he believes should fall beneath its judgment.

The encounter also brings forward the condition Marika tried to contain within her son. Messmer's remembrance explains that she removed his eye and replaced it with a seal of grace to restrain the abyssal serpent. Even after doing so, she remained afraid and hid him in the realm of shadow. His serpentine nature therefore wasn't an enemy he acquired during this meeting. It was already part of the son she sent away, and the seal in his eye was a mother's intervention that became inseparable from his confinement.

Under pressure, Messmer breaks that seal himself. He removes the restraint Marika placed in his eye and allows the Base Serpent to emerge. The change makes the cost of his service unusually concrete. His mother supplied the means to suppress something she feared, and he has carried it while leading her campaign. Now, faced with a Tarnished bearing her grace, he destroys her protection to continue resisting. The serpents that accompany him become part of an encounter in which the hidden condition of his body can no longer be kept apart from his royal identity.

His last words turn toward Marika in bitterness. They don't offer a detailed account of when their relationship broke down or what she privately promised him. They do reveal where he places the wound of his abandonment. The son who has maintained her crusade dies cursing his mother. If the hornsent follower has been summoned to help defeat him, that follower can experience the victory as revenge for his own losses. Neither response repairs the country outside the keep, where the dead remain dead and the survivors still live among the results of the campaign.

After Messmer's defeat, his kindling allows the Tarnished to continue toward the concealed tower beyond the sealing tree. His life has thus guarded another obstruction in the Land of Shadow, and his death makes passage possible. Behind that newly opened route stands the fortress he occupied, with its soldiers and specimens, and beyond it the settlements his forces burned. Marika's distant son has answered for the assault with his life. The history carried by his victims, and the abandonment recorded in his own remembrance, remain in the land he was ordered to subdue.


At the Cathedral of Manus Metyr, Count Ymir receives the Tarnished in a building whose religious purpose extends beneath its floor. He teaches sorcery, speaks about the failings of the world and provides a map directing the visitor toward a ruin associated with fingers. Swordhand of Night Jolan stands nearby as his protector. The cathedral gives Ymir a place from which to guide another person's investigation, and his knowledge makes him useful. His requests also lead toward a very particular ambition of his own, one that becomes clearer as the journeys continue.

His account turns toward the Two Fingers and the beings who have depended on their guidance. Ymir argues that the failings of the present order reach back through Marika and the Fingers that instructed her. In his interpretation, the problem began with their mother. This matters because the Two Fingers have addressed the Tarnished as interpreters of a higher will. At Roundtable Hold, their judgments were delivered through Enia, and their long silence became an obstacle when the path to lordship ceased to proceed as expected.

Ymir wants to replace the mother he condemns, giving him a personal stake in this diagnosis. 

That route opens beneath his seat. When the Tarnished finds the way below the cathedral, the journey leads into the Finger Ruins of Miyr. Swordhand of Night Anna appears along this hidden approach, extending the connection between the place beneath Ymir and the warriors associated with his household. At the final bell, the Tarnished can descend into the presence of Metyr, Mother of Fingers. The sequence of maps and journeys has brought the visitor beneath the very building where Ymir began giving instructions.

Her remembrance calls Metyr a daughter of the Greater Will and the first shooting star to fall upon the Lands Between. That places her arrival within the game's account of beings coming from beyond the world, rather than making her a creation of Marika's kingdom. Her existence also gives the Fingers an origin older than the instructions the Tarnished received at Roundtable Hold. The beings interpreting the order are descendants of a visitor whose connection to the Greater Will once belonged to her own experience.

The Staff of the Great Beyond describes that connection more closely. Metyr received signs from the Greater Will through the microcosm, then remained waiting after she was broken and abandoned. The description leaves her continuing to hope for another message. In that account, communication has failed at the level of the mother herself. The Two Fingers' confidence can therefore be reconsidered alongside a source that has fallen silent. Metyr's waiting gives a specific reason to question the continuing reliability of guidance passed down through her children.

Back in the cathedral, Ymir's desired place in this family becomes explicit. He presents himself as a prospective mother and turns against the Tarnished after Metyr's defeat. Fingercreepers appear during his confrontation, and his association with them is also present in the maternal staff connected with him. His grief for Yuri, expressed at the grave outside the cathedral, gives his longing a personal setting. The man explaining a failure in divine guidance also wants children and the position of a mother. His religious argument has developed beside that desire throughout the quest.

As the Tarnished travels deeper into the Land of Shadow, a message announces that Miquella's Great Rune has broken. The charm associated with it breaks as well. The change reaches people who may be standing far apart, each following the same absent demigod for reasons that had seemed compatible while his influence held them together. When the Tarnished speaks with them again, old loyalties and resentments return to the surface. Their journey can continue, but the company no longer has the same protection against conflict within its own ranks.

Ansbach understands the difference through his own experience. He served Mohg before joining the pursuit of Miquella, and he remembers attempting to challenge the demigod who had enchanted his lord. Miquella's influence overcame him too. Once released, Ansbach can recognize how thoroughly his feelings had been altered. His courtesy toward the Tarnished remains, yet his purpose changes. He wants to learn what has happened to Mohg and why Miquella's journey required him. In the Storehouse, that purpose leads him toward documents and discoveries that his former enchantment would have made difficult to pursue.

Leda responds to the same event by looking for threats. Her commitment to Miquella survives the broken charm, and she begins considering which companions might turn against him. The Hornsent attracts her suspicion first. His hatred for Messmer has always been present, but without the charm restraining the company, Leda sees his vengeance as a danger to their shared undertaking. The Tarnished can become involved in the conflict between them, and the outcome depends on that intervention. Their earlier fellowship provides no guarantee that both will continue along the road.

Moore has fewer resources for answering the change. He gathers supplies with the forager brood and takes comfort in belonging to the group. When Miquella's influence falls away, the pain that influence had eased becomes difficult for him to bear. He asks the Tarnished how to proceed. Depending on the answer and the course of his quest, he may continue with Leda or reach a very different end. His situation gives the broken charm a quieter consequence than the conflict among the knights. Fellowship had helped him live with rejection, and its loss leaves him asking someone else for direction.

Thiollier begins from another attachment altogether. His devotion is to St. Trina, and information from the Tarnished can lead him toward the Stone Coffin Fissure, where she lies in the Garden of Deep Purple. He wants to hear her voice and believes himself suited to serve her. The garden places that longing beside the physical condition of the person he reveres. St. Trina has been discarded during Miquella's passage toward godhood. Reaching her means traveling down to something he has left behind, away from the route his other followers are taking upward.

The Tarnished can repeatedly drink St. Trina's nectar and eventually hear her request. She asks for Miquella to be killed because she regards godhood as a prison awaiting him. Her appeal comes from someone intimately bound to him, abandoned as he proceeds with his transformation. It introduces an objection unlike Leda's concern about enemies or Ansbach's duty to Mohg. St. Trina wants an end to Miquella's ascent for his own sake. The course he has chosen appears to her as a captivity from which he should be spared.

Near the end of the followers' journey, Leda gathers those who remain aligned with her and confronts the Tarnished. Dryleaf Dane stands with her, while Freyja and Moore may also join according to their quests. Ansbach and Thiollier can assist the Tarnished if their own stories have reached the required point. The opposing sides are therefore assembled from decisions made after the enchantment ended. The confrontation gives those separate decisions a shared location, where people who once exchanged help on the road can face one another as enemies.

Leda also recognizes that the Tarnished's route has never been identical to theirs. Grace has carried another prospective lord into Miquella's path, and that claim cannot fit comfortably beside her loyalty. By the time the confrontation is over, the fellowship has become a collection of individual commitments with lasting consequences. Ansbach can act for Mohg, Thiollier for St. Trina, and Leda for Miquella. The broken Great Rune has left each surviving follower able to pursue the allegiance that remains when the charm no longer makes companionship feel effortless.


In the Specimen Storehouse, Ansbach searches through records while Freyja pursues questions about Radahn elsewhere in the same immense structure. The Secret Rite Scroll gives his investigation a direction. Its account of the divine gate describes a rite in which a lord brings about a god's return, and the lord's soul requires a vessel. For Ansbach, those instructions connect a religious procedure with the disappearance of someone he served. Mohg's body has been taken, and Miquella has a use for it that reaches beyond the death of its original owner.

Ansbach explains his discovery to the Tarnished with the distress of a former servant whose lord has suffered another indignity. He concludes that Mohg's remains are intended to receive Radahn's soul. His account brings together two demigods whose defeats belong to the conditions for entering the expansion. Radahn had to die, and Mohg had to fall before the Tarnished could follow Miquella through the withered arm at the cocoon. Within the story now unfolding, their deaths provide different necessities for the same attempt to establish a new god and lord.

The rite itself belongs to the divine gate at Enir-Ilim. Reaching it requires passage beyond the sealing tree and through the tower's surviving defenses. The destination gives Miquella's journey a physical center. He has left pieces of himself across the Land of Shadow while moving toward a place where divinity can be attained. The scroll explains why a consort matters to that arrival. A soul and a suitable vessel must be brought into the process, so the search for Miquella also becomes a search for the lord who will accompany him.

At the summit, the Tarnished encounters Radahn as the promised consort. He appears in a restored martial form, wielding paired swords and using the gravity powers associated with him. The figure at the gate no longer has the ruined condition in which the Tarnished met him in Caelid. His return gives Miquella's proposed succession a lord with a recognizable identity and formidable strength. The scroll's instructions have led toward someone who can stand at the entrance to a new divine age and physically oppose another claimant approaching it.

As the confrontation continues, Miquella returns and joins him. He settles upon Radahn's back, and his presence adds holy power to the consort's attacks. The god and lord described by the plan now occupy the same encounter. Miquella addresses the Tarnished as someone seeking the old order's lordship and asks for the way to be yielded. He has reached the point where his new age requires the approaching rival to give up a competing claim. Radahn supplies the force that makes this demand more than a declaration.

A memory available after the encounter shows Miquella making his request and referring to a vow. Radahn gives no audible response in that memory, and the encounter supplies no spoken account from him of the earlier agreement. The extent of his original willingness remains uncertain. Miquella's wish, the later rite and the consortship at the gate are explicit; a complete conversation establishing Radahn's consent hasn't been shown. Freyja's enthusiasm is her own judgment as his former soldier, and it can't fill in the missing response on his behalf.

If the Tarnished defeats the pair, the attempted succession ends at the divine gate. Ansbach, if present through his completed quest, has carried his allegiance to Mohg to the place where the stolen body was used. Miquella has attained the divine state he sought, and Radahn has returned as the lord at his side, yet neither establishes an age beyond that encounter. The remains of their plan are concrete: the rite recorded in the scroll, the consort fought at the summit and the memory of Miquella asking his chosen lord to join him.


The Circlet of Light remains after Miquella and his consort have been defeated. Its description associates it with the age of compassion he intended to establish. Small enough to be carried away from the divine gate, it is a surviving object from a future that hasn't come to pass. Miquella had reached godhood, acquired his promised lord and confronted the Tarnished who stood in his way. His defeat leaves the proposed age without the divine ruler who meant to begin it, while the Land of Shadow still contains the people and places through which he traveled.

In the Land of Shadow, his route passed through another body of suffering. The hornsent had endured the crusade conducted in Marika's name, and their ruined sacred places lay on the way toward his own ascension. Miquella's followers could therefore understand him as someone seeking to move beyond the violence of his inheritance. The Hornsent himself initially invested hope in that possibility. For someone living with the consequences of Marika's reign, her child's promise of a different age could have immediate value, even while the need for revenge remained unresolved.

The crosses along Miquella's path record how he attempted to separate himself from that inheritance. They identify places where he abandoned parts of his flesh and other aspects of himself. At first, these markers help the Tarnished follow an absent traveler. Read across the journey, they become a record of what he believed he had to surrender. His divine arrival required a body and identity remade through departure. The people following him encountered the marks of those departures while continuing to speak of the person they expected to find at the end.

One cross marks the abandonment of his love. Nearby, the path toward St. Trina gives that declaration a person and a place. She rests in the Garden of Deep Purple, separated from the demigod with whom she had been bound. Her request that Miquella be killed comes with her judgment that godhood will imprison him. By the time the Tarnished reaches the gate, her objection has already made the cost of his transformation specific. Someone he has left behind believes the position he is seeking will prevent the freedom he expects it to give.

Miquella's charm complicates his compassion through actions the Tarnished has already experienced. Ansbach's allegiance was altered despite his attempt to resist, and the followers shared an ease with one another that fractured when the Great Rune broke. The enchantment could relieve hostility and distress, as Moore's experience suggests, while also keeping people attached to a cause they might otherwise oppose. Once released, Ansbach sought answers about Mohg and Leda began removing perceived threats. Their choices showed how much disagreement had existed within a fellowship that previously seemed able to hold together.

The final encounter brings that method directly to the Tarnished. Miquella can embrace the approaching rival and place a mark upon them. A second embrace can take the Tarnished's heart, ending their resistance in allegiance to him. His age asks the existing claimant to surrender through a change in devotion. The gesture matches his promise of gentleness in its outward form, yet the consequence is decisive: the Tarnished can cease resisting because the desire to resist has been taken away.

Defeating Miquella prevents that outcome without settling every problem he hoped to address. Malenia's affliction belongs to the history that drove him toward new answers. The persecuted who sought the Haligtree still explain the need that made his projects attractive. The hornsent dead aren't restored by the defeat of Marika's son or her divine successor. What ends at Enir-Ilim is this particular attempt to establish a god and consort. The suffering that gave the project its appeal doesn't vanish when the person proposing it can no longer proceed.

The Tarnished's future remains tied to decisions in the Lands Between. Depending on the path taken, the Elden Ring can be repaired, a mending rune can reshape the resulting order, Ranni's departure can be joined, or the Frenzied Flame can consume the world. Those are conditional outcomes of the base journey. Completing the passage through the Land of Shadow doesn't select one on the player's behalf or provide a separate ending in which Miquella rules. The claimant who survives the divine gate still has a future to decide elsewhere.

Behind that departure, Miquella's crosses remain at the places where he abandoned himself. They stand beside roads, within ruins and near the approaches his followers used, recording losses that can't be gathered back into the age he proposed. The circlet can leave Enir-Ilim with the Tarnished, while St. Trina's blossom can leave the garden below. The gate itself remains above the tower, without the god and lord who stood before it. Far away in the Lands Between, the Elden Ring's fate depends on the ending the Tarnished has chosen, or has yet to choose.

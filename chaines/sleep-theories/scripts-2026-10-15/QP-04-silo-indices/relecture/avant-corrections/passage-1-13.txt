Helen is pregnant when she and Daniel are separated in the Before Times story. She goes into Silo 18, while Daniel goes into Silo 1. That gives her pregnancy a particular place in the history of Juliette's home. A woman carrying a child enters the installation where Juliette will later live. It's understandable that this draws attention toward a possible family connection. There's a real connection between the places in their stories, and the pregnancy introduces the possibility of another life continuing inside Silo 18.

For Helen, though, the immediate situation is separation. The unveiling and the explosion belong to the circumstances in which she and Daniel end up in different silos. Her pregnancy means that the consequences extend beyond where the two adults are sent. Daniel is separated from a pregnant Helen, and Helen enters Silo 18 without him. Before trying to connect that child to someone in Juliette's time, it's worth keeping that particular family situation in view. Their lives have already been divided by the arrangements around them.

Silo 18 makes the ancestry idea feel more substantial than a resemblance between two characters. Helen has entered a named place that the audience knows through Juliette, her father, and the people she works with in Mechanical. We can put Helen and Juliette within the history of the same installation. That's useful geographical information. It gives a family connection somewhere to have developed, although a shared place can also connect people through work, institutions, and the rules they inherit without making them relatives.

Juliette's relationship with her father, Pete Nichols, gives us a helpful comparison. The television story identifies them as father and daughter, and their encounters have the weight of a relationship both of them know. Their family history affects how they speak to each other and how they respond to loss. With Helen, the proposed connection has to cross a stretch of history that neither woman supplies through an equivalent encounter. Recognizing the place Helen enters doesn't fill in the people between her and Juliette.

A date would be one useful starting point. We would need to place Helen's pregnancy within a chronology that also places Juliette's birth, with enough detail to understand the interval. The Before Times setting tells us which part of the larger story Helen belongs to. It doesn't, on its own, give us a complete family calendar. Even if an interval allowed a certain number of generations, that would only tell us whether the proposed relationship could fit. It wouldn't identify the parents and children who actually occupied those generations.

The next step would concern Helen's child. A pregnancy gives the audience a reason to consider a continuing family line, while leaving the child's later life to be established. A named child, an identified parent, and an account of what happened to them would each add something concrete. Those details matter because an ancestry claim follows particular people. Without them, it's easy to move straight from Helen entering Silo 18 to Juliette living there later, skipping the very lives that would have to connect them.

Then the same work would have to continue through the intervening generations. A family tree needs links that meet: this person's child is the parent of that person, whose child can in turn be identified. One secure relationship near Helen and another near Juliette would still leave a gap if nothing joined them. The people in that gap would have their own births and families. Treating them as a blank passage of time makes the theory sound smoother, but it also removes the information needed to test it.

Written records could help, provided they connected identifiable people. A record naming Helen and her child would answer a different question from a record naming one of Juliette's grandparents. Together, they might begin a chain, and further records could join it. The useful detail would be the relationship written down and how it connected with the next entry. An old document mentioning Helen somewhere in Silo 18's history would give her a place in that history without automatically attaching her to the Nichols family.

There's also a difference between a family connection the audience can recognize and one Juliette could investigate. We see Helen's Before Times story alongside the later life inside the silo. Juliette hasn't been given that same view merely because the television narrative places both women before us. For her to discover such a relationship, she would need something she could encounter and follow: an identified relative, a record, or an account that led to another person. The audience's ability to compare scenes doesn't put that information into her hands.

The strongest reason to consider the ancestry theory is therefore quite specific. Helen is pregnant, and she enters Silo 18. Those facts put a possible descendant within the history of a place already central to the series. The reason to leave the theory open is equally specific: the released story hasn't supplied a complete chain connecting her child to Juliette. Familiarity can make that missing stretch feel shorter. We've spent time with both women, so joining their lives can seem natural even when the family relationships remain unaccounted for.

Helen's pregnancy still matters without a named later descendant. She enters the silo carrying expectations about a child whose life will begin under circumstances very different from the life she knew outside. Daniel's separation from her changes who can share that immediate experience. We don't have to decide which later character belongs to their family to understand the consequence. The pregnancy brings the future into Helen's situation in a direct, personal way, before anyone can turn that future into a genealogy.

And if a family connection were eventually documented, it would add a particular history to Juliette's life. It wouldn't replace the relationships already shown: Pete as her father, the losses within her family, and the work through which she makes a life in Mechanical. Those experiences have their own causes and consequences. For now, Helen's place is precise enough to hold onto. She's pregnant, she's been separated from Daniel, and she enters Silo 18. A family tree would need to begin by following the child she carries.

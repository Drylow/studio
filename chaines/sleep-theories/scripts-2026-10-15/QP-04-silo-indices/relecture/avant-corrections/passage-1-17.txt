Camille gives Juliette footage of a life Juliette can't check against her own recollection. In the season-three opening, Juliette's memory loss makes that a particularly effective kind of deception. A recording appears to bring the missing past within reach. There are images to examine, something outside her own uncertain recall. But Camille supplies false footage, so the apparent help introduces another problem. Juliette has to consider an account of herself whose persuasive force comes partly from the very gaps that prevent her from testing it.

There's a practical difficulty here that goes beyond forgetting a name or losing track of an event. Juliette can't place the material beside a dependable recollection and notice where the two disagree. The footage seems to offer a way around that difficulty. It can give an account a visible form, with something for Juliette to return to when her own past remains inaccessible. Camille is therefore providing both the account and its apparent corroboration. Those two things can look independent when they're arriving together.

Juliette's earlier investigation gives this encounter a particular weight. When she worked through George's discoveries, objects and recordings opened routes into things the silo had concealed. Looking at a recording was a way to find something that people in authority didn't want her to know. Now Camille uses recorded material in the opposite direction. Juliette's willingness to examine something concrete leaves her exposed when the person supplying it has prepared a false account.

The change doesn't make Juliette foolish for looking. Refusing to examine the footage would also mean refusing a possible source of information about a period she can't remember. She's in a situation where useful help and deliberate deception can arrive in the same form. Camille doesn't have to persuade her to abandon inquiry altogether. She can place false material within the inquiry Juliette already needs to undertake. That makes the encounter more troubling than a straightforward order to stop asking about her past.

The suppressants belong to this same immediate situation. Camille uses them while Juliette is experiencing memory loss, alongside the false footage. For Juliette, the immediate consequence is that even the process of recovering her bearings is vulnerable to interference.

Juliette needs a way to connect an assertion about her life to an experience someone can actually account for. Her impaired recollection makes that ordinary act of comparison unusually difficult.

Victor measures the distance between Colony House and the trees by walking it. In the third episode of From, he starts from a corner of the house and counts his steps toward the nearby forest. It's a small task beside a large building, and at first it can seem like something that concerns only him. The other residents have immediate reasons to worry about the woods. Victor is paying attention to where those woods begin. He's checking whether the trees have moved, using a stretch of ground he can cross on foot.

The corner matters because it gives his walk a particular starting place. Colony House is a familiar building, somewhere he can return to and begin again. The trees provide the other end of the distance he's considering. Between them is ground that people can walk over during the day. Victor doesn't have to travel into unfamiliar territory to find something troubling. A change might be happening beside the place where he lives, close enough that he can look for it through an ordinary movement of his own body.

Counting steps makes the observation feel very much like Victor's work. He carries out the task himself, with the surroundings in front of him. There's no elaborate equipment involved in this scene. He has his pace, the corner, and his familiarity with the grounds. That gives his concern a physical form. A person who hears that the forest might be changing can dismiss the idea in passing. Victor has to cross the distance, one step after another, while considering whether it matches the place he remembers.

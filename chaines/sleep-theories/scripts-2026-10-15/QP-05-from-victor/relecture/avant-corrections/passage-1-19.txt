Henry's hospital visions offer him a Victor whose life seems to have continued somewhere beyond the town. Across the sixth through ninth episodes of season four, Henry encounters the possibility of an ordinary life with another version of his son. For a father who has found Victor after a separation lasting decades, that possibility has a particular appeal. Henry has his son back, yet the man he has found has grown up through experiences Henry couldn't share or prevent. The visions give him an image of family life without that same distance.

The hospital setting helps make that image persuasive. A hospital belongs to a world where frightening experiences might receive an explanation, where someone might recover and return home. Henry is already living somewhere that resists those expectations. The town has brought him into contact with the son he lost, but it hasn't restored the life they lost together. Against that situation, the hospital offers a familiar kind of setting for the possibility that things could still be put right.

The other Victor gives that possibility a person Henry can recognize. An ordinary life is easier to imagine when someone familiar appears to inhabit it. Henry doesn't have to consider an empty promise of happiness. He's presented with a son who appears to belong to a different account of what has happened. That makes the vision an intrusion into the reunion itself. Henry has been trying to understand the Victor beside him, and now he's being offered another Victor as someone he might have instead.

There's a painful difference between finding a missing child and meeting that child as an adult. Henry can recognize Victor and still have no way to recover the intervening years. He wasn't there for the routines Victor developed in the town, the dangers he learned to avoid, or the people he came to know. Their reunion brings those missing years into their relationship every day. The visions seem designed to exploit that difficulty by presenting a family life that doesn't require Henry to work through so much unfamiliar history.

Victor's survival has also made him someone with habits and attachments Henry didn't help form. His drawings matter to him. So do the things he has preserved and the friendships he has made. Henry has entered a life that was continuing before he arrived, however strange or painful parts of it may look from outside. The alternate-looking son invites a different response. Henry can imagine being returned to a more recognizable place within his son's life, instead of learning where he belongs in the life Victor actually has.

That appeal comes with a condition. The visions demand that Henry remove the real Victor, and the promised life becomes tied to harming the son who is there with him. This gives the offer its coercive shape. Henry's desire to be reunited with Victor is turned against the person whose survival made their reunion possible. What first appears to offer relief from separation starts requiring another separation, one Henry would have to bring about himself. The demand changes what the hospital and its apparent normality mean.

It also puts an especially cruel pressure on Henry's role as a father. He came to the town and found that Victor had survived. Now the visions ask him to approach that surviving son as an obstacle to getting his family back. To accept the offer, Henry would have to let an image of Victor outweigh the adult he can speak to and spend time with. His longing for the years they missed gives the manipulation something personal to use. The promised future is attached to the very loss he's already endured.

The hospital encounters don't establish an actual alternate universe, and they don't confirm that the town is a dream or a coma experience. Through these season four episodes, the visions are presented as manipulation, with their promised normal life tied to a demand for harm. The other Victor belongs to that offer. His appearance gives Henry something emotionally persuasive to respond to, but it doesn't provide a verified second home where another version of the family is waiting.

For Victor, this creates a danger he can't address merely by explaining another piece of the town's history. Henry's uncertainty concerns Victor himself. The father who recognizes him is being encouraged to imagine that recognition has led him to the wrong life. Victor could describe what happened during Henry's absence and still face an image that promises to make the absence unnecessary. The visions don't have to account for those years in detail. They offer Henry a way to imagine living without their consequences.

That makes Henry's situation different from the difficulty Victor faces when he tries to remember. Victor has to return to experiences he'd rather leave alone, and sometimes correct the account he first gives. Henry is receiving an account with something he desperately wants inside it. The hospital and the other son make that account easier to welcome. Its emotional appeal helps explain why it can exert pressure without supplying a dependable explanation of the town or a workable route out.

Henry's distress doesn't make his affection for Victor unreal. The visions gain their force because the relationship matters to him. A father with no wish to recover life with his son would have little reason to care about the alternative being offered. Here, the promised ordinary life reaches into an attachment that already exists. The strain comes from the condition attached to it. Henry is asked to direct his hope toward an imagined reunion while becoming a danger to the son with whom he has already reunited.

The real Victor remains an adult Henry has only begun to know again. Their shared family history gives them a place to start, while everything Victor lived through during the separation still requires time and patience. The visions offer Henry relief from that difficult process, with a hospital, a normal life, and another Victor who appears to fit within it. Yet the son Henry has found is still there in the town. Any life they build together has to include the years Henry missed and the person Victor became during them.

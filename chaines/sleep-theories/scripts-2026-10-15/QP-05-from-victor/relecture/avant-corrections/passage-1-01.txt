The correction gives us a useful way to approach Victor's other accounts. When he remembers a person being present, that's one part of the story. What that person said, what Victor understood at the time, and what he now thinks it meant are further parts. They don't always return together. In the Christopher conversation, the identity of the speaker needs revising. It would be a mistake to carry on treating Jasper as the confirmed speaker once Victor has recovered the Boy in White's involvement.

It would also leave us with very little if we discarded everything Victor knows because of that mistake. His childhood in the town happened. His drawings and the possessions he's kept are there for other people to encounter. Ethan meets him as a person with habits, knowledge, and fears that have developed over a long residence. Victor isn't supplying an account of a place he's only imagined. The difficulty is working out which parts of his experience he can describe clearly, and where something remains missing or confused.

There's a practical reason the residents keep returning to him. A newcomer can explore a building or inspect an object, but that won't reveal everything that happened there before they arrived. Victor sometimes supplies that earlier context. He can connect a present discovery with people who are no longer available to explain themselves. His account may give someone a place to look or something to ask about. That remains useful even when the first explanation needs changing, provided the search can accommodate a correction.

For Victor, though, retrieving that context isn't as straightforward as answering a question about directions. The people involved belong to his childhood, including his mother and sister. Recalling them means returning to events in which he was a child with limited information. He couldn't have followed every adult or understood every decision. Even a fully accurate recollection of what he saw would leave things outside his view. His years in the town don't remove that original limit, and they don't give him access to conversations he never heard.

Sara's involvement in Thresholds matters within those limits. Victor doesn't reach the revised account entirely on his own; another person helps him work through what he's trying to remember. That gives the correction a social setting. He can say something, encounter a response, and continue. Sara hasn't brought a recording of Christopher's conversation, so her presence can't independently verify every detail. Still, the exchange allows Victor to move beyond the account that had sent him after Jasper. Someone listening to him becomes part of how the investigation progresses.

One possible response is to become suspicious of the correction itself. If Victor was certain enough about Jasper to seek him out, why accept a different speaker now? There's a reason to ask that, because confidence alone didn't settle the earlier account. The useful answer is to keep the revised account available for comparison with what else happens. The Boy in White is already someone Victor knows within the town's strange history. Naming him changes the lead under consideration; it doesn't require us to decide that Victor has now recovered every word perfectly.

The same patience applies to the things Victor preserves. A drawing can remain available when its maker can't explain it, and an object can bring a person back into conversation after a long silence. Neither supplies all its own context. Someone still has to ask what Victor associates with it, and whether anything else fits that association. This is where his exchanges with Ethan become valuable. Ethan gives Victor someone to share things with, while Victor's knowledge gives Ethan ways to approach a place the adults are also struggling to understand.

So the Jasper correction changes how much weight we place on a particular recollection, without leaving Victor's entire history unusable. We can retain the conversation as something he remembers, accept that he has revised its speaker, and leave uncertain details open. We can also notice the effort involved in making that revision. Victor has to return to an upsetting part of his past and allow another person to hear that his earlier account was wrong. In Thresholds, Sara stays with that process, and Victor manages to tell her something different.

Sara helps Victor reach a correction that makes his earlier search look mistaken. He'd believed Jasper was the one speaking to Christopher. In the season three episode Thresholds, he remembers the Boy in White in that conversation instead. The dummy had occupied the place of the speaker in his account, and recovering it hadn't produced the answer he expected. Victor has to change something he'd already asked other people to take seriously. That makes the exchange with Sara a useful comparison for what Henry is being offered.

Victor's correction concerns a particular event. Christopher was involved, there was a conversation, and Victor had attached the wrong speaker to it. Keeping those details separate lets him revise the account without abandoning everything around it. Jasper's presence in his recollections doesn't require Jasper to have spoken. Once Victor recognizes the Boy in White's role, the question becomes what that figure told Christopher and how Christopher responded. The correction gives them a more specific account to work with.

Sara's part in this matters because Victor doesn't reach the revised account entirely on his own. She helps him return to something he's struggling to remember. Still, she wasn't standing beside Christopher during the original conversation. Her presence gives Victor someone who can listen and help him work through his account; it doesn't give the town another eyewitness to that childhood moment.

Henry's hospital and normal-life visions create a different situation during season four. The apparent alternative comes to him with its own explanation of what he should do. Another Victor appears within a life Henry has reason to want, and the offer requires him to remove the son he's found in the town. There isn't an equivalent of Sara sitting with Henry and independently confirming that this promised life exists. The vision supplies both the attractive destination and the instruction for reaching it.

There's a practical place to begin checking that instruction. Henry has met the adult Victor in the town. They've recognized each other and begun confronting their separation. Those encounters happen within a shared world, where other residents can also speak to Victor. The alternate-looking son appears within Henry's visions. His appearance may persuade Henry, but it doesn't independently confirm the account surrounding him. Everything that makes the alternative seem available arrives through the same experience that's asking Henry to act against his son.

That difference also helps explain why emotional comfort can't settle the matter. Victor's conversation with Sara is painful and frustrating, yet it leads to a correction he can state plainly. Henry's visions contain relief, but their pleasantness doesn't answer whether their instructions lead anywhere safe. Neither man's feelings can establish who spoke to Christopher or where Henry would go after obeying the demand. For those questions, the details outside the feeling matter: who else was there, what can be checked, and what the proposed action would actually involve.

Henry's offer places pressure on disagreement in another way. The promised ordinary life is conditional: he must harm the real Victor to obtain it. Refusing that condition means refusing the route the visions have supplied. That structure makes hesitation costly for Henry, because the offer has attached his hopes to obedience. Victor's correction allows him to keep talking with Sara after his first account fails. Henry's vision directs him toward an action against someone who could otherwise remain beside him while he questions what he's been shown.

Sara can help Victor reconsider who spoke to Christopher without making anyone's safety depend on accepting his revised account. Henry's offer ties the life he wants to harming Victor, putting pressure on him to obey rather than keep questioning.

The comparison applies to these encounters; it doesn't establish a common source or a single rule for every vision in the town.

For Victor, having Sara listen means a mistake can be corrected in company. He can admit that Jasper wasn't the speaker and still have someone there to hear what he remembers next. Henry needs that room to hesitate around the hospital visions, too. The adult Victor is available for another conversation, however difficult their reunion has become. Keeping him safe preserves that possibility. Henry can continue learning about the son who survived, with other people present, without first accepting the demand made by the son who appears in the vision.

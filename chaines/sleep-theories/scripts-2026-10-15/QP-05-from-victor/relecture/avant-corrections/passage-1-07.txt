Miranda tells Victor and Eloise to remain hidden while she leaves. For the children, the instruction has an immediate, practical meaning. They're supposed to stay where she has put them and wait for her return. Victor doesn't accompany his mother on the journey she intends to make. His part in her plan is to remain behind. That's a small role compared with the task Miranda has taken on, though it becomes terribly difficult to live with once she doesn't come back.

In the remembered scene, Miranda says goodbye to her children in the cellar. She expects them to follow her instruction even though she won't be there to enforce it. The hiding place separates them from whatever she's going out to face, and it also leaves them unable to see what happens to her. Victor can remember his mother leaving. He can remember what she asked him to do. The rest of her journey has to reach him through something other than watching her take it.

Eloise leaves after Miranda, while Victor stays. That difference matters because it gives Victor a particular position in the family story. He's the child who follows the instruction to remain hidden, and he's separated from both his mother and his sister as a result. Staying was what Miranda wanted him to do. Yet when the people he waits for don't return, the same action can become something he goes back over. He has a decision of his own to remember, even though he had no say in Miranda's decision to leave.

It's easy to place an adult understanding inside that cellar when looking back at it. Miranda has spoken about children who need help, and Victor later associates her departure with an attempt to save them. But a child hearing that his mother has to go somewhere doesn't receive all the knowledge she carries. He doesn't automatically know how she reached her conclusions, which dangers she expects, or why she believes she must act then. Her explanation gives him a reason for her departure without making him a partner in planning it.

When Victor tells Tabitha about his mother, he brings together things that reached him in different ways. There's the goodbye he experienced, the stories Miranda told her children, and what he encountered after leaving the hiding place. Those parts belong to the same loss, so it's natural for them to come out as one account. For Tabitha, though, they answer different questions. Miranda's instruction explains why Victor stayed in the cellar. Her accounts of the children help explain what Victor believes she was trying to accomplish.

The pictures associated with Eloise add another route back to what Miranda had told the family. They preserve subjects that Victor hadn't been able to place in his remembered childhood. When he recognizes that his sister made them, he can reconnect those pictures with their mother's stories. This gives Tabitha more to work with than a bare statement that Miranda went away. It still leaves Victor describing an adult undertaking from the position of a son who heard about it and was then told to wait.

There's a painful practical limit to waiting in that situation. Victor can't ask Miranda whether she needs longer. He can't check whether she has changed her route or decided to return. The instruction depends on the adult coming back to end it, and Miranda never does. Once Victor eventually emerges, he has to make sense of a town whose inhabitants have been killed. The person who could have told him what to do next is among the people he has lost. Her last direction remains available to him after her guidance is gone.

His later account includes finding Miranda's body near the tree she had been trying to reach. For Victor, that discovery gives his mother's absence a terrible certainty. It also connects her intended journey with a place he can identify. But he wasn't beside her throughout that journey. Finding her afterward doesn't give the child access to every action that brought her there. The distinction matters when Victor's account helps someone else consider following a route his mother attempted. He knows where he found her, and he knows she didn't return to the cellar.

Tabitha has a different position when she hears this story. She's a mother trying to understand a place that has trapped her own children, and Victor is telling her about a mother who left her children hidden while she tried to help others. That resemblance gives the conversation weight without making the two women interchangeable. Tabitha can ask about the destination and the children Miranda hoped to save. Victor has to answer through a goodbye that, for him, never became a reunion. A practical question can bring him back to that separation.

Victor's childhood obedience also deserves to remain part of the account. Miranda didn't ask him to rescue her or supervise Eloise through every possible danger. She told the children to stay hidden. Eloise's departure puts Victor in a situation that her instruction hasn't resolved for him: his sister has gone after their mother, and following her would mean leaving too. We can read his continued waiting as an attempt to do what Miranda asked. There isn't a guaranteed rescue he could have carried out simply by making the other decision.

That helps explain the burden in remembering without assigning Victor a diagnosis. Looking back, he knows the outcome that he couldn't know while he waited. Every remembered opportunity to move or speak now sits beside the knowledge that his family didn't come back together. An adult listener can recognize how little he could have done, while Victor can still be troubled by having remained behind. Those responses can exist in the same conversation. Correcting the scale of his responsibility doesn't require dismissing the loss he associates with that cellar.

Miranda's instruction leaves Victor with something clear enough to repeat: she wanted her children to hide while she went to act. His account gives Tabitha that moment from inside the family, including the separation between the mother who leaves and the son who waits. Whatever Tabitha learns about Miranda's purpose, Victor was a child under her care when she said goodbye. He stayed where she'd asked him to stay, and the return he was waiting for never happened.

Henry raises a gun against the son he came to find. In the season four finale, the demand attached to his visions reaches an actual confrontation with Victor. Ethan interrupts, and Victor disarms his father. Both men survive.  Henry has been offered a life with another Victor. The Victor in front of him now has to stop his father from acting on that offer.

Ethan's interruption brings someone else into a confrontation that Henry's visions have framed around a father and his son. Ethan knows Victor through their encounters in the town. He's asked him questions, spent time with him and received things Victor has struggled to share with adults. His presence matters because the real Victor already has a life involving other people. Henry's offered replacement leaves that life out. Ethan enters the moment as someone for whom losing Victor would have consequences of his own.

Ethan's interruption gives Victor the chance to get the gun away from Henry. His intervention matters even though the scene doesn't explain everything he intends. He doesn't have to explain the visions or persuade Henry through a complete account of what's happened. The confrontation changes through an action by a child who knows Victor. Whatever Henry has been led to expect, the situation now includes another person who can affect what he does.

Victor then has to deal with the weapon himself. Disarming Henry requires him to respond to what his father is doing, however bewildering or painful that may be. Their family connection doesn't make the gun harmless. Henry's distress doesn't remove the need to stop him. Victor's action protects him from the immediate threat and also prevents Henry from carrying the attempt further. Both survive the encounter, which leaves them facing what Henry has done rather than an irreversible outcome delivered on the visions' terms.

For Victor, this is a different demand from recalling a childhood event. He isn't trying to identify a speaker or recover the order of something that happened long ago. His father is in front of him with a gun. He can respond to that danger without solving the origin of Henry's visions. That's an important part of how the confrontation works. The town can leave its larger explanations out of reach while still placing a person in a situation where the necessary immediate action is clear.

Taking the gun away ends the immediate threat, but it doesn't establish that Victor feels safe with Henry again. Both men survive with their relationship still unsettled.

Victor is left in an unfamiliar position with someone he once lost. Henry's return had allowed him to be recognized as a son whose childhood began outside the town. After the confrontation, Victor also has to take responsibility for an immediate danger involving his father. That burden arrives through a specific action, with no assurance that affection will make it easy. Ethan interrupts Henry's attempt, Victor disarms him, and the father who threatened his son is still there for Victor to face.

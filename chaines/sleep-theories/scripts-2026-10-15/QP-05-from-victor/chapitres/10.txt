Jasper gives Victor something he can go and find. Christopher is gone, and the conversation Victor wants to remember belongs to his childhood, but the ventriloquist's dummy is still somewhere in the town's underground passages. Victor believes that bringing it back will help him recover what Christopher heard. That's a practical task with a difficult journey attached to it. He can search a place, recognize an object and carry it home. Getting the conversation back is going to require something the journey itself can't supply.

The dummy already has a place in Victor's account of Christopher. Before Christopher became absorbed in the symbol, he entertained people with Jasper. A child could remember the man and the dummy together because that was how he encountered them. Christopher supplied the performance, while Jasper was the figure people looked toward. Later, when Victor tries to describe an alarming conversation, that familiar pairing is still present in his recollection. He attributes speech to Jasper, and he treats the dummy as something that might speak again.

For Victor, this isn't a random object chosen because it looks unsettling. Jasper belonged to the person whose change he remembers, and Victor connects it with a particular exchange he couldn't understand as a child. That connection explains why he pursues it. If the difficulty is remembering something that happened around Christopher, then finding something Christopher used seems worth trying. Victor has spent years preserving things connected with people who are gone. In this case, though, he's asking an object to do considerably more than help him remember its owner.

Retrieving Jasper means returning to the tunnels where the creatures rest during daylight. Victor has been underground before. He knows enough to guide someone through passages that would leave a newcomer with very little sense of where to go. Even so, familiarity doesn't make the place safe. He previously helped Tabitha find her way out while the creatures were waking. Returning for the dummy puts him back inside their resting place for an investigation whose outcome he can't guarantee. Jasper may be recognizable, but the surroundings belong to the creatures.

Henry accompanies him on this trip. The father who has only recently found his son now follows him into a part of the town Victor knows far better than he does. Their positions are unusual and quite concrete. Henry has the relationship of a parent, while Victor has the experience needed for this journey. Victor knows what they're looking for and why he wants it. Henry can go with him, but he can't draw on a shared recollection of Christopher's conversation. He wasn't there when Victor was a child in the town.

That gives Henry's presence a particular value. He can witness the retrieval and see where Jasper has ended up. He can help Victor face the journey without being able to settle the question that brought them there. The two men come away with an object whose present location they've both encountered. The older event still belongs to Victor's account. Having his father beside him changes the experience of searching, and it gives him someone to return with, even though Henry can't fill in the missing exchange.

The tunnels also make the search more troubling than opening a box of childhood belongings. The creatures occupy this place, and possessions associated with human lives have accumulated underground. Jasper is among things that Victor has to approach in their territory. During the retrieval, a creature speaks to him and makes clear that his visits aren't passing unnoticed. Victor and Henry get out with the dummy, but the encounter gives the trip a cost of its own. Victor has exposed himself and his father to danger to recover something he hopes will help.

Once Jasper is above ground, the task changes. Victor no longer has to locate it or work out how to reach it. He has the figure he remembers in front of him, and he tries to get it to speak. Nothing useful follows from that effort. The dummy doesn't repeat the conversation, explain Christopher's response or supply the missing connection Victor wants. All the work of recovering it has brought him to a moment of waiting for an answer from an object that remains silent.

Jasper's ordinary purpose makes that mistake especially understandable. A ventriloquist's act encourages an audience to follow a conversation between a person and a figure whose voice comes from that person. Christopher's performances gave Jasper an apparent presence long before Victor began searching for an explanation. The town also contains creatures and experiences that make an impossible speaker difficult to dismiss on appearance alone. Victor therefore has reasons to try. The difficulty comes when he expects Jasper's recovery to confirm the speaking role he's already assigned it.

Sara's involvement allows the search to continue without another expedition into the tunnels. She can sit with Victor and help him work through what he remembers. By then, Jasper has done what a recovered possession can do: it has given them something associated with Christopher to gather around. It hasn't answered their questions. The correction that follows in season three's "Thresholds" means the dummy can't remain the confirmed speaker in that childhood exchange. Recovering Jasper was a genuine achievement, but Victor still needs help recovering the event. With the journey finished, the work becomes a conversation with someone who's willing to listen.

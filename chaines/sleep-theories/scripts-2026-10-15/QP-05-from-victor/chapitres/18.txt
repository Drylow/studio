The revelation connects Tabitha to Miranda and Jade to Christopher through previous lives. Boyd brings that information to Henry and Victor. In the fourth-season episode Best Laid Plans, information that Tabitha and Jade have been struggling to understand enters the family sitting in front of him. Miranda was Henry's wife and Victor's mother. Tabitha is someone they've met as a separate person, with children of her own. The news puts those relationships beside each other without explaining how anyone is supposed to live with the combination.

Henry responds badly. Finding Victor had offered him a chance to be a father again after a separation that consumed much of their lives. Now Boyd is asking him to reconsider the person Miranda was as well. This comes while Henry is already being affected by Sophia's manipulation. His distress has more than one immediate source, and the disclosure doesn't arrive in a calm interval where he can set the rest aside and think it through.

Victor turns toward Tabitha rather than simply rejecting the connection. In the graveyard, he asks whether she is his mother. It's a direct question from someone whose memories of Miranda are tied to the loss of childhood care. The longer history of the town has become personal at precisely that point. Knowing that people can return in different lives is one kind of answer. Knowing whether the woman in front of him can now occupy his mother's place is a different need.

Tabitha also has an immediate family to answer. She and Ethan tell Julie about the connection to Miranda. Remembering things from another life has not removed Julie or Ethan from the present one. They remain her children, with their own experiences of the town and their own reasons to question what they're hearing. The disclosure reaches both families in the episode. It cannot be treated as a private revelation that concerns only Victor and leaves everyone around him untouched.

This helps explain why the question in the graveyard has no easy ordinary form. Victor isn't meeting a stranger who happens to resemble an old picture. Tabitha has recovered experiences that connect her to Miranda, and those experiences matter to the attempt to understand the town. Yet she has lived as Tabitha. The people she recognizes as her family have a claim on her attention that doesn't disappear when another part of her history becomes accessible. Victor can need her help without the story having established a complete replacement for the mother he lost.

He asks her and Ethan to come with him to investigate the Man in Yellow's car. That request gives his response a practical direction. He is drawing Tabitha into something he knows about the town and wants to understand better. Ethan comes too, so the investigation puts the child Victor cares about beside the woman now connected with Miranda. Rather than keeping the new information inside a conversation about the past, he brings it into an action they undertake together.

The car visit has its own discoveries and fears, which deserve their own attention. Here, the change is that Victor seeks Tabitha's company and help. He has spent a long time with knowledge other residents struggle to obtain from him. Now he is approaching someone who may know part of that earlier life in another way. Their relationship can become a place where accounts meet, even though neither person is able to supply a complete explanation of the town.

Henry's position is harder to steady. He has known Miranda within a marriage and family that the new account has made unfamiliar. The claim doesn't simply tell him where a missing possession has gone or identify the person in an old photograph. It asks him to place the woman he knew within a history extending beyond that relationship. His present contact with Tabitha doesn't give him the years of shared life he had with Miranda, however strongly the town connects the two women.

Victor's question suggests a wish for maternal connection, while Henry is trying to understand what the revelation does to the family life he remembers. They hear Boyd's information together, but each responds from a different relationship with Miranda.

There is a useful limit to what the disclosure changes. Tabitha's connection to Miranda is part of the town's revealed history. It doesn't make Henry and Tabitha an established married couple in their current lives, or make Ethan's needs disappear behind Victor's. The people involved still have to decide what help they can give and what a relationship can become now. A past-life connection has brought them into contact; it hasn't written every obligation or answered every question between them.

The bottle tree comes down in the season-four finale, and the protections the residents have depended on fail amid the crisis that follows. Victor's objection now has an outcome beside it. Before the uprooting, the others had his account of a warning from the Boy in White. Afterward, they have a dangerous change in the town to consider alongside what he said. The work has gone ahead, the tree is no longer standing, and the residents don't get the reassuring result they needed.

That gives this particular warning greater weight. Victor opposed the removal before anyone could point to its consequences. He wasn't looking back at a disaster and finding a reason he might have expected it. His concern was already attached to the bottle tree and the proposed action. When the finale brings a failure of protection after that action, the timing matters. It gives the residents a concrete reason to reconsider the objection they overruled, even while they're occupied with surviving what has happened.

There's a limit to how far that sequence takes the explanation. The uprooting and the crisis belong together in the unfolding events, but the finale doesn't supply a complete account of how the tree, the town's dangers and its protections connect. Perhaps the tree's removal affects those protections directly; the outcome makes that a serious possibility. The exact mechanism remains unresolved. Victor's warning can gain credibility without becoming a detailed description of every supernatural process involved, and the fallen tree doesn't come with instructions for repairing the damage.

Tabitha's passage established a route to the lighthouse, not what holds the town's protections in place. A route to the lighthouse and a barrier against the creatures are different things the residents have encountered. The finale brings those parts of their experience into a troubling proximity, because the removal of the tree is followed by a crisis in which protection fails. That connection deserves attention. It doesn't require the residents to assume that every strange object in town belongs to a single system they can now explain.

The talismans make that distinction especially important. Boyd found them before the present crisis, and the residents have used them to make enclosed spaces safer at night. Families gather indoors because those arrangements have worked against the creatures under conditions they've learned to respect. The season-four finale changes what they can safely expect in the situation now confronting them. It doesn't erase the nights when the talismans protected people, or turn every earlier decision to shelter behind one into a misunderstanding. Those earlier experiences remain part of the town's history.

Victor's position changes within that same problem. He's the person who tried to stop the removal, so his account has become relevant to understanding the danger everyone is facing. That doesn't make him responsible for producing a remedy. He passed on a warning against an action. He didn't claim to have a procedure for restoring the tree or renewing the protections after it fell. Asking him what the Boy in White said could help preserve the warning accurately. Expecting him to reverse the crisis would place a new burden on him.

Boyd also has to live with an outcome that wasn't available when he restrained Victor. At the time, he allowed the plan to proceed despite the objection. Once the tree is uprooted and the crisis follows, that decision acquires consequences beyond the disagreement between the two men. Boyd's responsibility now includes recognizing that Victor brought information the plan didn't adequately accommodate. He doesn't have to settle every question about the Boy in White before acknowledging the particular warning that reached him through Victor.

The Boy in White's part in this also becomes more significant without becoming fully explained. He warned against uprooting the bottle tree, and subsequent events give that warning force. His earlier help to Victor and his connection with Tabitha's lighthouse passage already placed him near the town's unusual routes. Here, he appears to know a danger attached to changing one of them. That makes his guidance worth examining closely. It still leaves the residents dealing with brief interventions from someone who hasn't given them a full account of the place.

At the dinner in Arrakeen, the Guild has a place among the people Duke Leto must learn to deal with on Arrakis. Its representative is a banker. That's a useful detail to keep beside the great ships and the guarded business of navigation. The Guild handles banking as well as interstellar transport, and here one of its officials sits within the social world of the new Atreides government. Leto can meet him across a dinner table, in company with people whose wealth and standing depend on the planet. The institution reaches into provincial affairs through someone who can take part in an ordinary political conversation.

The occasion gives Jessica a chance to study the guests while they study her family. The Atreides have taken over Arrakis from the Harkonnens, but the change of ruler hasn't swept away the people who did business under the previous regime. Water merchants, a manufacturer of stillsuits, a smuggler, and an Imperial planetologist all bring different interests to the gathering. A polite reception can accommodate them together. Governing them will be harder. Jessica has to consider who welcomes the new household, who fears it, and who might already be working against it, even while everyone observes the courtesies expected at the Duke's table.

The banker's official position answers only part of that inquiry. Jessica reads him as a Harkonnen agent. Her judgment comes through her attention to his behavior and reactions, with the Bene Gesserit training that makes such observation part of her daily life. The distinction matters within the scene. He has a recognized financial office, and Jessica believes he also has a private allegiance to the family the Atreides have displaced. A guest can arrive under an institution's respectable name while carrying commitments that don't appear in his introduction. For Jessica, receiving him politely doesn't remove the need to watch him.

That puts Leto's household in an uncomfortable practical position. A banker connected with the Guild belongs to a system the Atreides can't casually set aside. Their new government must operate in an empire where the Guild's services are part of established life. If Jessica's assessment is right, a useful financial representative is also someone through whom a hostile House retains influence. The immediate consequence is a more guarded relationship with this particular man. Nothing at dinner warrants treating every Guild official as a Harkonnen agent. The difficulty is already substantial at the scale Jessica actually faces: an unwelcome allegiance attached to a legitimate office.

Water gives the gathering a concrete subject on which these competing interests can emerge. On Arrakis, water determines whether people can survive outside shelter, what they can grow, and how much protection they need against their surroundings. It also supports businesses whose owners have something to lose when conditions change. Lingar Bewt, the water seller, doesn't encounter the planet's scarcity in the same way as someone struggling to obtain a drink. He sells what other people need. His presence beside the Guild banker brings local commerce and imperial finance into the same room, without making their interests identical or turning them into a single enterprise.

Leto has already disturbed one custom connected with the household's water. The old practice allows guests to use water and discard the wet towels, which are then valuable to the people waiting outside. He orders a change so that the water goes to those people directly. It's an immediate act within his own residence, and it tells the dinner guests something about their new ruler. Leto is willing to interfere with a familiar arrangement when he finds its treatment of the poor offensive. People accustomed to the Harkonnen household now have to consider whether other established practices will also face examination.

Kynes brings another kind of standing to the table. As Imperial planetologist and Judge of the Change, he has official responsibilities connected with Arrakis and the transfer of its government. He also knows the desert in ways that the newly arrived Atreides don't. During the dinner, Jessica notices that the banker fears him. That response gives her a more useful measure of Kynes than a title alone could provide. A man associated with the Guild's financial business is uneasy before him. Jessica can take account of that unease while she listens, even before she understands the full extent of Kynes's place on the planet.

The smuggler Esmar Tuek adds to that impression. Jessica recognizes an alignment between Tuek and Kynes during the gathering. Smuggling operates outside the ruling House's authorized arrangements for the spice trade, so Tuek is an especially revealing guest at an official reception. His presence means Leto's table includes someone whose activities don't fit neatly within the commerce the Duke is supposed to administer. Yet Tuek's relation to Kynes suggests that Arrakis has working associations which a newcomer won't discover merely by consulting the formal ranks of Imperial service. Jessica is watching those associations become visible through the guests' responses to one another.

Paul, meanwhile, has to take part as Leto's heir. The guests can assess his preparation, his manners, and his ability to respond when conversation becomes pointed. His exchanges about Arrakis allow Kynes to encounter something beyond the usual assumptions of an offworld ruling family. Paul has studied the planet, and he takes its possibilities seriously. For Jessica, his participation carries the added strain of watching her son perform a public duty among people she is still evaluating. He can't withdraw from the conversation simply because a guest may be hostile. His place in the family requires him to remain visible.

When Leto leaves on security business, Paul and Jessica remain with the guests. The Duke's departure changes the demands on both of them. Paul must continue bearing the expectations attached to the Atreides name, while Jessica keeps attending to the room's less explicit exchanges. The banker remains a financial representative whom they have received, a suspected Harkonnen agent in Jessica's estimation, and a man whose reaction to Kynes has attracted her notice. She has to hold those particulars together while the dinner continues. Across the table, his office stays respectable, his loyalties remain suspect, and Kynes is still someone he fears.

Thufir Hawat encounters the gap in imperial knowledge when he tries to make sense of Fremen fighting ability after the attack on House Atreides. He's an experienced Mentat, accustomed to weighing soldiers and resources. Yet the desert people describe fighting Sardaukar with an ease that unsettles his calculations. The Emperor's troops have a reputation that shapes military planning throughout the Imperium. The Fremen have been fighting them on Arrakis, and their account gives Hawat reason to reconsider the people the Harkonnens have treated as a troublesome desert population.

That error matters to the Guild because the same desert population supplies its satellite bribe. Accepting spice from the Fremen means dealing with people who can gather and deliver a substance the Guild needs. It doesn't necessarily mean understanding how those people live, how widely they're connected, or what they could accomplish together. The bargain gives the Guild a useful relationship with the desert. It also allows the Guild to keep benefiting from conditions that leave the Fremen's full strength difficult for outsiders to judge.

The Harkonnens' experience helps explain how that misunderstanding survives. They govern Arrakis through its settlements and spice operations, while the Fremen maintain lives beyond the areas the administration knows well. Harkonnen violence can kill Fremen without producing an accurate picture of their society. A patrol's encounter with desert fighters tells the authorities something about an immediate danger. It won't reveal every community that can shelter those fighters, teach their children, or continue resisting after the patrol has gone. Repeated conflict can coexist with a surprisingly poor understanding of an opponent.

Duke Leto approaches the problem differently. He sees the Fremen as a possible source of military strength for House Atreides, and Duncan Idaho makes contact with them on his behalf. Leto's interest already challenges the Harkonnen view of the desert population. Still, the alliance he hopes to build begins with only part of the picture. Paul and Jessica later enter Fremen life under circumstances Leto never intended. Their survival brings them into contact with obligations and capabilities that an agreement between a duke and a desert representative couldn't reveal all at once.

Paul and Jessica enter a society already able to shelter fugitives, judge newcomers and enforce its own decisions. The Fremen already have leadership, customs and ways of settling dangerous disputes. Paul's later military success grows within that established society. His training and position matter, but so do the people capable of carrying messages, supporting fighters and sustaining a campaign across terrain their enemies find punishing.

For the Guild, a continuing supply of spice could therefore give a misleading sense that the important business on Arrakis remains manageable. Spice is being gathered. Transport can continue. An arrangement with the Fremen preserves access to an additional source. As a political interpretation, the danger is that these working arrangements encourage attention to deliveries while leaving the society behind them poorly understood. A payment can arrive reliably even while the people making it are developing the means to challenge the planet's rulers. Nothing in the successful exchange requires their ambitions to remain small.

Paul's campaign makes that distinction concrete. Fremen attacks interfere with spice production, bringing desert warfare into the business on which the Imperium depends. The people who have supplied spice through a private bargain can also make its extraction more difficult. Their knowledge of Arrakis helps them choose where and how to fight. The Guild's need for the product doesn't give it the Fremen's experience of moving through the desert, surviving there, or recognizing the opportunities that terrain provides. Receiving the cargo leaves those practical advantages with the people who live on the planet.

The southern regions expose another weakness in outside estimates. They contain Fremen communities beyond the familiar centers of imperial activity, and the Emperor's forces encounter resistance there that contradicts the idea of a marginal population. During the raid in which Alia is captured and Paul's young son is killed, even people outside the main fighting force prove dangerous to the attackers. The losses within Paul's family are severe. At the same time, the raid reveals how badly imperial expectations fit the society those troops have entered. Fremen resistance extends into households the attackers might have expected to overwhelm.

Arrakis also has a living environment that the Guild's commercial dependence can't reduce to cargo. Liet-Kynes understands the desert through ecological relationships, while the Fremen inherit practical knowledge and a collective project shaped by his work and his father's. Their interest reaches beyond the spice available for collection now. They consider what the planet could become and what changes would require. The Guild's immediate need gives it a different concern: continued access to melange. Those concerns overlap while spice keeps moving, though they don't amount to the same understanding of Arrakis.

There's no need to suppose the Guild knows nothing about the desert. Its dealings with the Fremen already place it closer to hidden activity than an uninformed customer would be. The narrower failure is visible in what that access achieves. The Guild can secure spice and accommodate secrecy without preventing the emergence of a Fremen force capable of overturning the political arrangement around production. Its bargain works for the purpose for which it was made. The consequences of leaving that society underestimated reach far beyond the bargain.

By the final confrontation, Paul speaks with the strength of that society behind him. The Guild representatives face someone who has lived among the Fremen and can act through them on Arrakis. Their dealings over spice give them access to the Fremen, but access alone doesn't establish how fully they understand the society now supporting Paul. He knows the people prepared to fight for him and the desert on which their lives depend. The Guild has continued carrying the planet's wealth between stars. Now it has to reckon with what the people beneath its routes can do.

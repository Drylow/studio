The Guild's gift to Paul has Duncan Idaho's face and artificial eyes. In Dune Messiah, Edric presents him as Hayt, a ghola made by the Bene Tleilax from the flesh of Paul's dead swordmaster. Paul recognizes the man he lost, although the person before him doesn't possess Duncan's remembered life. That makes the presentation difficult to receive as an ordinary diplomatic courtesy. The Guild has brought someone into the imperial court whose appearance reaches back to Paul's life before he became Emperor, before his rule and religion spread across the inhabited worlds.

Duncan died protecting Paul and Jessica during the destruction of House Atreides on Arrakis. He fought the Sardaukar while they escaped, and Paul has lived with that loss. By returning his likeness in a living body, the conspirators give Paul something he has reason to want near him even while he suspects it. Duncan belonged to the household that raised him. He knew Paul as the duke's son. Hayt therefore arrives with an emotional importance rooted in Paul's life with Duncan, even before Paul knows what to make of the man before him.

There are separate contributions behind this one presentation. The Bene Tleilax produce the ghola and condition him. The Guild brings him to Paul and presents him through Edric's diplomatic position. Scytale participates in the conspiracy with knowledge of the Tleilaxu purpose. These roles overlap around Hayt, but they don't make the institutions interchangeable. Nothing about the Guild's delivery gives it authority over Tleilaxu manufacture. The arrangement works because the conspirators can combine different capabilities, then place the result before Paul as a single gift.

A ghola also has a particular connection to the dead person whose flesh supplied its beginning. Hayt isn't an actor selected because he resembles Duncan, and Paul can't dismiss the resemblance as a disguise. The Tleilaxu have recovered living human possibility from a dead body. Yet Hayt's lack of Duncan's personal recollections leaves Paul facing an unsettling separation. He can recognize a familiar face and manner without receiving the familiar relationship intact. Every exchange with Hayt has to take place across that separation, even when recognition makes it feel momentarily smaller.

The artificial eyes make the separation visible. They belong to the living Hayt Paul meets, rather than to the Duncan he remembers. The Tleilaxu workmanship has left him changed in ways the court can see. His training has changed him too. Hayt is a Mentat and a Zensunni philosopher, able to analyze and to question. Paul has been given someone who can speak with intellectual weight as well as emotional significance. That combination gives Hayt reasons to remain useful at court beyond the resemblance that first draws Paul's attention.

Hayt's philosophical conversations matter because Paul already carries the burden of the violence committed in his name. An adviser who questions his actions can reach concerns that imperial ceremony doesn't resolve. With Hayt, those questions come through Duncan's returned presence. Paul must consider what the speaker means while also encountering the face of someone who once protected him. The intended manipulation seems to depend on that closeness: words that might be dismissed from an enemy can become harder to set aside when spoken by this particular man.

Paul doesn't receive Hayt in innocence. He understands that the gift may contain a danger, and his suspicion remains part of their relationship. Accepting Hayt nevertheless makes sense within Paul's circumstances. Sending him away would mean rejecting the living presence connected to Duncan, along with whatever Paul might learn by keeping him close. The conspirators don't need Paul to mistake Edric for a generous friend. They need his attachment to Duncan to survive his awareness that the presentation has a hostile purpose. That's a much more personal approach than disguising an assassin as an unknown attendant.

The Guild's involvement helps bring that approach into an official setting. Edric can present Hayt through the recognized business of diplomacy, where rulers receive representatives and gifts from institutions they may distrust. Paul has to deal with the Guild because interstellar movement continues under his reign. Its representative therefore has a reason to stand before him. Here the carrier's service extends into access to the ruler's household. A man produced elsewhere enters Paul's immediate surroundings through an institution whose ordinary work already connects distant worlds.

There's a useful comparison with the Guild's transport of House forces. A carrier can move soldiers whose orders come from another power. With Hayt, the separation between carriage and purpose becomes harder for the recipient to manage. Paul can identify soldiers as another ruler's troops. Hayt arrives with several purposes attached to him, and his usefulness encourages continued contact. The Tleilaxu conditioning isn't displayed like a military allegiance. Edric's presentation gives Paul a living person whose possible danger must be judged through conversations and conduct.

Hayt also begins to have relationships beyond the one the conspirators intend to exploit. Alia takes an interest in him, and their encounters give him a place in the household that can't be described only through Paul's grief. His observations, discipline and responses matter to the people around him. Herbert lets the ghola occupy that space as a participant in court life. Calling him a weapon describes what others hope to do with him, while leaving much of his actual presence unaccounted for.

That presence is what makes the gift so uncomfortable. Hayt can discuss the purposes behind his arrival, question Paul and respond to others, even though the Tleilaxu have shaped him for an assignment. Paul encounters someone capable of thought inside an arrangement designed to manipulate him. The Guild has completed the delivery, and Edric's diplomatic gesture has placed Hayt within reach. What remains in the palace is a man with Duncan Idaho's face, looking at Paul through Tleilaxu eyes, speaking in a voice Paul has reason to keep listening to.

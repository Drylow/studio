A passenger can cross the distance between worlds without ever meeting the person whose foresight helps the ship arrive safely. In Dune, the Atreides move their household and forces to Arrakis through the Guild's transport system. Leto has a destination, an imperial appointment and people who must travel with him. The departure belongs to the practical business of taking possession of a planet. Somewhere within the organization carrying them, though, navigation depends on a human capacity that Leto's soldiers and servants can't provide.

That separation makes the Guild easy to accept as part of ordinary imperial life. A House can prepare its own vessels and decide whom to send, while relying on the carrier for interstellar passage. The people aboard have their own reasons for going. For the Atreides, leaving Caladan means entering an unfamiliar home and a dangerous political assignment. They don't need to understand a Navigator's existence to experience the consequences of the service. The journey puts them on Arrakis whether they know the method behind it or not.

Herbert gives that hidden work a bodily presence through Edric in Dune Messiah. When the Navigator appears at the conspiracy meeting, he's enclosed in a tank containing orange spice gas. His elongated body, webbed extremities and fishlike appearance place him at a distance from the ordinary human figures around him. The gas is part of the environment in which he lives. A service that can seem remote and administrative to its customers has, here, a representative whose dependence on melange is visible around his body.

Edric isn't introduced while guiding a passenger vessel. He's taking part in a meeting with the Reverend Mother Gaius Helen Mohiam, Princess Irulan and the Tleilaxu Face Dancer Scytale. That's a useful setting for seeing him, because there's no voyage to distract from his presence. The others have to deal with a Navigator as a participant in their plans. His tank accompanies him into political business, and his physical circumstances remain in view while the discussion concerns Paul and the imperial order around him.

For someone like Leto, Guild passage can be one stage in a much larger undertaking. Once his household reaches Arrakis, he has to deal with spice production, the people already living there and the danger posed by the Harkonnens. Edric's reliance on spice doesn't end when a customer's journey ends. The contrast suggests how differently the same transport system occupies the lives of the people using it and the people making it work. Arrival allows the passenger's attention to move elsewhere. The Navigator's requirements continue.

That appearance shouldn't turn every Guild worker into another Edric. The organization has representatives and employees whose roles don't require them to share his form. A customer dealing with the Guild could encounter its rules and decisions without encountering a Navigator's living environment. Edric gives us a specific person through whom to understand one demanding part of the institution. His body helps explain the cost of that work, while the distinction between his role and the wider organization leaves room for the ordinary business necessary to carry imperial traffic.

The Guild's secrecy makes sense in those circumstances. Its customers depend on the result, and the organization guards the means by which it delivers that result. In the first novel, Paul's understanding of its spice requirement gives him leverage that the Guild's clients ordinarily lack. By the time Edric appears in Dune Messiah, that requirement has a visible setting. The orange gas around him makes the connection between Arrakis and navigation immediate. Spice has to reach the organization, and a Navigator has to continue living within the conditions his work requires.

Edric's political role doesn't remove the needs of his altered body. He takes part in imperial politics with his bodily needs present throughout the discussion. His colleagues can consider what he contributes to their plans while looking at the enclosure he occupies. Even here, away from the account of any particular passage, Edric remains suspended inside his tank, surrounded by orange spice gas.

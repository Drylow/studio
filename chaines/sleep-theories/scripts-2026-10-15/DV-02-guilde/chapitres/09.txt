The Harkonnen forces that descend on Arrakis need the same interstellar carrier that brought House Atreides there. The Guild carries a ruling household to its new possession, and later carries the forces that will drive it out. For Duke Leto, successful passage says very little about the safety of his position after arrival. His people can reach Arrakis under the Guild's arrangements while his enemies prepare to use that service themselves. The transport system remains available across a conflict whose purpose is to destroy his House.

Leto doesn't arrive without understanding that Arrakis is dangerous. The transfer takes him away from Caladan and places him in the territory the Harkonnens have exploited and then surrendered under imperial orders. He expects a trap. His preparations involve soldiers, defenses, and the hope of an alliance with the Fremen. None of those preparations gives him an independent way to prevent enemy armies from crossing interstellar space. The Guild has already performed its service for him by delivering his household. That service doesn't include a promise to keep the Harkonnens away.

It's worth keeping the different jobs separate here. Atreides soldiers answer to Atreides officers. Harkonnen soldiers answer to their own commanders. The Guild provides the passage that lets those forces reach another world. A House retains its military identity while relying on an institution outside its chain of command for movement between planets. Leto can decide how to deploy his men once they're on Arrakis. He can't treat the imperial transport network as an extension of his own defenses, even after using it for a relocation ordered by the Emperor.

The attack brings another force into that arrangement. Sardaukar, the Emperor's soldiers, fight alongside the Harkonnens while disguised as Harkonnen troops. Their presence gives the assault a strength beyond the Baron's own military resources. The concealment matters because Shaddam's participation in the destruction of a Great House would have consequences among the other Houses. They have reason to fear an Emperor who helps eliminate one of their peers. The uniforms help hide whose soldiers are fighting, while Guild passage makes it possible for those soldiers to be there at all.

That gives the Guild a real place in the disaster. The attacking force has to arrive before it can overwhelm the Atreides, and interstellar transport is part of how the assault becomes possible. Still, the betrayal has identifiable participants performing different tasks. The Baron pursues the destruction of his enemies. Shaddam supplies Sardaukar. Yueh's betrayal opens the Atreides defenses from within. Calling the Guild the author of this entire arrangement would add an explanation the novel doesn't give. Its logistical involvement is substantial enough without assigning it every decision made by the conspirators.

The word neutrality can become misleading if it suggests that the Guild keeps armies apart. Its business allows hostile powers to travel. A carrier can accept opposing customers and continue to describe its position as neutral, even when the journeys have violent consequences at their destination. In this setting, neutrality doesn't give Leto protection from a Harkonnen landing. It allows the Guild to deal with Houses whose purposes are incompatible. The same institution can complete a move for one customer and enable a military operation against that customer afterward.

There's a practical reason for the Houses to accept such an uncomfortable arrangement. Each House needs passage for its own affairs. If the Atreides demanded that the Guild refuse Harkonnen transport merely because the two Houses were enemies, the Harkonnens could seek the same restriction against the Atreides. A transport service tied to every feud would make passage uncertain throughout the Imperium. That's a political implication of serving rival Houses: each client benefits from access that doesn't depend on having friendly relations with every other client, while also facing the danger of enemies enjoying that access.

The Emperor's later arrival at Arrakis makes the arrangement even clearer. Shaddam comes with military strength to confront the crisis surrounding Paul and the Fremen. Imperial rank doesn't remove the need to move soldiers and equipment between worlds. The Emperor has resources beyond those of an ordinary House, yet his intervention still takes place inside an interstellar system maintained by the Guild. The institution that carried Leto and enabled the Harkonnen assault also provides the means for imperial forces to reach the planet when the conflict has grown beyond the Baron's ability to settle it.

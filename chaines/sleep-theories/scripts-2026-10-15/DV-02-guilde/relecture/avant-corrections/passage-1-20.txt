Hayt's conditioning reaches its crisis when Paul learns that Chani has died. The Tleilaxu have prepared the ghola to turn against him at this moment of grief. But the man they made from Duncan Idaho's body encounters a command he can't carry out without a struggle. Duncan once died defending Paul. Now that same body has been prepared to kill him, and the pressure between those demands brings Duncan's former identity back into reach. The intended attack produces a person the conspirators can no longer use as they intended.

That recovery changes what the Tleilaxu can offer Paul. Until this point, Hayt's existence has left a painful uncertainty around the returned body: how much of Duncan can actually come back? His recovered identity supplies an answer. The Tleilaxu can restore someone Paul knew, including the remembered life that makes recognition possible. For a grieving Paul, that discovery immediately reaches beyond Duncan. Chani has died, and the people responsible for this intervention now have something more persuasive than a promise about their skill.

Scytale uses that opening to offer Chani's return as a ghola. The proposal belongs to the Tleilaxu part of the conspiracy, with its particular knowledge of bodies and conditioning. Edric's participation helped shelter the plot from Paul's prescient observation, but concealment hasn't made these collaborators interchangeable. The Guild Navigator can't supply the restored Chani himself. Scytale needs Paul to accept Tleilaxu terms, and Duncan's recovery gives him a concrete example to place beside the offer. Something that has just escaped its assigned purpose becomes useful to the bargaining.

There's an uncomfortable detail here. Duncan's return doesn't mean the Tleilaxu have suffered a failure in every respect. They wanted to discover whether a ghola could recover his original identity, and the crisis gives them that result. Yet their success leaves Duncan able to act against their immediate demands. He can recognize Paul through their shared past, understand the danger around him and resist further manipulation. The Tleilaxu gain knowledge about their own process while losing the obedience they need from the person standing in Paul's household.

Bijaz gives that danger another route into the room. He has helped carry the conditioning placed within Hayt, and he continues the pressure after Scytale's death by renewing the possibility of bringing Chani back. Paul recognizes how difficult that offer will be to keep refusing. He tells Duncan to kill Bijaz, and Duncan obeys. The recovered man now helps Paul end the approach that depended on his altered existence. Edric's concealment couldn't prevent this exchange between Paul and someone whose allegiance had survived the Tleilaxu intervention.

Paul's conduct also unsettles the arrangement because his enemies can't treat his grief as a dependable instruction. They have good reason to expect Chani's return to matter to him. His attachment to Duncan has already shown how powerfully a restored presence can affect him. Still, wanting Chani back doesn't make Paul accept whatever conditions accompany the offer. He understands that the Tleilaxu could keep using that desire against him. His refusal costs him something the conspirators correctly identified as precious, which is why the refusal carries such practical force.

Scytale's confrontation with Paul makes the limits of the plot more immediate. He threatens Paul's children and tries to compel agreement while Paul is physically blind. Paul has been moving and acting through prescient vision since the stone burner destroyed his eyes. In this encounter, however, his access to the situation changes. He sees through the eyes of his infant son, Leto, and uses that unexpected view to strike Scytale with a knife. The Tleilaxu negotiator dies before he can enforce his terms.

Paul hadn't foreseen the twins' birth as it actually happens. That matters alongside the sudden view through his son's eyes, because the resolution includes a surprise for Paul himself. His enemies are dealing with someone whose foresight has real limits, yet those limits don't leave him helpless in the way they expect. A child whose arrival escaped his anticipated future provides the sight he needs. The outcome depends on an encounter within the family, at close range, far removed from the Guild's familiar work of finding a safe passage between stars.

Edric's contribution remains substantial. At the conspiracy's opening meeting, his presence protects participants from Paul's prescient scrutiny. Irulan, Reverend Mother Mohiam and Scytale can discuss their plans with the Navigator there for a reason. They need shelter from a ruler who might otherwise perceive their activity before they can act. But the shelter leaves each participant with work to do afterward. Irulan must live within the imperial household. Scytale must approach his intended victims. Hayt must respond when the conditioning presses against Duncan's old loyalty.

The comparison with an ordinary secret meeting helps here. Keeping Paul from observing the conspirators through foresight doesn't remove the people who can meet them, hear their proposals or resist their actions. Hayt spends time with Paul and Alia. His behavior gives them opportunities to respond to him as a person, despite the purposes behind his arrival. Once Duncan returns, those encounters have helped place a capable ally near Paul. The plot's protected preparation has delivered someone who can assist its intended victim when the final pressure comes.

Paul eventually walks into the desert after losing the prescient sight that had allowed him to live as a sighted man. He follows the Fremen custom for the blind, leaving the imperial household and his children behind. That departure isn't the submission Scytale tried to purchase through the offer of Chani's restoration. Paul has refused the bargain, Scytale and Bijaz are dead, and Duncan remains himself. Edric helped hide the conspiracy's preparations. He couldn't make Duncan kill Paul, make Paul accept the Tleilaxu offer, or decide what Paul would do when that offer was gone.

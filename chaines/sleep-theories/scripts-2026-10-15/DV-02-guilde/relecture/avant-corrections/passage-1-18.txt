Paul can't inspect the conspiracy's discussion through his visions while Edric shields its participants. That gives the meeting in Dune Messiah an unusual kind of privacy. The people gathered there are planning against an Emperor whose foresight has become part of how he rules. An ordinary locked room wouldn't answer their concern. Paul might learn about an undertaking through the future it produces, without anyone bringing him a report. Edric's presence helps keep their deliberations outside that view, so they can discuss actions Paul would otherwise have an extraordinary means of anticipating.

The arrangement depends on Edric being prescient himself. A Navigator's ability to look ahead, already useful to the Guild in moving ships, has a political use here. Mohiam, Irulan and Scytale can meet under the concealment he provides. They don't each have to possess his abilities to benefit from them. This makes Edric valuable before the group has carried out any proposal. His contribution gives the others room to argue and plan, though they still have to decide what to attempt and persuade one another to take part.

It helps to keep the practical situation in view. Scytale can put an idea before the others without assuming that Paul is following the exchange through a vision. Mohiam can consider what that proposal means for the Bene Gesserit. Irulan can speak as someone who lives within Paul's household. Edric allows this discussion to happen under conditions more favorable to them. He hasn't supplied agreement, and his presence doesn't remove their separate interests. Everything they say still has to pass between people capable of misunderstanding, resisting or using one another.

An ordinary witness would present a different problem. If someone heard the conspirators talking and later told Paul what they'd heard, Edric's prescience wouldn't make the conversation unspoken. The concealment concerns Paul's access through foresight. It doesn't erase testimony or prevent a participant from carrying information elsewhere. Irulan will leave this gathering and continue her life near Paul. What she does there will involve conversations and conduct that other people can encounter. The meeting's unusual protection exists alongside the familiar difficulties of keeping a political undertaking secret.

Paul's position in Dune Messiah makes that distinction especially important. He's an Emperor with a household, officials and a priesthood surrounding his rule. People approach him with information, demands and interpretations. His life doesn't consist of looking into the future in isolation. A plot concealed from his visions still has to operate in the world those people inhabit. The conspirators must eventually do something beyond talking to Edric. As soon as their plans require someone to approach Paul or influence his household, they face his attention in its more ordinary forms as well.

The novel doesn't give an exact mechanism for how Edric's foresight obscures another prescient observer. It gives the conspirators a usable advantage, with Edric's presence central to their concealment. Thinking of that advantage as a blind region is helpful, provided we don't imagine a mapped boundary around the tank. Paul lacks a view the conspirators want to deny him. Inside that uncertainty, they still need workable plans, suitable opportunities and cooperation. None of those arrives automatically with the Navigator, and the gathering spends its time discussing them because they remain unresolved.

There's an earlier encounter that makes Paul's difficulty easier to recognize. Near the end of Dune, Count Fenring stands before him, and Paul recognizes that this dangerous man has been absent from his prescient visions. Fenring's presence matters immediately because the Emperor wants him to kill Paul. Paul has reached a confrontation he'd seen approaching, yet a person capable of changing its outcome has escaped his view. The room contains a danger that his previous visions haven't allowed him to account for in the way he might have expected.

Fenring isn't another Guild Navigator, and his absence from Paul's visions shouldn't be treated as an explanation of Edric's ability. The useful comparison is Paul's experience of meeting someone his foresight hasn't disclosed. Fenring can act, and Paul must confront that possibility in the room. Fenring then refuses the Emperor's demand. That refusal comes from Fenring himself. Even in this earlier encounter, a blind spot leaves Paul facing a person whose response matters, rather than an empty place where the future has stopped happening.

Edric's protection creates a similar practical burden for everyone involved in the conspiracy. Mohiam can't regard Paul's missing view as a substitute for judging Scytale's proposals. Irulan can't assume that an action within the household will work because the meeting that produced it was concealed. Edric himself has to take part in the discussion and defend his position. The Navigator can help them avoid one extraordinary form of discovery, while the undertaking still requires decisions about Paul as a person. They have to consider his attachments, his obligations and the responses they hope to draw from him.

Scytale's attention to Paul's emotional life fits those conditions. The conspirators are dealing with someone who cares about particular people, including Chani and the Duncan Idaho he lost. Their proposals seek ways to reach him through those attachments. Concealment makes it easier to discuss such approaches without warning him through his visions. It can't determine how he'll respond when an approach reaches him. That remains a difficulty the plotters have to work on, using what they know about him and accepting the risk that their understanding may fall short.

For Paul, the missing view also changes the experience of being surrounded by people. Irulan is both his wife in a political marriage and a participant in this hidden discussion. Her place in his household gives her an ordinary reason to be near him. Edric doesn't have to accompany her through every conversation for the conspiracy's concealed planning to matter. Paul must live with people whose intentions he can't settle by appealing to a complete vision of what they've arranged. His foresight leaves him with the continuing work of listening, judging and responding.

The conspirators therefore gain an opportunity they have to use with care. They can exchange proposals under Edric's protection, then carry those proposals into places where other people will react. A private agreement may encounter resistance. A participant may pursue a separate priority. Paul may respond in a way their discussion hasn't prepared them for. Those difficulties are already present around the opening meeting, in the differences between Mohiam, Irulan, Scytale and Edric. The Navigator conceals their deliberations from Paul's prescient sight while they continue the uncertain business of deciding what to do.

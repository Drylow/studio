Edric attends the meeting in a tank filled with the spice-rich gas he needs. Around him, the other conspirators can speak and move without carrying their living environment with them. The opening gathering in Dune Messiah brings together a Guild Navigator, the Bene Gesserit Reverend Mother Gaius Helen Mohiam, Princess Irulan, and the Tleilaxu Face Dancer Scytale. They share an opponent in Paul Atreides, though each has come to the meeting with a different understanding of what needs to be recovered or changed.

The tank makes Edric an unusual presence in a political discussion. His altered body belongs to the same imperial world as Irulan's marriage and Mohiam's breeding program, but his everyday needs are far removed from theirs. He depends on melange at a level that gives Paul's command of Arrakis an immediate personal significance. The Guild's difficulty with the Emperor reaches into the environment surrounding its representative. Edric can discuss action against Paul only because he continues to receive the substance Paul has shown he can endanger.

His presence also makes the meeting possible in a particular way. Edric has prescient abilities, and those abilities conceal the conspiracy's participants from Paul's prescient vision. The conspirators need somewhere to deliberate beyond the reach of the faculty that has helped Paul survive and gain the throne. A closed room alone wouldn't answer that concern. Bringing Edric into their company gives them protection against an observer who might otherwise discover their undertaking through the future. His contribution begins before any proposal wins agreement.

That gives the Guild representative a place at the discussion that goes beyond lending his institution's name. Mohiam and Scytale need what he can do. Irulan, who lives within Paul's imperial household, needs it too. The arrangement is striking because Edric's service to these people resembles his navigational work in one respect: other people's plans depend on an ability they don't possess themselves. Here, however, he isn't helping passengers reach another planet. He's helping a political undertaking develop beyond the Emperor's sight.

Mohiam comes to the meeting with the concerns of the Bene Gesserit. Paul is the product of generations of breeding and preparation, yet he has escaped the role the Sisterhood intended for him. His marriage to Irulan hasn't given them the access to his descendants that they want. Chani remains his chosen partner. For Mohiam, the imperial household therefore contains a continuing problem for the breeding program. Paul's survival, his children, and the Sisterhood's influence over that inheritance can't be separated into tidy, unrelated matters.

Irulan has a more intimate place in the same difficulty. She's the daughter of the displaced Emperor Shaddam and Paul's wife in a political marriage. Her title places her beside the man whose rise ended her father's rule, while Paul's devotion belongs to Chani. The marriage has preserved a connection between the old dynasty and the new throne without giving Irulan the partnership she might have expected. She enters the conspiracy as someone with access to Paul's household and reasons to resent her position within it.

Irulan is also Bene Gesserit trained, so her interests overlap with Mohiam's without becoming identical to them. Mohiam considers a long breeding program; Irulan has to live inside the marriage that program seeks to use. That difference matters when they discuss Paul's family. A proposal about heirs can concern the Sisterhood's future and Irulan's own standing at the same time. The conspirators haven't gathered as interchangeable enemies. Their shared undertaking has to accommodate people who attach different importance to the same possible outcome.

Scytale brings the Tleilaxu into the gathering. As a Face Dancer, he belongs to a tradition of bodily alteration very different from Edric's, and his approach to Paul draws on Tleilaxu capabilities and aims. He takes an active part in shaping the discussion rather than accepting that the others already understand how Paul can be approached. His attention to Paul's attachments gives the conspiracy a direction that military opposition alone couldn't provide. Paul has defeated an Emperor, but he still has people he loves and losses that matter to him.

For the Guild, participation carries a particular risk. Its ships continue to work within the order Paul rules, and its Navigators still require the spice associated with his power. Open hostility would threaten that continuing arrangement. Edric's concealment offers a way to act without beginning with an open break. The appeal is understandable: if the Guild can help alter Paul's position through a hidden undertaking, it may improve its own circumstances while avoiding a direct confrontation over the supply it needs.

That motive is a political reading of the Guild's position, rather than a private explanation Edric gives for every part of his conduct. His participation is concrete enough. A representative of the institution that carries imperial traffic is now cooperating with people who seek to act against the Emperor. The Guild's preference for predictable conditions hasn't prevented this risk. Paul's victory has left it dependent on a ruler capable of threatening its survival, and accepting the present arrangement offers no comfortable escape from that dependence.

Chirrut Imwe walks toward the master switch on Scarif while blaster fire crosses the ground around him. He's blind, he's carrying his staff, and he keeps repeating his expression of faith in the Force. The switch has a practical purpose. Bodhi Rook needs the connection restored so he can contact the Rebel fleet. Chirrut reaches it and activates it, allowing the attempt to transmit the Death Star plans to continue. For those few moments, he gets through a place where the fighting has stopped the others.

It's easy to understand why that walk can look like proof that the Force protects him. Shots pass without stopping his progress, and his composure stands out against the battle around him. But Rogue One never shows an invisible barrier catching the bolts. Chirrut doesn't raise a hand and hold the incoming fire in place. What we can follow is his movement, his repeated prayer, and his arrival at the switch. The exact contribution of the Force to his survival across that distance remains unmeasured.

By then, the film has already given us reasons to take Chirrut's abilities seriously. On Jedha, he fights stormtroopers with his staff despite being unable to see them. His movements are practiced and effective. He also notices Jyn's kyber crystal without relying on ordinary sight. That detail matters because his awareness reaches beyond the skill of striking an opponent who's close enough to hear. Even so, neither encounter gives us a neat measure of his sensitivity, or tells us how much he can deliberately call upon.

Chirrut belongs to the Guardians of the Whills, whose life on Jedha was connected with the Temple of the Kyber. The Empire's removal of kyber from the city gives his devotion a damaged, physical setting. Imperial forces occupy streets near a place he considers sacred, and the material associated with that place is being taken away. His faith continues under those conditions. He doesn't need the temple to remain secure, or the Guardians to retain their former place, before he can speak about the Force and act on that belief.

Baze Malbus has lived through that loss too, and his response gives us another way into Chirrut's behavior. Baze carries a heavy blaster and repeatedly protects his companion with it. He's impatient with Chirrut's religious confidence, yet he stays beside him. Their scenes make room for affection and disagreement at the same time. Chirrut may move forward with complete conviction, while Baze still sees the stormtroopers who need to be stopped. Their survival on Jedha includes Baze's intervention as well as Chirrut's own considerable skill.

That combination helps keep the beach walk in proportion. Chirrut hasn't spent the film treating danger as imaginary. He fights, travels with armed companions, and takes part in a military operation with a specific objective. On Scarif, the damaged communications connection leaves the Rebels with a problem someone has to solve outside cover. His faith appears to let him commit himself to that task without becoming caught in hesitation. That's a reading of his conduct, though. The film doesn't let us separate courage, trained awareness, and guidance from the Force into individual parts.

There's also a larger reason his connection needn't resemble a Jedi's training demonstration. In A New Hope, Obi-Wan describes the Force as arising from living things and binding the galaxy together. That account leaves room for a Guardian on Jedha to have a meaningful relationship with it. Chirrut doesn't have to lift a fighter or carry a lightsaber before his devotion belongs in that world. At the same time, the Force's presence throughout life doesn't tell us that every person can perform the same actions with the same degree of awareness.

The strongest reason to consider some form of guidance on Scarif is the way Chirrut's concentration accompanies his successful passage through the fire. Rogue One gives the moment time and draws attention to his prayer. A purely practical account, based on his skill and the shots happening to miss, doesn't capture all of its religious meaning. Still, that meaning doesn't supply a hidden mechanism. We can understand the walk as an expression of his connection to the Force without claiming that he redirects each bolt or knows exactly where every shot will land.

Once Chirrut activates the switch, an explosion mortally wounds him. Baze reaches him, and Chirrut dies with his friend beside him. This happens within the same action that makes the walk seem protected. There's no long interval in which the film lets us imagine that Chirrut has become untouchable. He completes what he set out to do, and his body remains vulnerable to the battle. Any explanation of his apparent protection has to make room for that immediate outcome, rather than treating his successful crossing as a promise of continued safety.

Baze's response carries the religious meaning further. After Chirrut dies, Baze takes up the words associated with his friend's faith and advances against the Imperial troops. He fights with the weapon he's relied on throughout the film. His renewed expression of belief doesn't remove the consequences of that fighting, and he dies too. The connection between the two men survives their disagreement long enough for Baze to speak in Chirrut's terms. Nothing in the scene requires us to assume that Baze has suddenly acquired a new set of supernatural abilities.

The other deaths on Scarif widen that limit. Bodhi gets his message through and is killed in the shuttle. Jyn and Cassian get the plans transmitted, but they don't escape the Death Star's attack. Chirrut's achievement belongs among these completed tasks, each of which helps someone else carry the mission onward. His faith doesn't secure an exception to the danger shared by the group. It gives his part in the operation a particular character: he steps toward the switch with conviction, reaches it, and leaves Bodhi able to make contact.

So there's a useful answer here even without deciding how every shot missed. Chirrut's practiced skills are real, his devotion is explicit, and his awareness sometimes exceeds what his blindness would ordinarily allow. How those qualities combine on the beach stays open. What doesn't stay open is whether his prayer guarantees survival. The film follows him beyond the successful crossing and shows the cost. The switch is activated, Bodhi can reach the fleet, and Baze is there when Chirrut can go no farther.

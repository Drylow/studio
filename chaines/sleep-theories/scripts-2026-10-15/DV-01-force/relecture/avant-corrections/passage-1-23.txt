Luke walks out of the abandoned Rebel base on Crait and faces the First Order's walkers alone. Inside, Leia and the surviving Resistance have almost nowhere left to go. Their attempt to stop the siege cannon has failed, and the weapon has broken through the base's entrance. Luke's arrival gives Kylo Ren something else to look at. The man Kylo has blamed and pursued is suddenly standing within range of his guns, apparently prepared to face the army that has trapped his sister.

Kylo orders the walkers to fire on him. The barrage covers the place where Luke stands, and Kylo keeps demanding more fire. When the dust clears, Luke is still there. He brushes his shoulder, a small gesture that Kylo reads as defiance. For the Resistance, this is an extraordinary interruption in an otherwise collapsing defense. For Kylo, it's intolerable. He leaves the protection of his forces and goes down to confront Luke himself, bringing the advance to a halt while he settles something personal.

The film eventually shows Luke seated on Ahch-To, far from Crait. His body has never arrived on the battlefield. Through the Force, he has made himself present enough for Leia to speak with him and for Kylo to believe he's facing his former teacher. That explains how the bombardment leaves him standing. The walkers have fired into the appearance Luke is projecting. Their weapons can tear apart the ground, but there's no physical body in front of them for their fire to strike.

Before Luke goes outside, his visit with Leia gives the appearance an intimacy that the confrontation with Kylo lacks. He acknowledges his failure toward her son, and she recognizes how much has been lost. Luke also leaves Han's dice with her. The meeting allows a farewell between brother and sister while the enemy waits beyond the entrance. Leia can receive comfort from him even though he can't carry her out of the base or stand beside the fleeing survivors with his physical body.

There are details that look different once Luke's location becomes clear. He appears younger than the Luke Rey left on Ahch-To, with a changed haircut and beard. He carries the blue Skywalker lightsaber, although that weapon has already broken during Rey and Kylo's struggle aboard the Supremacy. These details make his appearance suitable for the encounter he has chosen. Kylo is confronting a recognizable version of his former master. His anger leaves little room to examine whether everything in front of him fits what has happened elsewhere.

The surface of Crait gives another clue. Kylo's movements disturb the white salt and expose the red ground beneath it. Luke's steps don't leave the same marks. During the confrontation, Luke avoids Kylo's blade without meeting it with his own. These signs belong to the way this particular encounter unfolds. We don't receive a complete guide to what every projection must look like or how every observer might detect one. Here, the clues allow the audience to reconsider a fight that Kylo has mistaken for an opportunity to kill.

Kylo's mistake grows out of the history he brings down onto the salt. Earlier, Luke has described going to Ben's hut, sensing the darkness around him, and briefly igniting his lightsaber before recoiling from what he's done. Ben wakes to the sight of his teacher with a weapon drawn. On Crait, Luke faces the person who has lived with that betrayal and made it part of his anger. Kylo expects another confrontation with the man who failed him, and Luke gives him a figure against whom that anger can spend itself.

Luke doesn't try to win through a faster strike. He keeps Kylo speaking, moving, and concentrating on him. He acknowledges his failure, while rejecting Kylo's assumption that destroying Luke will end the Jedi or the Resistance. Kylo has brought an army to the base, yet his attention has narrowed to the space between himself and his teacher. Luke seems to understand that he'll remain there as long as the confrontation feels unfinished. The delay depends on Kylo's expectations as much as it depends on the extraordinary effort reaching across the distance.

Inside the base, Poe begins to understand what's happening. Luke hasn't come with a weapon capable of defeating the walkers. He's giving the survivors time to find another way out. Poe notices that the crystal foxes have disappeared and follows the route they've taken deeper into the tunnels. The Resistance still has to move, search, and help one another through the escape. Luke's presence creates the opening for those actions. It doesn't perform them on behalf of the people sheltering behind him.

The exit is blocked by rocks, and Rey clears them with the Force. The survivors can then reach the Millennium Falcon. Their escape involves several people doing different things in different places, with Luke unable to see or direct every movement inside the base. Poe has to recognize the opportunity. Rey has to reach the far side. Leia and the others have to leave while Kylo remains occupied. Luke's apparent isolation outside the entrance can obscure how much the rescue depends on everyone else continuing to act.

When Kylo finally drives his blade through Luke, it passes through him. Luke's appearance disappears, and Kylo is left to realize that the man he meant to destroy was never within reach. Back on Ahch-To, Luke comes out of the effort exhausted. He looks toward the setting suns, then his body disappears. The sequence ties his death to the strain of what he's done without providing a measured account of how much such an act demands. His final intervention has been enough to let the Resistance escape Crait.

Kylo enters the base too late to stop them. He finds the projected dice, which vanish as well, leaving him without the people his army had cornered. The Falcon carries the survivors away, and Leia understands that Luke is gone. Their last meeting has given her a farewell, and his time outside the entrance has given the others a route off the planet. On Ahch-To, the place where Luke sat is empty, and his robe is left on the rock.

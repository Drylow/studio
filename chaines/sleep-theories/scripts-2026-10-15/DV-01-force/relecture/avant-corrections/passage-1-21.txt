Hera sees the purrgil near the Ghost as a danger to her ship. Ezra begins to sense something in them that the rest of the crew can't hear. In The Call, the rebels are searching for fuel when they encounter these enormous creatures moving through space. Their presence complicates an already difficult journey. The Ghost needs fuel, and Hera has reason to worry about animals that can cross a ship's path. Ezra's attention gradually shifts toward where the purrgil are going and what they need.

Hera's caution comes from the hazards purrgil pose to travelers. She's familiar with accounts of ships lost in encounters with them, so she doesn't welcome Ezra's interest as an obvious solution. From the pilot's seat, their size and movement are immediate concerns. Ezra is responding to a different kind of information, an impression of living creatures reaching him through the Force. Both reactions fit the situation. Hera has a vulnerable ship to protect, while Ezra has begun to notice behavior that might explain the encounter.

The purrgil lead them toward a Mining Guild operation that processes Clouzon-36 gas. The rebels want fuel from the facility, and the creatures are drawn to the gas themselves. That shared need puts the crew and the purrgil in the same place, though they arrive with very different ways of taking what they need. The Mining Guild treats the animals as pests around its operation. Ezra starts to understand that their presence has a purpose beyond obstructing the Ghost.

That gives the episode a physical basis for Ezra's growing connection. The purrgil feed on the gas. Their journey brings them into conflict with an industrial facility exploiting the same resource. Ezra isn't receiving a detached lesson about respecting life while standing somewhere safe. He's trying to survive an armed encounter, and understanding the animals changes what he can do. Their needs become part of his understanding of the place, alongside the fuel supply and the Mining Guild's weapons.

When Ezra falls into the gas-filled depths beneath the facility, a purrgil saves him. His connection with the creatures develops through an encounter in which he's small and vulnerable beside them. He can't meet their strength with his own, and his lightsaber doesn't supply the answer to his predicament. The creature's intervention lets him survive. For Ezra, the purrgil have now become beings he can engage with directly, and their behavior carries consequences he couldn't have reached by treating them as background hazards.

The purrgil help turn the struggle at the facility, and the crew gains the fuel it came for. Then the animals make the discovery larger than the immediate rescue. They travel into hyperspace under their own power. Hera has heard the old stories linking purrgil to the beginnings of hyperspace travel, and now she sees creatures make that passage without a starship. The biological capacity is real within the story. The episode leaves the technical history of hyperdrive development outside this encounter.

For the Ghost's crew, hyperspace usually means a ship, fuel, and a route that can carry them away from danger. The purrgil make the same kind of departure as living animals. Their need for Clouzon-36 connects that extraordinary ability to feeding. They have bodies with needs, and those needs bring them into contact with settlements and industry. Ezra's discovery gives the space around the rebels another dimension. A route through it can belong to a migrating creature as well as a freighter or an Imperial patrol.

Ezra has already begun learning to reach other living creatures through the Force. The purrgil extend that experience beyond the scale of animals he can approach on solid ground. Kanan can guide his development as a Jedi, but this encounter asks Ezra to attend to beings whose lives continue far outside Jedi teaching. Their feeding and travel don't depend on his arrival. He's learning how to participate in something that's already happening, and the help he receives grows out of that contact.

By the liberation of Lothal, Ezra has found a way to bring that connection into the struggle against Thrawn. His plan includes sending Mart, with help from the others aboard the Ghost, to summon the purrgil. This part of the rescue takes place beyond Thrawn's immediate view. While the Grand Admiral confronts Ezra aboard the Chimaera, the creatures approach the Imperial ships above Lothal. An encounter that once interrupted a fuel run now gives Ezra an opening against the fleet threatening his home.

The purrgil attack the blockade and surround the Chimaera. Their tentacles reach into its bridge and hold Thrawn as Ezra remains with him. Thrawn's advantage has depended on what his ships can threaten, including the people on the planet below. The arrival of creatures capable of taking those ships into hyperspace changes the situation around him. Ezra follows through even though departing with Thrawn means leaving his friends behind. The purrgil carry the Chimaera away, removing that immediate threat from Lothal.

Ezra's success here depends on more than discovering a useful ability in an animal. He first encountered the purrgil while his own crew was short of fuel, listened despite Hera's understandable objections, and survived with their help. Later, he makes their arrival part of a coordinated plan that requires his friends to act beyond his reach. The finale gives this particular connection an extraordinary result. There's no reason in these events to expect any Jedi to summon the same creatures and receive the same response on demand.

The Call also changes how Hera can understand what she once feared. Purrgil remain enormous creatures whose movements can endanger a ship. Her experience now includes their help, their needs, and the sight of them entering hyperspace. Ezra's attention has allowed the crew to encounter more of their lives than a collision warning could tell them. Above Lothal, those lives cross his again. As the Chimaera disappears with Ezra and Thrawn aboard, his friends are left to finish freeing the city below.

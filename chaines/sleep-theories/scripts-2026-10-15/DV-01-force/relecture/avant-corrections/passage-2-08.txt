Luke is balancing upside down on Dagobah when his friends' suffering breaks into his training. He's been learning to reach beyond whatever happens to be directly in front of him, and now that wider perception brings him something he can't set aside. He sees a city in the clouds. Han and Leia are there, in pain. His concentration breaks, and he falls. The exercise has reached him in a way that lifting stones hasn't. People he cares about may need him, somewhere far from Yoda's home.

Yoda recognizes that Luke has perceived the future. That gives Luke a reason to take the vision seriously, but it doesn't answer his most pressing concern. He wants to know whether his friends will die. Yoda concentrates, then admits that he can't see their fate clearly. The future is still moving. So Luke has reached something real through the Force, while his teacher, with far greater experience, still can't turn that glimpse into a definite account of what will happen.

On Bespin, Vader is arranging that situation. Han, Leia, and Chewbacca have reached Cloud City aboard the damaged Millennium Falcon, hoping that Lando Calrissian can help them. But the Empire has arrived before them, and Lando's attempt to protect his city has put his guests in Vader's hands. Vader has Han tortured without even questioning him. That detail makes the suffering especially disturbing: Vader doesn't need information from Han. He's using Luke's attachment to his friends to draw Luke toward Bespin.

Luke has no way to hear the conversations in which Vader changes his arrangement with Lando. He doesn't know that Boba Fett is there to collect Han, or that Vader intends to use the carbon-freezing chamber on him. The vision gives him no briefing on the equipment waiting below the city. Vader, meanwhile, has chosen a trap suited to the person he expects. Luke's willingness to help his friends is familiar enough for Vader to build a plan around it.

Back on Dagobah, Yoda urges Luke to continue his training. Obi-Wan appears and adds his warning. Both understand that Vader wants Luke, and both believe he's unprepared for that encounter. Their concern includes the possibility that he'll be captured or drawn toward the dark side. Luke hears them, but staying carries its own terrible possibility for him. If Han and Leia die while he's still practicing with Yoda, he will have remained away despite having seen their pain. His teachers can't offer certainty that waiting will keep them safe.

This is where Yoda's uncertainty has a practical weight. He can explain why Luke is vulnerable, and he can recognize the danger of abandoning his preparation. He can't provide a safe departure time or tell Luke exactly how the rescue will unfold. Luke is asking for an answer that would settle the decision. What he receives is experienced advice about a situation whose outcome his teachers also can't fully see. He leaves with R2-D2, while Yoda and Obi-Wan remain behind.

By the time Luke reaches Cloud City, Han has been frozen in carbonite and handed over to Boba Fett. Luke sees the others being escorted through the city, and Leia manages to warn him that he's walking into a trap. Her warning is much more direct than the vision. She knows Vader is waiting. But Luke has already traveled to Bespin to help them, and he continues into the confrontation. Vader draws him away from the people he came to rescue and toward the freezing chamber.

The chamber reveals how much preparation Luke's glimpse left out. Vader has already tested the process on Han because he wants Luke alive for delivery to the Emperor. Luke escapes the freezing pit, and the fight moves through the city's machinery and passages. His training helps him survive the first part of the encounter. It hasn't prepared him to defeat Vader. As the duel continues, Luke loses his hand, and Vader tells him that he's his father. None of that was available to Luke when he saw his friends suffering.

Meanwhile, Leia and Chewbacca's circumstances change through Lando's actions. After Vader repeatedly alters their agreement, Lando has his guards turn against the Imperial escort and releases the prisoners. Chewbacca is furious with him, with good reason, but they still have to get away. Their attempt to reach Han comes too late to stop Fett leaving. They escape aboard the Falcon with R2-D2, who gets the ship's hyperdrive working. Luke's arrival doesn't account for these decisions or these acts of help.

Luke ends up needing the rescue himself. After refusing Vader's offer, he falls into the shaft and is carried out beneath Cloud City, where he clings to an antenna. He reaches toward Leia through the Force. She senses him and insists that the Falcon turn back. That's a smaller, more immediate exchange than the earlier vision, and it gives her something she can act on: Luke is nearby and needs help. Lando brings him aboard while Leia waits inside. The friends Luke feared losing are now carrying him away.

The warning on Dagobah was genuine. Han and Leia suffered, and Vader was a danger to them. Luke leaves before completing his training and continues despite warnings that Vader is waiting for him. In the confrontation, he loses his hand and hears a truth about his father that the vision hadn't prepared him for. Aboard the Falcon, Leia tends to him as they flee. He'd crossed the distance to Bespin because he'd seen her in pain. Now she's beside him, helping him endure his own.

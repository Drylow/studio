Kylo Ren reaches toward a connection he can't explain, and afterward there's water on his glove. Rey is on Ahch-To, where the sea belongs to her surroundings. He's somewhere else. Their conversations in The Last Jedi already let them see and hear one another across that distance, but the water adds something physical. A detail from Rey's location has reached him. For two people who begin these encounters expecting an attack, there's now a stranger problem to consider: what, exactly, can pass between them?

Their first exchanges are awkward because neither understands the conditions. Kylo tries to account for what Rey is doing, while Rey responds to the man who killed Han Solo. They can address each other without a transmitter or a shared room. Yet their view isn't an ordinary view across space. Kylo can see Rey without seeing everything around her. The connection brings another person into reach while leaving parts of that person's situation hidden, which matters whenever either starts treating a conversation as a complete understanding.

Rey still has reasons to hate him. She's seen his violence, and she knows what happened to Han. Across their encounters, though, she also hears his account of Luke's confrontation with him at the Jedi temple. Luke later gives Rey a fuller account of his own failure that night. The bond lets Rey hear Ben describe his past, but she still has to compare what he tells her with what Luke says. Being able to speak across the galaxy doesn't settle the disagreement between them.

The encounter in the hut on Ahch-To goes further. Rey and Ben extend their hands and touch. Each experiences something they interpret as a glimpse of the other's future. Rey comes away believing Ben can turn away from the dark side. Ben believes Rey will stand with him. That shared contact gives them confidence in incompatible expectations. They don't leave the moment with an agreed plan. Rey travels to meet him carrying a hope that feels more certain than their actual agreement warrants.

Snoke later claims that he joined their minds. In his account, he used Kylo's conflict to draw Rey into reach, expecting her compassion to bring her before him. He's explaining a trap while Rey is his captive, and the trap has worked well enough to put her in his throne room. His boast deserves to be heard in that setting. He's claiming authorship over the encounters that helped bring her there, with every reason to present himself as the person who understood both of them all along.

Then Ben kills Snoke. He and Rey fight the Praetorian Guards together, but afterward their intentions separate. Rey wants him to help the Resistance. He asks her to join him as he seeks to replace the existing orders. Their ability to fight alongside each other hasn't settled what either will do once the guards are gone. By the end of The Last Jedi, the connection is still present after Snoke's death. Rey sees Ben once more before closing the Falcon's door on the encounter.

The Rise of Skywalker gives their connection a name: a dyad in the Force. Aboard his Star Destroyer, Ben tells Rey that they share this bond. Later, on Exegol, Sidious recognizes it when he draws on them. Snoke's earlier claim doesn't provide a full explanation of what these later scenes reveal. He may have manipulated their contact, as he said, but the films leave the origin of the dyad unexplained. His account of a successful lure can't settle every property of their connection.

By then, their encounters have become physically disruptive. While Rey is inside Kylo's quarters aboard his Star Destroyer and he is on Kijimi, they clash through the bond. Their lightsabers meet even though their bodies occupy different places. During that fight, the display holding Vader's helmet is broken, and the helmet passes into Kylo's surroundings. He recognizes it and realizes where Rey is. An object has crossed between their locations, and its arrival gives him information that their conversation alone hadn't supplied.

On Exegol, Rey uses that possibility deliberately. Ben has arrived without his lightsaber and faces the Knights of Ren, while Rey stands before Sidious holding the Skywalker saber. Through their connection, she passes the weapon to Ben. He draws it from behind his back and can defend himself against the Knights. The exchange is easy to follow because the earlier encounters have given it a physical history. Water crossed first, then an object crossed during a fight. Now Rey sends something Ben needs.

Their understanding has changed along with the exchange. Earlier, Rey went to Ben expecting his future to match the one she believed she'd seen. On Exegol, she responds to what he's doing there: he has come to help her. The saber reaches a person who has already turned away from his former allegiance. Their bond makes the handoff possible, while Ben's decision explains why she can place the weapon in his hands.

Sidious can exploit the same connection, so intimacy doesn't make it safe. Rey and Ben have gained an extraordinary way to reach each other, and another Force user can draw upon what they share.

Anakin dreams of Padme suffering in childbirth, and he wakes with a fear he already knows. In Revenge of the Sith, her pregnancy has given them something to look forward to beyond the war. The dream brings the possibility of losing her into that hope. He sees her in pain, but he doesn't receive an explanation of what brings her there or a set of instructions for keeping her alive. Anakin fills that absence with a decision of his own. He has to find a way to stop her dying.

His earlier dreams about his mother give him a concrete reason to take this seriously. In Attack of the Clones, he dreams that Shmi is suffering, returns to Tatooine, and discovers that Tusken Raiders have taken her. He reaches her before she dies, but too late to bring her home alive. So when he dreams about Padme, he isn't approaching a strange experience without any history. He's remembering a warning that corresponded to real suffering, followed by a rescue he couldn't complete. That makes waiting feel dangerous to him.

Padme knows about the dreams, and Anakin tells her that she dies in childbirth. Their conversation takes place within a marriage they've kept secret. That secrecy narrows the help he can ask for openly, especially among the Jedi. Padme considers whether Obi-Wan could help them, while Anakin resists involving him. Obi-Wan is his closest teacher and friend, yet bringing him fully into this fear would also mean revealing the life Anakin has concealed. The problem already reaches beyond the dream itself. Anakin needs advice while protecting the secret that would explain why he's so frightened.

He does speak to Yoda, though he leaves out Padme's identity and their marriage. Yoda hears that Anakin fears losing someone close to him. He advises Anakin to accept death as part of life and work against the attachment that makes loss unbearable. For Anakin, who's looking for a way to prevent a particular death, this gives him no practical remedy. Yoda's counsel asks him to change his response to loss. Anakin remains focused on changing the outcome, and the conversation ends without giving him the reassurance he wants.

At the opera, Palpatine presents a different kind of answer. He tells Anakin about Darth Plagueis, a Sith who supposedly learned to influence life and keep others from dying. The story meets Anakin's fear with the suggestion that a technique exists somewhere beyond Jedi teaching. Palpatine also places himself in the position of someone who knows about it. Anakin can ask Yoda for help and hear about letting go, or listen to the Chancellor describe a power that sounds fitted to his exact need. Palpatine makes the second path inviting without demonstrating the promised result.

When Palpatine reveals that he's a Sith Lord, Anakin initially reports him to Mace Windu. He hasn't yet accepted the bargain. But when he enters the confrontation and finds Mace ready to kill Palpatine, his fear for Padme becomes part of his decision. He intervenes against Mace, and Palpatine kills the Jedi Master. Anakin has helped destroy the person who came to arrest the Sith because he believes that Sith may be necessary to save his wife. The dream hasn't told him to do any of this. He's acting on Palpatine's claim about what might prevent it.

After Anakin submits to him, Palpatine admits that they still have to discover the secret together. That qualification leaves Anakin without the knowledge he thought he was preserving. Nevertheless, he continues as Darth Vader. He attacks the Jedi Temple and later kills the Separatist leaders on Mustafar under his new master's orders. His commitment now includes acts he can't undo, all while Padme remains unaware of what he's become. The promise of saving her has carried him into a course of action that makes an honest return to her increasingly difficult.

Padme travels to Mustafar after Obi-Wan tells her what Anakin has done. She offers Anakin a way to leave with her, but he speaks about overthrowing Palpatine and ruling together. His proposed solution has grown far beyond keeping her safe through childbirth. When Obi-Wan appears, Anakin assumes Padme has betrayed him and uses the Force to choke her. The person whose death he's been trying to prevent is now being hurt by him. His fear has become entangled with suspicion, and he attacks before allowing her words to alter what he believes.

Obi-Wan takes Padme away from Mustafar, and she gives birth to Luke and Leia on Polis Massa. The medical droid says that her condition can't be explained medically and describes her as having lost the will to live. Padme dies after the births. Elsewhere, Anakin survives his injuries in the suit Palpatine provides. When he asks about her, Palpatine tells him that he killed her in his anger. Anakin knows he attacked her, so the accusation has something terrible to attach itself to, even though Palpatine wasn't present at her death.

The sequence invites a self-fulfilling reading: Anakin's efforts to avoid the dream help bring about the catastrophe surrounding Padme's death. He accepts a bargain, destroys people who might have helped him, and finally assaults Padme herself. Still, the film never shows a version in which he responds differently, so we can't know whether her death was otherwise avoidable. The dream gives him a possible future to fear, without settling how fixed it is. What we can follow is his response, all the way to the harm he causes. Before Padme dies, she tells Obi-Wan that she still believes there's good in Anakin. Obi-Wan is beside her to hear it. Anakin is far away, unable to hear the faith she still has in him.

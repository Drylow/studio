Luke has Vader at his mercy when he stops fighting in Return of the Jedi. He's driven his father back, struck him down, and severed his mechanical hand. The Emperor is pleased. Everything about the encounter has been arranged to make Luke's anger useful to him, and for a moment Luke has fought exactly as Sidious hoped. Then Luke looks at Vader's damaged wrist and at his own artificial hand. The resemblance gives him something immediate to consider while his opponent is still on the floor.

Luke already knows that Vader is his father. What's changed is how close he's come to killing him in anger. Vader's threat against Leia has drawn Luke out of hiding and into an attack he struggles to stop. His concern for his sister is real, and Vader has used it against him. When Luke looks at their hands, the family connection has a physical form. He can see how the fight has brought him closer to the person he came hoping to save.

He switches off his lightsaber and throws it away. That action leaves him exposed in a room with the Emperor and Vader, aboard a battle station his friends are trying to destroy. He hasn't received a guarantee that his father will help him. Sidious remains capable of hurting him, and soon does. Luke's refusal carries a practical cost: he gives up the weapon he's just used to win the duel, while the man encouraging the killing still stands nearby.

The Force has helped Luke reach this encounter, but it doesn't settle the decision for him. He can sense that something of his father remains within Vader. Vader's conduct has also given him reasons for hope, mixed with reasons for fear. On Endor, Vader listens to Luke's appeal and still delivers him to the Emperor. Luke enters the throne room with that contradiction unresolved. His belief in his father matters because he's acting without being able to make his father respond.

When the Emperor attacks him with lightning, Luke suffers and calls for help. Vader watches before turning against his master. Luke's appeal has reached someone who can still act, although Luke couldn't compel that response through affection or certainty. Anakin lifts the Emperor and throws him into the shaft, receiving the lightning himself. The rescue comes through his intervention. Luke's decision to stop fighting has left room for that intervention, with his own life dependent on whether his father takes it.

There's a smaller encounter in The Rise of Skywalker that gives this question a different setting. Beneath the sinking fields of Pasaana, Rey and her companions find a wounded serpent in the tunnels. Its presence threatens their way forward. Rey approaches it and places her hand against its injury. The wound closes, and the creature withdraws. Its movement reveals an opening through which the group can leave. A dangerous animal has been hurt, and Rey has a way to help it.

The healing requires something from her. Rey explains to BB-8 that she's transferred some of her life energy through the Force. She survives the act and continues with the others. Within this encounter, the cost is bearable, and the result is visible on the serpent's body. There's no need for the creature to understand the search that brought them underground. Rey attends to an injury in front of her while her companions still have urgent reasons to keep moving.

Luke and Rey are dealing with very different kinds of danger. Vader has made decisions that harmed other people, and he can understand his son's appeal. The serpent is an injured animal blocking a passage. Helping it doesn't carry the same moral difficulty as appealing to Vader. Still, both encounters turn on a person pausing during a threat long enough to notice something beyond the immediate need to defeat or escape it. Luke notices his resemblance to his father. Rey notices the creature's wound.

The description of the Force as arising from living things gives Rey's encounter a concrete place in this world. The life beneath Pasaana belongs to the same wider reality that Jedi learn to sense. The serpent doesn't need a place in their Order or an understanding of their teachings to be part of it. Rey can meet it through the Force while still facing an animal whose size and injury make the encounter dangerous. Her attention reaches beyond the people traveling with her, and the group benefits when the creature moves aside.

Luke's attention stays with a particular person, despite everything that person has become. After Anakin saves him, Luke tries to bring him away from the battle station. Their final conversation happens with Vader's mask removed, at Anakin's request. The man Luke refused to kill is dying, and Luke can't prevent it. What he can do is remain with him. The encounter ends with a father able to look at his son directly, after years in which the mask stood between him and everyone else.

These moments give an unfinished understanding of the Force somewhere definite to rest. Luke doesn't have to know every possibility in his father's future before he can put down his weapon. Rey doesn't need an account of the serpent's place in the galaxy before she can tend its wound. Each has enough awareness to recognize an opportunity, and each still has to act. On Pasaana, the others can continue their search because Rey has helped the animal in their path. The serpent moves away, and the opening behind it is clear.

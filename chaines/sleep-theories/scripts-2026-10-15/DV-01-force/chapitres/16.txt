Palpatine tells Anakin about Darth Plagueis while they sit together at the opera on Coruscant. The setting gives the conversation an ordinary public cover, even as Palpatine introduces something Anakin desperately wants to hear. He describes a Sith Lord who could influence life through the Force and prevent others from dying. Anakin has been dreaming about Padme's death in childbirth. Palpatine doesn't need to promise him a throne in this conversation. The possibility of keeping Padme alive is enough to draw his attention.

The story has a peculiar shape. According to Palpatine, Plagueis possessed extraordinary knowledge, taught his apprentice what he knew, and was then killed by that apprentice in his sleep. Even within Palpatine's account, possessing this knowledge didn't keep its owner safe. Anakin concentrates on the part about saving someone else. That's understandable given his fear for Padme, but the murder remains part of the offer he's listening to. The tradition Palpatine presents includes a teacher whose pupil uses the opportunity of sleep to kill him.

Palpatine also places the knowledge outside the instruction Anakin receives from the Jedi. That gives Anakin a reason to treat his own teachers as an obstacle to the answer he needs. He can't go to the Temple and ask for an ordinary lesson in the technique Palpatine describes. He's being directed toward a different teacher, under circumstances where fear makes patience difficult. The conversation leaves him with a possibility he can't verify and a man who appears willing to discuss it. For Anakin, that apparent willingness carries enormous weight.

The account of Plagueis reaches us through Palpatine's effort to recruit him, so its claims about creating life and preventing death remain testimony rather than a demonstrated procedure. What follows in Revenge of the Sith makes the uncertainty particularly uncomfortable. After Anakin helps him defeat Mace Windu, Palpatine says that they must work together to discover the secret of overcoming death. Anakin has already committed himself by then. The knowledge that sounded available during their earlier conversation has become something his new master expects them to pursue.

The Rise of Skywalker eventually brings the question of Sidious's own survival into a physical setting. On Exegol, Kylo Ren finds him attached to a large mechanical apparatus. His body is damaged, his eyes are clouded, and the machinery supports him as he moves. Around him are the facilities and followers associated with the Sith Eternal. Whatever has allowed him to return after the destruction of the second Death Star, the condition Kylo encounters requires support. Sidious can speak, threaten, and make plans, but his continued presence hasn't restored him to ordinary bodily health.

Exegol also contains tanks holding bodies resembling Snoke, and Sidious claims responsibility for making him. Those details place manufactured bodies and Sith activity in the same setting. They give his return a material context alongside his command of the dark side. The film doesn't walk through every step by which he reached this condition. It does let us spend time with the result: an emperor whose plans still reach across the galaxy while his damaged body hangs from equipment in a hidden stronghold.

Sidious initially presents Kylo with an offer involving the Sith fleet and the death of Rey. Later, when Rey reaches Exegol, he wants her to strike him down in anger so that his spirit can pass into her. His proposed continuation therefore requires another person's body and a carefully directed act of violence. Rey would have to perform the action he demands under the pressure of watching her friends face destruction. The situation resembles his approach to Anakin in a concrete way. Someone fears losing people they love, and Sidious attaches his instructions to the possibility of saving them.

His plan changes when he discovers the connection between Rey and Ben. He draws power from their dyad, and his body visibly recovers. He no longer needs to proceed with the transfer he had demanded from Rey. This recovery gives us a direct example of his dependence on life outside himself. Two people have come before him, and he drains them to repair his own condition. His renewed strength then allows him to attack the Resistance fleet with lightning. The improvement in his body becomes another means of continuing the battle.

Obi-Wan continues teaching Luke after death without restoring his former physical life. Sidious instead seeks another person's body or takes the vitality he needs from Rey and Ben.

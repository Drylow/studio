When Mark grows close to Helena at the retreat, he believes he's spending time with Helly. That belief matters to everything between them. Their familiarity began on the severed floor, where Helly resisted the job and Mark gradually became someone she could turn to. Helena enters that familiarity without telling him that the person who lived those moments is absent. Mark can agree to spend time with the woman beside him, but he hasn't been given the information he'd need to understand who she's asking him to be close to.

Helena has an unusual advantage before they reach Woe's Hollow. Lumon has recordings of the severed workplace, and she watches footage of Mark and Helly together, including their kiss. She can see an expression of affection that belongs to a relationship she hasn't experienced herself. The recording gives her something to study, though it can't give her Helly's experience of wanting to kiss Mark. For Helena, an intimate moment between two innies is available for viewing outside the life in which it happened.

Mark has no comparable view of Helena's life outside Lumon.

The rest of the team also gives her a place within a history she didn't share. Helly participated in their effort to reach the outside world. She stood with people who were taking enormous risks to discover what Lumon had concealed from them. When Helena rejoins those people under Helly's name, their willingness to include her carries that history forward. She doesn't have to earn her place as a stranger. She can listen to their concerns while they assume she's exposed to the same dangers they are.

Her position outside the severed floor makes that assumption especially costly. Helena is Jame Eagan's daughter and a public participant in Lumon's campaign for severance. The refiners are trying to understand the institution that employs and confines them. Having an Eagan among them could change what they say, what they disclose, and whom they ask for help. By concealing her identity, Helena keeps those decisions from them. Even an ordinary conversation about their situation takes place under conditions they've misunderstood.

At Woe's Hollow, that misunderstanding reaches into Mark's intimate life. He and Helena become physically close while he believes she's Helly.  His agreement rests on recognizing a particular person, someone with whom he already has affection and shared experience. Helena allows him to continue on that basis. Whatever Helena feels during the encounter, she knows Mark has mistaken her for Helly, and that distinction would matter to his decision.

Helly is affected too, though she isn't present to object. When she returns, other people have experiences involving her face and body that she hasn't lived. Mark's recollection of closeness now includes an encounter with Helena. Helly has to learn about that encounter from outside her own recollection. Severance already requires her to accept that her body spends time elsewhere. Here, the overlap reaches into a relationship she's formed for herself, with a person who believed he was continuing that relationship with her.

Helena's interest in Mark may include genuine attraction. Her attention to the recordings and her conduct with him give reason to consider that possibility. But attraction doesn't settle what she owes him. She can want his company and still conceal information essential to his participation. After her exposure, the distress attached to losing that closeness can be real as well. Neither feeling changes the circumstances in which Mark approached her. He was responding to someone he believed had returned his affection as Helly.

Jame's later attention to Helly makes Helena's situation within her own family look painful. He expresses dissatisfaction with his daughter while speaking of a spirit he believes he recognizes in her innie. That leaves Helena facing a father who finds something admirable in the person her family has subordinated. It may help explain why Helly's life could hold an attraction for her, though Helena doesn't give a complete account of her motives. Within that life, Mark's affection is directed toward Helly without depending on the Eagan name.

For Mark and Helly, the consequence continues after the substitution ends. They have to speak about an encounter they don't remember together and decide how to approach each other with that knowledge. Helena could enter their workplace wearing the face Mark loved, and Lumon could keep Helly from returning while she did it. The affection Helena received had grown through Helly's time with him. Mark and Helly are left to work out what they still want to share.

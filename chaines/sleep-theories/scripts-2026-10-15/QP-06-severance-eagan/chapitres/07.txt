Helly records a resignation request because Lumon tells her there's a way to ask for release. She can explain that she doesn't want to remain on the severed floor, send that explanation to her outie and wait for an answer. It's a small opening in a workday whose rules she hasn't agreed to. She doesn't know Helena's name or position in the Eagan family. As far as Helly understands, she's appealing to the person who shares her body and can decide whether it comes back.

That arrangement puts an unusual burden on the request. Helena can leave the building, spend time elsewhere and consider whether to return. Helly's experience runs between arrivals and departures downstairs. When she enters the elevator at the end of a shift, her next conscious experience is another arrival at work. She doesn't experience Helena's evening as rest. A resignation would end the entire routine she knows, so she has a direct reason to ask for one even while her outie keeps choosing to enter Lumon.

Her earlier attempts to leave have already shown how this division works. At the stairwell door, Helly tries to get out and finds herself coming back inside. Crossing the threshold changes which identity is awake. Helena can respond to what happens outside that boundary and send the body back through it. For Helly, the result is bewildering. She can move toward an exit with every intention of leaving, yet the next moment available to her places her back where she started. The door works. Her intention doesn't survive the crossing as an instruction Helena must follow.

Mark tries to explain the arrangement using the language he's learned at Lumon. The outie wants to be there, and the innie has to live with that decision. He's accustomed to treating this as an ordinary condition of employment. Helly has just arrived, so she keeps encountering the strangeness his explanations have made familiar. Mark can tell her what happens at the boundary. He can't give her a route through it that lets her remain awake outside, or persuade the other version of her to stop returning.

Writing a message doesn't solve the problem either. Lumon's code detectors are supposed to prevent information from travelling out through the elevator. Helly tries to get a written appeal past that restriction, including by swallowing a note. The company treats her attempt as a breach of procedure. Her message has a specific purpose: she wants someone beyond the severed floor to know she needs help leaving. Even that appeal must pass through a system designed to keep the two parts of her life from communicating freely.

The recorded resignation offers a permitted channel, but Helena's answer closes it. In her response, Helena rejects Helly's standing as a person and tells her the decision won't change. She also threatens consequences if Helly harms their shared body. It's an unusually direct encounter between them. Helena knows that someone will watch, understand and react to this recording. She addresses Helly as someone capable of making decisions while denying that those decisions should carry weight over whether the work continues.

Helena's warning comes after Helly threatens injury with a paper cutter to force a response. Helly has discovered that an appeal about her own suffering can be refused, so she reaches for something that will matter upstairs. Helena will have to live in the same body. That gives Helly a way to make her distress harder to set aside, although it also puts her at risk. Their exchange shows how little room the approved process leaves for a refusal that Helena doesn't want to accept.

The Eagan connection changes how we understand that refusal. Helena's participation helps Lumon promote severance, and her return keeps that demonstration going. Helly has no access to the family commitments attached to the project. She's being required to supply the working experience for a decision made by someone with a very different place in the company. Helena can regard participation as something she has volunteered to do. The person who experiences the hours downstairs is repeatedly asking for that participation to end.

Helly's crisis later becomes life-threatening, and Mark finds her in the elevator. The aftermath makes the cost of Helena's decision impossible to keep within a resignation form. Helly survives and returns to the severed floor. The body has needed care, but the routine that brought her to that point resumes. For Helena, coming back can continue the undertaking she chose. For Helly, waking downstairs means discovering that even this crisis hasn't secured the release she asked for.

Mark's response matters because he has to meet the person who returns. He knows her as a colleague, and he's seen the danger her distress can reach. Helena can deliver an instruction through a screen and leave managers to enforce it. Mark works beside Helly afterward. Their conversations give her refusal an everyday setting: the office, the task at her desk, the people who notice how she's doing. She's able to form attachments there without accepting that Lumon should keep her there.

Helly's growing commitment to Mark, Irving and Dylan doesn't withdraw her resignation. It gives her people she wants to help as she learns more about their circumstances. Helena's answer treats the shared body as sufficient grounds to settle who may authorize its working hours. Downstairs, that answer has to be carried out by a person with her own account of those hours. Helly has already recorded that account in the form Lumon provided. She asked to leave, Helena heard her, and the next shift still began.

The pages contain Harmony Cobel's own work on severance. In Sweet Vitriol, the research she recovers gives her grievance against Jame Eagan a concrete form. She has drawings and notes connected to the technology for which he receives credit. Until this point, Cobel's standing at Lumon has depended on her position within its management. Here, there's something she can point to that predates the authority she exercised over Mark and his colleagues. Her contribution exists on paper, outside the company's account of who deserves recognition.

That changes how we hear the story of Jame as severance's inventor. Cobel identifies the work as hers and says he took the credit. The recovered material gives substance to that accusation. It connects her to the development of the procedure in a way that her former job title never did. Managing a severed floor and producing original severance research are different accomplishments. Lumon's public version leaves employees and outsiders with the Eagan name attached to both the company and its celebrated technological achievement.

There's a useful distinction within that correction. Cobel's original research doesn't require us to imagine her manufacturing every part of a working severance system alone. A procedure can involve research, engineering, surgical practice, and later development by other people. The wrong in her account concerns the attribution of the original work. Jame receives the inventor's standing while Harmony's contribution disappears behind his name. We can take that seriously without expanding her achievement into an unsupported claim that every device and application came directly from her hands.

Keeping those pages also means that her years of service haven't amounted to a complete surrender of her own account. Harmony continued working for Lumon while evidence of her authorship survived elsewhere. That combination can seem difficult to reconcile, especially given the severity of her devotion. But people don't have to settle every grievance before returning to work. For Cobel, preserving the research may have allowed her to maintain a claim she wasn't ready, or able, to pursue openly. The episode doesn't supply a complete explanation of that private calculation.

Her technical background gives another context for her interest in whether Mark recognizes Ms. Casey. She's observing the limits of a procedure her own research helped develop.

That doesn't turn her treatment of Mark or Gemma into a benevolent project. Cobel allows people to remain inside arrangements that cause them suffering, and she conceals information they urgently need. The authorship revelation gives her another dimension without taking those actions away. A person whose work has been appropriated can still participate in harming others through that work. Harmony's notebooks make her loss of recognition easier to understand, while her behavior on the severed floor remains part of what she's responsible for.

Her dismissal makes the distinction especially uncomfortable. The company removes a manager whose history with its defining technology is far greater than her position suggests. From Lumon's side, she can be handled as an employee who has become troublesome. From Harmony's side, the dismissal comes after an earlier dispossession of credit. Reading those events together suggests why recovering the research has practical weight for her. She has something to bring into a future conversation besides an appeal to how faithfully she served.

Reghabi occupies a different place in this history. Her work with reintegration concerns reconnecting the experiences that severance has divided. Petey undergoes that process, and Mark later turns to her because he wants access across his own partition. Cobel's recovered research concerns the development of severance itself. These contributions shouldn't be folded together simply because both women understand the technology better than most of the people around Mark. Their work addresses different operations, and their involvement reaches him through very different circumstances.

Cobel also brings knowledge of the institution holding Gemma and of what Mark's refinement is doing. That knowledge becomes useful to the rescue effort without making her responsible for Reghabi's reintegration work.

There's also a contrast with the way Lumon preserves the Eagans' achievements. The company provides its leaders with an official history and places for employees to encounter it. Harmony has to recover her own working material from a family home. Her pages haven't received the same institutional care or public standing. That difference makes the question of credit very tangible. Jame's association with the invention circulates through the company that bears his family name. Cobel's contribution depends, for now, on the survival of records she can produce herself.

Recovering the pages doesn't produce an immediate public correction. Jame retains the inventor's credit, while Cobel now has material with which to challenge it.

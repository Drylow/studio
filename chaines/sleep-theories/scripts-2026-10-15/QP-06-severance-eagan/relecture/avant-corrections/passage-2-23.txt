Gemma reaches the exit with a man who looks like her husband, and he stops on the other side of the doorway. She can call to him from the doorway, and he could still follow her. But the Mark standing there is the innie, and the life waiting beyond that boundary belongs to someone he knows about without having lived it. The rescue has brought Gemma within reach of her marriage. It has also brought Mark to a place where continuing forward means surrendering his own present awareness.

That difficulty is already part of the rescue before he reaches the testing floor. Outie Mark wants his wife back. For him, the discovery that Gemma is alive changes the meaning of the grief he's been living with. His innie has a different connection to her. He knew her as Ms. Casey, the wellness counselor who worked within Lumon's rules and then disappeared from the floor. Learning who she is gives him a reason to help, but it doesn't supply the marriage he can't remember.

The two Marks have to communicate through recordings because they can't sit together and discuss the plan. Each can leave a message for the other, then yield the body so the other can respond. There's something very practical about this arrangement. They share the means to carry out the rescue, while neither can personally perform every part of it. A decision made by one becomes a situation the other must wake into. Cooperation requires each Mark to ask something of a person whose whole experience he hasn't shared.

Innie Mark also has reason to be cautious about what happens after Gemma is safe. His outie has a home outside Lumon, a sister, and the possibility of recovering his marriage. The innie's continuing life depends on returning to circumstances the outie wants to escape. If the rescue succeeds and Mark never goes back to work, the innie won't get to enjoy the freedom his actions helped secure. His concern enters the conversation before the final doorway. It isn't something that appears only when he sees Helly again.

Helly matters here because she's part of an actual life Mark has developed on the severed floor. They've worked together, taken risks together, and learned that the company's account of their situation can't be accepted. Their attachment grows within those experiences. Gemma's marriage to outie Mark is equally real, with a history the innie can't retrieve. Calling one relationship authentic doesn't make the other disappear. The same face can be familiar to both women while the person looking back at each has different memories and commitments.

Lumon's building makes those differences unavoidable during the escape. Movement between its spaces can change which identity is present. Mark and Gemma don't pass through every boundary as a steady pair who remember the whole journey. The rescue has to accommodate those interruptions. A person can arrive somewhere with intentions formed elsewhere, then lose access to the experiences that explain them. For the people attempting to leave, knowing the route isn't enough. They also need someone present who understands why they must keep moving.

Inside Cold Harbor, Mark finds Gemma occupied with the crib. He gets her out of the room, and outside it she recognizes her husband. Her response brings their marriage into the rescue in a way no explanation from Cobel could have done. Gemma has been held apart from the life she knew, and now Mark is there. For the Mark who shares that past, reaching her is the recovery he's been pursuing. The testing floor briefly allows them to meet with that recognition available to both.

Their passage back through the building interrupts that reunion. Gemma's appearance as Ms. Casey is especially painful in this context because the escape is underway, yet her awareness of being rescued can fall away. Mark has to continue through a system that can remove their shared understanding while leaving both bodies in motion. Lumon has used that separation to organize her confinement. Getting her physically out requires working through the same boundaries that have kept her from carrying her whole situation with her.

Helly's presence gives him somewhere else to go. When Mark turns back toward her, he makes a decision through the relationships available to him. That decision leaves Gemma calling after a husband whose body is still there, which makes the moment painful without requiring either woman to become an obstacle in someone else's romance. Gemma has every reason to want him to follow. Helly has reason to matter to him. Mark's divided experience has brought these reasonable desires into conflict at a doorway none of them designed.

For the Eagans' company, this is a problem its procedures have helped create. Lumon assigns innies duties on the assumption that their time can be directed toward purposes chosen outside their awareness. Mark can complete work, follow instructions, and help deliver an outcome his outie desperately wants. Yet he can also consider what that outcome will mean for his own continued existence. At the exit, his concern changes what he does with the body. The company's separation has produced someone whose interests can interrupt the intended result.

The refusal doesn't give Mark a secure future. He remains inside Lumon, and Gemma's escape hasn't restored the marriage in the form she hoped for. But she's beyond the threshold, alive, while he turns toward Helly. The people whose experiences Lumon has divided are now acting on those experiences in ways its arrangements can't comfortably contain. Mark helped bring Gemma to the door. When the moment came to follow her, the Mark who was present chose to run back down the corridor.

Paul drinks the Water of Life without knowing whether he'll survive it. Among the Fremen, this substance belongs to the making of a Reverend Mother. It's poisonous before a trained woman changes it within her body, and Jessica has already endured that ordeal. Paul now attempts something the Bene Gesserit regard as fatal for a man. His mother's training has given him unusual command over his body, but there's no teacher beside him who can assure him that his preparation will be enough.

The Water of Life comes from a young sandworm drowned in water. For the Fremen, the substance connects their religious life to the creatures that shape their desert home. Jessica's own transformation allowed her to take up the accumulated inner lives of the Reverend Mothers before her. It also affected the unborn Alia, who shared her mother's ordeal. Paul knows that this ceremony can change a person's awareness beyond anything ordinary instruction could accomplish. He chooses to expose himself to that danger while his future remains difficult for him to read.

His visions have already carried him beyond the immediate surroundings of a sietch. He's seen possible consequences of the movement gathering around Muad'Dib, including the violence that might spread under his name. Yet those visions haven't given him a dependable route through every danger. Possibilities intersect, and some moments remain obscure. Drinking the Water of Life is an attempt to reach further into that uncertainty. It also means risking the life on which Jessica, Chani, and his followers have begun to depend. Paul makes that decision himself, without arranging for them to share in it beforehand.

Afterward, he lies unresponsive for weeks. The outward result gives Jessica no reassurance that her son has achieved what he intended. Her own Bene Gesserit abilities don't let her bring him back into ordinary contact. This is a difficult position for the woman who trained him to notice and govern processes most people never consciously feel. She can recognize that something extraordinary is happening and still be unable to reach him. The young man who has become so capable among the Fremen now needs other people to attend to a body that appears unable to answer them.

Chani is summoned from the south because Paul hasn't recovered. Her arrival brings someone who knows him through a different part of his life. Jessica has raised and instructed him; Chani has become his partner among the Fremen. Chani also knows the religious practices surrounding the Water of Life. She's Liet-Kynes's daughter and has served as an assistant to the Fremen Reverend Mother. When she considers Paul's condition, she has both her closeness to him and that practical religious knowledge to draw upon.

Chani recognizes that the Water of Life is involved and uses a little of it at his lips to help call him back. Paul responds, and the long period of helpless waiting ends. The encounter gives Chani an active place in a transformation that can otherwise sound like an achievement Paul completes entirely within himself. He undertook the ordeal alone, but his return to the people around him depends on her intervention. For Jessica, too, Chani's success matters immediately. Someone she called for help has reached her son when she couldn't.

Paul's account of the experience makes the interval stranger still. His inward experience hasn't matched the weeks that have passed around his unresponsive body. Jessica and Chani have had to live through that absence in ordinary time, with no certainty that he'd return. Paul comes back with an enlarged awareness, while they have endured the possibility of losing him. That difference belongs within the achievement. Whatever he has gained, the risk has already been shared by people who weren't present when he decided to drink.

Jessica's encounter with the awakened Paul also brings her face to face with the result of the Bene Gesserit breeding program. The sisterhood sought a man who could enter regions of inner awareness closed to its Reverend Mothers. Paul can now reach beyond the limit Jessica knows from her own experience. Their earlier relationship can't remain quite as it was. She still has the knowledge of a Reverend Mother and the authority of his mother, but she can no longer assume that she understands the extent of his abilities better than he does.

The sisterhood had planned to reach its intended result through arranged bloodlines and carefully managed marriages. Jessica disrupted that schedule when she bore Leto a son. Paul's survival on Arrakis has carried him further outside their arrangements: he has a Fremen name, a Fremen partner, and followers whose expectations the sisterhood can't direct through a marriage instruction. His transformation answers something the Bene Gesserit have sought for generations. It arrives in a person whose commitments have developed beyond their supervision, and whose immediate purposes belong to the struggle on Arrakis.

There are limits even within Paul's expanded sight. His awareness reaches toward the Guild and the dependence of its navigators on spice, and he perceives possibilities with a clarity he didn't possess before. Still, the futures he describes include obscurity and danger. Prescience doesn't become a complete record of everything that will happen. Paul must interpret what he perceives and decide how to act. Seeing a terrible consequence ahead can affect that decision without removing his ability to make it, or his responsibility for what he does.

Jessica's earlier ordeal offers a useful comparison. She entered a Fremen office with a place in the community and predecessors whose experience became available to her. Paul awakens into a position for which neither the Fremen nor the Bene Gesserit have an ordinary role ready. Chani has helped restore him to their company, and Jessica can recognize the reach of his transformation. Neither can make his new awareness easy to live with. When Paul resumes speaking to them, he brings possibilities into their conversation that they can't examine as he does.

The Water of Life leaves Paul better equipped to pursue the struggle that has occupied his life since his father's fall. It also makes it harder for him to approach that struggle as a young duke seeking the recovery of his House. He now perceives consequences extending beyond an Atreides victory, with people who haven't met him drawn into the futures before him. Chani has brought him back to the room and to the people waiting there. He returns able to see much further beyond them, while still having to decide what he'll ask them to do.

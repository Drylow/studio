Chani has appeared in Paul's dreams before he meets her among the Fremen. When she becomes part of his waking life, she's a person with responsibilities and a history of her own. She's the daughter of Liet-Kynes, the planetologist whose work connects the desert people with the possibility of changing Arrakis. She belongs to the community that has taken Paul and Jessica in, and she knows how to live in conditions that would have killed them without help. Paul's earlier glimpses of her don't give him that experience.

Chani is also a Sayyadina, with a place in Fremen religious life. Jessica's arrival and eventual position within the community don't turn Chani into someone without duties or standing. Paul is getting to know a woman whose life already includes obligations to other people. In the sietch, religious practice, survival and family life occupy the same shared world. Being close to Chani brings him further into that world, where the people sheltering him have work to do and expectations that weren't created for the surviving Atreides heir.

During Paul's years among the Fremen, Chani becomes his beloved and the mother of his child. Their partnership develops as his importance within the community grows.  There isn't a separate stretch of time set aside for him to finish becoming a leader before he begins a family.

Their first son is named Leto, after Paul's father. This is the infant born to Paul and Chani during the original Dune novel. He's a different child from the later twin Leto II, who will be born in Dune Messiah. Keeping them distinct gives this first family its proper place in Paul's life. Before the imperial household and its later struggle over succession, Paul and Chani already have a son together among the Fremen. His father's name has entered another generation, under circumstances Duke Leto never lived to see.

Giving the child that name carries Paul's earlier family into his new one. Duke Leto died after the move to Arrakis, leaving Paul with an inheritance he couldn't take up through any ordinary succession. With Chani, Paul now has a child who belongs to both his Atreides line and her Fremen community. That connection has political implications, though the infant's place in the story also gives Paul something immediate to care about. He has become a father while still living with the consequences of losing his own.

There's an earlier household that helps explain the shape of this relationship. Jessica was Duke Leto's concubine and Paul's mother. The duke's unmarried status had political value, since the possibility of a marriage could matter in dealings between Houses. Paul grew up within that arrangement. He therefore knows that a devoted partnership and the formal calculations of a ruling family can exist together, with unequal costs for the people involved. His relationship with Chani develops against that familiar background, even before his own position makes marriage an imperial matter.

Chani's attachment to Paul also has consequences within Fremen society. As his reputation grows, men seek to challenge him. Chani takes on would-be challengers herself, reasoning that someone who can't defeat her has no business demanding a fight with Muad'Dib. Her action protects Paul's time and discourages claims upon him. It also shows how closely their partnership is becoming tied to his public standing. Even an encounter involving the woman he loves can turn into a judgment about who deserves access to the leader he's becoming.

Paul hasn't withdrawn from the struggle against the Harkonnens to raise this family. His place among the Fremen gives him the means to pursue the enemies who destroyed his father's household. He contributes the fighting knowledge he brought from that household, while Fremen skill and organization make the desert campaign possible. Chani belongs to the people carrying that struggle with him. Their common life therefore includes a cause that neither of them can put aside merely by returning to the sietch. The dangers outside remain part of what their family must live with.

For Paul, there are also the futures he's already begun to fear. He has seen the possibility of religious war spreading under his banner, and his growing reputation brings that possibility into the same life in which he loves Chani. Prescience doesn't prevent him from forming the attachment or fathering their child. He continues to act, and those actions create obligations to particular people. Chani and Leto give those obligations names and faces, beyond the larger responsibility he has taken on among the Fremen.

It's tempting to imagine that loving Chani gives Paul a straightforward alternative to everything gathering around Muad'Dib. The novel makes that separation difficult. He meets her because he's been driven into the desert, and their relationship grows within the community whose support allows him to survive and pursue his family's cause. A private future with her would still have to exist on an Arrakis ruled by his enemies. The safety of his family is entangled with the struggle that's making him more prominent.

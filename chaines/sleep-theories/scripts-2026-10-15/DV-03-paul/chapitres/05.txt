A spice harvester can't escape a sandworm by driving faster. The machine works on the open desert, gathering the substance that makes Arrakis valuable, and it needs a carryall to lift it away when a worm approaches. That arrangement is part of what Duke Leto and Paul learn during their inspection with Gurney Halleck and the planet's Imperial ecologist, Liet-Kynes. From above, the operation can look like a piece of industrial equipment doing its work. Its safety depends on another machine arriving when it's needed.

Kynes accompanies them as someone whose knowledge the new rulers need. He understands the desert in ways that an imperial appointment can't give Leto, and his position requires him to deal with the incoming household. His loyalties, though, don't begin with House Atreides. Paul and his father are being assessed while they inspect their inheritance. Their questions, their equipment and their conduct all give Kynes reasons to judge whether these offworld nobles have any idea what living on Arrakis requires.

Even before the rescue, Paul gives Kynes something to notice. He's fitted his stillsuit with an instinctive understanding that surprises the ecologist. The suit preserves the body's moisture, and wearing it properly matters out in the desert.  Kynes connects what he sees with Fremen religious expectations. Paul doesn't have to make a grand claim about himself for another person to begin reading significance into an ordinary act of preparation.

When a worm approaches, the expected evacuation breaks down. The carryall that should remove the harvester isn't available. Whatever arrangements were supposed to keep the crew safe have failed at the point when they matter. Leto has aircraft nearby, but he hasn't brought them out as a planned replacement for the missing carrier. He's on an inspection with his son and members of his party. The emergency makes him use what's present, while the worm continues toward the harvesting operation.

Leto orders the workers taken off. The aircraft must make room for them, and the harvester will be left behind. He doesn't delay the rescue to preserve the equipment or the spice it holds. That decision has a cost for a household already under pressure to maintain production. The machine belongs to the economic system he's now responsible for, and replacing lost equipment on Arrakis isn't a casual matter. Still, he puts the available transport to work carrying people away from it.

Paul is there while his father makes that decision. He sees a duke dealing with workers whose names and histories he hasn't had time to learn. Their claim on Leto's attention comes with their danger and with his responsibility for the operation. There's a useful difference here from the personal loyalty Paul already knows at home. Gurney and Duncan have close attachments to the Atreides family. The harvester crew can't be assumed to have those attachments. Leto acts on their behalf before receiving that kind of devotion from them.

For Kynes, the rescue changes the assessment of the man. Leto's concern for the crew reaches him more effectively than an announcement of benevolent intentions could have done. The ecologist has reasons to distrust an incoming ruler whose authority comes from outside Arrakis. Now he's watched that ruler sacrifice valuable property when the alternative would endanger workers. Kynes finds himself drawn toward Leto despite his reservations. The encounter gives a concrete reason why the duke can inspire allegiance among people who didn't arrive already committed to his house.

The rescue can earn Leto political support without having been undertaken merely to recruit followers.

Paul also encounters spice here as part of the actual work of Arrakis. Away from discussions of imperial wealth, it reaches him through the harvesting operation and the spice-laden air. His unusual awareness is developing in a place where the substance is woven into daily life. The encounter keeps that development close to physical circumstances: a working machine, desert exposure, workers needing transport. Paul's education on this flight includes both the resource everyone values and the people whose labor makes it available. Those experiences arrive together.

The missing carryall also leaves Leto with a problem that courage can't settle. He can order a rescue once he knows the crew is stranded. He can't make the original safety arrangements reliable merely by holding the title of duke. The Atreides have taken charge of an operation they haven't yet had time to examine fully, on a planet where failures can become fatal very quickly. Saving these workers answers the immediate danger. It also shows how much Leto must learn about the systems now operating under his authority.

The worm takes the abandoned harvester, and the rescued crew survives to leave the sand. Paul has watched his father accept a material loss to protect workers who had little prior reason to trust their new duke.

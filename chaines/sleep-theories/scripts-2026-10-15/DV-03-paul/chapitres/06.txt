When Leto leaves the dinner in Arrakeen on security business, Paul has to carry on receiving people whose livelihoods his family has begun to disturb. Jessica remains with him, so he isn't alone with the responsibility. Still, his father's departure changes what his words can cost. A remark from the Duke's son can now sound like a statement of Atreides policy. The guests include people who profit from scarce water, an Imperial planetologist with influence beyond his official position, a smuggler, and a banker whose sympathies lie with the Harkonnens.

The dinner brings together interests that the transfer of Arrakis hasn't swept away. The Atreides have taken possession of the residence and assumed responsibility for spice production, but the people who understand local trade are still there. They've lived under the previous rulers. They have arrangements to protect and reasons to measure the newcomers carefully. Leto can invite them into his household without making them loyal to it. For Paul, continuing the evening means speaking to people who are listening for advantages, weaknesses, and signs of how his family intends to govern.

Water gives those calculations something very concrete to gather around. On Arrakis, a change in the availability of water could alter the standing of people who earn their wealth by supplying it. The water interests at dinner hear discussion of a greener planet with a financial concern that Paul doesn't share. An abundance that sounds generous to a newcomer could threaten an established business. There's an awkwardness built into this hospitality: the Atreides want a healthier world, while some of their guests have prospered under the conditions the family would like to improve.

The household's treatment of water has already made its own statement. Leto rejects the custom in which guests wet their hands and throw down towels, leaving the squeezed-out water to be sold to beggars. He orders that water be given directly to those outside instead. It's a small administrative decision beside the management of spice, yet everyone familiar with the practice can understand it. The Duke is changing an arrangement that turns the waste of the comfortable into another person's livelihood. His guests have been given a practical example of what his disapproval looks like.

Paul's education helps him participate in this room. He can follow a challenge beneath polite conversation, and he doesn't become helpless when an older guest tries to unsettle him. But his confidence sometimes runs into boasting. Jessica notices both his ability and the risk in the way he uses it. He's quick enough to answer, and young enough to enjoy answering well. At a family meal, that might only invite a correction. Here, a successful reply can embarrass someone who has money, connections, and reasons to make trouble after the evening ends.

Jessica's position makes that especially delicate. She can be proud of the son she's trained while hearing consequences he hasn't fully weighed. She has to consider how a remark will travel beyond the table and whether a guest is being drawn out or driven into hostility. Her attention gives the dinner a second rhythm alongside Paul's more direct exchanges. He meets the challenge in front of him. She also considers what his response may require of the household afterward. Their shared training doesn't make their judgments identical.

Kynes complicates the gathering because his official title doesn't account for the response he gets. He's the Imperial planetologist, and discussion of Arrakis's environment belongs naturally within his work. Yet the banker fears him in a way that gives Jessica something else to consider. A technical adviser shouldn't necessarily command that sort of apprehension from a man accustomed to dealing with wealth. Kynes's presence suggests that local standing can grow through channels the new ruling family hasn't yet understood. Paul is dining with someone whose importance reaches beyond the office through which the Atreides first encounter him.

For the water interests, Kynes is also an uncomfortable person to have in a conversation about changing the planet. He understands Arrakis as an environment, with processes that extend beyond the sale of a precious resource. Paul can speak about the possibility of change, but Kynes brings knowledge of what life on this world actually involves. The difference matters. A political heir can express an ambition at dinner and move on to another subject. People whose work concerns water, or whose hopes depend on it, have to live with the practical meaning of that ambition.

Esmar Tuek brings another kind of local experience into the Atreides household. As a smuggler, he belongs to activity that continues alongside the official administration of Arrakis. His attendance puts Paul in conversation with someone the family can't understand merely by consulting the formal ranks of the Imperium. Tuek has interests of his own, and dealing with him requires a degree of flexibility. The Atreides need to learn who can be approached and where cooperation may be possible. Receiving him at dinner makes that learning a social task, conducted in front of other interested people.

The banker presents a different difficulty. His Harkonnen allegiance hasn't disappeared because the Duke now governs Arrakis. Paul encounters hostility inside the forms of acceptable company, where he can't treat every provocation as an open declaration of enmity. The banker's fear of Kynes makes the situation more revealing. He's willing to test the newcomers, yet he has to take care around another guest. To Jessica, that unevenness is useful. It shows her something about the local balance among people who would otherwise arrive under respectable titles and offer carefully managed accounts of themselves.

Compared with Paul's weapons training, dinner gives him a much less definite sense of when an exchange is finished. A practice opponent can acknowledge a successful move. A guest can remain courteous after being offended, or concede a point while keeping his allegiance unchanged. Paul's verbal ability is real, and the pleasure he takes in using it is understandable. The danger is that a good performance can feel more decisive than it is. He can hold his place in the conversation while the banker's commitments and the water merchants' concerns remain much as they were.

Then a coded message from Leto interrupts the evening with information involving Harkonnen lasguns. Jessica has to receive it while the household is still entertaining its guests. The security business that took the Duke away continues alongside Paul's attempt to manage the dinner. Lasguns are a particularly grave concern in a society that uses defensive shields, because the interaction between a beam and a shield can produce a catastrophic explosion. Paul is being given room to act as his father's heir, while his father is occupied with a danger that courteous conversation can't settle.

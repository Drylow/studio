The Emperor comes to Arrakis with Sardaukar, his court, and the expectation that imperial force can settle the crisis. For Paul, that arrival brings the man who helped destroy House Atreides within reach. Shaddam had allowed his soldiers to fight beside the Harkonnens against Duke Leto. Now Paul has Fremen fighters who know the desert, and the struggle has drawn its most powerful participants onto the planet whose spice they all need. His opportunity to recover his family's position arrives alongside a much more personal loss.

An imperial raid strikes the southern Fremen settlement where Paul's young son has been living. The child, named Leto after Paul's father, is killed. He's the first son of Paul and Chani, separate from the later child who will bear the same name. Paul receives the news while preparing the final confrontation. The military success he's approaching can't give this child any share in the future he's trying to secure. Chani, who has fought beside him and helped him survive his transformation, has also lost their son.

The raid reaches into a part of Fremen life that the Emperor's campaign treats as vulnerable. Yet the Sardaukar encounter fierce resistance even there. The report of their losses unsettles Shaddam because it challenges his understanding of the people he's fighting. His elite troops have helped sustain the imperial throne through their reputation for superiority. On Arrakis, the Fremen have repeatedly demonstrated that they can meet them in combat. The Emperor has brought an army whose standing elsewhere doesn't prepare him for the society Paul has joined.

Paul's sister Alia is captured during that raid and brought before the Emperor. In the original novel, she's already born, a young child with the awareness she acquired when Jessica underwent the Reverend Mother's ordeal while pregnant. She can speak and act for herself. Her presence disturbs the adults around her because her understanding doesn't fit her age. Mohiam recognizes something in her that the Bene Gesserit regard with horror, while the Emperor faces a prisoner who won't behave like a frightened child awaiting instructions.

Alia's capture also brings her close to Baron Harkonnen. During the collapse of the imperial position, she kills him with a gom jabbar. Paul doesn't personally confront and kill the Baron in this novel. His sister ends the life of the man who arranged their father's destruction, and she does so inside the gathering of imperial power. The family connection makes the encounter stranger still: the Baron is Jessica's father, and therefore Alia's grandfather. The Atreides victory includes an act of vengeance carried out by a child born after the House had fallen.

Outside, Paul's assault depends on his understanding of Arrakis. A great storm gives the Fremen an opening. He uses the Atreides family atomics against the Shield Wall, the natural barrier protecting the imperial position, and Fremen riders bring sandworms into the attack. The breach allows the desert to enter a place its defenders expected to keep sheltered. These are abilities and resources Paul has acquired through different parts of his life. His father's inheritance supplies the weapons, while the Fremen supply the knowledge and fighting strength that make their use decisive.

The atomics carry a political danger of their own. The Great Convention forbids their use against human targets, and Paul maintains that he has used them against a natural feature. Even during an assault, he has to consider how the other powers will describe what he's done. An accusation under the Convention could provide grounds for retaliation against his House. The young heir trained to understand imperial rules is still present in the Fremen commander. He's prepared to press those rules as far as he can while arguing that he remains within them.

Once the imperial defenses fail, Paul still has to deal with forces beyond the battlefield. Ships belonging to the Great Houses are above Arrakis, and the Guild's decisions determine whether those forces can descend. Defeating the Emperor's troops on the ground hasn't removed the possibility of another attack. Paul therefore turns to the substance that brought all these powers to Arrakis. He threatens the continued existence of the spice itself. His terms reach people who might otherwise regard a change of ruler as a dispute they could prolong or exploit.

Paul's threat rests on his knowledge of the spice cycle and the possibility of using the Water of Life to destroy it. He presents the Guild with a danger its representatives can recognize through their own limited prescience. They depend on spice for navigation, and they accept that he's prepared to carry out the threat. The ships above Arrakis consequently can't be treated as an army ready to rescue Shaddam. Paul has made intervention dangerous to the very institution needed to transport and sustain an imperial civilization spread across worlds.

There's an uncomfortable connection here with Duke Leto's rescue of the harvester crew. Leto abandoned valuable equipment to save the workers aboard it, and Paul saw what that decision meant to people accustomed to being expendable. Now Paul is willing to threaten the resource on which whole populations depend. The circumstances differ, and he uses the threat to force a surrender. Even so, the scale of what he's prepared to risk has grown enormously. The son who learned how a duke earns loyalty has become a commander able to impose terms through a danger reaching far beyond his enemies.

Feyd-Rautha then offers Paul a more familiar kind of confrontation. The Harkonnen heir challenges him, and their fight puts Paul's trained body back at the center of events. Jessica recognizes that Bene Gesserit preparation could give Paul a hidden advantage, but he refuses that route and faces Feyd himself. Feyd uses concealed treachery during the fight; Paul survives and kills him. Gurney, who has his own reasons to hate the Harkonnens, must leave this encounter to the young man he helped train.

By the time Paul can dictate the settlement, Shaddam's soldiers have failed, the Baron is dead, and Feyd can no longer carry the Harkonnen claim forward. Paul has survived the confrontation that might have killed him within reach of victory. With the Emperor before him, Paul begins arranging the succession.

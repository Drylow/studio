Jamis challenges the arrangement that allows Paul and Jessica to remain with Stilgar's people. Jessica has overcome him during their encounter, and he won't accept the outcome under the terms Stilgar has made. The dispute leads to a formal challenge, with Paul required to fight as his mother's champion. Among these Fremen, a challenge can place a newcomer in a fight to the death. Jessica's skill has helped secure their protection, but she can't settle this encounter for her son. Paul has to enter it himself.

For Paul, the fight brings together preparations that have never quite belonged to the same world. He's learned weapons from men who served House Atreides, and Jessica has trained his body and attention with Bene Gesserit methods. Jamis has grown up with the demands of Arrakis. He knows the weapon and the customs governing its use. Paul has advantages Jamis doesn't expect, but Jamis understands what everyone around them considers an ordinary consequence of the challenge.

Chani gives Paul practical advice about Jamis before they fight. She knows the man he's facing and helps Paul understand the danger. Her assistance is a small, direct intervention in an encounter Paul must finish on his own.  Jamis wants the challenge answered. Stilgar maintains the rules. Chani helps the stranger survive them. Paul depends on people whose responses to him are already different.

Once the duel begins, Paul's training creates an unexpected difficulty. He's accustomed to fighting against a shield, where a fast attack is stopped and a slower blade can pass through. Jamis has no shield. Paul's practiced timing includes a restraint that doesn't belong in this fight, so his attacks don't immediately produce the result his speed seems to promise. To the people watching, his behavior can look deliberate. Jessica recognizes the problem because she knows the training behind it. The others don't have that explanation.

Paul also tries to give Jamis a way to yield. He's used to combat in which a beaten opponent might surrender, and he hasn't yet understood the finality of this particular challenge. The Fremen make clear that the fight must end in death. His hesitation therefore carries a meaning he doesn't intend: it can appear that he's prolonging Jamis's defeat. For a boy raised among instructors who could stop a lesson, the absence of a stopping point is part of the shock.

Paul kills Jamis. His education has enabled him to survive a man who could have killed him, and this is his first actual killing. Jessica understands the danger of what that success might do to him. Afterward, she confronts him with the fact that he's taken a life, refusing to let the victory pass as an occasion for pride. Her severity belongs to her concern for Paul as well as to her training. She has prepared him to be capable of this, and now she has to face what that preparation has made possible.

Jamis remains present in the community after the duel through the obligations surrounding his death. The Fremen recover the water from his body, returning what can sustain the living to the group. For Paul, raised on a wet planet, this treatment of the dead requires a considerable adjustment. On Arrakis, allowing that water to be lost would carry its own moral weight. The people handling Jamis's death live with shortages Paul has only begun to understand, and their customs answer those conditions.

The funeral gives Paul another kind of introduction to the man he's killed. Members of the group speak about Jamis and acknowledge what he contributed to their lives. The opponent Paul met through hostility has a place among people who knew him before this encounter. Paul has to listen to that larger account. There's no need for anyone to excuse the challenge for the loss to matter. Jamis can have endangered Paul and still leave friends who grieve for him.

When Paul speaks in turn, he recognizes Jamis as someone who has taught him about Fremen life. The relationship is brief and terrible, but it has changed what Paul understands about the people he's asking to shelter him. He weeps, and the Fremen receive his tears as an extraordinary gift of moisture to the dead. A bodily response familiar to Paul becomes, in their eyes, an act of generosity. Even his grief needs interpretation across the distance between Caladan and Arrakis.

Jamis's death also brings Paul into contact with Harah and her sons. Under Fremen custom, his victory gives him responsibilities toward the household Jamis leaves behind. Harah explains the position she occupies and the arrangements available to him. Paul doesn't take her as his wife. Even so, he can't treat the duel as a closed event between two fighters. There are living people whose circumstances have changed because he won, and his new place among the Fremen includes obligations toward them.

In his father's household, Paul was the heir whom Gurney and Duncan trained to protect an existing family and its people. Here, his survival has placed him inside another household's loss. He needs Harah's explanations just as he needed guidance during the challenge and the funeral. His abilities have carried him through the fight, but they haven't supplied an understanding of everything that follows it. As he begins living among the Fremen, Jamis's family is part of the life he must learn to meet.

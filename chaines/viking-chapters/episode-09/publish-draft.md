## YouTube description

A bearskin on your shoulders, a roar that empties a beach, and a place in the bow of the king's ship where no blade can touch you: that was the legend. In reality, the front of the ship was the most exposed place on board, the berserk fury left men weaker than before, the later sagas turned berserkers into the villains of every feast, and the law finally made going berserk a crime. Follow Ulf, Hallvard's imagined younger son, from the family farm to a jarl's bench in the tenth century: the wolf-skins of Harald Fairhair's poets, Odin's shield-biting warriors, Kveldulf the evening wolf, the mushroom and henbane theories, the duels that made berserks hated, two Swedish brothers given away like property and the lava road in Iceland that still bears their name, and the laws that outlawed them and even the men who failed to restrain them. Then we look at the ivory chess pieces from the Isle of Lewis that show warriors biting their shields. This original illustrated scenario is built from skaldic poetry, sagas, Snorri Sturluson's writings, Icelandic law, archaeology and modern scholarship; Ulf is fictional.

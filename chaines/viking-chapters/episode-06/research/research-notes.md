## How to read these notes

Each fact is tagged with the kind of evidence behind it, so the script never presents one kind as another:

- **[SETTLEMENT BOOK]**: *Landnámabók* (the Book of Settlements, versions from the 1200s to the 1300s) and *Íslendingabók* (Ari the Wise, about 1122–1132), written centuries after the settlement. The script says "Iceland's Book of Settlements", "According to the book", "Iceland's own medieval historians".
- **[SAGA]**: Icelandic sagas written in the 1200s (*Eyrbyggja saga*, *Njáls saga*). The script says "a saga" or "saga tradition".
- **[ARCH]**: archaeology, tephrochronology, ice cores, DNA.
- **[MUSEUM]**: a museum or heritage body's summary.
- **[SCHOLARSHIP]**: a historian's book or article, a university summary, an encyclopedia or a reference site with named sources.
- **[ESTIMATE]**: a modern estimate (woodland cover). The script says "estimates".

Numbers in the script are written as words. Each was checked against at least two sources. Where a detail rests on one source, the script names that source in the sentence ("One version of the Book of Settlements", "The historian Jesse Byock", "The Icelandic Forest Service estimates") and the general claim behind it has a second, independent source (see each entry), or the detail was cut (see "Cut or softened").

## What is fiction

Grim (the neighbor of episodes 1 to 4, named here for the first time), Halla, their two grown sons, the sale of the farm, the ship's cargo and voyage, the first sight of Iceland, the farm bought in a northern valley and its price, the twisted rafter called the Snake, the old man and his memories, the haystack counting, the stream dispute with the new neighbor, the leaking booth, the old woman at the harbour, and Grim's old age are invented. P03 says so ("you are an imagined person"). The story starts "around the year nine hundred and fifty" (P08), the same period as episodes 1 to 5, and ends with a flash-forward "thirty years later" to about 985 (P63). Every historical example is labelled with its date or source (Ingólf about 874, the wall before about 871, settlement within about sixty years, Eldgjá 939–940, the Althing 930, Christianity around 1000, Greenland about 985).

Endless free land at the edge of the world, and not a single neighbor: that was the dream. In reality, you cross the ocean in an open cargo ship with your cattle, arrive generations after the best land was claimed, build a house out of grass around twisted driftwood, count your haystacks all winter, and find a new neighbor with a new boundary dispute. Follow Grim, Eirik's quarrelsome neighbor from earlier chapters, as he moves his family to Iceland around 950: the settlers' ships and animals, Flóki's ravens and dead livestock, the land-claim rules of the Book of Settlements, the vanished birch woods, turf houses, the Eldgjá eruption that turned the sun red, the Althing and its lawspeaker, and the settlers who came as slaves. Then we meet the red-haired man who gave Greenland its name. This original illustrated scenario is built from museum research, archaeology, volcanology, the Book of Settlements, sagas and Icelandic law; Grim and his family are fictional.

## How to read these notes

Each fact is tagged with the kind of evidence behind it, so the script never presents one kind as another:

- **[PRIMARY]**: a contemporary or near-contemporary written source in translation (Anglo-Saxon Chronicle, Annals of Ulster, Annals of Saint-Bertin, Alcuin, runestone inscriptions).
- **[ARCH]**: archaeology or scientific analysis.
- **[MUSEUM]**: a museum or heritage body's summary of the evidence.
- **[LATER LAW]**: Norwegian law written down in the 1100s to 1200s, after the Viking Age. The script always labels it as later.
- **[EXPERIMENT]**: modern reconstruction voyages (the Sea Stallion). The script always labels them as modern.
- **[SAGA]**: Icelandic or Norse saga literature, written down centuries later. The script labels it as such.
- **[INTERPRETATION]**: a modern scholar's reading. The script says "suggested" or "a reasonable guess".

The script's numbers are written as words. Each number was checked against at least two sources. Where only one source exists, the script either names that source in the sentence or leaves the number out (see "Cut or softened").

## What is fiction

Eirik, Bjorn, Aslaug, the neighbour, the dead young oarsman, the raided settlement and its church, the silver dish and fittings, the two captives, their sale, Eirik's shield-rim wound, his share and the "exchange rate" scene are all invented. P03 says so in the script ("an imagined person"), and P37 repeats it ("Eirik is imagined"). Eirik's voyage is set a few years after episode 1, which was around 950. The script does not name a year for it, and it dates every historical example (793 to the early 1000s) so the viewer can tell those examples apart from the story.

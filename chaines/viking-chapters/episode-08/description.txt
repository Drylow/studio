A seat at the king's table, a gold ring from his own arm, and your verses remembered for a thousand years: that was the dream. In reality, Viking court poets worked in one of the hardest verse forms ever invented, performed for armed men who knew exactly what really happened, could lose everything for one mocking stanza, and sometimes had to compose all night to save their own heads. Follow Brand, Bjorn's imagined nephew, from the family hearth to a jarl's hall in the tenth century: the rules of court metre, kennings and the mead of poetry, rings and rivals, the poet who lost his gold ring for mocking a king, Egil's Head-Ransom, Iceland's laws against insulting verse, a war of lampoons against a Danish king, and a poet turned away from every door on the road. Then we read the only complete skaldic stanza ever found on a runestone. This original illustrated scenario is built from skaldic poetry, sagas, Snorri Sturluson's writings, Icelandic law and modern scholarship; Brand is fictional.

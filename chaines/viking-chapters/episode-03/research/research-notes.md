## How to read these notes

Each fact is tagged with the kind of evidence behind it, so the script never presents one kind as another:

- **[PRIMARY]**: a contemporary or near-contemporary text in translation (runic inscriptions, the Arabic report of al-Turtushi).
- **[POEM]**: Eddic poetry (*Rígsþula*). Date of composition debated. The script calls it "old poetry" or "a poem about society, not a record of real people".
- **[SAGA]**: Icelandic saga literature, written down in the 1200s about earlier events. The script always says "a saga tells" or "in the saga".
- **[LATER LAW]**: law codes written down after the Viking Age (Icelandic *Grágás*, oldest parts 1117; Norwegian provincial laws, 1100s to 1200s), usually reached here through scholarship. The script says "written down later" or "in the law".
- **[ARCH]**: archaeology, osteology or scientific analysis.
- **[MUSEUM]**: a museum or heritage body's summary of the evidence.
- **[SCHOLARSHIP]**: a peer-reviewed paper, university course or academic book chapter summarising the evidence.
- **[EXPERIMENT]**: modern reconstruction work. The script labels it as an estimate.
- **[ESTIMATE]**: a demographic or labour estimate. The script says "estimate" and gives the range.

Numbers in the script are written as words. Each was checked against at least two sources. Where a detail rests on one source, the script names that source in the sentence and the general claim behind it has a second, independent source (see each entry), or the detail was cut (see "Cut or softened").

## What is fiction

Aslaug, Eirik, Bjorn, the neighbour and his boundary stone, the merchant's visit, the "richer farm down the valley" and its enslaved workers, Aslaug's pregnancy, the neighbour woman who helps at the birth, the daughter, the silver from the two captives (fiction from episode 2, built on episode 2's F20) and every scene of Aslaug's year are invented. P03 says so ("you are an imagined person"). The story runs from early summer (Eirik leaves, as in episode 2) to the end of summer (his return with the cut arm and the small pouch, matching episode 2), autumn, winter, late spring (the birth) and early summer (the merchant), then "next spring". Every historical example is labelled with its place, and with its date when it falls outside Aslaug's lifetime (Oseberg 834, L'Anse aux Meadows "some decades after your time", Dynna "about a century after your time", Birka excavated 1878). The sagas' Gudrun and Gudrid are always introduced with "a saga tells" or "in the saga".

### F04: Keys: long read as the housewife's badge, but found in a small minority of women's graves, and in men's graves too [MUSEUM + ARCH + SCHOLARSHIP]
- **Used in:** P10 to P12.
- **Sources:**

  2. J. K.   3. ### F18: In medieval Iceland, the law fixed a standard woollen cloth, vaðmál, used as money for taxes and fines; women wove it but had no political say [ARCH + LATER LAW + SCHOLARSHIP]

## How to read these notes

Each fact is tagged with the kind of evidence behind it, so the script never presents one kind as another:

- **[PRIMARY]**: a contemporary or near-contemporary text in translation (Irish annals, the *Life of Ansgar*, runic inscriptions).
- **[POEM]**: Eddic poetry (*Rígsþula*). Date of composition debated. The script calls it "the poem Rígsþula, which imagines how the classes of society began".
- **[SAGA]**: Icelandic saga and settlement literature written down in the 1100s to 1200s about earlier events (*Heimskringla*, *Landnámabók*). The script says "a saga written in the twelve hundreds" or "Iceland's Book of Settlements, written down centuries later".
- **[LATER LAW]**: provincial laws written down after the Viking Age, reached here through scholarship.
- **[ARCH]**: archaeology, osteology or scientific analysis.
- **[MUSEUM]**: a museum or heritage body's summary of the evidence.
- **[SCHOLARSHIP]**: an encyclopedia article by a named historian, a peer-reviewed paper, a university project or an academic book summary.

Numbers in the script are written as words. Each was checked against at least two sources. Where a detail rests on one source, the script names that source in the sentence and the general claim behind it has a second, independent source (see each entry), or the detail was cut (see "Cut or softened").

P03 says so ("you are an imagined person"). Colm is one of the "two villagers" taken in episode 2 (P03, P05, P09 continue episode 2's raid: the two captives near the mast, Eirik's cut forearm, the sale "at a trading place on the way home"). P35 to P43 continue episodes 1 to 3 (the escaped sheep, Eirik at his fence, Aslaug expecting the child born in episode 3; Hallvard's farm is the "richer farm down the valley" of episode 3). The story runs from late summer (the raid) through autumn, winter, early spring (the funeral, the ploughing) and the next summer. Every historical example is labelled with its place and date (Dublin 871, Birka in the 800s, Flakstad, Erling Skjalgsson "a couple of generations after your time", Hørning, Hjörleif in the 870s, Sweden 1335). The ploughing scene (P50) is fiction modelled on the *Landnámabók* episode (F17), which the script then names.

## Facts used

### F01: Enslaved people had no personal rights; in the oldest Nordic provincial laws a thrall was movable property with no legal standing; owners could punish severely [MUSEUM + SCHOLARSHIP + LATER LAW]
- **Used in:** P18 to P20.
- **Sources:**
  1. National Museum of Denmark, *Slaves and thralls in the Viking Age*: https://en.natmus.dk/historical-knowledge/denmark/prehistoric-period-until-1050-ad/the-viking-age/power-and-aristocracy/slaves-and-thralls/ ("Slaves were people without any personal rights"; owners could punish them severely).
  2. Per G. Norseng (professor emeritus of history, University of South-Eastern Norway), *Trell*, Store norske leksikon: https://snl.no/trell ("I de eldste nordiske landskapslovene oppfattes trellen som løsøre uten rettsstatus": in the oldest Nordic provincial laws the thrall is treated as movable property without legal status; a thrall who betrayed his lord faced death).
- **Caveat:** "You cannot take a case to the assembly" (P20) is the script's plain-language reading of "no legal standing". The museum also mentions sexual exploitation; it is deliberately not narrated (brief: nothing sexual).

### F02: Thrall status could be inherited [SCHOLARSHIP]
- **Used in:** P21 ("historians note").
- **Sources:**
  1. Norseng, *Trell*, SNL (F01): "Om de fikk barn, kunne trellestatusen gå i arv" (if they had children, thrall status could be inherited).
  2. Wikipedia, *Thrall*: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrall ("a status that could be inherited from thrall parents", citing Lindkvist and Ågren, *Sveriges medeltid*, p. 40).

### F03: Other roads into thralldom: debt and poverty, crime [MUSEUM + SCHOLARSHIP]
- **Used in:** P22, P23 (Ketil), P52 ("debt thralldom could be temporary").
- **Sources:**
  1. Swedish History Museum, *Free and unfree in the Viking Age*: https://historiska.se/en/explore-history/history-hub/free-and-unfree-in-the-viking-age/ ("Even a free person could voluntarily enter temporary thrallship to another due to poverty or to settle a debt").
  2. Norseng, *Trell*, SNL (F01): free Norwegians could become "gjeldsslaver" (debt slaves) under the oldest laws if they could not repay debts.
  3. National Museum of Denmark (F01): offences such as murder and theft could be punished with slavery.
  4. *Medieval Histories*, summary of Stefan Brink, *Thraldom: A History of Slavery in the Viking Age* (Oxford University Press, 2021): https://www.medieval.eu/a-history-of-slavery-in-the-viking-age/ ("One could be sentenced temporarily to enslavement for some offense but later purchase his manumission"; "It was possible to hand oneself over as a slave to someone else in exchange for protection and food").
- **Caveat:** Ketil's belief that Hallvard "must let him go" once the debt is paid is his own expectation (P52), not a quoted legal rule.

### F04: The word "thrall" comes from Old Norse; "in thrall" means held or absorbed [SCHOLARSHIP]
- **Used in:** P06, P07.
- **Sources:**
  1. Merriam-Webster, *thrall*: https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/thrall ("Middle English thral, from Old English thræl, from Old Norse thræll"; noun sense "a state of complete absorption", usually with "in").
  2. Wikipedia, *Thrall* (F02): the English word was borrowed from Norse; the native Old English word was *þēow*; the verb "enthrall" is an Early Modern formation from it.
- **Caveat:** "English borrowed it" follows both sources; the script does not date the borrowing.

### F17: *Heimskringla*: Erling Skjalgsson's thirty slaves, their evening work, land of their own, freedom within one to three years, and more slaves bought with the money; reliability doubted [SAGA + SCHOLARSHIP]

- **Sources:**

  2. Keep the accents in the captions. If the voice stumbles, give the TTS a spelling without accents (Rigsthula, Horning, Hjorleif, Ingolf, Toki) but keep the accented form in the SRT.
- **New cast (describe in every prompt; never draw them as Eirik, Bjorn, the Neighbor or the Merchant):**
  - **COLM** (hero, all chapters): young adult man about twenty, cream round face, black dot eyes, SHORT DARK-BROWN almost black hair, NO beard, rough UNDYED grey-brown knee-length wool tunic with a plain rope belt, bare shins, worn dark-brown shoes, black line forearms and mitten hands. No cloak in summer; a ragged grey blanket over his shoulders in winter. Never in chains, collar or ropes on screen except P01, where his wrists are shown only as resting in his lap (no visible restraint needed).
  - **HALLVARD** (owner): big older man, cream round face, full BLACK beard and black hair, DARK BLUE cloak with a BROWN FUR COLLAR, one round SILVER brooch, mustard-yellow tunic, brown trousers and shoes. Rich, never armed.
  - **KETIL** (older thrall): Norwegian man about fifty, cream round face, GREY STUBBLE and short grey hair, slightly bent, limps (a stick in some shots), faded brown tunic covered in visible PATCHES of different colours, rope belt.
  - **HALLVARD'S SON**: young man, black hair, short black beard, mustard tunic like his father, no cloak. Only in P50.
  - **THE DEALER** (P14): thin man in a long green coat with a fur hat, surrounded by furs, salt sacks and iron bars. Not the episode 1 Merchant.
- **Returning cast:** Bjorn at the steering oar and Eirik at the oars (P01, P05), seen from Colm's place by the mast. Eirik at his fence with his forearm scar, Aslaug in her mauve dress, visibly expecting (P37 to P39). The escaped sheep in P35 to P38 and P71.
- **No violence on screen:** P01 shows Colm sitting by the mast looking back at the coast; no ropes needed. P45 to P48: maps of Lofoten, a museum drawer with labelled bones covered by cloth, researchers with notebooks; no skulls, no bodies, no beheading. P62 to P65: the abandoned plough, the ox in the grass, the boat, the islands; no killing.
- **Graves:** P44 and P49 show the mound being built and the father's sword and cloak laid out; the body is never shown.
- **Thralls at work (P28 to P31, P50):** dignified, tired adults at work; dung basket, turf spade, fence stakes, firewood. The plough harness is a wooden yoke over the shoulders, shown from the side, not cruel.
- **No horned helmets, no fantasy armour.** The Annals of Ulster, the *Life of Ansgar* and *Landnámabók* appear as parchment pages, not as portraits of real people. Erling Skjalgsson and Tóki are unnamed extras seen from a distance, never drawn as the named cast.

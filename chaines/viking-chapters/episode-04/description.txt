The songs remember the raiders. Nobody wrote songs for the people they carried off. In reality, you are taken from your village, weighed against silver at a trading place, and sold to a farm where the law treats you as property: fences, dung, turf, firewood, and a plough with no ox. Follow Colm, an imagined captive from Bjorn's raid, through his first year as a thrall in a valley in western Norway: the slave markets of Dublin, Hedeby and Birka, the law, the insults of Norse poetry, the heaviest work, the double graves of Flakstad, and the ways out, from buying freedom to the freed smith of the Hørning runestone. Then we follow the runaway thralls who gave Iceland's Westman Islands their name. This original illustrated scenario is built from museum research, archaeology, Norse laws, sagas, Irish annals and runic inscriptions; Colm and his household are fictional.

Silk, silver and the golden city of Constantinople: that was the dream. In reality, you drag boats between rivers, wade through rapids with names like Do not sleep, carry a dugout on your shoulders for six miles while nomad riders watch from the steppe, and queue at a single gate with fifty unarmed men. Follow Asmund, Aslaug's imagined younger brother, on a trading voyage of the tenth century from Birka to Kiev and down the Dnieper to Constantinople: the fur trade and the people sold beside it, the rapids described by Emperor Constantine VII, the Rus seen through the eyes of Ibn Fadlan, the treaty rules of the Great City, and the silver weighed back home. Then we read a painted runestone on Gotland raised for a man who, it seems, never came back from the rapid called Aifur. This original illustrated scenario is built from museum research, archaeology, Byzantine and Arabic texts, the Primary Chronicle and runic inscriptions; Asmund and his master are fictional.

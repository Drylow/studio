## How to read these notes

Each fact is tagged with the kind of evidence behind it, so the script never presents one kind as another:

- **[PRIMARY]**: a contemporary or near-contemporary text in translation (Constantine VII's *De Administrando Imperio*, Ibn Rustah, Ibn Fadlan, runic inscriptions, Photius via a historian).
- **[CHRONICLE]**: the Rus *Primary Chronicle*, compiled later in Kiev, which preserves the texts of earlier treaties. The script says "A chronicle kept later in Kiev".
- **[ARCH]**: archaeology, numismatics or scientific analysis.
- **[MUSEUM]**: a museum or heritage body's summary of the evidence.
- **[SCHOLARSHIP]**: a peer-reviewed paper, university project, scholarly reference work or encyclopedia summary.

Numbers in the script are written as words. Each was checked against at least two sources. Where a detail rests on one primary text, the script names that text or its author in the sentence ("Constantine says", "Ibn Fadlan says", "Ibn Rustah adds") and the general claim behind it has a second, independent source (see each entry), or the detail was cut (see "Cut or softened").

## What is fiction

Asmund (Aslaug's younger brother, introduced here), his master, the master's house and boats, the cargo of furs, honey and wax, the languages, the dugout bought at Kiev, the chickens (one spared by lot and named), the riders on the ridge who do not attack, the queue at the gate, the green glass beads, the profit, the silver bag, the return "by the second autumn" and the visit to the farm are invented. P03 says so ("you are an imagined person"). The voyage follows the order and season given by Constantine VII (Kiev in June, the rapids, Saint Gregory, the Black Sea) and the treaty rules of the *Primary Chronicle*. Ibn Fadlan's scenes (P39 to P47) are explicitly placed on "the other great river road, the Volga", and in 921, before Asmund's time. Every historical example is labelled with its date or "the late nine hundreds" (Pilgårds), or "Nobody knows exactly when" (Halfdan).

You have a shopping bag full of food when the woman on the other side of the glass falls down.

You put the bag down to help her. By the time you get her through the staff door, the food is gone. Someone has dragged it into the crowd gathering around the escalator. The woman holds up a security pass. She's worked here for years. You ask whether there's another way to the food court. She looks at the people pressing against the glass and says there used to be.

You're Patch. You came to this mall for a phone charger. Your phone is now dead, your charger is still in the shop, and the woman you've rescued wants you to move away from the door.

The security guard introduces herself as Nia and leads you into a shuttered shoe store. A delivery worker named Otis is sitting on a bench. He offers you a chocolate bar, then realizes he's already eaten it. He keeps the wrapper in his hand while you explain what happened outside.

Through the shoe shop's display window, you can see the food court across the atrium. A cleaner's cart stands beside the sandwich counter. Bags and jackets cover the empty tables. The people moving between them have stopped checking the exits. One presses his green hands against a drinks machine, over and over, waiting for something to fall out.

Nia lowers the shop's front shutter. The rear staff door opens into a service corridor, and that corridor should lead behind the food court. You ask why she didn't mention it earlier. She points down at her swollen ankle. She can manage a slow walk along the corridor. If anything follows, you will have to stay with her.

On the first night, you share the biscuits from Otis's delivery bag. He keeps checking his watch as if someone might complain that their package is late. You sleep on a bench with your orange backpack underneath it. Nia sits beside the rear door, listening. Nobody gets much sleep, but by morning you still have both ways out.

The service corridor smells of cardboard and spilled coffee. Nia shows you the doors as you pass: loading yard, staff kitchen, food court. At the far end, a fallen shelving unit blocks the passage. Behind it, someone drags a foot across the floor. Nia takes your arm before you step closer. You retreat with a packet of crackers from an abandoned staff locker. It is enough to get you through the morning.

On day two, Otis remembers a stockroom above the sandwich counter. His trolley was supposed to go there before everything happened. There is a stairwell behind the shoe shop, and the upstairs walkway reaches the same end of the building. You leave Nia with the crackers and take Otis to look.

Upstairs, you find a kiosk that has already been stripped. Its open fridge is warm. You leave the food inside alone. At the far end of the walkway, sealed drinks sit on a stockroom shelf behind a wired window. Otis recognizes his delivery labels. Between you and that door, an infected guard stands beside the railing. His keys still hang from his belt. Otis quietly puts his trolley down. For now, you go back.

Nia has been counting the unopened bottles in the shoe store's staff cupboard. She makes a mark beside each bottle as you move it onto a shelf. You tell her about the guard, expecting a plan. Instead, she asks whether you saw the lower staircase. It comes up behind him. She used to take it when customers stopped her to ask where the toilets were.

On day three, you and Otis use that staircase. The guard has wandered toward the kiosk. You reach the stockroom door and find it unlocked. Inside are sealed water cartons, snack boxes and somebody's abandoned lunch. You take what you can carry. Otis starts loading his trolley, but its loose wheel rattles against the door frame. The guard turns before you've finished.

You pull Otis back into the stairwell. The trolley hits the railing, spills its load and stays there. Downstairs, Nia gets the rear door open before you reach it. You return with your backpack and the carton Otis held against his chest. He sits on the floor, shaking. You nearly tell him the wheel was his fault. Then he opens the carton and hands you a bottle.

The guard now waits near the stockroom. From the upstairs landing, you can see cartons scattered across the walkway. Food is close enough that you can read the pictures on the packets. Reaching it will mean passing the place where you left the trolley. You decide to use what you have before risking another trip.

By day five, the shoe store has a sleeping corner, a food shelf and a bucket nobody wants beside their bed. Nia's ankle is improving. Otis has stopped talking about getting his trolley back. When you check the front shutter, a green hand pushes through the gap at the floor. You move the sleeping bench farther into the shop. The hand stays there until evening.

On day seven, you hear somebody banging from the pharmacy across the service corridor. The shop was closed when the outbreak started. A woman has been sheltering inside. She passes sealed bottles across the pharmacy threshold before she asks for anything. Her name is Mara. She knows a way to the loading yard, and she would very much like someone else to look at it first.

Mara takes you past the staff kitchen to a gray exit door. Outside is a fenced loading yard. Beyond the fence, a delivery road curves toward a bus shelter. From here, the shelter looks empty. Then a person walks out from behind a van and presses both green hands against the fence. Others come around the van, and you close the door before they reach the fence. Mara had been watching that same shelter for days, hoping the road would clear.

Back in the shoe store, Otis pulls a folding map from a customer-service drawer. You spread it on the bench. The public entrance faces the car park. The loading yard faces the delivery road. Nia points to a third route through the old department store. Its rear doors open onto a pedestrian path beside the building. She hasn't checked that end of the mall since the evacuation.

On day nine, you reach the department store through the upstairs walkway. With its shutters down, you have to peer through a narrow gap beside a display. From there, you can see the pedestrian exit. A fallen clothing rail blocks it from inside. The store looks quiet, but Nia can't see the floor behind the counters. Otis suggests trying the goods door downstairs, where his deliveries used to arrive.

That door opens from the blocked service corridor. You return to the fallen shelving unit and listen. The dragging sound has stopped. Through a gap, you spot a pale green hand beside a storeroom door. Mara pulls you away before you call out. You mark the blocked stretch on the map and leave it alone. Otis folds the map carefully. Everyone has been pointing at the same exit for days, and you still haven't reached it.

On day twelve, rain starts coming through the shoe shop's ceiling. You move the food shelf. Then you move it again. A dark patch spreads over the stock cupboard, and water begins dripping onto the sleeping bench. You carry the dry supplies into the staff kitchen. The room has a small window looking into the loading yard. Otis checks it before he sits down. He doesn't want another night without knowing what's outside.

During the move, Mara finds Nia's old radio in a desk drawer. Nia had left it there when its battery ran down. A charging cradle is still connected to the desk. For part of the afternoon, the emergency supply comes on. The radio crackles while you are stacking biscuits. A voice says a collection point is operating beyond the pedestrian path. Nia reaches for it, but the announcement finishes before she can reply.

You spend the next morning at the window with the radio. The message repeats. There is a staffed gate beyond the old department store, and anyone arriving is supposed to wait where the path meets the road. Nobody promises to enter the mall. Otis asks how far away that is. Nia points through the wall behind you. From where you are sitting, the route should take minutes. You've already spent two weeks trying to reach its first door.

On day sixteen, you return to the upper stockroom. You need enough supplies to move everyone together. The guard has wandered away, but Otis's trolley still blocks part of the walkway. This time, you leave it where it is. Mara watches the staircase while you and Otis carry sealed cartons back in small loads. The final trip is almost easy. Then you notice the guard standing in the stairwell below you.

You stop with the carton in your arms. Otis is behind you, and Mara is already downstairs. She sees the guard before you can warn her. She backs through the staff door, waits for him to follow, and lets the door close between them. You and Otis stay upstairs until she calls from the next landing. When you finally get down, Mara is furious that you both kept trying to carry the carton. You leave it on the step.

That evening, Nia admits she has been waiting for someone else to choose the route. She knows this building, and every time she points somewhere, someone nearly gets hurt. Otis gives her the map. He asks her to show him where his old deliveries went. They sit together until the route fits the corridors you've actually seen. The loading yard, staff kitchen and department-store goods door all join the same blocked passage.

On day twenty, you discover that the shelving unit hasn't moved, but the infected person behind it has. You can see him farther down the corridor through a side doorway. Nia stays back with the radio while you, Mara and Otis clear enough fallen boxes to reach the goods door. Otis's hand shakes as he tries the handle. It opens into the department store. You leave the remaining obstruction behind you and close the door.

The shop floor is full of overturned clothes racks and abandoned bags. The exit you saw from upstairs is on the far wall. You cross slowly, looking behind each counter. At the door, Otis moves the fallen rail while Mara watches the aisles. You pull the handle and see daylight along the pedestrian path. Then an infected woman steps out beside the wall. You shut the door before she reaches it.

Nia wants to wait for morning, when someone at the collection point might hear the radio. Otis wants to leave immediately. He stands beside the open goods door with his bag in his hand until Mara asks whether he's coming back to the kitchen with you. He looks at the path through the exit window once more. Then he follows. Nobody has to carry him back this time.

Over the following days, you watch the pedestrian path through the department-store window. The infected woman wanders between a drainpipe and a broken bench. Others pass at irregular intervals. You can see a bend in the path, but you can't see the staffed gate. Nia makes short calls whenever the radio has power. You keep the route clear inside the store and move the group's supplies to the goods entrance. When you leave, you want everyone ready.

On day twenty-seven, somebody answers. Nia nearly drops the radio. The collection point is still open, but its staff cannot come inside. They tell her to call once the group reaches the bend. Otis asks whether that means today. Through the window, you can see the infected woman standing outside the door. You spend another night beside the packed bags. Mara gives Otis the last biscuit from her packet. He breaks it and gives half back.

On the thirtieth morning, the path outside the door is empty. You check the nearest counter, then the goods entrance behind you. Nia checks that everyone is there. She holds the radio in one hand. Otis carries the supplies he once tried to take upstairs on his trolley. Mara opens the exit while you watch the aisles. You all step outside together.

Halfway to the bend, Otis's bag splits. Bottles roll across the path. He crouches to gather them, and the infected woman appears behind the broken bench. You put your orange backpack down beside him and reach for his arm. He looks at the bottles. Then he gets up. Mara is waiting at the bend. Nia is calling the collection point, repeating where you are until someone answers.

A gate opens ahead. People wave you toward it. You help Otis through and turn back for Mara. Nia comes last, still holding the radio against her cheek. Someone closes the gate and puts a bottle of water in your hand. Otis starts apologizing for the supplies. You tell him to sit down before he drops anything else.

Across the yard, you can still see the mall's roof. Your charger is somewhere under it. So is your orange backpack, lying open on the pedestrian path beside the bottles. Mara asks whether you're going back for it. You pull your chair closer to the water table and shake your head.

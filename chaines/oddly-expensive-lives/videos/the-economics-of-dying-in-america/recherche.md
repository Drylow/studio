# Recherche et consignes — The Economics of Dying in America

Faits vérifiés et règles données au script.

VERIFIED FACTS for this video (use them, rounded, with the source named in the sentence; do not invent other statistics):
- 3,090,964 people died in the US in 2023 (CDC, National Center for Health Statistics, final data).
- Out-of-pocket health spending in the last five years of life averaged $38,688 per person (Amy Kelley and colleagues, Mount Sinai, Journal of General Internal Medicine, Health and Retirement Study data on 3,209 Medicare beneficiaries, 2002-2008). A quarter of households spent an average of $101,791, and a quarter spent more than their total household assets. Dementia cost the most: more than twice the average of cancer or gastrointestinal disease.
- Medicare does not pay for long-term custodial care (help with bathing, dressing, eating in a nursing home); it only covers short skilled-nursing stays after a qualifying hospital stay.
- Nursing home private room: national median $129,575 a year (CareScout / Genworth Cost of Care Survey 2025).
- Medicaid pays for long-term care only after the person spends down their assets; there is a 5-year (60-month) look-back on gifts and transfers; after death, states must try to recover what Medicaid paid for long-term care from the estate, which can include the house (estate recovery).
- Hospice under Medicare: most people pay nothing for hospice care; about $5 per prescription for pain and symptom drugs; up to 5% for inpatient respite days.
- Medicare spent $34,529 on average on each traditional Medicare beneficiary in their last year of life, nearly four times the $9,121 spent on survivors; the 4% of beneficiaries who died accounted for 13.5% of traditional Medicare spending (KFF analysis of 2014 data).
- Funeral with viewing and burial: national median $8,300; with viewing and cremation: $6,280 (National Funeral Directors Association, 2023 data). These medians do NOT include the cemetery plot, the opening and closing of the grave, the vault, the headstone or marker, or cash advance items.
- Cremation is projected at 63.4% of US deaths in 2025, versus 31.6% burial (NFDA 2025 Cremation & Burial Report).
- FTC Funeral Rule: a funeral home must give an itemized price list, show casket prices before showing caskets, and must accept a casket bought elsewhere without a handling fee.
- Probate (the court process that settles an estate) commonly costs about 3% to 7% of the estate's value in court, attorney and executor fees (typical estimate cited by estate lawyers; varies by state).
- Debts after death (Consumer Financial Protection Bureau): family members, including spouses, are generally NOT personally responsible for a dead person's debts unless they co-signed, held a joint account, or a state rule applies; the debts are paid from the estate first, which shrinks what the heirs receive.
- Federal estate tax: the exemption is $15 million per person in 2026; only about 0.1% of deaths (roughly 1 in 1,000) produce any federal estate tax.
- Life insurance: 51% of US adults say they own a policy (LIMRA 2025 Insurance Barometer).
ANGLE: everybody fears the funeral bill, but the funeral is the cheap, visible part. The expensive part is the slow years before death that Medicare does not cover, and the paperwork after death that eats the estate. Twist: the dying itself, under hospice, is almost free for the family. Follow ONE typical case (a constructed example, say so): a widowed retiree in their late 70s in Pennsylvania, a paid-off house worth about $400,000, Social Security, two adult children, a five-year decline, dies in hospice, funeral with viewing and burial, house goes through probate.

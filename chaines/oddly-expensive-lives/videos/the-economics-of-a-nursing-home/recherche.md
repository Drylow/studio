# Recherche et consignes — The Economics of a Nursing Home

Faits vérifiés et règles données au script.

VERIFIED FACTS for this video (use them, rounded, with the source named in the sentence; do not invent other statistics):
- CareScout (Genworth) Cost of Care Survey 2025 (released March 2026), national medians: nursing home semi-private room $315 a day ($114,975 a year); private room $355 a day ($129,575 a year); assisted living $6,200 a month ($74,400 a year); home caregiver $35 an hour ($80,080 a year at 44 hours a week). Prices vary a lot by state.
- Medicare.gov: Medicare (and Medigap) does not pay for long-term "custodial care" (help bathing, dressing, eating) when that is all you need; you pay all costs.
- Medicare Part A covers a skilled nursing facility (SNF) only after an inpatient hospital stay of at least 3 days in a row (time "under observation" or in the ER does not count), entering the SNF within about 30 days, and only while you need daily skilled care (Medicare.gov). 2026 amounts (CMS): hospital deductible $1,736; SNF days 1-20 $0; days 21-100 $217 a day; then everything. It is "up to" 100 days, based on needing skilled care, not on "improving" (CMS, Jimmo settlement, 2013).
- MedPAC: the average Medicare-covered SNF stay was 30.7 days in 2024; more stays end on day 20 (3.6%) than on day 19 or 21 (about 2.5%), right before the copay starts (2023 claims).
- KFF (2023): 13% of people in traditional Medicare (3.5 million) have no supplemental coverage. Medigap plans C, D, F, G, M and N pay the full SNF copay (Medicare.gov).
- KFF (July 2025): of about 1.2 million nursing home residents, Medicaid is the main payer for 63%, Medicare 14%, private and other 23%; 73% of nursing homes are for-profit.
- Medicaid rules (KFF, April 2026): most states cap a nursing home applicant's savings at $2,000 (California is an exception: $130,000 since 2026). Once enrolled, almost all income goes to the nursing home; the resident keeps a "personal needs allowance", median $70 a month in 2026 (federal floor $30).
- CMS 2026: the home is generally not counted if equity is under $752,000 (states may allow up to $1,130,000); a spouse at home keeps $32,532 to $162,660.
- Look-back: since the Deficit Reduction Act of 2005 (CMS), Medicaid reviews 60 months (5 years) of gifts and transfers; assets given away trigger a penalty period without Medicaid coverage.
- Estate recovery (Medicaid.gov): states must try to recover what Medicaid paid for nursing home care from the estate of enrollees 55 and older, which can include the house (not while a spouse, a child under 21 or a disabled child survives; hardship waivers exist); some states put a lien on the home.
- KFF: from January 1, 2027, the 2025 federal budget law cuts Medicaid's retroactive coverage for older adults from 3 months before the application to 2 months.
- ASPE/HHS 2022 (Johnson and Dey): 56% of Americans turning 65 will need long-term care (women 64%), 3.1 years on average but only about 0.8 years of paid care; 55% never use paid care; 14% will spend $100,000 or more out of pocket.
- ASPE/HHS 2019 (Johnson, Health and Retirement Study 1995-2014): 70% of people who reach 65 develop severe care needs (this is where the "70%" comes from), but only 48% ever receive paid care and 28% spend 90 days or more in a nursing home (women 34%, men 20%). Half of those long-stay residents stay two years or less.
- Kelly and colleagues, J. Am. Geriatrics Society 2010 (residents who died, 1992-2006): median stay 5 months (women 8); one in four stayed 20 months or more.
- Long-term care insurance: about 5.8 million people had a stand-alone policy at the end of 2024, falling 1-3% a year (Milliman, NAIC data). AALTCI 2026: a 55-year-old woman pays about $1,500 a year for a flat $165,000 benefit ($3,750 if it grows 3% a year).
- Federal Reserve Survey of Consumer Finances 2022: median net worth of households 75+ about $335,600, home included; our tabulation of the Fed's public file gives median financial assets of only about $50,000 (say "Federal Reserve survey data").
- Social Security (SSA 2026 COLA fact sheet): average benefit for a widow living alone about $1,919 a month; the 2026 Part B premium, $202.90 a month (CMS), usually comes out of the check.
- CDC: more than 1 in 4 adults 65+ fall each year; nearly 319,000 older people are hospitalized for hip fractures each year, mostly after falls.
- MedPAC (March 2026): nursing homes made a 24.4% margin on traditional Medicare patients in 2024 (over 10% for 25 years in a row) but -2.3% on everything else. MACPAC: the median Medicaid rate covered 86% of costs (2019).
- Federal rule (42 CFR 483.15): a nursing home may not require a relative to personally guarantee payment, nor a promise not to apply for Medicaid.
ANGLE: most families think Medicare pays for the nursing home. It pays for rehab, briefly, then the care becomes "custodial", which no health insurance covers. The system makes a person pay privately until she is nearly broke; then Medicaid, the low-income program, takes over and can reclaim the house after death. Medicare rehab pays nursing homes well, Medicaid pays below cost, and the private payer in between pays the posted price. Follow ONE typical case (a constructed example, say so): an 82-year-old widow in traditional Medicare with no supplement (like 13% of enrollees), a paid-off house and $194,000 in savings (more than most people her age), Social Security $1,919 a month. She falls, breaks her hip, is admitted for 4 days, then rehab; on day 31 Medicare stops: she can no longer live alone. She stays in a semi-private room until her savings are under $2,000.
RUNNING TAB (every line must be said aloud with this exact amount, then the running total):

- Rehab days 1 to 20 (Medicare pays in full): $0 -> total $1,736
- Rehab days 21 to 30, 10 days at $217 (CMS 2026 copay): $2,170 -> total $3,906
- Day 31, Medicare stops; year one of custodial care, 365 days at $315 (CareScout median, semi-private): $114,975 -> total $118,881
- Year two, same room at today's price: $114,975 -> total $233,856
Check: 1,736 + 0 + 2,170 + 114,975 + 114,975 = 233,856.
The running tab must end on one final total, $233,856, that becomes the thumbnail number. Savings check (say it simply): a month costs $9,581.25; Social Security after the Part B premium covers $1,716.10, savings $7,865.15. After 24 months: $194,000 - $3,906 - $188,763.60 = $1,330.40, under $2,000, so Medicaid starts in month 25 (Social Security paid $41,186.40, savings $192,669.60). Not counted: doctors' Part B bills, house costs, price increases.
Shown WITHOUT adding to the tab:
- Private room instead: 730 days at $355 = $259,150, or $29,200 more.
- With Medigap plan G (or C, D, F, M, N), the $2,170 line is $0.
- The observation trap: if the hospital had kept her "under observation", Medicare would pay nothing for rehab.
- After Medicaid: she keeps about $70 a month; the rest of her Social Security still goes to the nursing home. After her death, estate recovery can claim the house.
- If married: her husband could keep $32,532 to $162,660.
- A $165,000 insurance policy (AALTCI) would have paid about 17 months of her room (165,000 / 315 = 524 days).
- Other options, no Medicare either: assisted living $74,400 a year, home caregiver $80,080.
- Who gets paid: owners (73% for-profit); Medicare 24.4% margin vs -2.3% on the rest.
- Her stay is not extreme: half of long-stay residents stay two years or less (ASPE 2019); many die sooner (Kelly 2010).
RULES:
- Say once: general information, not legal or financial advice; rules vary by state. Say once that elder-law attorneys exist; nothing more.
- Never explain how to hide or transfer assets (gifts, trusts, annuities) or beat the look-back; only say it exists and that gifts can delay Medicaid.
- Numbers only from the facts above. Not the older CareScout figures ($111,325 etc.): the 2025 survey is the latest.
- Myths: "Medicare covers 100 days" (up to 100, average 30.7). "70% of us end up in a nursing home" (wrong: 70% or 56% need help, much of it unpaid; about 28% stay 90+ days). "The state takes your house when you go on Medicaid" (not at enrollment; estate recovery after death, liens in some states). "Medicare stops when you stop improving" (no: Jimmo). No insurer-marketing stats ("average stay 2.5 years").
- No allegations against named companies or private equity; margins are MedPAC facts. Never say Medicaid means bad care. Outrage at the system, never at the widow or her family.
- No logos on images (no Medicare card, insurer or nursing home brand); the widow is unnamed.
- Practical end: ask the hospital "am I an inpatient or under observation?"; check your Medigap for the SNF copay; free Medicare counseling from SHIP, a federal ACL network (shiphelp.org); never sign as personal guarantor; call the state Medicaid office before savings run out (retroactive coverage drops to 2 months in 2027); long-term care insurance is bought decades before.

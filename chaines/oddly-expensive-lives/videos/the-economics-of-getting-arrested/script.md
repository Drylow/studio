# The Economics of Getting Arrested

In our constructed case, built from Volusia County fee schedules and Florida law, your first DUI costs $4,613 before your car insurance company even opens the file. In this case, nobody crashes, nobody is hurt, and it's a first offense. The Council on Criminal Justice, summarizing FBI estimates, put DUI arrests at about 805,000 in 2024. People think the bill starts in court, with a lawyer and a judge. But the receipt printer starts at the roadside. Towing, one day of storage, booking, jail fees, bond, time off work, lawyer, court costs, probation, DUI school, and license reinstatement arrive. $2,720 is spent before any verdict.

## The case
The $2,720 pre-verdict bill belongs to a very ordinary file. One unnamed worker in Volusia County, Florida. The file says no record, first DUI, breath or blood under 0.15, no crash, and nobody hurt. This is a constructed case, and it is general information, not legal advice. Fees move by county, state, year, and clerk window.

Florida Statute 316.193 says a DUI arrestee stays in custody until one of three things is true: they're sober, they're under 0.05, or 8 hours have passed since arrest.

The serious part is unavoidable. Drunk driving killed 11,904 people in 2024, about 30% of U.S. traffic deaths, according to NHTSA. A sober ride home would have avoided the tow, booking, bond, and impound.

The bills arrive before guilt is decided. Running tab: empty.

## The tow yard
The first physical bill comes from the tow yard behind the fence.

The car is not at home, not at court, and not free. In Volusia County, the maximum non-consent tow rate is $150, and one day of storage is $35. So the tow company gets $150 for moving the car, and $35 for keeping it behind the gate.

That is $185 before the criminal case has started in any meaningful way, and the tow yard has already printed the first receipt.

One detail sits in the background. Florida Statute 316.193 also lets a first-DUI sentence include 10 days of impoundment or immobilization at the defendant's cost. We are not adding that yet.

Running total: tow and one day of storage, $185. Total, $185.

Now the person is at the jail, where the county also has a price list.

## The jail meter
At the booking desk, the wallet goes in a property bag, the phone number gets written on paper, and the clock starts doing what clocks do best. Charging rent.

Volusia County Corrections lists a $30 booking fee and $5 a day. For this constructed case, one day means $30 plus $5, or $35.

The Brennan Center has found that at least 43 states allow jails or prisons to charge people for their own incarceration. The county lists the $30 at booking, and the $5 is collected for each day in custody.

Scale matters here. The Bureau of Justice Statistics report Jail Inmates in 2024 says local jails held 658,700 people at midyear 2024, and 68% had not been convicted. BJS also counted 7.9 million jail admissions in a year, with an average stay of 31 days.

At $5 a day, the BJS average stay would turn that small daily charge into $155 before the booking fee.

Running total: jail booking fee and one day's jail fee, $35. Total, $220.

A $500 bond does not mean a $500 cost.

## The bond premium
A bond schedule is a preset amount used for release before the first court appearance. It is not the final word from a judge.

Florida's 2026 statewide bond schedule sets $500 for a first DUI before the first court appearance, and Volusia County adopted it. A bondsman is a commercial bail agent who posts the bond and charges a fee for doing it.

The Florida Department of Financial Services rule is simple. The premium is 10% of bail, with a $100 minimum. Once the person is released, that premium is not refundable.

So 10% of $500 would be $50. The minimum makes it $100. The bond fee does not come back. The receipt comes back. Very useful, if you enjoy paper.

PolitiFact reported in 2018 that only the U.S. and the Philippines have bail systems dominated by commercial bail bondsmen, though other countries may use cash deposits. Governing reports Illinois, Kentucky, Oregon, and Wisconsin outlaw commercial bail. Illinois ended cash bail on September 18, 2023.

Running total: bondsman's fee, 10% of the $500 DUI bond with the $100 minimum, never refunded, $100. Total, $320.

## The lost shifts
Now the bill moves to the kitchen table.

It is 7 a.m. There is a phone, a supervisor's voicemail, a courthouse envelope, and a work schedule that no longer matches real life.

The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported median weekly pay for full-time workers was $1,251 in the second quarter of 2026. Rounded, that is about $250 a day. In this constructed case, two missed workdays cost $500.

Two days is conservative. There is the arrest night, release logistics, the tow lot, a lawyer appointment, the first court date, and the quiet workplace disruption nobody itemizes.

Do not overstate it. One arrest does not automatically mean one lost job. But Dobbie, Goldin, and Yang found in the American Economic Review in 2018 that just three days held before trial cut formal employment 3 to 4 years later by 25%, using Miami-Dade and Philadelphia data.

That is the lost income.

If this is already changing how you see an arrest, subscribe, because the next bill is worse.

Running total: two missed workdays at about $250 a day, $500. Total, $820.

Now we get to the bill people expected first: the lawyer.

## The lawyer
The lawyer's office is the bill people recognize. Small conference room, yellow pad, retainer agreement, court date circled in ink.

An attorney fee is payment for legal time, negotiation, motions, court appearances, and advice. Nolo's 2022 first-DUI reader survey found average attorney fees of $1,900, and that number included people who used public defenders. A private lawyer often costs more. The running tab uses $1,900 because it's the verified survey figure.

There is a public defender option if income qualifies. In Florida, Statutes 27.52 and 938.29 create a $50 application fee and at least $50 on conviction, for incomes up to 200% of the poverty line. That is not our tab, because this constructed case hires a private lawyer.

The Brennan Center called Florida's fee system "cash register justice" in 2010, after more than 20 new charge categories since 1996.

Running total: private defense lawyer, Nolo first-DUI survey average, $1,900. Total, $2,720.

Now the plea turns the arrest into a sentence.

## The conviction stack
The courtroom bill arrives through a plea form, a clerk window, and one envelope with many small statutory labels.

In this constructed case, the person pleads and receives the standard first sentence used here: minimum fine, 12 months of probation, DUI school, a revoked license, and then reinstatement.

For a first Florida DUI under 0.15 with no injury, the fine is $500 to $1,000. The law also allows up to 6 months in jail, probation up to 1 year, 50 hours of community service, a DUI course at the defendant's cost, 10 days of impoundment or immobilization, and license revocation for 180 days to 1 year.

We use the minimum fine, $500. Court costs add at least $378 under Florida Statutes chapters 938 and 775.083. That includes $135 for DUI, $60 in court cost, $50 for crime victims, at least $50 for prosecution, $20 for crime prevention, $20 for Crime Stoppers, $15 for the county alcohol and drug fund, $3 for the clerk, and 5% of the fine, which is $25.

Counties can add more. Collier County's clerk lists $388 in misdemeanor DUI costs before the fine, but that is not the Volusia tab.

Running total: fine and court costs, the $500 minimum fine plus $378 in costs set by Florida statutes, $878. Total, $3,598.

Now the punishment becomes monthly.

## Probation rent
The next stop is a probation office. Appointment card, payment receipt, calendar with 12 boxes.

Misdemeanor probation is court-ordered supervision in the community instead of, or after, jail time. Under Florida Statute 948.09, misdemeanor probation costs at least $40 a month.

For this case, 12 months at $40 equals $480. The probation system or provider collects the supervision fee, one receipt each month. Miss the payments, and the payment problem can become another court problem.

Florida first DUI also includes 50 hours of community service. That is unpaid time, but it is not added to this tab because the tab is tracking cash costs.

Running total: probation, 12 months at the $40 legal minimum, $480. Total, $4,078.

Now comes the part that decides whether normal life can restart.

## School and license
The next receipts come from a classroom and a motor vehicle counter.

FLHSMV listed DUI School Level I at $330 in 2023. That pays the DUI school provider, not the court clerk.

Running total: DUI school, Level I, $330. Total, $4,408.

Then the license has to come back. Under Florida Statute 322.21, reinstatement after a DUI is $75, plus a $130 DUI fee. Total, $205. That goes through the state motor vehicle system.

FLHSMV says the license revocation for this offense class is 180 days to 1 year. During those months, the suspension affects work, child care, court, probation, and DUI school.

Running total: license reinstatement, $75 plus the $130 DUI fee, $205. Total, $4,613.

That is the official running tab. But the license receipt comes back later in the insurance file, and that part is outside the board.

## The extra shadow
These next costs are real, but they stay outside the $4,613 tab.

Start with the first half. The first $2,720 was paid before any verdict. So a person found not guilty has still spent it. The $100 bond premium does not come back. Nolo's 2022 survey found that 20% of first-DUI readers were not convicted.

If the charge is dropped, sealing is another small bill. FDLE charges $75, and the Volusia clerk lists $42. That is $117, not added here.

Cash bail can avoid the $100 bondsman premium. But Florida Statute 903.286 lets the clerk keep cash bail posted by a relative to pay costs and fines.

Impoundment is also outside this tab. Ten days at Volusia maximum rates would be a $150 tow plus 10 days at $35. Total, $500.

Insurance is outside too. Nolo found insurance rose about $800 a year, and Florida requires 3 years of high-limit FR-44 coverage after DUI.

Collection is the last shadow. A Florida payment plan can cost $25, or $5 a month. After 90 days, a collector may add up to 40%, which would be $351.20 on the $878 fine-and-costs amount.

The Brennan Center found in 2019 that governments spend more than 41 cents of each dollar of fees and fines collected on hearings and jail time.

## Who gets paid
The board becomes a pie chart. The tow company gets $185, county jail $35, and bondsman $100. Its insurer stands behind the $500 bond. Defense lawyer gets $1,900. State trust funds, State Attorney, and clerk divide fine and court costs. Probation gets $480. DUI school gets $330. The motor vehicle agency gets $205. Car insurer may collect later.

Prison Policy Initiative reported in 2016 that typical felony bail was $10,000, while Texas adds a $3,000 fine to a first DWI conviction under Transportation Code 709.001 and California abolished 23 criminal fees under 2021's AB 1869, so the same arrest can produce a different bill depending on the state. What can legally reduce it?

## Paying less legally
This is general information, not legal advice. It isn't a guide to avoid arrest, refuse a test, beat a charge, or dodge fees.

Low income? Ask about a public defender. Florida charges $50 to apply and at least $50 on conviction, potentially less than a private lawyer.

Ask the clerk for a payment plan before 90 days. OPPAGA reports Florida plans cost $25 or $5 monthly. After 90 days, a collector may add up to 40%.

Can't pay the fine? Ask the judge about community service. Florida credits it at no less than minimum wage. Keep receipts for towing, jail, bond, court, probation, school, and reinstatement.

If the charge is dropped, check sealing. The example costs $75 to FDLE plus $42 to the Volusia clerk, or $117. These steps may lower costs after an arrest, but they don't erase the costs that started at the roadside.

## The final receipt
The system works for someone. It processes arrests at scale, funds public-safety programs, pays vendors, and lets courts supervise people outside jail.

The timing is the problem, because several bills arrive before the court decides guilt. That is why the first half is paid while the case is still only an allegation.

The receipt: tow and storage, $185. Jail booking and one day, $35. Bondsman, $100. Two missed workdays, $500. Lawyer, $1,900. Fine and court costs, $878. Probation, $480. DUI school, $330. License reinstatement, $205.

Final total: $4,613.

It excludes higher insurance, three years of FR-44 coverage, 10-day impoundment, sealing if dropped, collection penalties, and 50 hours of unpaid community service.

The payees are the tow yard, jail, bondsman, lawyer, state trust funds, State Attorney, clerk, probation, DUI school, motor vehicle agency, and possibly the car insurer; the remaining noncash costs are unpaid hours, deadlines, and insurance risk.

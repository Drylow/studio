VERIFIED FACTS for this video (use them, rounded, with the source named in the sentence; do not invent other statistics):
- About 7.5 million arrests in the US in 2024, 25% fewer than in 2019; DUI was the second-largest category, about 805,000 (FBI estimates, summarized by the Council on Criminal Justice, 2025).
- 658,700 people were in local jails at midyear 2024, 68% of them not convicted; 7.9 million jail admissions in a year; average stay 31 days (Bureau of Justice Statistics, Jail Inmates in 2024).
- Typical money bail for a felony: $10,000, about eight months of income for the typical person held in jail (Prison Policy Initiative, 2016, federal data). A felony figure, not a DUI figure.
- Only the US and the Philippines have a bail system dominated by commercial bail bondsmen (PolitiFact 2018: other countries use some cash deposits). Illinois, Kentucky, Oregon and Wisconsin outlaw commercial bail (Governing); Illinois ended cash bail on Sept. 18, 2023.
- Florida: the bondsman's premium is 10% of bail, $100 minimum, not refundable once you are out (Florida Department of Financial Services). Florida's 2026 statewide bond schedule sets $500 for a first DUI before the first court appearance (Florida Supreme Court; adopted in Volusia County).
- Florida law: a DUI arrestee stays in custody until sober, under 0.05, or 8 hours after arrest (Fla. Stat. 316.193). Cash bail posted by a relative is kept by the clerk to pay costs and fines (Fla. Stat. 903.286).
- At least 43 states let jails or prisons charge people for their own incarceration (Brennan Center). Volusia County, Florida: $30 booking fee and $5 a day (Volusia County Corrections).
- Florida first DUI (under 0.15, no injury): fine $500 to $1,000, up to 6 months in jail, probation up to 1 year, 50 hours of community service, DUI course at your cost, 10-day vehicle impoundment or immobilization at your cost (Fla. Stat. 316.193); license revoked 180 days to 1 year (FLHSMV). Judges cannot withhold adjudication for DUI (Fla. Stat. 316.656), so a DUI conviction can never be sealed or expunged in Florida (Fla. Stat. 943.0585, 943.059).
- Court costs Florida statutes attach to a DUI conviction: $135 DUI cost (crime lab, EMS, brain and spinal cord injury fund), $60 court cost, $50 crime victims fund, at least $50 "cost of prosecution" for the State Attorney, $20 crime prevention, $20 Crime Stoppers, $15 county alcohol and drug fund, $3 clerk cost, plus 5% of the fine (Fla. Stat. chapter 938 and 775.083). Counties add more: Collier County's clerk lists $388 in costs for a misdemeanor DUI, before the fine.
- Misdemeanor probation: at least $40 a month (Fla. Stat. 948.09). DUI school Level I: $330 (FLHSMV, 2023). License reinstatement after a DUI: $75 + a $130 DUI fee (Fla. Stat. 322.21).
- Florida public defender: $50 application fee plus at least $50 on conviction (Fla. Stat. 27.52, 938.29), for incomes up to 200% of the poverty line. Brennan Center (2010) calls Florida's system "cash register justice": more than 20 new categories of charges since 1996.
- Unpaid Florida court debt: $25 or $5 a month for a payment plan; after 90 days a collector may add up to 40% (OPPAGA, Florida Legislature, 2019). Brennan Center (2019): governments spend more than 41 cents of each dollar of fees and fines collected on hearings and jail time. Fines and Fees Justice Center (2021): at least $27.6 billion in US court debt.
- Jail phone calls: the FCC's 2024 caps were rolled back in October 2025; 2026 caps are 8 cents a minute in jails of 1,000+ people, up to 17 cents in the smallest, plus up to 2 cents (Federal Register, Dec. 2025). 15 minutes from a large jail: up to $1.50.
- Median weekly pay of full-time workers: $1,251 (BLS, 2nd quarter 2026), about $250 a day. Just three days held before trial cut formal employment three to four years later by 25% (Dobbie, Goldin and Yang, American Economic Review 2018, Miami-Dade and Philadelphia data).
- Nolo survey of first-DUI readers (2022): average total $6,500; attorney fees $1,900 on average (public defenders included); insurance up about $800 a year; 20% were not convicted.
- Elsewhere: Texas adds a $3,000 state fine to a first DWI conviction, waived for the indigent (Tex. Transp. Code 709.001); a Texas county fee schedule charges $5 for an arrest without warrant, $5 to jail, $5 to release (Fort Bend County, 2026). California abolished 23 criminal fees, including probation and public defender fees, in 2021 (AB 1869).
- Drunk driving killed 11,904 people in 2024, 30% of US traffic deaths (NHTSA).

ANGLE: an arrest is not a verdict, but the bill starts at the roadside. Before any judge decides anything you pay a tow yard, the jail, a bondsman whose fee never comes back, a lawyer and your lost shifts. A conviction then adds a fine, plus a stack of "costs" that fund the crime lab, victims' funds, the prosecutor and the clerk, plus a monthly fee to be supervised. Follow ONE typical case (a constructed example, say so): a worker in Volusia County, Florida, no record, arrested for a first DUI, under 0.15, no crash, nobody hurt; car towed; held the 8-hour minimum; out on the $500 schedule bond through a bondsman; hires a private lawyer; pleads and gets the standard first sentence: minimum fine, 12 months of probation, DUI school, license revoked then reinstated.
RUNNING TAB (every line must be said aloud with this exact amount, then the running total):
- Tow and one day of storage, $150 + $35 (Volusia County maximum rates): $185 -> total $185
- Jail booking fee and one day's jail fee, $30 + $5 (Volusia County Corrections): $35 -> total $220
- Bondsman's fee, 10% of the $500 DUI bond with the $100 minimum, never refunded (Florida bond schedule; Florida DFS): $100 -> total $320
- Two missed workdays at about $250 a day (BLS median pay): $500 -> total $820
- Private defense lawyer (Nolo first-DUI survey average): $1,900 -> total $2,720
- Fine and court costs, the $500 minimum fine + $378 in costs set by Florida statutes: $878 -> total $3,598
- Probation, 12 months at the $40 legal minimum (Fla. Stat. 948.09): $480 -> total $4,078
- DUI school, Level I (FLHSMV): $330 -> total $4,408
- License reinstatement, $75 + $130 DUI fee (Fla. Stat. 322.21): $205 -> total $4,613
Check: 185 + 35 + 100 + 500 + 1,900 + 878 + 480 + 330 + 205 = 4,613. Costs: 135 + 60 + 50 + 50 + 20 + 20 + 15 + 3 + 25 = 378.
The running tab must end on one final total, $4,613, that becomes the thumbnail number. Then show WITHOUT adding to the tab: the first $2,720 is paid before any verdict, so someone found not guilty has still paid it (the bond fee never comes back), and sealing that arrest costs $75 (FDLE) + $42 (Volusia clerk) = $117; cash bail avoids the $100 fee but the clerk keeps it for costs; the 10-day impoundment at Volusia's maximum rates, $150 + 10 days at $35 = $500; insurance, about $800 a year more (Nolo) and 3 years of high-limit FR-44 coverage in Florida; 50 hours of unpaid community service; unpaid after 90 days, up to 40% more, $351.20 on the $878; at 0.15 or more, a $1,000 to $2,000 fine and an ignition interlock; Texas +$3,000; California, no such fees since 2021. Who gets paid: tow company, county jail, bondsman and the insurer behind the bond, the lawyer, state trust funds, the State Attorney, the clerk, the probation provider, the DUI school, the state motor vehicle agency, the car insurer.

RULES:
- Say once, clearly: general information, not legal advice; fees change and vary by county and state; the case is constructed and unnamed.
- Never a how-to to avoid arrest, beat or refuse a breath test, get charges dropped or dodge fees. Never make drunk driving look harmless: say once that it killed 11,904 people in 2024 (NHTSA); the cheapest way out of this bill is a ride home. Outrage at the fee system, never at the person or at victims.
- Numbers only from the facts. Say "at least" for court costs (counties add more). The $1,900 includes public defenders: a private lawyer often costs more. $250 a day is rounded from BLS weekly pay. Probation length is part of the constructed case.
- Myths: "only the US and the Philippines have money bail" (say commercial bail bondsmen); "the bond fee comes back" (no); "a first DUI is just a fine"; "you can expunge a Florida DUI" (no); the $10,000 median bail is a felony figure; the 2024 FCC phone caps are no longer current; Texas's $3,000 is Texas only; FBI arrest counts are estimates. The bond schedule binds jail release, not judges.
- "Cash register justice" is the Brennan Center's phrase: attribute it.
- No real people or companies (bail, law, towing, DUI school, insurance); no logos, real sheriff badges or seals, no alcohol brands.
- Practical, legal tips at the end: ask for a public defender if your income is low, ask the clerk for a payment plan before 90 days pass, ask the judge about community service if you truly cannot pay the fine (Florida allows it, valued at no less than minimum wage), keep every receipt, check sealing if the charge is dropped.

# The Economics of a Car Accident

A low-speed crash where both cars drive away can still leave you with a $5,023 personal tab. NHTSA reported 6.18 million police-reported crashes in 2024, about one every five seconds. The common belief is that full coverage means the insurer pays the painful part. But the largest bill in this case doesn't come from the body shop. It comes from three renewal notices after the crash, which LendingTree priced at about $3,093 over three years in a February 2026 rate study. The crash happens fast. Somebody is already adding up the next 36 months.

## The typical crash
In stop-and-go traffic, a 30-year-old driver taps the car ahead at low speed. Both cars still drive. A police report is filed. Nobody needs an ambulance. By evening, the driver has a sore neck and goes to the emergency room.

This is a constructed typical case, not a real person. It's built from cited averages. The setup is full coverage, a $500 deductible, no rental reimbursement, no medical payments coverage, employer health insurance, and a state without no-fault auto insurance.

Full coverage usually means liability, collision, and comprehensive coverage. It does not mean every bill disappears. A deductible is the first part of your own repair claim that you pay before the insurer pays the rest.

A claims number appears on the phone. A rental key waits on the kitchen counter. Three renewal notices sit beside it. The damage is minor, but the claim file is already getting complicated.

This is general information, not legal, insurance, financial, or medical advice. Rules and prices vary by state and insurer. If anyone is hurt, call 911.

So which bill arrives first when nobody went by ambulance and both cars drove away? Keep the claims number. We'll start at the ER check-in desk.

## The ER check
That evening, the sore neck leads to a bright ER waiting room, a plastic wristband, and an intake form asking for health insurance. In this state, there is no no-fault auto insurance and no medical payments coverage. So the driver's injury goes first to the employer health plan.

The Peterson-KFF Health System Tracker reported in December 2022 that, for large-employer plans in 2019, the average emergency visit cost $2,453. The patient paid $646 on average. That's across ER visits, not a quoted price for neck pain.

The hospital gets paid. The health plan gets billed. The patient gets whatever share the plan rules allow.

Running tab: ER visit for the sore neck, your share with employer insurance: $646. Running total: $646.

That is the first surprise. Auto insurance is involved, but the medical bill may start somewhere else. The first bill is clear. The next question is transportation.

The car goes to the body shop tomorrow, and the declarations page has no rental reimbursement add-on. That missing line will come back fast.

## The rental gap
The car is still drivable, but it goes to the body shop. A clipboard records the damage. In the estimate bay, the pickup date is not tomorrow. The rental key from the kitchen counter now has a job.

Enterprise Mobility reported in its second-quarter 2026 results that collision rentals lasted 15.1 days on average, or 14.1 days when the car could still be driven. We round this case to 14 days.

Hertz reported in its 2025 annual report that it collected about $56 per rental day in the Americas. Fourteen days times $56 is $784. That's two weeks of transportation for a low-speed tap. The car can be repaired, and the driver is paying for transportation by the day.

Rental reimbursement is optional. Progressive and State Farm both describe it as separate coverage, not an automatic part of full coverage. Progressive says typical limits run from $40 to $70 a day.

Running tab: rental car, 14 days at about $56 a day: $784. Running total: $1,430.

Full coverage can still leave you holding the rental key and the receipt. Pay attention to the next detail, because it explains why a small tap can price like a major repair.

## The smart bumper
At the shop, the bumper cover comes off. Behind the plastic are parking sensors, wiring, and a camera. The repair sheet adds a line for calibration.

CCC Intelligent Solutions reported in its 2026 Crash Course that the average repair cost for a fixed vehicle was $4,818 in preliminary 2025 data. CCC also found that 28.3% of repair estimates included sensor or camera calibrations.

Calibration means the cameras and sensors are aimed and tested after repair, so the safety systems can read the road correctly. That plastic bumper cover hides sensors, wiring, and a camera that may need calibration.

The Insurance Information Institute, using ISO and Verisk data in 2024, reported an average collision claim of $5,489. Our running tab doesn't add the full repair, because the insurer mostly pays it. The driver pays the deductible.

CCC reported in April 2026 that $500 remained the most common deductible on an own-damage claim.

Running tab: collision deductible, $500. Running total: $1,930.

The insurer's share is about $4,318. That stays off the personal tab.

A small bumper repair can include electronics work now. If the driver has paid $1,930 so far, where does the rest of the $5,023 come from? The next bill comes from the insurer's pricing model.

## The renewal notice
Months later, the car is fixed, the bumper is painted, and the first renewal notice lands in the app like a parking ticket with better branding.

LendingTree, in a February 2026 study by Carol Pope using Quadrant Information Services rates, priced a 30-year-old with full coverage, a $500 deductible, and one at-fault accident with at least $2,000 of damage. The average annual premium went from $2,277 to $3,308.

That is $1,031 more per year. About a 45% increase in that study.

For this typical case, the three equal premium lines are an estimate. Real surcharges vary by insurer, state, claim size, and year.

Running tab: Higher premium, year one: $1,031. Running total: $2,961.

The shop work ends in weeks, and the premium increase starts at renewal. The car is back in the driveway, and the accident remains on the insurer's pricing record.

The driver has two more renewals before the charge usually clears, and the clock has already started.

## Three renewals
Now put three renewal notices on the kitchen table. Year one, year two, year three.

The Insurance Information Institute says an accident increase generally stays on your premium for three years after the claim. LendingTree notes that three to five years can happen, depending on the insurer and state. This case uses three years because the tab is built on the verified three-year figure.

So the second envelope opens.

Running tab: Higher premium, year two: $1,031. Running total: $3,992.

Then the third one arrives, when the crash feels old enough to have its own forwarding address.

Running tab: Higher premium, year three, when the surcharge generally ends: $1,031. Running total: $5,023.

Those three premium lines are $3,093, the same three-year increase in the February 2026 LendingTree study.

Write this one down. The surcharge alone is bigger than the ER share, the rental, and the deductible combined.

If this is already changing how you see full coverage, subscribe, because the next bill is worse.

The detail most people miss is that $5,023 is not the whole cost of the crash.

## The insurer’s card
Now keep the personal running tab clean. Do not add this next card to the $5,023. This is what other payers absorb in the typical case.

CCC's 2026 Crash Course puts the average repair cost for a fixed vehicle at about $4,818, based on preliminary 2025 data. Subtract the $500 deductible, and the driver's auto insurer pays about $4,318.

Then there's the other car. ISO and Verisk data, cited by the Insurance Information Institute, put the average property damage liability claim at $6,770. The auto insurer's share for both vehicles is about $11,088.

The ER has its own card. Peterson-KFF puts the average ER visit at $2,453, with the patient paying $646. So the health plan pays about $1,807.

The auto insurer pays the repair shop and the other driver. The health plan pays the hospital. The driver pays the deductible, rental counter, and renewal notices.

The repair, hospital, and other car each have their own file.

Up to this point, the car still drove and nobody else was hurt. That can flip with one detail.

## The worse branches
These branches do not go into our running tab. They show how fragile the $5,023 number is.

If the car cannot be driven, the rental clock gets longer. Enterprise Mobility's Q2 2026 report put the average collision rental at 20.7 days when the car is not drivable, compared with 14.1 days when it is.

If it needs a tow, the city matters. Houston's regulated accident tow was $232 for the first 20 miles in 2026. In Texas, the Department of Licensing and Regulation allows storage lots to charge up to $22.85 a day.

If the car is totaled, the bill changes again. CCC's Crash Course 2026 said 23.1% of claims were total losses. Almost one in four.

If another person is hurt, ISO and Verisk data cited by the Insurance Information Institute put the average bodily injury liability claim at $28,278. CCC said that rose 10.3% in one year.

In a no-fault state, your own personal injury protection pays medical bills first. The Insurance Information Institute says 12 states and Puerto Rico use that system.

Now the cost spreads to people who were never in the crash.

## The national bill
Pull back to the national ledger.

NHTSA's economic study of 2019 crashes, revised in 2023, estimated the money cost at $340 billion in 2019 dollars. That works out to about $1,035 per American.

The same study used a broader figure near $1.4 trillion, but that wasn't money spent. It included pain, lost quality of life, and other social harm. The actual money bill was $340 billion.

NHTSA counted 22.9 million damaged vehicles, and 84% were in crashes where nobody was hurt. Those property-damage-heavy crashes alone cost $101.3 billion.

About 60% of property-damage-only crashes were never reported to police.

Who paid? Private insurers covered about 54%. People in the crash paid about 23%. Public money covered about 9%, or roughly $30 billion. The rest moved through premiums, taxes, and traffic delay.

NHTSA's breakdown means people not involved carried roughly three quarters of the cost.

That is not a typo.

Premiums and taxes put crash costs on people outside it. The real question is what happens behind the invoices.

## Who gets paid
Follow the money.

First, the body shop gets labor, parts, paint, scanning, and calibration money. Calibration providers aim and test the cameras and sensors behind the bumper.

Next, the rental company gets paid because repairs often take one or more weeks. The hospital gets paid when a sore neck becomes an ER claim.

The auto insurer pays covered repairs and other damages, then collects three years of higher premiums in the LendingTree example.

Tow and storage lots enter when the car can't be driven. Houston's regulated tow was $232 for the first 20 miles in 2026, and the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation allowed storage up to $22.85 a day.

Lawyers appear mainly in injury disputes, not this light case.

These are normal businesses touching one event. The Insurance Research Council reported in February 2025 that 15.4% of drivers were uninsured in 2023. Missing coverage shifts collection problems back into the system.

Liability and collision coverage can still leave rental, deductible, medical-share, and surcharge gaps.

## Paying less legally
These are general moves, not personal insurance, legal, financial, or medical advice.

Ask about accident forgiveness before a crash. Depending on the insurer and state rules, it may reduce or avoid the surcharge.

This case carries a $3,093 surcharge over three years, the biggest target.

Price rental reimbursement before you need it. Progressive lists typical limits of $40 to $70 a day for up to 30 or 45 days. Our rental gap is $784, so compare the add-on with that possible cost.

At renewal, compare insurers legally and honestly. NerdWallet reported in October 2026 that a 35-year-old with $10,000 of damage saw a 49% average increase, about $1,163 a year. NerdWallet's range was about 30% to 60%, while Insurify found about 34% in October 2026.

Know whether your state is no-fault, because the medical path changes.

Keep the police report, photos, and itemized estimate. Disputes run on documents.

ValuePenguin by LendingTree said in June 2026 that repaired cars can lose value. Most insurers reject diminished value claims on your own policy, so the at-fault driver usually can't recover it. No national average is verified.

Those moves can change the number in a real case. For our base case, before any discount or forgiveness, the final ledger is still $5,023.

## The final ledger
The system works best when coverage is enough, the car is repairable, nobody is badly hurt, and the driver can carry the deductible, rental gap, and premium increase.

That is the upside.

Full coverage still doesn't mean zero out-of-pocket cost, a covered rental, a free ER visit, or no future surcharge.

Here is the personal ledger.

ER share, $646. Rental, $784. Collision deductible, $500.

Premium increases: $1,031 in year one, $1,031 in year two, and $1,031 in year three.

Those three lines total $3,093.

Final personal tab: $5,023.

Keep the separate payer card off this total. The auto insurer pays about $11,088 for the driver's repair share and the other car. The health plan pays about $1,807 for the ER remainder.

Together, those are not the driver's $5,023.

Who gets paid? The body shop, calibration provider, rental company, hospital, and insurers. On the worse branch, tow lots, storage lots, and lawyers join them.

Who carries risk? The driver carries the deductible, rental gap, health share, and renewal notices.

The largest charge arrived later through the renewal notices.

Both cars drove away, but the bill followed for 36 months.

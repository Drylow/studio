# Recherche et consignes — The Economics of a Car Accident

VERIFIED FACTS for this video (use them, rounded, with the source named in the sentence; do not invent other statistics):
- NHTSA (Overview of Motor Vehicle Traffic Crashes in 2024, April 2026): about 6.18 million police-reported crashes in 2024; 4.47 million, about 72%, damaged only property; 2.42 million people injured, 39,254 killed. That is about one police-reported crash every five seconds (simple division).
- NHTSA economic study (crashes of 2019, revised 2023): $340 billion in 2019, about $1,035 per American; 22.9 million vehicles damaged, 84% of them in crashes where nobody was hurt; those crashes alone cost $101.3 billion; about 60% of them are never reported to police. 2019 dollars.
- Same NHTSA study, who pays: private insurers about 54%, the people in the crash about 23%, public money about 9% ($30 billion); people not involved pay roughly three quarters, mostly through premiums, taxes and traffic delay.
- ISO / Verisk data via the Insurance Information Institute (2024): average collision claim $5,489, about 4 claims per 100 insured cars a year; average property damage liability claim (the other car) $6,770; average bodily injury liability claim $28,278, up 10.3% in one year (CCC Crash Course 2026).
- CCC Intelligent Solutions (Crash Course 2026, March 2026): average repair cost for a car that gets fixed $4,818 in 2025 (preliminary); 28.3% of repair estimates now include sensor or camera calibrations; a record 23.1% of claims were total losses.
- Deductibles (CCC, April 2026): $500 is still the most common deductible on your own claims; $1,000-or-higher deductibles grew more than 6 points in two years. CCC (Q3 2025): repairs of $2,000 or less fell from 41.5% of repair appraisals in 2019 to 25.5% in 2025, as small damage is paid out of pocket or left unfixed.
- Repair time (Enterprise Mobility, Q2 2026): a collision rental lasts 15.1 days on average; 14.1 days if the car can still be driven, 20.7 if not.
- Rental price: Hertz's 2025 annual report: it collected on average $56.49 per rental day in the Americas in 2025. Say "about $56 a day".
- Rental reimbursement is an optional add-on, not included in full coverage (Progressive; State Farm). Progressive: daily limits typically $40 to $70, for up to 30 or 45 days.
- ER visit (Peterson-KFF Health System Tracker, December 2022, 2019 claims, large-employer plans): $2,453 per emergency visit on average, of which the patient paid $646.
- Twelve states and Puerto Rico have no-fault auto insurance (Insurance Information Institute): your own personal injury protection pays your medical bills whoever caused the crash. Elsewhere, your own injuries go to your health insurance, or to optional medical payments coverage.
- LendingTree study (Carol Pope, February 2026, Quadrant Information Services rates; a 30-year-old, full coverage, $500 deductible, one at-fault accident with at least $2,000 of damage): the average full coverage premium goes from $2,277 to $3,308 a year, +45%, "about $1,031 more per year, or roughly $3,093 over three years". California +70.7%, Pennsylvania +16.5%. About $2,024 more a year at age 20, about $1,072 in your 50s and 60s.
- Other studies (name which one): NerdWallet (October 2026, 35-year-olds, $10,000 of damage): +49%, about $1,163 a year, from about 30% to 60% depending on the insurer. Insurify (October 2026): about 34%.
- How long: the Insurance Information Institute says the increase generally stays on your premium for three years after the claim; LendingTree says three to five years.
- Diminished value (ValuePenguin by LendingTree, June 2026): a repaired car can be worth less; most insurers don't allow a diminished value claim on your own policy, so the driver who caused the crash usually can't recover it. No national average was verified.
- Towing (local examples only): Houston's regulated accident tow is $232 for the first 20 miles (2026); Texas storage lots may charge up to $22.85 a day (Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation).
- Insurance Research Council (February 2025): 15.4% of drivers, about one in seven, were uninsured in 2023.

ANGLE: the crash takes two seconds, the bill takes three years. "Full coverage" sounds like everything is paid, but the deductible is yours, the rental car is an optional add-on, and in most states, without medical payments coverage, your sore neck goes on your health plan, not your car insurance. The biggest line is not the repair (the insurer pays that): it is the surcharge built into your next three renewals. And modern bumpers are full of sensors, so a little tap now costs thousands to fix. Follow ONE typical case (a constructed example, say so): a 30-year-old with employer health insurance, full coverage with a $500 deductible, no rental add-on, no medical payments coverage, in a state without no-fault insurance. Stop-and-go traffic, they tap the car ahead at low speed; police report, both cars still drivable, nobody else hurt; their neck is sore, so they get checked at the ER that evening and go home the same night; the car spends about two weeks in the shop.

- The big picture: $340 billion a year (NHTSA, 2019), about $1,035 per American, three quarters paid by people who were not in the crash; about one driver in seven uninsured (IRC).
- Who gets paid: the body shop and its calibration gear, the rental company, the hospital, your auto insurer (three years of higher premiums), tow and storage lots, lawyers in injury cases.

RULES:
- Say once: general information, not legal, insurance, financial or medical advice; rules and prices vary by state and insurer. If anyone is hurt, call 911; get checked if you feel pain after a crash. Never suggest skipping care to save money.
- Never suggest hiding a crash from police or the insurer, lying about fault, faking or exaggerating an injury, or dodging a surcharge. Legal tips at the end only: ask about accident forgiveness before you need it; price the rental add-on; compare insurers at renewal (the increase ran from about 30% to 60% by insurer, NerdWallet); know if your state is no-fault; keep the police report, photos and the itemized estimate.
- Numbers only from the facts. Say "one study, by LendingTree, found..."; never present 45% as a rule. The three equal premium lines are an estimate (the real surcharge varies by insurer and by year): say so once.
- Myths: the "$1.4 trillion" is NHTSA's societal harm including a value for quality of life, not money spent (the money cost is $340 billion, 2019). "A crash every 17.9 years" was not verified: don't use it. "One in eight uninsured" is outdated (15.4% in 2023). "An accident cuts a car's value by 10% to 25%" was not verified: no diminished value number.
- The $646 is an average for all ER visits (2019 claims), not a price for neck pain; the $56 is Hertz's average per rental day, not a quoted price.
- Keep it light: a tap in traffic, no blood, no ambulance, nobody else hurt. Never blame the driver: the outrage is at how the bill is built. No allegations that insurers, tow lots or shops cheat.
- No real people as characters (Carol Pope and the companies are sources only). No car brand logos, badges or recognizable grilles; no rental, insurer, tow company or hospital logos on cars, trucks, keys, forms or signs; cards without Visa or Mastercard logos.

# The Economics of a Divorce

Your divorce can settle before trial and still average $10,600 per person, according to Martindale-Nolo's 2019 national divorce survey, and that's the cheap version of the fight. The CDC's National Center for Health Statistics counted 673,989 divorces in 2022, even without California and four other states. A lot of people think the scary bill is the lawyer. But the lawyer is usually the front desk. The larger bill arrives when one mortgage becomes two rents, the house has to be sold, and a retirement account needs its own court order. We'll follow one contested divorce that settles, line by line. Somebody is already adding it up.

## The case file
So how does a normal household become a $61,000 invoice before either person feels richer? Put a plain case on the board. A married couple in Ohio, both in their 40s, married 12 years, two kids, one house estimated at $350,000, one 401(k), and three fights: parenting time, house equity, and retirement money. That is not a celebrity divorce. That is a kitchen table with two stacks of envelopes.

This is general information, not legal or financial advice, because divorce costs change by state, county, income, assets, and conflict level. Our calendar has 12 to 18 months circled, because contested cases often live there. Beside it is one growing receipt labeled running tab.

That tab counts out-of-pocket and transaction costs for the whole divorce household. It does not count emotional damage. It does not count the full wealth loss. For now, the total is zero, because the first real bill starts when someone hires counsel or pays the court.

## Three levers
A divorce puts property, debt, parenting rights, and future income obligations under court supervision. The bill stands on three levers. First, conflict. Second, assets. Third, children.

A contested divorce means the spouses do not agree on one or more major issues. So the case needs negotiation, motions, discovery, mediation, or trial pressure. Discovery just means both sides demand documents. The relationship is ending, and now both sides are asking for bank statements.

For our case, use a 12-month timeline, with an 18-month delay shown as the expensive version. Translate that into normal life. That is one to one and a half years of attorney emails, bank statements, temporary schedules, and duplicate housing costs.

Our case settles before trial. That matters. They avoid the worst courtroom bill, but they still pay for the machine that prepares for court. Even without trial, the final number will look closer to a new car than a filing fee.

## The lawyer meter
Start with the line item everyone expects: attorney fees. Martindale-Nolo's 2019 national divorce survey found the average divorce lawyer rate was $270 an hour. A retainer is a down payment on a lawyer's time. You hand over several thousand dollars, and the meter starts eating it in six-minute slices. Very dignified. Very billable.

The same Martindale-Nolo survey found average attorney fees of $11,300 per person, with a median of $7,000. It also found 42% paid $5,000 or less. That means the average is pulled upward by messier, longer cases.

For our running case, use Martindale-Nolo's average for disputes settled before trial: $10,600 per person in total divorce cost. For both spouses, that is $21,200. Call this the legal-process bucket for a contested case that settles.

At $270 an hour, $10,600 buys roughly 39 hours of lawyer time. Then remember there are two sides buying separate hours to argue over one pile of money. Add settled contested legal process, $21,200.

Running total: $21,200.

## The children calendar
Pay attention to the next detail, because the calendar is where custody starts costing money. Children do not create a price tag because they are children. They create a price tag because the court needs a schedule, a support calculation, and sometimes an outside opinion.

A custody evaluation is a professional review of parenting, home life, records, and sometimes interviews. It is used when parents cannot agree or when the court wants more information before making an order.

In this worked example, treat the county-style evaluation as a local estimate: $1,000 to $2,500. Treat the private evaluator as a high-end local estimate: $15,000 or more. For this case, use a county-style middle estimate of $2,000, because the divorce settles and does not use a high-end private evaluator.

The detail people miss is that even the cheaper version can create new costs. It can mean delays, time off work, and fresh lawyer hours to argue about what the evaluator wrote.

Add custody evaluation, $2,000.

Running total: $23,200.

Now the couple has 12 to 18 months before settlement, and the housing clock has already started.

## The second front door
Now leave the courthouse hallway and look at the first hidden giant: housing.

The Census Bureau's 2023 American Community Survey put median U.S. gross rent at about $1,400 a month. In the Census measure, gross rent includes rent plus the usual tenant-paid utilities.

For this Ohio case, one spouse rents a modest place during the 12-month low end of a contested divorce timeline. Write this one down. $1,400 times 12 months is $16,800.

Add second household for one year, $16,800.

That is not a legal fee. That is the price of needing a second front door while the first one is still being divided. The court can decide who owns what before either person can afford the extra rent.

The catch is time. If the case runs 18 months instead of 12, that same rent line becomes $25,200, before deposits, furniture, or moving costs.

Running total: $40,000.

The tab has almost doubled after leaving the lawyer's office.

## Selling the house
So what happens to the house that used to hold the whole system together?

Back at the kitchen table, there is a mortgage statement, a listing agreement, repair receipts, and a calendar full of showings. The common options are simple on paper. One spouse buys out the other. Both keep owning temporarily. Or the house is sold, and the equity is split.

For this running case, use a sale. Neither spouse can comfortably carry the mortgage alone after legal costs and second rent.

For this case, use an estimated sale commission of 5.5% of the sale price. Our house value is a rounded case estimate, $350,000. That is not a national statistic. At a 5.5% case-estimated commission, the toll is $19,250.

The commission comes off the top before either spouse divides the equity. So both sides can feel like they lost money, even when the sale price looks good.

Add estimated sale commission, $19,250.

Running total: $59,250.

The house is visible. The retirement account is quieter, but it has its own trapdoor.

## The retirement order
The retirement order is the quiet form in this whole stack.

A QDRO, spelled Q-D-R-O, is a Qualified Domestic Relations Order. It is the court order that tells a retirement plan how to split a 401(k) or a pension.

For this worked case, estimate $1,000 to draft the QDRO and $750 in plan administrator fees. That is $1,750 total.

The form matters. The IRS says money paid to an ex-spouse from a 401(k) under a QDRO avoids the 10% early-withdrawal penalty. Cashing out outside a QDRO does not.

That is the trap. People see a retirement account and think cash. But mishandling it can trigger taxes, penalties, and a permanently smaller retirement balance.

Add QDRO and plan fees, $1,750.

Running total: $61,000.

By the time the case reaches the trial threat, the couple understands what settlement saved and what it did not save.

## The trial cliff
This running case does not go to trial. That is the point.

Settling still produced a $61,000 household tab. Martindale-Nolo's 2019 national divorce survey found that disputes settled before trial averaged $10,600 per person.

Trial changes the meter. Martindale-Nolo found that trial on one issue averaged $20,400 per person. Trial on two or more issues averaged $23,300 per person.

Compared with $10,600 per person, one trial issue adds about $9,800 per person. For two spouses, that is $19,600. Two or more trial issues add about $12,700 per person, or $25,400 for the household.

Trial adds preparation, exhibits, witnesses, hearings, and lawyer hours before anyone reaches the witness chair. The machine gets fed before anyone sits in the witness chair.

Do not add trial to our running tab. But the alternate total is $80,600 with one trial issue, or $86,400 with two or more.

If that sounds bad, the next bill is the one no courthouse prints.

## The missing bill
The running tab is still $61,000, but that is only the cash we can point to on receipts. It is the transaction cost of turning one household into two.

The larger number is wealth damage.

Jay Zagorsky at Ohio State University used National Longitudinal Survey of Youth data on 9,055 people from 1985 to 2000. His finding was blunt. Divorce cut a person's wealth by about 77% on average. Staying married nearly doubled wealth, up 93%.

Write the caution next to that number. It does not mean every divorce causes the same loss. It does not say what anyone should do. It shows the average financial shock around divorce in that dataset.

The hidden bill is time off work for court, moving costs, duplicate furniture, tax filing changes, refinanced debt, lower savings rates, and missed compounding.

Some costs arrive as invoices. Others show up later as a lower 401(k), less home equity, or smaller savings.

The checks go out during the case, and the lower balances stay with the couple afterward.

So who is sitting on the other side of the checks?

## Who gets paid
Follow the money and the room gets crowded.

Lawyers get paid by the hour. Martindale-Nolo's 2019 survey put the average divorce lawyer rate at $270 an hour. Every email has a meter. Very efficient, in the way a taxi in traffic is efficient.

Mediators get paid when the couple needs a neutral person to push a settlement. Nolo's divorce mediation cost survey puts a typical full mediation at about $3,000 to $8,000 total, usually split between spouses.

Evaluators get paid when parenting disputes need outside review. County evaluations may be $1,000 to $2,500. Private evaluators can reach $15,000 or more.

Forensic accountants get paid when income, business value, or hidden money is disputed. They bill for tracing accounts, valuing a business, and turning messy records into exhibits.

Real estate agents, retirement-plan administrators, appraisers, and sometimes therapists and refinancing lenders all sit near the case.

If the spouses fight over fees, the lawyers can bill for that fight too.

## Paying less
This is general information, not legal or financial advice for any specific divorce. State law matters. Safety matters. Power inside the marriage matters. The cheapest option is not always the safe one.

But there are levers.

First, settle early where it is safe and realistic. Martindale-Nolo's 2019 survey found disputes settled before trial averaged $10,600 per person. Trial on one issue averaged $20,400. That is about $9,800 per person avoided, or $19,600 for the couple.

Second, use mediation to compress conflict. A $3,000 to $8,000 mediation bill, usually split, can be far cheaper than adding dozens of lawyer hours at $270 an hour.

Third, keep the custody dispute narrow. A county process at $1,000 to $2,500 is a different world from a private evaluator at $15,000 or more.

Fourth, decide whether keeping the house is affordable after the divorce, not before it. A 5% to 6% sale commission is painful. A house neither spouse can carry can become a slower, more expensive problem.

Fifth, handle the retirement split with a proper QDRO. It may cost $500 to $1,500 to draft, plus $300 to $1,200 in plan fees. But under IRS rules, 401(k) money paid to an ex-spouse under a QDRO can avoid the 10% early-withdrawal penalty.

## The final ledger
The system works best when it turns a dangerous, unfair, or unworkable household into two legally separate lives with enforceable rules.

The math breaks because that same clarity charges hourly rates, duplicates housing, tolls the house sale, and puts retirement behind paperwork.

Here is the receipt. Settled contested legal process, $21,200. County-style custody evaluation estimate, $2,000. Second household for 12 months at Census median gross rent, $16,800. Estimated 5.5% commission on a $350,000 house sale, $19,250. QDRO drafting and plan fees, $1,750.

Final running total: $61,000.

Who benefits is reasonably clear: lawyers, mediators, evaluators, plan administrators, and real estate agents. Who carries the risk is also clear: two people rebuilding one financial system into two.

The lawyer was only the first bill. The rest showed up in the house, the rent, and the retirement account.

# Recherche et consignes — The Economics of a Divorce

Faits vérifiés et règles données au script.

VERIFIED FACTS for this video (use them, rounded, with the source named in the sentence; do not invent other statistics):
- 673,989 divorces were recorded in the US in 2022, 2.4 per 1,000 people (CDC, National Center for Health Statistics; that count leaves out California and four other states).
- Martindale-Nolo 2019 national survey of people who divorced: average attorney fees $11,300 per person (median $7,000); average divorce lawyer rate $270 an hour; 42% paid $5,000 or less.
- Same survey, average total cost per person: uncontested with lawyers $4,100; disputes settled before trial $10,600; trial on one issue $20,400; trial on two or more issues $23,300.
- A contested divorce usually takes 12 to 18 months.
- Mediation: about $3,000 to $8,000 in total, usually split between the spouses.
- Custody evaluation: roughly $1,000 to $2,500 through the county, $15,000 or more for a private evaluator (typical ranges).
- Forensic accountant: about $100 to $500 an hour, retainers of $2,000 to $10,000.
- QDRO (the court order that splits a 401(k) or pension): usually $500 to $1,500 to draft, plus plan administrator fees around $300 to $1,200.
- IRS rule: money paid to an ex-spouse from a 401(k) under a QDRO avoids the 10% early-withdrawal penalty; cashing out a 401(k) outside a QDRO does not.
- Selling the house: agent commissions have historically run about 5% to 6% of the price.
- Second household: the median US gross rent was about $1,400 a month (Census Bureau, American Community Survey 2023).
- Jay Zagorsky, Ohio State University (National Longitudinal Survey of Youth, 9,055 people, 1985-2000): divorce cuts a person's wealth by about 77% on average; staying married nearly doubles it (+93%).

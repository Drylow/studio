# Recherche et consignes — The Economics of a Wedding

Faits vérifiés et règles données au script.

VERIFIED FACTS for this video (use them, rounded, with the source named in the sentence; do not invent other statistics):
- The Knot 2026 Real Weddings Study (10,474 US couples married in 2025, recruited from The Knot and WeddingWire members): average wedding cost $34,000 (The Knot's article says $34,200), but the MEDIAN was $24,000; a quarter spent $12,000 or less, a quarter more than $41,000. About 2 million US couples married in 2025, more than $100 billion of wedding spending.
- Same study: 117 guests on average, $292 per guest; about 13 vendors hired; engaged 14 months on average. By size: 1-50 guests $17,100, 51-100 guests $27,200, over 100 guests $43,300.
- The Knot says its average covers the ceremony and reception only. It leaves out the engagement ring (average $4,600), the rehearsal dinner (average $2,800) and the honeymoon (average $5,500).
- The Knot 2026, average among couples who hired each one: venue $12,900 (89% book one), catering $80 per person (85%), photographer $3,000 (88%), wedding dress $2,100 (91%), florist $2,800 (59%), DJ $1,800 (71%), live band $4,500, wedding rings $3,000, alcohol $2,800, videographer $2,300, planner $2,100, cake $540, officiant $260. Not everyone hires every vendor, so adding all categories gives more than $34,000.
- Place (The Knot 2026 averages): Virginia $34,000, same as the nation; New Jersey $57,000 (highest state); Wyoming $17,000 (lowest); New York City $88,000. Off-season (Jan-March, Oct-Dec) $33,200 vs July-Sept $35,400.
- Zola First Look Report 2026 (11,500+ engaged couples, PLANNED budgets): $36,000 average budget, 145 guests; 71% get some family help, 29% pay everything themselves; 32% are "optimizing" credit cards, 4% took a loan; 52% put other goals on hold (savings, buying a home).
- Engagement ring (The Knot 2026): average $4,600, down from $6,000 in 2021 ($5,200 in the 2024 Jewelry & Engagement Study). 61% now have a lab-grown center stone; natural diamond average $7,000 vs lab-grown $4,300.
- The ring "rule" (history as told by Francis-Tan and Mialon, Economic Inquiry 2015): "A diamond is forever" was a late-1930s diamond-company slogan; a 1980s campaign pushed "two months' salary". Before World War II about 10% of Western engagement rings had a diamond; by the end of the century about 80%.
- Who pays (The Knot 2026): parents and family cover 52% of the total cost on average, the couple 48%; 41% of couples went over budget.
- LendingTree online survey (1,050 newlyweds, March 2025): 67% took on debt for the wedding; main way they paid: savings 46%, credit cards 24%, parents 16%, personal loans 11%; 34% felt pressured to overspend to impress guests.
- Federal Reserve (G.19): the average credit card interest rate on accounts that pay interest was 22.15% in the second quarter of 2026.
- "Wedding markup", Consumer Reports 2016: secret shoppers asked 40 vendors in 12 states for the same service for a wedding and for a 50th anniversary party; the wedding was quoted higher in more than a quarter of comparable cases. Also found: built-in gratuities up to 26%, a $7-per-person cake-cutting fee in fine print.
- Albers and colleagues, Journal of Consumer Policy 2021: 47 matched pairs from the same sellers; the "wedding" version cost about 54% more on average (e.g. limousine $600 vs $900; same dyeable shoes $60 for prom, $80 for bridesmaids). Careful: they kept only pairs where prices differed, so it is not an industry-wide markup. Vendors cite more work, customization and liability.
- Fine print (The Knot): venue service charges typically add 20%-25% to the food-and-drink bill; set aside at least $800 for tips.
- Guests pay too. The Knot Guest Study (1,000 adults, 2024): $610 on average to attend; $270 hometown, $1,680 if they fly; gift $150. LendingTree 2024 (2,025 adults): 31% of guests have taken on debt to attend weddings. The Knot Bach Study (2023): $1,300 per bachelorette attendee, $1,500 per bachelor attendee.
- Honeymoon (The Knot): $5,500 average; in 2024, 74% of couples paid it from savings.
- Francis-Tan and Mialon (Emory), Economic Inquiry 2015, online survey of 3,151 ever-married US adults: men who spent $2,000-$4,000 on the ring had 1.3 times the divorce risk of those at $500-$2,000; women whose wedding cost $20,000+ had 1.6 times the risk of those at $5,000-$10,000. High attendance and having a honeymoon (whatever it cost) went with LOWER risk. The authors say it is an association: couples who spend little may simply be better matched.
- Census Bureau 2025: median age at first marriage 30.8 for men, 28.4 for women.
ANGLE: the industry sells one number, the "average wedding", and it hides three things: it leaves out the ring before and the honeymoon after; it is pulled up by big weddings (median $24,000, not $34,000); and the bill is built per head, so every name on the guest list has a price. Around it: the word "wedding" itself can raise a quote, service charges and tips hide in the fine print, the ring "rule" was written by an ad campaign, and the bill spreads to parents, credit cards and even the guests. Follow ONE typical case (a constructed example, say so): a couple around 30 in Virginia (average wedding $34,000, same as the nation), engaged about 14 months, 117 guests, a fall Saturday, a DJ not a band, parents chipping in. Virginia only has a total, so each line uses The Knot's national averages; say so once.

RULES:
- Say once that this is general information, not financial advice. Warm, never mocking: not against weddings, couples or parents who pay. The outrage is at the hidden pricing, never at the people.
- Numbers only from the facts above, source in the sentence. The case is constructed and unnamed; real people appear only as sources. No celebrity weddings.
- Never present $34,000 as what a "typical" couple spends without the $24,000 median. The Knot and Zola survey their own users (not "all Americans"); Zola's $36,000 is a planned budget.
- Vendor prices are averages among couples who hired that vendor; do not say every couple pays all of them.
- Markup: "more than a quarter of comparable quotes" (Consumer Reports 2016, 40 vendors), "about 54% in pairs where prices differed" (Albers 2021). Never "vendors charge double". Give the vendors' side. No vendor named.
- Divorce study: correlation, not cause; say so. Never "an expensive wedding makes you divorce". Do not use the viral "3.5 times": it comes from a small sub-group; the main result is 1.6 times.
- The "two (or three) months' salary" ring rule is a marketing line, not a rule; tell it as history.
- Practical, legal tips at the end: the guest list is the biggest lever (The Knot by size), off-season dates cost less, ask in writing whether service charge and tips are included, watch per-person fees, a balance carried on a credit card costs about 22% a year (Fed).
- Images: no brand logos or recognizable designer packaging on rings, ring boxes, dresses, cars or bottles; no logos of The Knot, Zola or any jeweler; generic venues (barn, church, ballroom).

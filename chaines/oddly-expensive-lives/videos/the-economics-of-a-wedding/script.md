# The Economics of a Wedding

Your wedding can hit $34,000 on paper and still reach $47,860 before the card is paid off. The Knot's 2026 Real Weddings Study, based on 10,474 US couples married in 2025, puts the average wedding at about $34,000. That sounds like the bill. But The Knot says that covers the ceremony and reception only. The Knot puts the ring, rehearsal dinner, and honeymoon at $4,600, $2,800, and $5,500. So the clean average is short by $12,900 before service charges, tips, and credit card interest enter the room. The expensive part is the stack. Somebody is already adding it up.

## The fine print
Put the venue contract under a magnifying glass. These are real possible lines, but they are outside this constructed tab. The Knot's 2026 averages put alcohol at $2,800, a videographer at $2,300, a planner at $2,100, and cake at $540 among couples who hired each one.

Then comes the fine print. The Knot says venue service charges typically add 20% to 25% to food and drink, and couples should set aside at least $800 for tips. Consumer Reports in 2016 sent secret shoppers to 40 vendors in 12 states. Wedding quotes were higher in more than a quarter of comparable cases, with built-in gratuities up to 26% and a $7 per person cake-cutting fee.

Albers and colleagues, in the Journal of Consumer Policy in 2021, found wedding versions cost about 54% more in 47 matched pairs where prices differed. The study covered 47 matched pairs where prices differed, not every wedding vendor. Some of that gap may reflect extra work, timing pressure, customization, and liability.

## Who pays
Now put three payment sources on the board: savings account, parents, credit card. The Knot's 2026 study says parents and family cover 52% of the total cost on average, while couples cover 48%. It also says 41% of couples went over budget.

Zola's First Look Report 2026, from more than 11,500 engaged couples reporting planned budgets, put the average planned budget at $36,000 with 145 planned guests. In that group, 71% expected family help, 29% planned to pay everything themselves, 32% were optimizing credit cards, 4% took a loan, and 52% put goals like savings or buying a home on hold.

LendingTree's March 2025 survey of 1,050 newlyweds found 67% took on debt for the wedding. Their main payment method was savings for 46%, credit cards for 24%, parents for 16%, and personal loans for 11%. Another 34% felt pressured to overspend to impress guests.

The Federal Reserve G.19 report put average credit card interest on accounts paying interest at 22.15% in the second quarter of 2026. ## The guest bill
Return to the guest list. Now every name has two prices: what that guest costs the couple, and what that guest pays to show up. The Knot Guest Study 2024, based on 1,000 adults, found guests spent $610 on average to attend a wedding. A hometown wedding averaged $270. Flying to attend averaged $1,680. The average gift was $150.

LendingTree's 2024 survey of 2,025 adults found 31% of guests had taken on debt to attend weddings. The pre-wedding trips have their own economy. The Knot Bach Study 2023 put bachelorette attendees at $1,300 on average and bachelor attendees at $1,500.

Guests have their own bill, too. Guests pay for clothes, travel, lodging, gifts, and trips, and most of that never appears on the couple's spreadsheet.

## The levers
This is general information, not personal financial or legal advice. The first lever is guest count. The Knot's 2026 averages were $17,100 for 1 to 50 guests, $27,200 for 51 to 100, and $43,300 for more than 100. Our 117 guests at $80 each put food at $9,360.

The second lever is the date. The Knot says January through March and October through December averaged $33,200, versus $35,400 for July through September. The calendar has surge pricing now. Very romantic.

The third lever is fine print. Ask in writing whether service charges, tips, and per-person fees are included. The Knot says service charges can add 20% to 25% to food and drink, and tips need at least $800.

The fourth lever is financing. At that rate, financing can add interest charges on top of the vendor invoices.

The fifth is swapping categories. A DJ averaged $1,800. A live band averaged $4,500.

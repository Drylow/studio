# Recherche et consignes — The Economics of an Ambulance Ride

Faits vérifiés et règles données au script.

VERIFIED FACTS for this video (use them, rounded, with the source named in the sentence; do not invent other statistics):
- Ambulances take about 3 million privately insured patients to emergency rooms each year; local fire and rescue departments and other government agencies run nearly two thirds of those rides; about half of emergency ground ambulance rides for privately insured people involve an out-of-network charge (KFF / Peterson-KFF Health System Tracker).
- FAIR Health (private claims): in 2022, 62.0% of emergency ground ambulance services were out of network (68.3% in 2018).
- Health Care Cost Institute (employer-sponsored insurance, 2022): a typical commercial ground ambulance trip pays a base rate of $718 plus $17 per mile, about 2.0 times Medicare, which pays a $365 base rate plus about $8 per mile; the average employer-insurance ambulance trip is about 5 miles.
- Health Affairs 2020 (Chhabra and colleagues, about 1.5 million ambulance transports): 71% of ambulance rides involved a potential surprise bill; the median potential surprise bill was $450 for a ground ambulance and $21,698 for an air ambulance.
- The federal No Surprises Act (in effect since 2022) protects patients from surprise bills for air ambulances but NOT for ground ambulances; it created a federal advisory committee to study ground ambulance billing instead.
- About 22 to 24 states have their own laws with some protection from surprise ground ambulance bills (Commonwealth Fund / Georgetown CHIR, 2026); in the other states, out-of-network ground ambulance balance bills are generally still legal.
- GAO: the median price of an air ambulance transport in 2017 was $36,400 for a helicopter and $40,600 for a fixed-wing plane, up more than 60% from $22,100 for a helicopter in 2012; more than two thirds of air ambulance transports for privately insured patients were out of network.
- Emergency department visit: enrollees in large-employer plans pay $646 out of pocket on average for an ER visit (Peterson-KFF, 2019 claims).
- Average deductible for single coverage in employer plans: $1,886 in 2025 (KFF Employer Health Benefits Survey 2025); 34% of covered workers have a deductible of $2,000 or more.
ANGLE: calling 911 feels like a public service, like the fire truck, but in much of the US the ambulance sends a bill, and the price depends less on the care than on who owns the ambulance, whether it is in your insurer's network and which state you are in. The federal surprise-billing law skipped the ground ambulance.

## Description

The scariest retirement bill is the monthly gap that comes back after the Social Security check is gone.

This video follows one typical case: a 65-year-old single renter with $0 saved, Medicare starting, and an average Social Security retired-worker benefit. We put the envelopes on the table in order: rent, health care, groceries, transportation, household basics, and the missing $765 a month.

If this is changing how you see the bill, more of them are here: https://www.youtube.com/@OddlyExpensiveLives?sub_confirmation=1

The Economics of Retiring With Nothing Saved is about the real monthly math. The Elder Index from UMass Boston, Social Security Administration figures, CMS Medicare numbers, KFF health spending, GAO retirement savings data, and Harvard housing research all point to the same problem. A basic-needs month can be larger than the average check, before gifts, travel, repairs, or emergencies.

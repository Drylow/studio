**Title:** Why Being a Ronin Was Worse Than You Think
**Script:** `../script-en.txt` — 2,803 spoken words, 89 paragraphs (episode 1: 2,795 words = 19:39, so expect roughly 19:40 with the same voice and speed; measure on the final voice).
**Research date:** 14 October 2026. Every source below was actually opened and read for this episode, except where a line says "seen in search result only".

## Rules applied

- Every number spoken in the script is confirmed by at least two independent serious sources (two different encyclopedias count as two sources; two articles from the same encyclopedia do not).
- A detail found in only one serious source is used **only if it has no number** and the sentence **names the source** ("van Swet found…", "Yamamoto notes…"). These are flagged `single source, attributed` below.
- Kenji, Hana, his father, mother, superior, the merchant and the merchant's son are fictional. The script says so in paragraph 3. Real people (Arai Hakuseki, Yui Shōsetsu, Yamashita Kōnai, Asano, Kira, Ōishi, Terasaka) appear only as historical examples, never as characters in Kenji's story.
- Spoken spellings drop macrons for the voice: Oishi = Ōishi, Shosetsu = Shōsetsu, Konai = Kōnai, Kofu = Kōfu, Ako = Akō, Chushingura = Chūshingura, hoko kamai = hōkō-kamai.

## Evidence, legend and modern interpretation

- **Evidence:** attainder counts, the 1726 Tsuyama halving, the 1651 Keian plot and adoption reform, hōkō-kamai, Hakuseki's dated career, Kōnai's 1721 petition, the Akō dates, the 46 seppuku, Terasaka's survival, Ōishi's surviving ledger, the 1748 play.
- **Estimates:** total ronin numbers (150,000 to 500,000). The script says "estimates" and "nobody counted them reliably".
- **Legend / later retellings:** the lone ronin bodyguard, the ronin as wandering hero (twentieth-century film), the Chūshingura version of the Akō story (renamed, moved to the 14th century). Not used: Ōishi's legendary debauchery in Kyoto as a ruse (contested), snow during the raid.
- **Modern interpretation:** van Swet's argument that most ronin were integrated, not rebels (§5, §78); Smith's reading of Terasaka. Both attributed by name.

## Cut or kept out as uncertain

- **"About 300 Akō retainers" / "321 retainers"**: found only on a non-academic page and a book-listing blurb; not stated. The script only says many pledged and drifted away.
- **Exact Tsuyama dismissals (276 of 630) and relief rice (120 days, 2,845 people, 7,869 koku)**: van Swet only; numbers dropped, facts kept with attribution.
- **"Ronin sold weapons and daughters, some starved"** (Kitahara, early 1600s): single source, early period; not used.

## Notes for images and safety

- Real people are never drawn as identifiable characters (studio rule). Illustrate Hakuseki with a desk, books and a recommendation letter; the Keian plot with an empty Edo street at night and a closed school door; Akō with a ledger, coins, an empty corridor of a generic castle (no recognizable Edo Castle interior), a snowless gate, and a puppet stage for Chūshingura. No blood, no blades drawn, no seppuku imagery: the three seppuku mentions are spoken facts only.
- Kōnai's "demons of hell" notice can be shown as a comic paper notice with drawn oni in striped cotton (no readable text).

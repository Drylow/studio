# Fictional lives — writing contract, revision 2

Owner decision: 15 October 2026. Applies to future Oddly Fictional Lives episodes.
Improve and report this method before starting a new script or production run.
Codex remains the author and final editorial reviewer. This is a manual editorial
contract; it does not claim to enable a new automatic writer in TubeForge.

## 1. Faceless OS is mandatory at every episode

Read the actual files under `skills/facelessos/`, never a remembered summary:

1. `faceless-scripts-os-master.md` and `research-and-ideation-skill.md`.
2. `voice-anchoring-skill.md` and `variety-rotation-skill.md` BEFORE the outline.
3. `retention-mechanics-skill.md` and `script-structures-skill.md` for structure.
4. `visual-scripting-skill.md` for what each passage can actually show.
5. `humanizer-skill.md` and `greenlight-audit-skill.md` for the finished narration.
6. `REAL-FACELESS-HOOK-SWIPE.md` and `NICHE-SPECIFIC-HOOKS.md` for hook comparison.

Use `outro-psychology-skill.md` when planning the close and
`authenticity-audit-skill.md` for a channel/backlog review. Conflicts are resolved
by the user's decisions, then this channel contract, then generic templates.
In particular, do not inherit a long documentary rewind or a universal
1.5-second cutting rule just because a skill includes one.

TubeForge receives clean English narration in `script.txt`. Visual directions,
review evidence, rotation logs and research stay outside the spoken text.
No timestamps are presented as measured before the actual voice exists.

## 2. Benchmark the Saiyan reference properly

Primary reference: Quota Ladder11, **Your Life as Every Rank in the Saiyan Army**:
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Qb-h3lfgW7M . Its real transcript has already been
retrieved privately. Re-open it before using it; do not reconstruct quotes.
Also compare the relevant real openings of the channel's last 2–3 uploads.

Borrow these mechanisms:

- A concrete event exposes the world's rule immediately: a measurement changes
  how others treat the protagonist.
- Progression changes assignments, resources, social treatment and danger.
- A benefit introduces a new cost; strength attracts attention from above.
- The ending pays off the rule planted at the beginning.

Improve on these weaknesses rather than copying them:

- Show the promotion or consequence in an original scene, not only describe
  status, equipment and privileges in succession.
- Give the protagonist choices and recognisable recurring relationships.
- Make the cost different at each stage instead of repeating that someone more
  powerful is watching.
- Distinguish social categories, hereditary status and attainable promotions.
  A soldier does not become royalty merely by completing the previous section.
- Keep the material original: no translated hook, paraphrased section sequence,
  lifted jokes or replacement of franchise names in the reference's sentences.

Views identify useful comparison material, not proof of its retention or a
guarantee that our adaptation will perform. Lore in a competitor transcript is
not a verified canon source. Research the chosen universe independently.

## 3. Research brief before an outline

Write one brief containing:

- **Viewer promise:** one sentence answering what living this identity feels like.
- **Angle:** the specific tension or experience this episode explores.
- **Identity contract:** original protagonist or named canonical protagonist;
  their starting situation, abilities, limits and concrete objective.
- **Canon ledger:** verified setting facts, uncertainties, and explicitly original
  invented characters/events. No invented official ranks, powers or statistics.
- **Visual opportunity:** three different experiences unique to this universe
  that can be shown clearly, plus the ending this opening makes the viewer expect.

If the pitch works unchanged after replacing the universe with another one, it
is too generic. If the plot's daily work dominates the promised fantasy, re-outline.
Ordinary details earn their place when they create humour, contrast or a later
consequence; they are not banned, and action is not a compulsory fight every minute.

## 4. Build a causal experience, not a biography checklist

Choose 5–7 substantial story movements as a working range, not a required rank
count. A movement can be a mission, relationship shift, new ability, loss,
decision or change of living conditions. It must change the protagonist's options.

For every movement record:

| Field | Required answer |
| --- | --- |
| Immediate objective | What does the protagonist want here? |
| Specific obstacle | Who or what prevents it? |
| Visible action | What happens on screen, as one identifiable event? |
| Payoff | What question is actually answered or result achieved? |
| Cost/change | What has become harder, better or irreversible? |
| Next cause | Why does this result create the following scene? |
| New viewing experience | What will the audience see that it has not just seen? |

A conversation can be an action if a deal, threat, deception, discovery or
relationship changes. A standing briefing that restates the previous paragraph
does not count. Changing the background, pose or camera crop does not rescue it.

Check scene joins using Faceless OS's But/Therefore test. Do not literally insert
"but" into every paragraph. Remove an event if cutting it loses no setup,
payoff, necessary information or emotional change.

## 5. The opening must deliver, not lend excitement to a long rewind

Write three genuinely different opening approaches before choosing one. Compare
them with real reference openings and the last three channel openings. Keep the
comparison and selection reasons in the editorial review, outside narration.

Working opening plan, approximate until voiced:

- First sentence: a specific event or problem inside the promised identity.
- First 10–15 seconds: make the identity and immediate stakes understandable.
- By roughly 30–45 seconds: show a meaningful ability, choice, consequence or
  revealing rule of that life. Do not spend the whole opening explaining the hook.
- Within the first 60–90 seconds: deliver a real local result, then create a new
  problem. The viewer has already experienced part of what they clicked for.

These are editorial planning targets, not measured audience thresholds.
Prefer moving forward chronologically. A flashback is allowed after a payoff
if it resolves the active question quickly and makes returning to the present
easy. No urgent cold open followed by most of the episode's origin story before
the advertised event resumes. The existing Sith script's early duel setup and
return around 10:56 is the regression example to catch.

## 6. Retention ledger throughout the body

Use the Faceless OS cadence as a drafting diagnostic:

- New information, changed emotion or a changed situation roughly every 15–30 s.
- A fresh complication/question roughly every 30–45 s.
- An earned small payoff roughly every 60–90 s, followed by the next cause.

These are ranges, not mandatory cliffhanger phrases. Record opening and closing
word offsets for each loop; estimate time using this channel's actual narration
pace. Flag any flat stretch, any repeatedly deferred promise and any sequence
where three passages serve the same function. A deliberate quiet beat may stay
when it changes the emotional reading of what just happened.

The final payoff must answer the opening promise and the protagonist's central
problem. End on that consequence. No long recap, generic lesson or spoken
"thanks for watching" after the story has finished. Do not force every universe
into the same mentor betrayal or the same hollow-power moral.

## 7. Write for this channel's voice and future images

- Second-person lived experience, concrete verbs and natural spoken English.
- Anchor sentence shapes to real narration, not a dramatic imaginary narrator.
- Vary sentence rhythm without cascades of trailer fragments or tidy aphorisms.
- Humour comes from the situation, a reaction or a small consequence, not repeated
  generic sarcastic comments. Do not undermine serious moments with a quota of jokes.
- Make powers and decisions legible: the listener should understand who acted,
  what changed, and why it matters without a lore lecture.
- Every paragraph must earn a new image-worthy event, meaningful reaction,
  revealing detail or emotional pause. Name recurring cast and locations early.
- Keep short visual notes outside narration. Do not append a literal image prompt
  to every spoken sentence or ask the image generator to invent the plot.
- Maintain one recognisable room within its scene. Additional locations come
  from story events, not a quota of new backgrounds. Meaningful inserts preserve
  the established geography and show a specific detail at readable scale.

Draft for 15–16 minutes when that remains the requested runtime, without padding.
Use recent full-script/finished-voice measurements rather than a universal
145-words/minute assumption. The last two completed narrations measured roughly
180–184 words/minute, including pauses; this is an initial calibration only.
About 2,700–2,950 words is a planning range, never a duration guarantee.
Keep the configured Ryan Kurk voice. Confirm actual runtime after synthesis;
align actual spoken words before deciding shot timings. Do not speed up the
audio or add filler merely to pass a nominal duration target.

## 8. Evidence-based review before any production handoff

Run the complete Faceless OS Greenlight groups A–E on the actual final script:

- **A — Hook:** quote sentence one, count its words, identify the turn, compare
  real sourced opening samples, and map every promise to its actual payoff.
- **B — Retention:** keep the loop ledger, locate flat stretches, check causal
  joins, progression and the closing promise. Quote any offending passage.
- **C — Structure:** trace every movement to the episode's central experience.
- **D — Voice/humanizer:** anchor-shape test, spoken read-through, anti-slop and
  repetition sweep. Compare the new opening and plot engine with the last uploads.
- **E — Authenticity:** original scenes and language, documented canon versus
  authored fiction, no reference clone, no generic franchise-name swap.

Also run a **visual feasibility pass**: select three consecutive passages and
describe three different meaningful images they require. If all three become
"two people standing in the same room", fix the story beats before drawing.

Record findings with exact quotations and corrections. Any material revision
requires a fresh full A–E pass. A model saying PASS is not evidence of better
retention. A missing reference or unverified claim remains explicitly unresolved.
Keep audits and visual notes in one `editorial-review.md` per episode rather than
many separate handoff files. Keep `script.txt` clean for TTS.

## 9. Current production gate

This revision updates the method only. It does not approve future episode ideas,
write the six new scripts, start voice/image generation, alter existing uploads
or apply an experimental visual style to the hosted presets.

Next handoff: report this method update to the user; discuss/confirm the next

## 10. Improvement is measured after release

Compare the first 30 seconds, first minute, mean watch time/percentage and local
drop/replay points in YouTube Studio for comparable publication windows and
traffic sources. Separate packaging/CTR from viewing retention. Keep private
channel analytics out of the public repository. Small and different audiences
make early comparisons noisy. Never claim an improvement before those results
exist or interpret a single screenshot as proof of a specific causal diagnosis.

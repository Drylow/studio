Jabba the Hutt drops your first wage onto a tray. Before you can pick it up, his clerk takes it back.

Accommodation. Food. The tunic you're wearing. Apparently, you've already spent your pay. You ask when that changes. The clerk, Venn, turns his datapad toward you and adds the tray to your account. Jabba watches from his stone platform, chewing something you decide not to identify. You've been working for the most powerful gangster on Tatooine for one morning. You already owe him money.

Your name is Tavi. You came here because a freighter captain wanted payment before taking you off this planet. The palace offered regular meals and paid work. You understood those words separately. Venn has arranged them into something much less useful.

He puts a covered bowl on your tray and points toward Jabba. You carry it across the throne room, passing a green guard with a weapon longer than your arm. The guard catches your sleeve before your next step. Beneath your boot, a seam divides the floor. Something enormous moves below it. You hear claws scrape stone, followed by a roar that makes the bowl rattle. The guard pulls you around the edge. Nobody else stops eating.

You deliver the bowl without dropping it. Jabba takes what he wants and waves you away. The guard lets you pass, and you reach the corridor with every part of yourself still attached. Venn is waiting with another delivery. You ask whether the floor has any other surprises. He tells you to stop looking down so much. You're beginning to think the interview left some things out.

Outside, the light hits hard enough to make you cover your eyes. Two suns hang over the courtyard. Nima is securing a supply crate to a battered cargo skiff. She's another palace worker, with a faded scarf wrapped around her neck and dust worked into every fold of her clothes. She sees your tray and asks whether they've charged you for it yet. You tell her yes. She moves a loose crate aside so you can sit down.

Nima once planned to leave after her first payment too. Her account keeps acquiring charges. A damaged crate. A late delivery. Water during a shift that ran through the hottest part of the day. She still knows which ships are leaving Mos Eisley. She checks whenever she makes a run there, then returns with supplies for people who can afford the passage.

Venn comes into the courtyard with a sealed collection token. A parts merchant owes Jabba money. You're to collect it and come back. Nima will drive. You ask what happens if the merchant refuses. Venn looks toward the guard at the gate. You decide to ask the merchant first.

The trip to Mos Eisley is the first part of the job you've enjoyed. The skiff floats above the road, leaving a trail of dust behind it. Nima points out the palace shrinking behind you. You imagine continuing straight past the city, finding a ship and never coming back. Then she asks whether you're carrying the collection token. You check your pocket so quickly that she starts laughing.

Orr's parts shop is crowded with components you couldn't name if somebody offered to pay you. Orr spots your tunic and begins closing a shutter. You put the token on his counter before he can finish. He looks at Jabba's seal, looks at you, and opens the shutter again. For the first time today, somebody seems worried about wasting your time. You straighten your shoulders a little.

He has the payment ready, but asks you to count it in front of him. You do. Orr keeps his own small receipt and insists that you touch the token to his terminal before leaving. You'd rather get back quickly. He says quickly is how a payment becomes somebody else's problem. Nima waits until you've finished. Outside, she tells you to remember that conversation.

Venn receives the money without thanking you. Then he returns a small piece of your wage. You hold it so tightly that its edge marks your palm. In the kitchen, you buy a sweet fried thing from a cook who doesn't ask what your account says. You break it in half and take the other piece to Nima. She eats hers before asking whether you counted the payment yourself. She smiles when you tell her you did, then brushes the crumbs off her scarf.

Over the next few days, people stop handing you trays and start handing you messages. You carry one through a courtyard full of visitors who suddenly make space for your tunic. A pilot who ignored you yesterday offers you a chair. You nearly accept a drink before remembering whose message you're carrying. Being close to Jabba changes how strangers behave around you. It also makes them watch your hands.

One evening, you wait beside the throne while a bounty hunter named Kade presents a captured fugitive. Kade wears a short weathered poncho over his flight clothes. He keeps one hand near the fastening of his holster while Venn counts the agreed payment. The prisoner tries explaining something to Jabba. Jabba keeps looking at Kade. The hunter refuses to leave until the money is in his own hand.

Later, you find Kade checking his equipment near the gate. You ask whether he always gets paid before leaving. He asks whether you don't. You tell him about the account. He glances at your empty hands, then at the palace behind you. Before walking away, he tells you that a promise from this building is much easier to carry than its money.

Venn notices that you can count, keep quiet and return from errands. He gives you a better belt and puts you in charge of a small supply collection. Your first responsibility is a shipment of power cells waiting in Mos Eisley. Nima will load the skiff. You'll confirm the cargo. When you ask whether this means better pay, Venn says it means he trusts you. You wait for the rest of the answer. He has already moved on.

The loading bay contains a low transport, a lifting arm and rows of dark supply crates. Each crate in your collection has a broad yellow stripe. Nima starts moving them aboard while you check the consignment on a datapad. One more crate is listed than the loader brings out. You ask where it is. The loader gestures toward the warehouse and says it will follow on another vehicle.

You remember Orr making you count. You tell the loader you can't confirm the full collection until the last crate arrives. He calls Venn. A moment later, your own communicator sounds. Venn says the separate delivery is already arranged and orders you to bring back what's there. You record what you can actually see. Nima climbs into the skiff slowly. Neither of you likes the speed at which this became your decision.

Halfway across the desert, the rear fastening begins striking the deck. Nima brings the skiff down beside a low ridge. One crate has shifted against the rail. You both push it back while hot dust blows into your eyes. The yellow stripe is scraped where it caught the metal. Nima reties the fastening and makes you pull on it with her before climbing aboard. You thought being in charge would involve less lifting.

At the palace, Venn counts the crates. Then he asks where the missing one is. You remind him about the separate delivery. He says he approved no such arrangement. Nima steps forward. She heard the call. Venn tells her that she was driving and should concentrate on what happened to her vehicle. On his datapad, the full collection appears beneath your name.

You offer him the record you made at the bay. He doesn't look at it. The missing cargo goes onto your account instead. Nima's name appears beside yours. She brought the skiff, so she shares responsibility. For the first time since arriving, you raise your voice. Venn waits until the courtyard falls quiet around you. Then he asks whether you would prefer to explain the shortage to Jabba yourself.

You look through the open doorway toward the throne room. You remember the seam in the floor and lower your voice. Venn takes your collection token. Until the shipment is complete, neither of you is to leave the palace. The guard at the gate receives that instruction while you're standing close enough to hear it. Your little plan to buy passage has become much more expensive.

That night, Nima lays out the things she could sell. A tool. Her spare scarf. A small handful of credits. You put your own payment beside them, but the pile barely covers the bottom of her bag. She tells you the crate was missing before you touched the shipment. You need someone at the palace to accept that, and Venn has already refused. You leave the money where it is and ask whether she remembers the loader's transport.

She remembers a bent side rail and a white patch on its engine cover. You remember Orr's receipt terminal. Somebody at the bay accepted the last crate, even if Venn won't look at your record. You need someone who can get you out of the gate and bring back something Jabba will actually examine. The next morning, Kade arrives to discuss another contract.

You catch him before he reaches Venn. You explain the missing crate and ask what he'd charge to find it. He names a payment that makes Nima quietly gather her things again. You offer him the two workers' combined savings. Kade doesn't move. Then you tell him what the cargo contains, and whose shipment it was. Recovering Jabba's property may earn more than collecting your savings. Now he's listening.

Kade takes you into the throne room. You stay clear of the seam in the floor. He tells Jabba that a piece of his cargo has gone missing and offers to recover it for an agreed reward. Venn begins explaining the workers' carelessness. Kade asks whether Jabba wants a discussion or his power cells. Jabba points at you, then toward the exit. You're going to help find them.

At the gate, Kade shows the guard the recovery token he has just been given. The guard steps aside. Venn follows you into the courtyard and reminds you that you are still responsible. Kade asks him to say the cargo's value aloud so everyone agrees what is being recovered. Venn answers. Nima starts the skiff. You leave before anybody discovers another reason to keep you there.

Mos Eisley feels different when you're looking for something someone wants to hide. Every loading vehicle seems to have a white mark until Nima points out the right one. The bent rail is visible beside a warehouse across the street. Kade stays with the skiff while you enter the loading office. The clerk remembers you. He also remembers you refusing to confirm the missing crate.

He shows you the collection record. Your skiff took the cargo you counted. The remaining crate was released afterward to a different driver, using an authorization sent from the palace. Beside the entry is a destination: a private mooring pad. You ask who authorized it. The clerk looks at Kade waiting outside and decides he would prefer to provide a copy than become part of this conversation.

At the mooring pad, a small freighter is already taking on cargo. You see the yellow stripe as a lifting arm swings a crate toward its ramp. For a moment, you think you've found it easily. Then the driver with the patched engine spots Nima. He drops his communicator into his jacket and runs toward the freighter. Kade leaves the skiff before it has fully settled.

You follow, shouting that the crate belongs to Jabba. The men at the ramp don't seem reassured. One shoves the loading cart into your path. You collide with it, lose your footing and land hard beside the ramp. Above you, the yellow crate hangs from the lifting arm. A fastening slips. Nima pulls you clear just before one corner hits the ground where your hand had been.

Kade takes cover behind the cart as a blaster shot strikes its metal edge. You crawl behind the skiff with Nima. The driver is trying to board the freighter. Kade fires toward the ramp, forcing him back into cover. The ship's pilot starts closing up anyway. Whoever hired these people apparently didn't pay enough to make them wait for one another.

The crate remains outside. Nima brings the skiff alongside it while you hook its lifting strap onto the cargo rail. You both haul, dragging the heavy corner onto the deck. Another shot hits the pad behind you. Kade runs from the cart and catches the skiff's side as Nima accelerates. You grab his forearm until he has both boots aboard. The freighter rises behind you, leaving its abandoned driver shouting below.

You keep hold of the crate all the way into the next street. Only when the buildings hide the mooring pad does Nima slow down. Kade checks the mark where a shot caught his poncho. He tells you that his next agreement will include clothing. You almost laugh, then realise you're still shaking. Beside your boot, the missing yellow stripe is finally where Venn said it should have been.

The cargo seal is intact. Kade checks it against the recovery token, then opens the shipping record from the loading office. The palace authorization carries Venn's personal sign. You expected that. What you hadn't expected was the payment attached to the transfer. He sold Jabba's crate, then charged you and Nima for losing it. If the shipment had left, Jabba would have been paid for the same cargo with your work for years.

Kade calls that an ambitious arrangement. You ask whether Jabba will believe it. He taps the intact seal, then the copied release record. The missing goods are here. Their departure was authorized by the man who reported them missing. Jabba can check both without caring whether he likes you. You sit beside Nima for a moment before asking her to drive back.

You don't want to enter the palace carrying proof that one of Jabba's people stole from him. You also don't want to spend the rest of your life paying for that person's shipment. Nima looks toward the open road beyond Mos Eisley. You could leave the skiff and try to disappear. Kade reminds you that Jabba now knows your names. Nima turns the vehicle toward the palace.

Venn is waiting in the courtyard. His relief lasts until he sees the crate. He starts telling you to leave it with the other supplies. Kade takes the front lifting handle instead. You take the other. Together, you carry the crate past Venn and into the throne room. Nima follows with the loading record held against her chest. This time, you know exactly where to walk.

Jabba inspects the recovered cargo without getting up. Kade presents the seal and asks for the agreed reward. Venn tries to interrupt. Nima puts the release record beside the crate. You explain the separate delivery, the charge against your accounts and the freighter at the mooring pad. You keep it short. Jabba reaches for Venn's datapad before the clerk has finished his answer.

The throne room gets very quiet. Jabba studies the authorization and the sale payment. Then he points at Venn. A guard closes the doorway behind him. You take one step away from the floor seam, and Nima notices. She takes the same step. Venn begins offering to correct the accounts. Jabba is already speaking to somebody else. You don't wait to hear what arrangement Venn is offered.

Kade receives his payment in the courtyard. Before he leaves, you ask him to wait. Your account has been cleared, but you still haven't been paid for recovering anything. He brings you back to the doorway and holds up the token. Jabba turns toward you. For once, you say what you want before someone else describes what you owe.

Payment for the work. Nima's wages too. Passage on the supply freighter leaving Mos Eisley. You expect a roar or a gesture toward the guard. Jabba laughs instead. Kade has returned his property. You have exposed a more expensive problem. A departing worker is apparently cheaper than keeping Venn. Jabba gives an order, and an attendant goes to arrange the transfer.

You check the payment in front of him. Your hands tremble enough that you have to count it twice. Nobody takes the tray. Nima checks the passage confirmation herself and makes sure both names are on it. At the gate, the guard lifts his weapon aside. You look back at the palace long enough to see another nervous worker arriving with a small bag. You tell him to ask what the room costs.

The freighter captain checks your passage and waves you aboard. Nima takes the seat beside yours, still wearing her dusty scarf. You put a credit from your wages on the table between you. She asks whether you're keeping it as a souvenir. You use it to buy two meals before the ship leaves. When the attendant brings them, you wait until she's walked away before picking up your tray.

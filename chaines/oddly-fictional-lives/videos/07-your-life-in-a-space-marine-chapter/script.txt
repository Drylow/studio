You squeeze through the broken window, then realise the Space Marine behind you can't fit.

He's wearing power armour. You're still a Scout, wedged above a street full of armed cultists, with his enormous hand pushing your boot. Your rifle catches on the frame. He tells you to hurry. You'd like to point out that the Emperor hasn't made your shoulder any narrower, but something hits the wall and the conversation becomes less important.

You drop inside, land on a desk and take the desk with you. Through the dust, you see the control lever your squad came to find. You pull it. Outside, a loading gate grinds upward, and the armoured warriors move through the building beneath you. One gives you an approving nod. It feels quite good until your sergeant orders you to find another window.

Your name is Neral. You're already a grown warrior, taller and stronger than the ordinary people who used this building. You've survived the selection and the changes that began turning you into an Astartes. Your equipment is lighter armour, a camouflage cloak and a rifle. A veteran follows you everywhere, making sure you learn what happens before the main force arrives.

Your Chapter is the Cobalt Wardens, a brotherhood of Space Marines with a fortress above the industrial world of Veyr. It follows a familiar command structure, with its own customs and disappointments. If you survive long enough, you'll learn what changes between a Scout entering through a window and the Chapter Master deciding which world receives help.

For now, Sergeant Damos wants the loading gate held open. Your friend Corven is at the upper landing, aiming down a staircase. He's grinning because he saw the desk collapse. You tell him the building attacked first. He laughs once, then gestures toward the movement below. A cultist is running for the gate controls.

You fire into the mechanism beside the man's hand. He ducks away, and Corven drives him back from the stairs. The gate stays open. Your squad's transport crosses the threshold with a wounded warrior inside. Damos arrives and checks the corridor before he checks you. He puts a hand against your shoulder and tells you to keep the route clear.

Inside the transport, an Apothecary bends over the wounded brother. His tools look nothing like your rifle, and the other warriors give him room without being asked. You'll meet specialists whose duties run beside the command ladder. A medic, a Chaplain or a Techmarine doesn't become one by finishing a turn as captain. Each has a different training and a different job.

The Apothecary closes a case and moves the patient inside. You don't know whether he can save him. You do know the gate is still holding, and the transport carrying him is moving. Damos tells you to follow. As you leave, you glance at the window that made the whole route possible. It looks rather small from down here.

Training continues between deployments. You learn to observe without announcing yourself, to follow an order when you're frightened and to report what you actually saw. Damos gives you a firing lane. Corven keeps score until you point out that his own target has disappeared behind the wrong wall. The sergeant makes both of you start again.

When you're accepted as a full battle-brother, the armour comes with attendants who know its fastenings better than you do. A Chapter serf named Eda tightens the last connection at your wrist. You try to lift your arm before she's finished. She catches the fitting and gives you a look that makes you lower it immediately.

The armour closes around you. Servos answer your movement, the weight settles and the room becomes louder through your senses. You raise your hand, turn it and pick up the practice weapon. The grip that felt heavy in the firing lane now sits firmly in your palm. Corven stands beside you in his own new plate, pretending he wasn't waiting to see whether you'd drop it.

Your first operation as a battle-brother takes you back to Veyr's lower manufactorums. There's a blocked water station beneath the factory district. The workers above are running out of clean supply, and the cult has fortified the access doors. Damos wants the pumps working before the fighting traps everyone inside their own workshops.

This time you're supposed to be the figure an ordinary worker sees arriving through the smoke. Eda has prepared your equipment. Damos has given your squad the breach. Corven has found a route across a roof. You step onto it and remember the loading gate. Your armour makes certain problems much easier. Roofs are now among the problems you make for other people.

The roof starts giving way beneath you. You brace against a steel support while Corven crosses the last section. He looks back and stops smiling. You tell him to get onto solid ground. Damos sees the crack travel through the concrete and orders you down by a different route. Your first armoured lesson arrives before you've fired a shot.

At the pump station, you advance behind a squad carrying heavy weapons. Their fire clears the outer barricade. Your squad pushes inside, where the passages are too close for that firing position to help. Fire support, close support and battle line are different battlefield tasks. Moving among them teaches you the force around you; it doesn't turn each weapon into a separate rank.

You find the workers trapped in the control room. Their foreman points toward a pressure valve below the gantry. Someone has chained it open. You cover Corven while he breaks the chain, and the spray falls enough for the workers to cross. Damos brings them to the service exit. One keeps looking back at the machinery. You ask what he's forgotten. He says his brother is still downstairs.

Your squad goes down another flight. The missing worker is pinned behind a fallen cabinet. Corven pushes it aside while you hold the corridor. The man can't walk, so you carry him out. His brother runs to meet you at the service door. Damos watches the reunion and gives you a new position before you can enjoy the thanks. The cult still controls the next block.

Campaigns take you away from Veyr. You return with damaged plate, new brothers and names that no longer answer on the squad channel. Eda replaces a broken seal at your wrist. She remembers fitting the first one. You remember the look she gave you when you moved too soon. You keep your arm still without being told.

Eventually Damos calls you into the armoury and puts a sergeant's insignia on the bench. His damaged leg has taken him away from your squad's next deployment. The captain has chosen you to lead it. Corven smiles when he hears. Then he asks whether the decision can still be appealed. Damos tells him he'll have plenty of opportunities to complain in the field.

As sergeant, you discover your brothers can obey perfectly and still make your plan fail. You place a heavy weapon at a street corner, send Corven to the upper window and bring the others toward a shrine courtyard. An enemy gun opens from behind a statue you thought was solid stone. Your carefully arranged advance stops in the open.

Brother Leth is still moving toward the objective. He hasn't heard your warning through the interference. You run into his path and pull him behind a column. A shot chips the edge beside your head. You point at the upper window. Corven sees the gesture and shifts his fire toward the hidden position.

The gun falls silent long enough for you to move. You take the courtyard, but Leth's armour is damaged and your squad has used more ammunition than expected. At the debriefing, the captain asks why you crossed the street yourself. You show him the interrupted signal. He asks why you didn't arrange a second way to give that order before the shooting started.

You're angry until the next exercise, when you hear your own voice asking the squad to check hand signals. Leth practises them more carefully than anyone else. He hasn't forgotten the column. You put him beside a new brother who doesn't yet understand why a simple gesture matters. Teaching him gives Leth something useful to do with the memory.

Experience can earn you a place among the Chapter's veterans. It gives you equipment other brothers wait a long time to touch, and assignments that explain the waiting. On a boarding operation, you carry a shield into a ship whose corridors are barely wider than your shoulders. Corven follows with the squad. He asks whether you've measured the doors this time.

You reach a sealed bridge. A Techmarine studies the hatch, places a tool against its control housing and tells you where to stand. His red armour is covered in mechanisms you wouldn't know how to repair. You could give a squad an assault order. You couldn't make this hatch open by insisting harder. You keep your shield where he asked for it.

The door releases. A burst hits your shield and pushes you back into Corven. You catch an enemy's blade against the shield rim and drive him away from the controls. Corven covers you while the Techmarine seals the side passage. Your squad takes the bridge before its defenders can destroy the navigation records. When you return, the Techmarine asks who put a dent in his tool case. Corven suddenly becomes interested in the ceiling.

A veteran's honours don't guarantee a command appointment. The Chapter also needs warriors who can hold a position without wanting another chair. You keep training squads until the captain summons you to the strategium. He needs a lieutenant who can direct several units while the company fights somewhere else. He has read your boarding report. Unfortunately, he has also spoken to Corven.

Your lieutenant's assignment starts with separated squads and an unreliable map. A refinery is burning beside an ammunition depot. The captain takes the main force toward the enemy advance and gives you the remaining approaches. On your display, Leth's squad is closest to the fire. Corven is protecting the route the workers are using to leave.

Leth asks permission to push into the refinery. The ammunition depot is still intact, and taking it would make the entire operation easier. Corven reports that the evacuation road is filling faster than his people can clear it. Your first instinct is to send help to both places. Your available force won't stretch that far.

You direct Leth to the depot and move your own command group toward the road. At the bottleneck, a stalled vehicle has blocked the exit. You lift its front end while workers pull the passengers clear. Corven guards the junction. Through your channel, Leth reports that the depot's side door is open and nobody is coming out to defend it.

You tell him to hold. This time you ask what he can see beyond the doorway before you decide it means opportunity. He sends a camera view. Inside, a cable runs along the floor toward a control box. The enemy is waiting for him to enter. Leth withdraws beyond the blast radius, and your heavy team disables the box from cover.

The captain receives an intact depot and a clear evacuation road. He also receives a request from you to replace the vehicles blocking every industrial exit on Veyr. He reads it, looks at the casualty report and approves the recommendation. You leave the room smiling. Corven asks whether you've finally defeated the enemy truck.

Years later, the captain's chair is vacant. His final campaign has left the company short of experienced officers, and you inherit a force whose losses you can still name without a list. Corven joins your command group. Leth takes a squad of his own. In the hall where your appointment is announced, you look for Damos and find his name carved into a memorial instead.

As captain, you have a company to prepare, ships to coordinate and senior specialists who will tell you when a decision risks more than your pride. The Chaplain meets you beside the memorial. His skull-faced helm remains still while you explain your next deployment. He asks what you will tell the brothers who are still waiting to mourn. You arrange the remembrance before the ship departs.

Your next campaign takes you to an orbital defence station. The enemy has disabled its batteries and is boarding through the outer docks. Leth wants to strike directly at the command centre. Corven points to the damaged passages around it. You lay out a route through the service decks, using Scouts to identify which hatches still work.

A young adult Scout sends the first image. An armoured warrior is stuck outside an opening the Scout has already crossed. Corven looks at you. You tell him to keep his observations about ancient history off the command channel. The Scout opens a maintenance hatch, and the main force advances through the larger doorway below.

You reach the battery controls and find the enemy commander behind a sealed blast door. Leth's squad is approaching from the far side, unaware the commander has turned a defence gun into the corridor. Charging would put your own brothers in its line of fire. You call a halt and ask the Scouts which maintenance hatch connects to the gun's loading system.

Corven takes a squad through the hatch while you hold the commander's attention at the door. A burst strikes the wall beside your head. You stay behind the support pillar until Corven reports the loading system disabled. Leth advances, you break through the door and the commander retreats straight into his waiting squad. The station's batteries turn toward the enemy ships outside. Your company has taken them intact.

Back in the fortress, you meet brothers whose old wounds have carried them into a Dreadnought's armoured hull. One was leading operations before you wore a Scout's cloak. That condition is no promotion on your way to command. The voice from inside the machine asks how the station held. You describe the recovery. He asks why you haven't repaired Veyr's old loading gate yet.

By the time the Chapter Master dies, you've lived through generations of the ordinary people who maintain your fortress. Eda's place in the armoury belongs to a woman named Ora. She knows the same wrist fitting and has heard several versions of the desk story. Corven claims that the desk was smaller every time you told it. You tell Ora to believe the maintenance records.

The senior officers gather after the Chapter Master's funeral. Your service makes you a candidate, not an automatic successor. Other captains have victories, loyal companies and arguments of their own. They discuss the coming campaigns and the condition of the Chapter. When they choose you, the responsibilities arrive before anyone changes your armour.

The first requests come from different worlds. Veyr needs protection for its orbital approaches. A nearby system reports an enemy force gathering beyond its stations. You have damaged ships in the dock and companies still replacing their losses. The Chapter Master can decide where the Chapter goes. That authority doesn't produce another company when the room falls silent.

You call the captains together and ask for the state of each force. You bring in the officers responsible for the ships and the armoury as well. Ora presents a repair schedule with a mark beside the vessel you wanted to send first. You ask what the mark means. She says its launch mechanism will work perfectly, provided you don't intend to bring anyone back.

You change the deployment. One force secures the nearby system's evacuation route while another takes repaired ships to Veyr. You send Scouts ahead of both. Corven looks at the assignments, raises a question about the reserves and waits while you work through it. He's still willing to disagree with you in a room full of armour.

The first report from Veyr reaches you while the ships are preparing to leave. A factory district has lost contact with its water station. The upper access is blocked, but a Scout team has found an opening above the loading gate. Their sergeant asks whether they should wait for the main force. You look at the image and recognise the room.

The desk has been replaced. The window hasn't become any wider. You authorise the entry and ask the sergeant to keep someone at the gate controls. Across the display, a little green signal moves through the opening. You stay beside it until the larger signals begin to follow.

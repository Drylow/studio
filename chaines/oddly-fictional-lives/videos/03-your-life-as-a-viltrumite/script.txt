Your commander orders you to let go of the bridge while people are still crossing it.

You're holding the broken span above the water. On the road above you, Mira is waving the last vehicle forward. Commander Rusk is waiting for you to obey. You came to this planet to bring it into the Viltrum Empire. But the people crossing the road you're holding have spent months calling you their protector. Getting them across will mean fighting the man who sent you here.

Your first landing during training ends with you crashing through a storage door.

Your name is Varen. The training court has a high stone wall, a landing square, and an instructor who moves the square whenever you start getting comfortable. You can already lift things that would crush an ordinary person. Staying in the air is harder. Your approach carries you straight past the landing square. You get up from the broken doorway before Rusk reaches you, hoping the dent looks smaller from where he stands.

He asks you to land again. The second time, you arrive low and fast. Your boots tear across the square. Rusk makes you look at the mark they leave. A strong body is useful to the Empire. An uncontrolled one can destroy the equipment it was supposed to capture.

Talen trains beside you. He wears a blue sash and laughs whenever you try to pretend a bad landing was intentional. His own turn ends with him catching the wall with his shoulder. You help him down, and he tells you that your storage door now looks quite reasonable. Rusk watches both of you from the steps. He gives you another drill before either of you can finish laughing.

The drills get heavier. You carry a damaged transport frame across the court without crushing its mounting points. Talen flies beside you and calls out which corner is slipping. You try correcting everything at once. The frame tilts, and a support folds under your grip. Rusk stops the drill. He wants that frame useful when you put it down.

You begin slowing your hands before the rest of your body. On the next attempt, you let Talen settle his side before lifting yours. The frame reaches the square intact. Rusk checks the joints and moves on without praising either of you. Talen waits until he's gone, then taps the straight support with his knuckle. You both know what changed.

Later, you're sent out with an imperial transport. Rusk briefs you while a planet turns on the display behind his shoulder. Your assignment is a coastal region on Lethra, a world with working ports and cities the Empire wants usable. You're to learn its defences and prepare it for imperial control. Rusk puts the mission record into your hand before asking whether you understand.

You understand the parts about flying down, surviving, and sending reports. You have practised those. You haven't practised living among the people whose factories and roads you are listing as assets. Talen is assigned elsewhere. He presses his blue sash flat beneath his harness and tells you to keep your reports clear. He expects you to meet again when the region is ready.

The city below your landing site is built around a harbour. Cranes move cargo beyond a narrow bridge. Low houses climb the hill behind them. You touch down outside the freight yard and leave a shallow crack in the concrete. A worker notices the crack before he notices you. He looks at your boots, then at the space above the gate where you appeared.

You ask where the regional offices are. He points across the water. Before you reach them, a cable snaps above the yard. A suspended load tips toward the workers below. You catch it because it is falling into your path. Your hands close around the frame, and people scatter beneath you while its loose corner swings against the crane.

The load is heavier than the training frame, but its supports are softer. You feel one begin to buckle. You shift your grip and set the whole thing down on the clear part of the yard. Nobody is under it when it lands. A woman in a yellow work jacket runs toward the crane controls instead of toward you. She cuts the damaged line before it can pull another piece loose.

That is how you meet Mira. She checks the frame you put down and asks whether your wrists hurt. You tell her they don't. She asks whether you can lift that much every time. You say you can lift more. She considers the damaged crane, then points toward a service shed. Her crew could use someone who doesn't need the lifting equipment they've just lost.

You take the offer because it gives you access to the yard. Mira gives you a pair of jobs she expects to take all afternoon. You finish moving the first stack before she has found the replacement bolts. She changes the second job. Instead of carrying the entire assembly, you hold it steady while her crew repairs its underside. You have to wait while other people work.

Waiting turns out to be part of most days here. A mechanic has to line up a bearing before you lower a crane arm. A driver has to clear his cab before you straighten the bent door. Mira checks the ground before asking you to lift a container. If you ignore those pauses, somebody can be hurt by the piece you are trying to move.

Your first report lists a working harbour, a narrow bridge and limited defences against an attacker who can fly. Rusk asks you to inspect the road into the hill district. You tell him you have already walked it. He asks why you were walking. You describe the people who followed you after the crane rescue. They had expected you to stay long enough to answer their questions.

Rusk tells you to remember your assignment. He wants measurements and access routes. The next morning, Mira hands you a list of broken equipment. The bridge is on it. Freight vehicles keep crossing a damaged expansion joint, and her crew cannot replace its support while traffic shakes the deck above them. You say you can hold the span still.

Mira takes you under the bridge before agreeing. She shows you which beam carries the road and which pieces only keep the railings upright. You put your hands where she directs. Above, a lorry passes over the joint. You feel its weight move through the beam. Mira watches the gap beneath its mounting plate and asks you to keep that pressure steady while she bolts the replacement into place.

By the time the work is finished, your arms ache. Mira's crew has been taking breaks around you. You couldn't let the beam move while someone was beneath it, so you stayed. When you finally stand on the road again, a driver gets out and offers you a drink. You take it while Mira checks the joint from above. The bridge still needs work, but vehicles can cross without hammering the old support.

You mention the repair in your next report. Rusk asks whether the bridge can carry imperial equipment. You say it can. He marks it as an access point. You remember the driver handing you a drink beside that same joint. Before the transmission ends, Rusk tells you that an inspection team will arrive when the harbour facilities are ready.

You keep returning to the workshop. Mira gives you a locker and asks you to stop leaving loose tools near the landing area. Your arrivals can send them rolling across the floor. You learn to come down beside the shed rather than in front of its door. The crew stops looking up whenever you enter. Someone eventually asks you to pass a wrench without adding anything about your strength.

During a storm, the hillside road slips. Mira's crew heads out with temporary barriers while you lift a fallen tree from a van. The driver is trapped by a bent panel. Pulling it straight would move the whole van toward the broken verge. You hold the chassis against the road instead. Mira frees the door from the other side, and the driver crawls onto the part of the road that still supports him.

He wants you to fetch his bag. You check the verge before moving the van again. Rusk would call the delay unnecessary. The driver is already clear, and a damaged vehicle has little value. You retrieve the bag anyway. The man holds it against his chest while Mira leads him toward the waiting crew. He thanks you before asking whether anyone has reached the houses farther up the hill.

You spend the night carrying supplies past the broken road. On your last return, Mira is asleep beside the workshop table with a wet jacket folded beneath her head. You put the remaining supplies beside the door and sit down. Your mission record is waiting in the locker. You haven't told Rusk how much time the storm cost you.

When you do report, you describe the hillside as temporarily inaccessible. You leave out the people moving supplies. Rusk asks whether the population depends on the harbour bridge. You tell him it does. His next questions concern the workshop, the emergency stores and the route out of the city. You know why those details matter to him. You have helped the residents use every one of them.

Mira notices you watching the harbour instead of the repair in front of you. She asks whether someone is coming. You tell her there will be an inspection. She asks what they will inspect. You look at the mission record on the table. She reaches for it before you can decide how to explain. Its map shows the city divided into useful facilities and routes to secure.

She reads the marked bridge, then the freight yard. She asks whether that was what you were doing when you caught the crane load. You tell her why you landed here. She keeps reading until she reaches the regional surrender order. Then she puts the record down and calls her crew away from the workshop entrance. You ask her to wait. She asks what part of the record you expect her to wait for.

You let her leave with it. That is the first thing you do after she learns the truth. You could have taken it from her hand. You watch her cross the yard with the information Rusk wanted you to collect. The crew members beside her keep their distance from you. Later, she returns for the tools her team needs. She asks you to stand clear while they load them.

The inspection transport arrives before the workshop is empty. Rusk steps out with Talen behind him. Your old training partner still wears the blue sash. He looks around the yard, notices the workers leaving and asks what happened. Rusk answers before you do. The local population has become restless, and your reports have stopped matching your behaviour.

Rusk wants the regional officials brought to the harbour. He intends to use the bridge crossing to separate them from the residential district. You tell him the road is carrying people displaced by the storm. He says that makes them easier to gather. Mira is already guiding vehicles away from the inspection area. You see her yellow jacket moving between them while Rusk describes the order he expects you to enforce.

You tell him the bridge isn't fully repaired. He calls it an excuse and flies toward the road. Talen stays beside you. He asks whether your report was false. You tell him the facilities are real and the people depend on them. Rusk lands near the damaged joint before you finish. A vehicle brakes beside him. Drivers behind it begin getting out.

Mira tries to move the first vehicle past him. Rusk reaches beneath the damaged joint and tears out its temporary brace. The deck drops where the brace was holding it. You get beneath the span before it tears away. Your hands find the beam Mira showed you, and its weight settles across your shoulders. Above, a driver stumbles back into his cab. Mira waves him toward the intact approach.

Now Rusk gives the order from your first moment on this bridge. Let it fall. He wants the vehicles stopped and the people waiting where he can control them. You tell him to move away from the road. He asks whether you understand what you have just said to your commander. You watch the next vehicle reach the safe end. Mira is still directing traffic. You keep holding the beam.

Rusk hits you before the road is clear. Your grip slips, and the deck tilts. You catch the beam against your forearm while the broken railing slides into the water. Talen catches the opposite support. You shout for him to hold his side still. He looks from Rusk to the vehicles, then plants his hands beneath the steel. You both know this part from the training court.

With Talen supporting the span, you can let go long enough to meet Rusk. His first blow sends you into the freight-yard wall. You get up with pieces of masonry falling around your boots. He has fought longer than you, and moving straight toward him gives him the contact he wants. When he comes again, you turn aside and pull him above the empty part of the yard.

He breaks your grip and drives you down through a storage roof. You land among spilled crates. A worker is behind the shelving at the far wall. You push the fallen roof panel away from him before Rusk reaches the opening. The pause costs you another hit. Mira opens the side door from outside, and the worker gets out while you keep Rusk facing you.

You climb out through the damaged roof and draw Rusk toward the rocky headland. He follows because he expects you to run. You keep him above the water until the harbour is behind both of you. There, you stop turning away from his attacks. The next collision tears rock from the cliff. You hold him against the stone long enough to see the inspection transport lifting from the yard.

Talen is at its controls. Mira's vehicles are already moving across the road he held in place. The transport rises beside the broken bridge instead of over the town. Rusk sees it and pulls free. He turns toward Talen. You catch his boot before he clears the headland, and both of you fall against the lower shelf. The transport continues inland while you keep him below the ridge.

You stay until you can no longer see the transport. Rusk tries to rise again, and you meet him at the cliff edge. When he finally stops moving toward the city, you leave him on the broad stone shelf and fly back alone. Your suit is torn at the shoulder. You land badly in the yard and cannot make your hands stop shaking. The workshop door is open, but nobody is inside.

Mira has left a note beneath the locker latch. It names the inland freight depot where her crew took the stranded residents. You find them in the repair shed behind it. Talen is sitting against a crate with one arm held close to his body. He tells you the transport will have to stay here. Taking it back means explaining why he was flying civilians away from Rusk's inspection.

Mira lets you sit beside the entrance while her crew checks the road. She gives you a dry cloth for the cut beneath your sleeve. You tell her Rusk survived. She says that means they need to keep moving. Nobody treats the fight as a victory large enough to solve the invasion. They ask which routes the Empire knows, and you answer from the map you helped make.

You and Talen move the transport beyond the depot before the next morning. Mira takes its navigation record and keeps the vehicle routes separate from it. You carry supplies to a shelter Rusk never saw. When you return, she asks you to wait outside while the residents decide whether they want you nearby. You stay by the path until she opens the door.

The days after that are ordinary in ways you never trained for. You help repair a pump without pulling its pipes from the wall. Talen watches the approach while you work. Mira measures a support before asking you to lift it, and you wait for her crew to move clear. You still know how to seize the place. For now, you stand where she points and hold the beam until the bolts are tight.

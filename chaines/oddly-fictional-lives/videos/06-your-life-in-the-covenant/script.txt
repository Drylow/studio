You trade a holy relic for a fresh breathing tank.

You're an Unggoy, the small alien humans call a Grunt, crouching behind a fallen pillar with a cracked methane hose. Across the chamber, a silver object floats above a stone pedestal. Your commander, a blue-armoured Elite named Rhel, points at it again. Apparently the ancient gods can wait for you to suffocate, but they can't wait for someone else to carry their luggage.

You pull the hose against your chest until the leak slows. The commander sees you move and mistakes it for enthusiasm. Before he can give you another order, you reach the pedestal and find a problem neither of you expected. The relic is attached to the floor.

Your name is Pip. Your orange armour doesn't stop much, and the tank on your back makes hiding behind anything an argument with geometry. You wedge yourself beneath the pedestal, turn a handle and hear something unlock. The silver object drops into your arms. Underneath it, a narrow beam of light shoots straight through the ceiling.

That gets everyone's attention. The commander drags you behind the pillar just as human gunfire crosses the doorway. He wants the relic intact. You'd settle for your lungs. You point at his spare supply cylinder, then at the object in your hands. For a moment he looks offended that you've noticed an opportunity. He gives you the cylinder.

You leave the chamber breathing properly, holding something a great deal more important than yourself. At the transport ramp, your friend Bap tries to touch it. You slap his hand away with more authority than you've ever had. The commander tells him to help you carry it. Bap stares at you. You've been useful for half a minute and you're already unbearable.

Back aboard the ship, the relic goes through a guarded door. You go through a smaller one beside the engine machinery. The Covenant has brought different alien peoples into the same enormous religious empire. It hasn't given them the same lives. Your place in it comes with methane equipment, crowded quarters and people who expect you to stand where they're pointing.

You can rise among the Unggoy. Survive assignments, learn your equipment, become someone your own kind follows. You won't grow into an Elite, a Brute or a Prophet. Those are different species. Above you, this chain runs through warriors, ship commanders and the Prophets directing the war. You'll live inside different bodies to follow it. First, though, you have a promotion to survive.

Your next deployment gives you red armour and a group of frightened Grunts who keep asking you which direction the humans are. You remember asking that yourself. The commander says you're responsible for keeping them moving. Bap asks whether red armour comes with a bigger ration. You tell him you'll investigate immediately.

The landing goes badly. A blast knocks a supply container across the ramp and your group bunches behind it. Someone starts running before you've seen anything to run from. You catch Bap by his tank and push him against the wall. He complains until a shot hits the place his head occupied. After that, he listens.

You lead them through a drainage passage too low for the tall warriors. At the far end, you find the human transmitter that has been calling shots onto your landing site. Bap cuts its power cable while you cover the opening. The fire outside becomes less accurate. Your group comes back carrying the transmitter instead of losing half its members beside the ramp.

Your commander takes the device without learning any of their names. Your group learns yours. They leave space beside you at the feeding station. For the first time, you've got people who look relieved when you enter a room. Then you hear the relic has started transmitting again. The tall warrior who receives that report is about to inherit a much more expensive problem.

Now you're Rhel, the Sangheili Minor Pip negotiated with in the ruin. Humans call your people Elites. Your blue armour has energy shields, your weapon fits your hands, and Pip has to look up a long way to complain to you. You're standing in the same launch bay, watching a white light pulse through the sealed relic container.

You believe the Forerunners built the path to the Great Journey. Recovering their works is a sacred duty. That's what you were taught, and it's easier to believe aboard a warship whose walls hum with technology older than your civilisation. It's less comfortable when the crate starts calling human aircraft toward your position.

The Major above you orders a landing near the original ruin. You take Pip because he opened the pedestal, and Bap because Pip refuses to stop arguing until you do. Bap thinks this proves he's valuable. Pip tells him it proves someone should carry the replacement tank. They argue all the way down.

On the surface, your shield catches the first burst from a human emplacement. You feel the impact and keep moving. Pip is flat behind a rock. You order him forward, then look at the narrow strip of open ground between you. Your armour has just survived something his body won't. You move behind a larger stone and give him a covered route instead.

He gets across. Bap follows with the equipment, making considerably more noise than the situation requires. Together you reach an outer console, where the relic's light reveals the outline of a passage under the floor. Pip gives you a triumphant look. The passage is also large enough for the humans approaching from the other entrance.

You wanted a chance to distinguish yourself. You've found a corridor with no cover and an object everyone wants. Your Major tells you to hold it while he reports success. You can hear him leaving. Pip asks whether success always sounds like somebody else's transport taking off.

You overturn an empty cargo cradle and brace it across the passage. Bap drags a power pack behind it. You cover their work, firing only when a human silhouette reaches the doorway. The improvised barrier holds long enough for your own transport to return. You bring the relic back without pretending the corridor is secure.

Your Major doesn't appreciate that detail in your report. The shipmaster does. He asks who kept the passage open. Pip points at you before you can answer, then points at himself. The shipmaster permits him both gestures. You're pleased about the first one. Pip spends the rest of the day reminding you about the second.

Years of service follow that mission. Your harness changes from blue to red, and now you're the Major beside the landing display. A young Minor named Voro waits for your orders. He keeps glancing at your energy sword. You remember doing that. You also remember the warrior who left you in the corridor.

Your first command brings you back to a Forerunner site on another world. The relic has revealed a route into its lower chambers. A detachment of Kig-Yar scouts comes back with a damaged sensor and a demand for payment. Their leader points out a human position your official map hasn't bothered to show. You pay attention to the position before arguing about anything else.

You divide the approach. Voro takes a covered ridge with the rifle team while you bring Pip through a service opening. You're giving orders that put other bodies at risk, and every answer comes back in a familiar voice. When Voro says the ridge is exposed, you hear the same pause you once left in your own reports.

You stop the advance and look through his camera. The human gun is above his position, hidden beneath a broken roof. Your first plan would have put him directly underneath it. You send a transport around the far side to draw its aim, then let him move. Voro reaches the upper door and opens it from inside.

Inside, the relic fits a socket that looks like a receiving hand. Light travels across the chamber and resolves into the outline of a vast ring. Pip admires it for a moment before asking whether it's somewhere with better quarters. You tell him to stay back. An alarm on the wall has changed colour.

You call for a withdrawal while the floor is still holding. Voro brings his team through the upper door. Pip hesitates beside the socket, afraid you'll leave the relic. You pull him away as a circular opening closes over it. The chamber seals behind you, taking your prize and leaving the entire unit alive outside.

Your superiors ask why you've returned empty-handed. You show them the recording, pausing on the ring and the closing door. Nobody asks about the soldiers until the shipmaster reaches that part himself. He orders the recording sent upward. What you thought was a field failure has become something a ministry wants to own.

An Ultra's white armour comes after a longer record than a successful afternoon. By the time you wear it, Voro no longer needs you to explain where cover is. You've earned the respect of warriors who once checked your orders twice. You've also learned that experience doesn't give you command over every specialist who walks into the room.

A Zealot arrives with a separate mandate to recover the relic. His armour and escort tell everyone he expects compliance. This is an order with its own religious authority, not simply the next automatic promotion above your harness. He can demand access you can't grant yourself. You can point at the door that nearly buried your unit.

He asks for the warrior who opened the first pedestal. Pip stands very still behind you. You explain that Pip needs functioning breathing equipment and a route out. The Zealot looks at the little red-armoured figure, then at your face. He agrees to both conditions. Pip gives you a small nod and immediately ruins it by asking for food as well.

The next operation starts with everyone understanding the chamber. Pip turns the socket while Voro holds the upper doorway. The Zealot uses a separate control you hadn't recognised. The floor opens beneath the relic, revealing a smaller cradle. This time the silver object lifts free without sealing the room.

Pip hands it to you and grins behind his mask. The Zealot takes it before you can enjoy the moment. He has the authority to decide where it goes. You have the authority to get your people back to the transport. You exercise yours, and Voro walks down the ramp beside you instead of being carried.

More campaigns pass before you're entrusted with a ship. Your armour is ceremonial gold now, but the change you feel first is the distance. On the bridge, you can watch a battlefield without hearing anyone breathe. An officer can reduce Voro's entire landing party to a small cluster of lights.

As shipmaster, you can move the vessel that used to leave you waiting on the ground. The first time you order a transport launch, Pip is aboard it. He's older, louder and convinced that knowing you should get him a better cabin. You give him a repaired tank and tell him to stop filling the corridor with his possessions.

The recovered relic is travelling with you to the fleet. Its signal has attracted a human vessel into the system. Your bridge crew wants to pursue. The ministry's observer wants the relic delivered without delay. Voro reports that his surface team hasn't reached the pickup zone. All of them are waiting for the same ship to go somewhere different.

You turn toward the pickup zone and launch a separate transport for the relic. The observer calls that an unnecessary division of strength. You ask whether he wants to hold the container himself while you complete the recovery. He returns to his seat. On the display, the human vessel starts closing the gap.

Your shields absorb the first attack while the returning transport crosses behind your hull. The bridge shakes hard enough to throw the observer against his console. Voro's signal appears inside the docking field. You order the jump as soon as the bay seals. The human ship disappears from the forward view, and your bridge crew finally lets itself move again.

You've saved your landing party and delivered the relic. Your hull also needs repairs. The fleetmaster who receives you is going to decide which of those facts belongs at the top of the report. This time, leave Rhel on his bridge. You're about to sit in the chair that evaluates him.

As fleetmaster, you're responsible for ships you can't see from your own window. Rhel's damaged vessel appears beside others requesting fuel, escorts and orders. The silver relic has become a priority assigned from above. You don't get to decide whether the mission is sacred. You decide which captains will be close enough to pay for it.

You listen to Rhel describe the rescue. You also read the damage report. Sending him out again immediately would give you another available ship on the display. It would leave him with a shield system his crew hasn't finished repairing. You grant him time in the dock and assign the escort elsewhere. Somewhere below your bridge, a repair crew receives a much shorter working day than it expected.

That decision costs you an escort at the next assembly. A San'Shyuum official asks why the procession has changed. You explain the repairs. He asks why the arrival couldn't have been delayed instead. Every answer you've given moves the inconvenience toward somebody with more rank. The official decides he should speak to that person directly.

Now you're inside the religious court, a San'Shyuum Prophet in a hovering chair. You've never been Rhel, and you've never had Pip's body. Your species occupies a different place in the empire. The delegates below you have brought the recovered relic, a damaged ship's report and an argument about who should receive credit.

Your attendants place the silver object where you can reach it. You see an opportunity for a public ceremony. A divine discovery reassures the faithful and strengthens the ministry that announces it. Your interpreter asks whether the ring shown by its signal has been located. You tell him to prepare the ceremony while the fleet continues its search.

Outside the chamber, Sangheili guards stand beside Jiralhanae warriors who are receiving more attention from the court. The Brute captain makes a point of taking the position nearest your door. The Elite guard doesn't move. Your attendants ask whether you want the dispute settled. You let them wait while you decide whose support will be more useful.

That tension belongs to the Covenant's real breaking point. When the alliance tears apart in the Great Schism, a place in the command chain becomes a question about which side is giving the order. The empire's long war and its religious promises can no longer keep those orders pointing in the same direction.

Back on Rhel's ship, a transmission instructs the crew to admit a Brute boarding party. Voro is at the hatch. Pip is unloading fresh cylinders beside him. Rhel hears the order from his bridge and sees armed figures waiting beyond the docking seal. The insignia on their transport is familiar. Their weapons are already raised.

He orders Voro away from the hatch and closes the inner bulkhead. The boarding party cuts through the outer door into an empty compartment. Pip drags the last cylinder clear before the lock engages. On the bridge, Rhel turns his ship away from the command vessel whose signal he has followed for years.

The fleetmaster calls for obedience. The ministry calls it treason. Voro calls to say everyone is clear of the docking corridor. Rhel uses that report to decide when to break away. A damaged section tears from the boarding clamp, and the stars begin moving across the forward window.

Pip comes onto the bridge carrying the spare cylinder Rhel traded him for a relic years ago. Its casing is dented, its valve has been replaced and someone has painted a rude face on the side. He puts it beside the command chair. Rhel asks why he kept it. Pip says it was the first time someone important gave him enough air to get home.

The sacred container is still locked in the ministry's chamber. Pip is standing on your bridge, breathing. You give him an empty seat while the ship turns toward open space.

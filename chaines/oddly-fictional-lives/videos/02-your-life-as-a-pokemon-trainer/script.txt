Your first Pokemon battle ends with you carrying Charmander into a Pokemon Center. The trainer who beat you is already on his way to the next town. You're holding your partner's tail clear of your jacket, trying to remember whether its flame looked this small before the fight. You wanted eight badges and a shot at the Kanto League. But right now, you'd settle for Charmander opening its eyes. That trip to the Champion starts here, with you waiting outside the treatment room.

The nurse takes Charmander from your arms and asks what happened. You explain the battle against Squirtle, the water attack, and the command you gave after Charmander stumbled. She asks why you didn't call it back. You have an answer ready about getting through the opponent's defenses. It sounds ridiculous while she checks your Pokemon. She takes it through the doorway, and you sit down with an unused Potion in your pocket. You packed it that morning. You forgot it existed when someone challenged you.

Through the waiting-room window, you can see the road leaving Viridian City. Other trainers pass with partners walking beside them. Your map is spread across your knees, with Pewter City circled. You were supposed to get there before your rival, Milo. Eventually, the nurse comes back. Charmander will recover. You get to bring it home with you, and she suggests learning when to stop a battle before starting another one. Charmander reaches for your sleeve when you pick it up. You stay until it can walk without leaning against your leg.

Earlier that morning, choosing Charmander had felt easy. Professor Oak had shown you the starter Pokemon at his lab in Pallet Town. Squirtle had inspected Milo's shoe. Bulbasaur had settled beside the table. Charmander had tried to climb onto your chair, missed, and looked at you as if you were responsible for moving it. You held out a hand. It grabbed your fingers and hauled itself up. You pictured that little orange Pokemon growing into a Charizard, and agreed to take care of it without having much idea what that involved.

Oak gave you a Pokedex, and Milo challenged you almost as soon as you left. You'd watched enough battles to know what the commands sounded like. Unfortunately, knowing when to shout an attack didn't make you good at choosing one. Squirtle stayed out of reach, and you kept sending Charmander forward. Milo won, asked if you were all right, and left while you were still pretending the loss didn't bother you. Once Charmander tried to take another step, you finally noticed how tired it was. That's how your journey reached the Pokemon Center before it reached its first Gym.

Your second attempt at leaving town is less impressive. Charmander walks beside you, and you stop when it stops. You practice a recall with its Poke Ball before you attempt another battle. It takes you a while to get the timing right. At a fork in the path, Charmander waits for a command and you realize you've been studying the map instead of watching where you're going. You start making your plans before setting off. You also put the Potion somewhere you can reach without emptying every pocket.

By evening, you've discovered several things the televised battles leave out. Charmander wants food while you're still pitching the tent. A Pokemon with a flame on its tail needs a sensible place to sleep, away from the fabric. Your shoes are rubbing, and the town you thought was nearby is farther away than it looked on the map. You spend part of the night checking whether your partner is comfortable. In the morning, it wakes you by sitting on your chest. Apparently, you're getting better at the job, because it expects breakfast now.

On the way north, you try to catch a Pidgey. Your first throw lands beside it. Pidgey looks at the ball, looks at you, and flies into a branch you can't reach. Charmander follows it with its eyes while you retrieve the ball from the grass. Later, you find another Pidgey near a clearing and take the time to watch it. You give Charmander a short command, stop the battle before it goes too far, and make your throw while the bird is close. When the ball settles, you check it instead of celebrating over it.

Now you're responsible for a Pokemon that can leave through the top of your campsite. Pidgey needs room to fly, and Charmander dislikes being startled by something landing behind it. You spend a training session trying to get both of them comfortable around each other. Pidgey steals a piece of Charmander's food. Charmander snaps at it. You separate the bowls and wonder how anyone manages a larger team. By the time you reach Pewter City, you've started to understand your partners. Brock is about to show you how much work remains.

His Gym has a rock-covered battlefield and an Onix that makes Charmander look very small. You knew Brock used Rock-type Pokemon. You came anyway because you wanted to see whether enough determination would solve the matchup. Charmander's fire leaves Onix ready to continue. You call another attack, see Charmander struggling to find space, and reach for its ball. You withdraw it while it can still stand. Brock wins. You leave with an empty badge case, but this time your partner walks out beside you instead of being carried to the nurse.

Brock suggests building a team that can handle more than one kind of opponent. You find a Poliwag at a pond during your training outside town. Catching it takes patience, and getting it to work with you takes longer. It knows how to move in water. Your directions on dry ground confuse it. You practice short movements beside the bank, keep the battles brief, and watch how it responds to your voice. Charmander waits nearby, then joins in. You'd worried about replacing your starter. It seems quite happy to let someone else get wet.

When you challenge Brock again, you begin with Poliwag. A water attack gives you an opening, but you still have to call it before Onix closes the distance. You move your Pokemon back, let it settle, and try again. Charmander watches from beside your boot after its own turn in the fight. You win the Boulder Badge and turn it over in your hand while Brock talks. At the Center, you open the case to show Charmander. It sniffs the badge and goes back to eating. You'd imagined a more emotional response.

Milo is waiting near the next town with a badge of his own. You want to tell him exactly how you earned yours. He wants a battle. This time, you ask for a chance to rest first, and he agrees. When you finally face each other, you use Pidgey to give Charmander a break and make Milo adjust his plan. You still lose, but you understand the loss while it's happening. You saw the change he made, just too late to answer it. That evening, you write down the moment instead of blaming your Pokemon.

Your next badge comes from Misty in Cerulean City. Her Water-type team means Charmander spends part of the match watching you rely on someone else. Pidgey has to keep moving, and you have to resist giving a new instruction every time you're nervous. Later, an Electric-type opponent makes that same bird a poor choice. You're beginning to prepare for the Pokemon in front of you. Training sessions change with the next Gym, and so does the order you send your partners out. You keep the badge case closed while you're deciding.

After enough practice and battles, Charmander evolves into Charmeleon. You wake up with a partner that takes up more of the tent entrance and can no longer fit on your lap the same way. Its reach has changed. So has the space it needs to turn. During your next practice, you give a command that worked for Charmander and watch Charmeleon overshoot the target. You spend the afternoon adjusting. Evolution helps your team, but it also means learning the Pokemon you already thought you knew. Your familiar camping arrangement needs changing as well.

Pidgey eventually becomes Pidgeotto, and Poliwag becomes Poliwhirl. You make more room for them and change how they train together. Poliwhirl can stay on its feet through exercises that used to tire it out. Pidgeotto lands with enough force to knock over a food bowl if you put it too close to the perch. You learn which partner needs a quiet morning after a difficult fight. Your daily routine starts taking longer, even while battles get easier. You can't fit another training session into the day by skipping the time you spend looking after them.

In Celadon City, you meet a trainer who offers to trade for Charmeleon. His Pokemon would give your team a different option, and you spend longer considering it than you expected. Charmeleon is asleep against your leg while the other trainer describes his offer. You ask a few questions, then tell him you're keeping your partner. On the way out of town, you buy supplies and reconsider your next Gym strategy with the team you have. You still want to win. You just want this particular Pokemon with you when you do.

That choice gets tested outside the city when you find Team Rocket bothering a young trainer. They're trying to take his Pokemon, and he has backed himself against a wall. You could keep walking toward your next challenge. Charmeleon has already stopped. You step beside the trainer and send Pidgeotto out to give him room to move. The fight is messy, and a grunt's Pokemon keeps getting between you and the exit. You direct Charmeleon toward that gap while the trainer recalls his partner. Once he can leave, you get your own team clear.

Afterward, you need a Center and replacement supplies. Team Rocket hasn't given you a badge, and you've lost the afternoon you meant to spend practicing. The trainer you helped comes to thank you with his Pokemon beside him. Charmeleon takes an interest in the little creature, then returns to the nurse for another check. You miss your planned departure and send Milo a message saying you'll be late. He asks whether you need anything. You tell him where you are, and he turns back with food. It's the first time you're pleased to see him ahead of schedule.

Your rivalry changes after that. Milo still notices every mistake you make, but now he'll stay to explain what he saw. You watch him battle and notice that his Wartortle sometimes responds before he finishes a command. You try giving Charmeleon the same freedom. During practice, you call the goal and let it choose the last few steps. Sometimes it gets distracted. Sometimes it finds a better angle than the one you were about to demand. You keep working on it, especially when you're tired enough to want everything done your way.

At the Saffron Gym, that preparation meets Sabrina. Her Psychic-type Pokemon disrupt the rhythm you've been using to win. You send Charmeleon forward, lose track of the opponent's position, and issue two different commands before either helps. Milo watches from the side while you recall it and reset. You lose the challenge. Outside, you ask him what you missed. He tells you that once you started worrying about the badge, you stopped noticing what your Pokemon was doing. You spend the next session practicing an escape and a recall instead of another attack.

The rematch takes longer. You wait for openings you rushed past before and switch partners while they can still contribute later. When you finally earn the badge, Charmeleon needs a rest and you let it have one. You don't leave town straight away to preserve a lead over Milo. During that break, it climbs onto a low wall beside you and places a paw on your sleeve, the way Charmander did after the first trip to the Center. You're glad you kept it. You're also glad you've finally learned to stop moving sometimes.

Charmeleon eventually evolves into Charizard, and your carefully chosen campsite becomes too small again. You have a huge orange partner with wings and a tail you need to keep clear of the tent. It tests a wingbeat and sends your map into the bushes. You retrieve it while Pidgeotto watches from a branch. Charizard can do things Charmander couldn't have attempted. You also have new problems to plan around. A Flying type has weaknesses you need to respect, and the opponent who looks easy from a distance may have exactly the move you're hoping they don't.

At the remaining Gyms, you stop treating Charizard as the answer to every match. You use Poliwhirl where water gives your team an opening and bring Pidgeotto into fights where it can work safely. Your partners get their turns, and Charizard gets called back when continuing would waste it. Pidgeotto becomes Pidgeot before your final badge challenge. By the time you defeat Giovanni at the Viridian Gym, the case has all eight badges. You open it on the pavement outside, close it, and check whether your team has eaten. The League will still be there after dinner.

The approach to the Indigo Plateau makes you understand why those badges are required. On Victory Road, trainers who've completed their own journeys can punish a poor choice as quickly as a Gym Leader. You arrive at the League tired, with notes full of corrections. Inside, you look at the doors leading toward the Elite Four and feel the urge to start immediately. Your partners have been traveling all day. You arrange a rest, check your supplies, and ask Milo when he plans to challenge. He has already been turned away by a loss and is preparing to try again.

Your first League run ends against Lorelei. You recognize Ice-type Pokemon and assume Charizard should handle the fight. Then one of her partners uses a Water-type attack, and you realize how little your first glance told you. You bring Poliwhirl in without a clear follow-up and lose the opening you needed. Before long, you're outside with your team recalled and your eight badges still in their case. Nobody takes them away. They just haven't made you ready for this room. You sit on the steps until you can discuss the match without making excuses.

Milo meets you there with Blastoise beside him. He doesn't look surprised. You tell him you kept choosing moves to rescue the previous mistake, and he nods because he's done it too. You watch a practice battle together and start writing down possibilities before deciding on an attack. You also stop training when your partners get tired, even if you haven't finished what you planned. Charizard settles near you while you work. The Champion is close enough to tempt you into hurrying, but you stay outside the League until you can explain how your whole team will get through it.

On your next attempt, you handle Lorelei with the matchups you actually face. Against Bruno, you keep Pidgeot available for his Fighting-type Pokemon and let Poliwhirl handle the opponents it can counter. Agatha makes you work through a battle where attacking too quickly can waste a turn. Each victory leaves you with another decision about recovery and which partner needs help first. You check them between rooms. You reach Lance with enough left to continue, and he sends out a Pokemon you've been worried about since you started studying the League.

His Dragonite forces you to use a plan that takes your whole team. Poliwhirl contributes the Ice Beam you've been practicing, but you have to keep it ready long enough to use it. Pidgeot gives you another option when staying in would be costly. Charizard finishes a part of the battle without being asked to carry all of it. You win and check your partners while the result settles in. There's still a Champion through the next doorway. For a moment, you wish the League finished here. Then you hear a voice you recognize from Pallet Town.

Milo made it through before you. Blastoise is standing beside him, and your friend is wearing the expression he had when Squirtle inspected his shoe at Oak's lab. He congratulates you for reaching him. You look at the team he has ready and understand why he won. You also know what he expects you to do. He's watched you rely on Charizard when a match becomes difficult. You send Pidgeot out first. Milo pauses, calls his own opening, and starts the battle you've both been working toward since the day you left home.

He makes you use the habits he helped you build. You recognize a move he's preparing and switch before it catches the wrong partner. He answers by changing his plan. Poliwhirl earns an opening, and you bring it back while it can still help. When Charizard finally enters, Blastoise is waiting. You remember standing with Charmander while Squirtle stayed beyond its reach. This time, you check your partner before giving the command. Charizard has room to move and enough strength left. You wait for Milo's attack, call the response you've practiced, and watch your partner take it.

The referee calls the result. Milo returns Blastoise, walks across the field, and offers his hand. You've become the Kanto Champion. You're trying to answer him when Charizard presses its head against your shoulder, nearly knocking you over. Pidgeot lands nearby and Poliwhirl steps up beside it. Later, Oak records your team in the Hall of Fame. You keep looking at the Pokemon beside you while he works. They're hungry, and you're going to have to find somewhere with enough space for Charizard before anyone gets dinner.

On your return to Viridian City, you stop at the Center that treated Charmander after your first battle. The nurse comes out to meet your team. Charizard has to lower its head to see her through the doorway. You thank her, then notice a new trainer on the waiting bench with a frightened partner and a map spread across his knees. You sit beside him and ask what happened. Charizard settles outside the window, keeping its tail clear of the wall. You listen while the trainer explains the command he wishes he hadn't given.

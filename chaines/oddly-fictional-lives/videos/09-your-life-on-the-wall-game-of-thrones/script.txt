You nearly let a dead man through the gate.

He's wearing a black cloak, and he's come from the forest where your patrol disappeared. You lift the lantern to show him the entrance. Beside you, Pell grabs the gate bar before you can move it. The man outside keeps walking. His eyes catch the light, bright blue, and he doesn't slow down when his face hits the iron. Pell pulls you back, takes the torch from its bracket and thrusts it through the bars. The cloak catches fire. You hear something scrape against the gate until the thing outside finally stops moving.

You ask whether that was one of your missing rangers. Pell says it was. He tells you to fetch the men who knew him before they see what's left in the morning.

You've arrived at Castle Black, home of the Night's Watch, expecting the men in black to protect the realm from raiders. Beyond their gate is a forest that sends their own dead back to them. The Wall rises above you, an enormous face of ice, and somebody has decided you belong on the small side of it.

Your name is Edren. You came north after your village lost its harvest and the work you expected to find farther south never appeared. The Watch would feed you. The recruiter mentioned service and honour. He said considerably less about the food, and nothing at all about dead people walking home.

At breakfast, you sit beside a recruit named Hobb, who volunteered because he thought the Wall would be quieter than the army. He's staring at his bowl. Across the room, two veterans argue about a missing pair of socks. You find the argument reassuring. At least somebody expects to need dry feet tomorrow.

Training begins in a yard where the ground makes every fall memorable. Pell knocks your practice sword aside, hooks your ankle and introduces you to the frozen mud. Hobb laughs until it's his turn. You learn to keep your shield up, to listen while you're tired and to help another man stand without dropping your guard. The last part proves useful before the first week ends.

The lift takes you up the Wall for a work detail. The cage shakes, the rope groans, and Castle Black becomes a collection of roofs below your boots. At the top, the wind gets inside your clothes as if it has been waiting for an invitation. You try to say something impressive about the view. Your teeth make it difficult to finish.

Torr, a builder with a leather apron over his black clothes, hands you a shovel. Snow has drifted against the walkway and needs clearing before the next watch. Hobb leans on the rail to look down. Torr pulls him away with the shovel handle and points to the rope he should have clipped around himself. You clip yours before asking any questions.

Below, the castle needs just as much attention. The armoury has damp bowstrings. A kitchen door won't close. The winch makes a noise Torr dislikes, and you quickly learn that a builder disliking a noise is a good reason to stop standing beneath its source. The Wall looks permanent from the ground. The men keeping it useful spend their days finding things that aren't.

When the assignments come, you ask to become a ranger. Pell has seen what you do with a shield and asks whether you've ever tracked anything that didn't want to be found. You mention a lost sheep. He asks whether you found it. Hobb answers before you can make the story sound better.

You're assigned to the stewards. Hobb joins the builders, while better woodsmen go to the rangers. These are different orders with different work, not steps everyone climbs in the same direction. You'll cook, carry supplies and learn how the garrison stays alive. Pell tells you that a good ranger is still useless if nobody feeds his horse.

The joke stops being funny when the supplies arrive short. You help Orren, a senior steward with an ink stain on his sleeve, unload sacks that weigh less than their marks suggest. He opens one and finds grain spoiled by water. You spend the evening sorting what can be saved while the kitchen argues about portions. The men on the Wall will still expect a hot meal when they come down.

You carry a bowl to Pell after his watch. He eats standing beside the door, too tired to take off his gloves. Then he gives you the piece of bread he was saving. You tell him the kitchen will have more tomorrow. He says that's why he trusts you with it.

Before you become a sworn brother, you can still leave. You stand on the south road with Hobb, looking at the track that brought you here. A wagon is going down to the settlements. Its driver has room. Hobb asks what you'll do if you go back, and you find that the answer hasn't improved since you came north.

You take the vows with the others. Your service will last for life. You give up the prospect of a wife, children of your own and lands to pass down. The words about guarding the realm are easier to say than the parts about what you won't have. Later, Hobb sits beside you at dinner wearing his new black cloak. He has spilled broth on it already. You tell him it looks official now.

Winter makes the food problem worse. The road disappears under snow, and a supply party is late returning from a storehouse south of the Wall. You know which bags went out on the wagon and how much the kitchen needs back. You ask to go with the search. Pell agrees, provided you follow directions the first time he gives them.

The wagon has slipped off a frozen track. Its axle is broken, the horses are panicking, and a steward is pinned beside a wheel. You leave the food where it is and get the man free. Hobb brings a timber brace from the repair cart, and you help Pell lever the wheel away. Then you cover the injured man with your own spare cloak and load him onto a horse.

You get him home before the cold finishes what the wagon started. Most of the food comes back later with the builders. Orren asks whether you wrote off the damaged sacks. You tell him you haven't had time. He looks at the man by the fire and says the sacks can wait.

Pell begins taking you on short patrols. You learn to distinguish a broken branch from a branch somebody broke while passing, and to notice when birds stop calling before you can see why. Your first attempt at following tracks ends at a place where the patrol has been waiting for you. They ate while you were catching up. Hobb hears about it before you finish taking off your boots.

The maester wants a word before the next outing. Pell brings you into a room that smells of herbs and hot wax, where a black glass dagger rests beside a bundle of papers. The maester has been collecting reports from returned patrols. He wraps the blade in leather and gives it to Pell, explaining what the accounts say it can do against a White Walker. You ask whether an ordinary sword will work. He turns one of the reports toward you. Pell reads it without smiling and puts the dagger somewhere he can reach. Outside, you ask him whether he believes the account. He says the thing that came to your gate has made him willing to carry an extra blade.

That evening, Hobb repairs the strap on your glove. You repay him by helping fit a new bowstring he can't stretch alone. For a while, neither of you mentions the forest. You discuss whether the cook has been using the same onion in every meal, passing it over the pot and putting it back in a box. Hobb laughs hard enough to lose his grip on the string. You both have to start again.

Eventually, your transfer to the rangers is approved after training and experience. It doesn't make the stew you carried less necessary. It means someone else carries it while you learn how much farther the forest goes than it looked from the Wall.

The gate closes behind your first ranging party. Snow deadens the sound of the horses, and the Wall slips out of view between the trees. Pell points at the trail you must remember if you get separated. You look back twice. The second time, you realise that every direction has begun to look like the first.

You find an abandoned camp near a frozen stream. A pot lies beside the fire pit. Food is still in it, untouched. One of the rangers reaches for a blanket, and Pell stops his hand. Drag marks lead away from the camp into the trees. Nobody left this place to collect more wood.

At the ridge beyond it, you see people moving among the trunks. Free Folk, the people your brothers call wildlings. A woman in a faded red scarf draws a bow before you have time to get your shield up. Pell keeps his weapon lowered and asks where the camp's occupants went. She looks past him toward the deeper forest. The bow doesn't move.

Her name is Lysa, and she says the dead came after sunset. Her group ran south, but an older man fell crossing the stream and can't walk. She wants you to give him a horse. One of your rangers says she's leading you into a trap. Then you hear the man coughing from under the bank, and you can see the impression his body has left in the snow.

You give up your horse. Lysa helps the injured man onto it, still watching your hands. Pell orders the patrol toward a ruined shelter where you can make a fire and check the man's leg. You'll have to explain the missing horse when you get back. You're more concerned about whatever emptied that camp catching up while you're on foot.

In the shelter, Lysa shares dried meat with the ranger who called her a liar. He takes it without thanking her. Later, when he falls asleep sitting against the wall, she moves his boot away from the fire before the leather burns. You sit where you can see the entrance and keep your sword across your knees.

Something walks past the opening after dark. You see the black hem of a cloak and start to call out. Pell catches you by the shoulder. Beyond the doorway, a shape stops while another appears behind it. Their eyes glow through the snow. This time, you don't reach for the bar.

You move the injured man away from the entrance while Pell and Lysa drag a fallen beam across it. A hand comes through the gap. The ranger beside you strikes it, and the thing on the other side keeps pushing. You shove a burning length of wood against its sleeve. Fire crawls up the cloth, and the pressure against the beam eases.

There are more outside. The shelter has a broken section at the rear, half buried under snow. Lysa knows the slope below it. You help lower the injured man through, then follow him while Pell holds the doorway. Your boots hit the slope badly, and your knee folds beneath you. Lysa pulls you upright before you can decide how much it hurts.

Pell comes out last. His torch goes rolling down the snow, and the figures reaching through the gap turn toward the movement. You get the horse onto the trail and follow its tracks into the dark. Behind you, the shelter's roof begins to burn. You can see enough of the forest to wish you couldn't.

At the next ridge, a different figure waits between the trees. Taller, pale and still, carrying a weapon that catches the moonlight. The dead around it move while it watches. Pell puts you behind him. When its blade meets his sword, the steel breaks, and he stumbles back with the useless hilt in his hand.

You're facing a White Walker. The burning dead at the shelter were wights. This thing walks through the edge of a fallen torch's light without catching fire. You remember the small black blade the maester gave Pell before the ranging. Dragonglass, wrapped in leather so it wouldn't cut through his pack. Pell reaches for it while the Walker advances.

You pull him away from the next strike with your shield arm. The shield splits, and you both fall against a tree. Lysa drags a loaded branch down between you and the creature. Snow drops over its shoulders, buying Pell enough time to find the dagger. When the Walker leans through the branch, he drives the black point into it. Its body fractures into pale shards across the snow.

You stay against the tree, waiting for it to stand up again. Pell is waiting too. Lysa touches his empty sword hand and tells him to move. Whatever happened here hasn't made the rest of the forest safe, and the injured man is still waiting beside a horse that wants to go south more than any of you.

You reach the Wall with a damaged shield, a lame knee and a group of people your gate guards weren't told to expect. Lysa has put her bow down. Behind her, the older man shivers under the black cloak you wrapped around him. The guard on the walkway orders everyone to stay where they are.

You call for Pell, but he's bending over the horse, struggling to keep the injured man upright. You lift your face to the lantern and tell the guard who you are. He knows your name. He also knows that a dead brother came home wearing a black cloak. You make yourself wait while he fetches someone who can decide.

Hobb arrives at the gate with Torr. He sees you leaning on your broken shield and starts asking about your knee before he notices the Free Folk. Torr checks the approach while Hobb gets the inner bar ready. Pell comes forward, answers their questions and gets permission to bring the group into the yard under guard. The gate opens before the man on the horse stops shivering.

The argument afterward is louder than the fight in the forest. Some brothers want Lysa's group outside immediately. Others want every detail about the shattered weapon and the black blade. You put the pieces of Pell's sword on the table. Then you ask the men who want the group expelled which of them is prepared to carry the injured man back through the gate.

Orren brings bowls before anyone answers. Lysa watches him set one down beside her, and for the first time you see her hands stop gripping the edge of the bench. The Lord Commander permits a temporary guarded shelter while he questions the patrol. You haven't changed what the Watch believes with a speech. You've bought these people a night indoors.

Your knee keeps you off the next ranging. You spend your recovery helping Hobb and Torr prepare the gate, learning which fittings can be replaced and which need a smith. The forest has shown you what can reach it. You now care a great deal about a builder's opinion of a noise.

From the Wall, you watch another group approach before dusk. This time, you can see living people pulling a sledge. Farther behind them, movement appears along the tree line. You sound the alarm and give the gate crew something more useful than shouting. Number of people, distance, the direction they're coming from. Hobb repeats it below, and men begin taking their places.

Pell leads the escort out. You can't run beside him yet, so you stand with Torr at the upper controls and help keep the approach clear. Archers drive the pursuing shapes back long enough for the sledge to reach the tunnel. The last brother through slams his hand against the stone to signal that the passage is empty. Hobb and the gate crew close the barrier behind him.

Inside the yard, an exhausted stranger is given a bowl. He's looking from the fire to the black cloaks around him, trying to work out whether he's safe. You lower yourself onto the bench beside him and ease your aching knee toward the heat. From above, the next watch calls down that the gate is secure.

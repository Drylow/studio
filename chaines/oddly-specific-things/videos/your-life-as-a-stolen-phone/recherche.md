# Recherche et consignes — Your Life as a Stolen Phone

Faits vérifiés et règles données au script.

VERIFIED FACTS for this video (use them, rounded; name the source lightly inside the sentence for big facts; do not invent other statistics or sources):
- About 78,000 people in England and Wales had a phone or bag snatched on the street in the year to March 2024, up 153% on the year before (Home Office analysis, 2024); more than 200 snatches a day.
- In 2024, about 70,000 phones were reported stolen in London alone (Metropolitan Police figures).
- Only about 0.8% of "theft from the person" offences led to a charge; most investigations were closed without a suspect (Home Office, year to March 2024).
- Street thieves in London often work on e-bikes and grab phones from people's hands or tables.
- Thieves wrap stolen phones in aluminium foil or put them in a Faraday bag to block tracking signals; almost all of the roughly 1,000 phones in one seized box at Heathrow were wrapped in tinfoil.
- Operation Echosteep (Metropolitan Police, 2025): on Christmas Eve 2024 a victim tracked her stolen iPhone with Find My to a warehouse near Heathrow Airport; inside was a box of nearly 1,000 stolen phones bound for Hong Kong. Police say the gang may have smuggled up to 40,000 stolen phones from the UK to China in a year, about 40% of all phones stolen in London. Street thieves were paid up to £300 per phone; the same phones could sell for up to £4,000 (about $5,000) in China. 46 arrests.
- Hong Kong is a free port (no import tax, light customs), a common stopover; an industrial building in Kwun Tong (1 Hung To Road) houses wholesalers listing iPhones as "iCloud locked" (Financial Times investigation).
- Final stop is often Shenzhen's Huaqiangbei electronics district; the Feiyang Times building has been nicknamed "the stolen iPhone building" (Financial Times). A London entrepreneur tracked his stolen iPhone from a London repair shop to Hong Kong and then to Huaqiangbei.
- A phone locked by the owner's account (Activation Lock / Lost Mode) can't simply be reset and reused: locked phones are worth roughly 30% of an unlocked one (about $300 for a $1,000 phone) and are stripped for parts (screen, camera, chips, battery); an unlocked one can be resold whole.
- To unlock phones, gangs send the owner fake "we found your phone" texts that look like Apple's Find My, linking to a fake login page; once the owner types their password, the gang removes the lock. These texts can arrive weeks or months after the theft, often using the contact number shown on the lost phone's lock screen. Apple never texts people to say a lost device was found.
- In the US, the same pipeline exists: phones stolen at bars and festivals in the US East Coast are consolidated in Miami and shipped to Shenzhen, reaching China in roughly 10 to 30 days (NBC News).

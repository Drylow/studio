# Recherche et consignes — Your Life as a Totaled Car

Faits vérifiés et règles données au script.

VERIFIED FACTS for this video (use them, rounded; name the source lightly inside the sentence for big facts; do not invent other statistics or sources):
- What "total loss" means: the insurer totals a car when the repair cost (plus extras like rental) is more than the car's actual cash value minus what the wreck will fetch as salvage; actual cash value is set by comparing similar cars sold in the area (Copart 10-K 2025; Matthiesen, Wickert & Lehrer state chart, 2021). Each state sets its own rule: Maryland and New York 75% of the car's value, Iowa 70%, Texas 100%; California and Massachusetts use a "total loss formula" instead (same chart). Maryland Insurance Administration: an insurer "is permitted to treat your vehicle as a total loss when the cost to repair the vehicle is greater than 75 percent of the fair market value"; the payment is the actual cash value "minus the amount of your deductible"; the insurer sends the title to the MVA, which issues a salvage certificate. Gap insurance pays the difference between what the car is worth and what you still owe on the loan (Insurance Information Institute).
- How common: in 2025, 23.1% of auto claims were flagged as total losses, a record, nearly 1 in 4 (CCC Intelligent Solutions, Crash Course 2026, March 2026). Average total-loss car value about $13,700; over 72% are 7 years or older; average repair bill about $4,770 (CCC, Q4 2025).
- "Total loss" is not "worthless": the US Justice Department's title database (NMVTIS) says a total-loss decision "does not necessarily mean that a vehicle was destroyed or is worthless." Title washing (hiding a salvage history) exists; NMVTIS was built partly to stop it. Say only that, never how.
- The auction (Copart 10-K, fiscal 2025): the insurer assigns the car, a truck picks most cars up within 24 hours and takes them to the nearest yard; in most states it can be sold only once a salvage title is issued, generally 45 to 60 days later. Bidding is online: when bids stop, a countdown starts, and the car goes to the highest bidder. Copart opened a yard in Laurel, Maryland, in Nov. 2024. Copart sold more than 4 million vehicles last year (81% from insurers), to buyers in more than 165 countries; in fiscal 2025, 38.8% of its US vehicles went to international buyers (by IP address), nearly half of the money, and international bidders took part in more than 90% of auctions for cars expected to be rebuilt (Copart). IAA, the other big salvage auction, sold about 2.45 million automotive lots in 2025 (RB Global). Copart's CEO (Nov. 2025, via Repairer Driven News): more of the cars it sells for insurers "are actually cars that will be repaired and drivable again, both in the U.S. and overseas."
- Salvage and rebuilt titles: to return to US roads, a salvage car must be repaired and pass a special salvage inspection, then gets a "rebuilt salvage" title (Maryland MVA). Our car leaves the US with its salvage title and is registered abroad.
- Exports: from 2015 to 2018, 14 million used light vehicles were exported worldwide; 40% went to Africa; the US supplied about 18% (UN Environment Programme, 2020). An IAA vice president said lower parts and labor costs make it "profitable for buyers in Benin and the surrounding areas, like Togo and Nigeria, to purchase used or salvage vehicles from the U.S. to rebuild"; in April 2023 IAA teamed up with a broker in Benin so local buyers could shop online (NJ.com).
- The ship: a roll-on/roll-off car carrier's monthly route ran from Baltimore and New Jersey to Dakar, then Benin, Nigeria, Togo and Ghana; cars are "driven, towed or dragged up ramps" and strapped to the decks; dockworkers sort them as "runners" (they start), "tows" (they roll but won't start) and "forklifts" (NJ.com, Nov. 2023). Baltimore was America's top car port for more than a decade, until 2024 (Maryland Daily Record). A car-shipping company's published rate (2026, estimate): about $1,300 by RoRo from Baltimore to Cotonou, about 18 days. Baltimore to Cotonou by sea via Dakar is roughly 5,800 miles (computed, approximate).
- Cotonou: at Fifa Park, one of Cotonou's biggest car lots, cars stand in long rows, "some shiny and new-looking, others dented and dusty"; a dealer told CNN importers often bring cars that have been in accidents: "They repair these cars and resell them to make a profit." A 16-year-old US car sold there for 3 million CFA francs, about $4,500 (CNN, May 2023). East of Cotonou, toward the Nigerian border, at least 20,000 cars stand for sale on what Swiss public radio called "Africa's biggest car park" (SRF/RTS, March 2026). Registering a used car in Benin costs around 1,500 Swiss francs in duties (about $1,900), sometimes more (SRF/RTS). A Cotonou dealer's indicative prices for a mid-size sedan: about 5 million CFA francs for a 2015-2016 model, 9.8 million for 2018-2019 (dealer website, undated asking prices: estimate). About 570 CFA francs to the dollar (computed, Sept. 2026).
- Nigeria next door (context only): Africa's biggest buyer of used cars; most cars on the Cotonou lots end up there, much of it as contraband, as Nigeria has banned vehicle imports by land since Jan. 2017 (SRF/RTS 2026; Nigeria Customs Service). Never explain how cars cross.
- Benin details: zemidjans are Cotonou's moto-taxis, riders in yellow shirts; the name means "take me quickly" in Fon; an estimated 72,000 in Cotonou. The Route des Peches runs about 40 km along the Atlantic from Cotonou to Ouidah, beach on one side, palms and lagoons on the other.
- Context only: Carfax estimated up to 347,000 cars flood-damaged in the 2024 hurricane season, some cleaned up and resold far away (Carfax, Oct. 2024).

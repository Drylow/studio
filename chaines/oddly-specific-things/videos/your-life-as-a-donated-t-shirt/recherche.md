# Recherche et consignes — Your Life as a Donated T-Shirt

Faits vérifiés et règles données au script.

VERIFIED FACTS for this video (use them, rounded; name the source lightly inside the sentence for big facts; do not invent other statistics or sources):
- Most donated clothes are not sold in the store (estimates, say who estimates): author Adam Minter told NPR's Fresh Air (Dec. 2019) the average US thrift store "only sells about one-third of the stuff that ends up on its shelves". A Salvation Army official (Caring magazine, 2017): "Only one of three garments go into our stores"; the rest is baled "in lots that weigh just over a half ton" and sold to recyclers worldwide. At a Seattle-area Goodwill (Seattle Times, Sept. 2021) each item gets a color-coded tag and "a four-week life span on the floor", cheaper week by week; unsold items are "ragged off" to an outlet selling clothes by the pound, and what is left is "salvage" ("Pretty much everything is pennies on the pound", a Goodwill vice president). Salvation Army, Brooklyn: "exactly one month to sell" (Elizabeth Cline, Overdressed, 2012).
- Salvage price: the Seattle outlet presses textiles into 1,000-pound cubes; dealers paid "roughly 27 cents a pound" (Seattle Times, 2021). A T-shirt weighs about a third of a pound (about three garments per pound, Or Foundation): about 9 cents a shirt (computed, estimate).
- Graders: SMART, the trade group of used-clothing, wiping-rag and fiber companies, says graders sort, "assign a 'grade'" and re-sell, mostly overseas; its industry figures: 45% of used clothing reused as clothing, 30% wiping cloths, 20% fiber, 5% unusable. A New Jersey grader sorted close to 17 million pounds a year into about 200 categories, at least half "the bad and the ugly" (Cline, 2012). Business Insider's World Wide Waste (Feb. 2022): "hundreds of categories".
- Big picture: US EPA, 2018: 17 million tons of textiles in US trash, 14.7% recycled, 11.3 million tons landfilled. Ellen MacArthur Foundation (2017): less than 1% of textiles made for clothing is recycled into new clothes; "one garbage truck of textiles is landfilled or incinerated every second".
- Ghana: Bloomberg (Nov. 2022) called Ghana the world's biggest importer of used clothing in 2020. UN Comtrade (via World Bank WITS), 2023: about $93 million of worn clothing; the UK biggest supplier (about 35,000 tonnes), then China, Canada and the US (about 5,000 tonnes, declared at about $1.13 a kilo, computed). An American shirt is real but rarer: most bales come from Britain and China.
- "Obroni wawu": the Twi name for these clothes, "dead white man's clothes" (Bloomberg: "dead White people's clothes"). Or Foundation co-founders Liz Ricketts and Branson Skinner (Atmos, 2019): when secondhand clothes first arrived decades ago, people presumed the owner had died.
- Kantamanto, in Accra's business district just west of Accra Central railway station: the Or Foundation (nonprofit, spells itself "Or") estimates 15 million garments arrive every week, over 30,000 people work there, and 25 million pieces a month are recirculated through resale and repair (Jan. 2025). The estimate is computed (Atmos 2019): about 100 containers a week, about 400 bales of 120 pounds each, about three garments per pound. TIME (Aug. 2025): "Every Thursday, new bales arrive".
- Bales: 55 kg, bought sight unseen: $100 to $300 "without knowing exactly what's inside" (Business Insider 2022); £116-185, about $160-250 (Greenpeace Unearthed, June 2025, computed). A trader told the Irish Times (June 2022) she buys "bales of 400 white shirts" for 1,300 cedis. The retailer cuts the straps and sorts: first selection (clean, trendy, no holes), second, third, "under" (Atmos). Or Foundation survey: an average bale is 18% first, 30% second, 46% third, 6% under; 46% of retailers did not make their money back on their last bale (one bale the Or Foundation studied was over 90% stained: fewer than 20 sellable items, BI); first selection alone must bring back at least 75% of the bale price. Prices: a first-selection top 10 cedis, about $2 (Atmos 2019); good items about $1.60, ripped ones 16 cents (Business Insider 2022); shirts 10-40 cedis (Irish Times 2022). Tailors repair tears; pressers heat irons with coal (BI). About 11 cedis per dollar in Oct. 2025 (Bank of Ghana).
- Head porters (kayayei, "she who carries the burden", Or Foundation): young women, often from northern Ghana (TIME), carry 55 kg bales on their heads; 7-12 cedis a bale (New Lines, Dec. 2023).
- What nobody buys: the Or Foundation's two-year study: about 40% of each bale leaves as waste (traders to TIME: 30-40%). Accra waste chief Solomon Noi: "close to 40 per cent... complete chaff of no value" (ABC Australia, 2021). The city spends about $500,000 a year on the market's waste and handles about 70% (AFP, 2024). Business Insider: dumped at Old Fadama, a community beside a big landfill whose trash piles tower "over 30 feet". TIME: much is "blown into Korle Lagoon", the drain to the Gulf of Guinea. Bloomberg: at Chorkor beach, a wall of clothes "more than 6 feet high", huts on top. Greenpeace Unearthed (2025): dump sites inside the Densu Delta, a protected wetland west of Accra.
- The fire: the Ghana National Fire Service said it started at 1:18 a.m. on Thursday, Jan. 2, 2025 (many outlets say New Year's Day). The Or Foundation: more than 60% of the retail side destroyed, at least 10 of 13 sections, over 10,000 people affected; it committed $1 million at once. Cause never established. Traders rebuilt with timber frames and donated roofing sheets. One year on (Ghana News Agency, Jan. 2026): trading back but slow; everyone out at 6 p.m., fire officers on three shifts.
- Balance: Minter (Seattle Times 2021): many African buyers prefer secondhand clothes for their quality. Ricketts (BI 2022): "our goal... has never been to cancel the secondhand clothing trade, it has been to make it better".
- Story details: about 1.1 million people ran one of 936 Thanksgiving turkey trots in 2024 (Scripps News); average 5K entry $30.48 (RunSignup, 2024); Goodwill of Southern NJ & Philadelphia (May 2024): men's short-sleeve shirts "$4.99 and below"; the IRS allows a deduction only for clothes in "good used condition or better", at thrift-store value; Ghanaian keep-fit clubs meet early on Saturday or Sunday mornings in parks, school fields and along roads (academic study, 2025).
- Distances (computed): Port Newark to Tema about 5,140 miles in a straight line (18-25 days at sea, freight forwarders); Tema to Kantamanto about 17 miles; Kantamanto to Korle Lagoon about 1 mile.

VERIFIED FACTS for this video (use them, rounded; name the source lightly inside the sentence for big facts; do not invent other statistics or sources):
- Canada's private insurers paid more than $1.5 billion in auto theft claims in 2023, about triple the $556 million a year of 2021 (Insurance Bureau of Canada). Ontario was the first province to pass $1 billion in theft claims; its theft claim costs rose 524% from 2018 to 2023.
- Many modern SUVs are stolen with a "relay attack": a device fools the car into thinking its own keyless fob is nearby, so it unlocks and starts (Equite Association). Describe it only at that level, never how to do it.
- Police say thieves often leave a freshly stolen car parked somewhere quiet for a day or two, to see whether a tracker brings the police or the owner (present as what police describe).
- The Port of Montreal is the main exit port for stolen Canadian cars. In 2024, Ontario Provincial Police and the Canada Border Services Agency recovered 598 stolen vehicles at the Port of Montreal, worth about $34.5 million; about 75% had been stolen in Ontario; 390 shipping containers were searched. In 2021, more than 1,000 stolen vehicles worth over $40 million were recovered there.
- Stolen cars are packed into shipping containers (often two or more per container) with false shipping documents declaring something else, then moved by truck or train to the port (Michigan federal case 2025: stolen high-end vehicles packed into containers and sent to Iraq and the UAE while lying to customs about what was inside).
- Stolen Canadian cars often travel through European ports (Italy, Belgium) on their way to the Middle East and West Africa; Spain intercepted 117 stolen vehicles from North America at the port of Algeciras in six months in 2025.
- CBC News and Ghana's The Fourth Estate (2023) found dozens of cars stolen in Ontario and Quebec in Ghana; the VIN of a stolen Lexus appeared on a document after Ghanaian authorities raided a car lot in Accra; the cars were sold on social media, often close to market price.
- In the US, 850,708 vehicles were stolen in 2024, down 17% from about 1.02 million in 2023 (NICB).

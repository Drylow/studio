Returning it there can banish the Pale Lady and put the disturbance to rest. The action answers Ra'jirr's fear in a way his theft alone could not accomplish. Carrying the sword away from Kyr was only part of returning it. Someone still had to reach its resting place and complete the act while the spirit remained a danger.

The Dragonborn can instead defeat the Pale Lady and keep the weapon, known as the Pale Blade. Its enchantment combines frost with fear, giving the recovered object an effect that later enemies can experience without knowing its history. This is a different outcome from leaving it on the monument. The immediate encounter can be resolved through combat, with the artifact carried back into Skyrim, or through restoration of the blade to the place from which it was taken. Neither route gives the Dragonborn a complete account of the spirit's original life or binding.

At Valthume, Valdar asks for help bringing a contained dragon priest out so that he can be defeated. Frostmere offers another possibility: an object can be put back, allowing a disturbed presence to fall quiet. Both quests bring a living visitor into an old arrangement, but the useful intervention differs. Here, the gang's ownership dispute has already collapsed before the Dragonborn makes the choice. The blade can remain below the trees, or leave the grove with a new bearer who has seen what its earlier discoverers disturbed.


Paarthurnax waits at the summit of the Throat of the World, above the monastery where the Greybeards teach the Voice. Reaching him requires a lesson before the meeting can happen. Arngeir teaches the Dragonborn Clear Skies, a Shout that opens a path through the fierce conditions surrounding the upper mountain. The master the Greybeards have kept apart from visitors turns out to be a dragon. By then, the player has already fought dragons elsewhere in Skyrim. This one begins a conversation and offers instruction.

His first lesson involves fire. Paarthurnax teaches a word of Fire Breath, then shares his understanding of it so the Dragonborn can use it. He asks the player to direct the Shout at him. A power usually encountered in combat becomes part of an exchange between teacher and pupil. He recognizes the Dragonborn through that demonstration, and the meeting settles into a discussion about Alduin, the ancient struggle against him, and the limits of what Paarthurnax himself can teach.

Those limits matter. Paarthurnax can't supply Dragonrend, the weapon the player needs against Alduin. Mortals created that Shout out of their experience of oppression by dragons. Its meaning reaches into mortality, something a dragon doesn't experience as a human does. Paarthurnax can explain where the search must lead, but he can't hand over every answer. His usefulness comes partly from his willingness to acknowledge that another kind of experience produced knowledge outside his own.

The peaceful teacher on the mountain has a past that the Blades won't allow the Dragonborn to set aside. Paarthurnax served Alduin during the age when dragons ruled over mortals. Delphine and Esbern identify him as a leading figure in that rule and hold him responsible for terrible suffering. His later assistance to humanity doesn't erase those charges in their eyes. To the Blades, the dragon advising their ally is also an old enemy who has survived beyond the people who suffered under him.

Paarthurnax acknowledges his earlier allegiance. He also helped mortals learn the Voice and resist Alduin. Skyrim therefore places both parts of his history in the same living character. The Dragonborn doesn't discover that the kindly teacher was innocent all along, or that his help against Alduin was imaginary. His teaching has practical results. The Greybeards' tradition has grown around his guidance. The accusation concerns what he did before those centuries on the mountain, and whether his conduct since then can change what should happen now.

Delphine makes the dispute immediate by demanding his death. Until the Dragonborn kills Paarthurnax, she and Esbern withhold further assistance from the Blades. Sky Haven Temple has become a place where the order might be rebuilt, with recruits and dragon hunts giving it work beyond the search for Alduin. Keeping Paarthurnax alive leaves that renewed association interrupted. Delphine is asking the person she has helped to act against someone who has also helped them, and she attaches the future of their cooperation to the answer.

That demand also exposes a difference in how the two groups treat the Dragonborn. The Greybeards approach the Voice through discipline and restraint. Their lessons at High Hrothgar require the player to listen, practice, and demonstrate understanding. The Blades look to the Dragonborn as the person able to defeat dragons and help restore their order. Both groups have provided shelter and instruction. Their disagreement reaches the player through familiar people with whom the player has already worked, which makes it harder to treat either side as a distant faction making an abstract claim.

Arngeir strongly opposes the demand. He regards Paarthurnax as the Greybeards' master and sees his long guidance as reason to defend him. Speaking with Arngeir brings the dispute back to High Hrothgar, where the Dragonborn first received formal instruction in the Voice. Killing the dragon above the monastery would break a connection that has shaped the monks' lives. Arngeir's anger comes from that particular loss as well as his distrust of the Blades. For him, their request threatens a teacher whose help has continued across generations.

Paarthurnax's own response is unusually direct. He doesn't ask the Dragonborn to assume that dragons are harmless, and he regards caution around his kind as sensible. He describes an inborn urge to dominate, something he has had to resist through sustained effort. His account gives the years of meditation on the mountain a purpose. A dragon who can speak gently and teach patiently still has impulses he considers dangerous. According to Paarthurnax, his present behavior depends on the practice of overcoming them.

The player can spend more time with that practice through his instruction in meditation on words of power. Paarthurnax discusses particular words and can deepen the Dragonborn's understanding of a Shout. These conversations give his teaching a continuing use after the first meeting. They also place the question of his death beside ordinary return visits to the summit. The person the Blades want killed remains available to explain a word, share knowledge, and speak about the Voice. That's a concrete part of the player's experience of him, alongside what they learn of his past.

The player may nevertheless carry out the Blades' demand. Paarthurnax can be killed, and reporting his death allows cooperation with Delphine and Esbern to resume. The Greybeards respond with grief and anger, and their willingness to guide the Dragonborn suffers. Refusing to kill him preserves the teacher at the summit while leaving the Blades' condition unmet. There's no conversation in the unmodified game that persuades Delphine to withdraw it and accept a permanent compromise. The player can leave her demand unanswered, but the disagreement remains.


The Time-Wound lies at the summit of the Throat of the World, where an ancient use of an Elder Scroll left a lasting disturbance. Paarthurnax directs the Dragonborn to this place because an ordinary lesson can't provide the Shout needed against Alduin. Dragonrend was created by mortals who fought the dragons long ago. Paarthurnax understands why it matters, but he can't teach its meaning himself. To learn it, the Dragonborn must bring an Elder Scroll to the place where those mortals once stood and look into their confrontation.

Opening the Scroll there brings the ancient battle into view. The mountain becomes a meeting place between the Dragonborn's present and a struggle that Skyrim remembers through scattered accounts. Three Tongues, Gormlaith Golden-Hilt, Hakon One-Eye, and Felldir the Old, face Alduin. They have already committed themselves to resisting the dragons. Through the Scroll, the player can watch them act together and hear the weapon they've made from the Voice. The knowledge sought on the summit belongs to people who had to use it while their enemy was still above them.

Dragonrend forces a dragon to experience the idea of mortality. Its words carry concepts that an immortal dragon can't readily comprehend, and that disruption brings the creature down where it can be fought. This explains Paarthurnax's difficulty with the Shout. He can teach the Dragonborn words of power, but this particular creation comes from mortal experience and the hatred of people living under dragon rule. Learning it requires contact with the understanding its makers placed inside it. The ancient Tongues supply that understanding through their battle.

Their use of Dragonrend also reveals its limits. Bringing Alduin within reach doesn't make the fight safe, and the Tongues can't finish him with it. Gormlaith falls during the confrontation. Felldir turns to the Elder Scroll, using it to remove Alduin from their time. The victory available to them is a desperate one. They can end his presence on their battlefield, even though they haven't achieved the death they were fighting for. The Dragonborn witnesses both the invention that can ground him and the failure that made the Scroll necessary.

Alduin is cast forward through time. His appearance in the Dragonborn's age follows from that ancient act, which also leaves the Time-Wound behind. For the people who fought on the mountain, he vanished. Skyrim's later inhabitants have to face the enemy those people couldn't destroy. The disturbance on the summit lets the Dragonborn recover something useful from the same event: the Shout that gives a mortal fighter a way to challenge him. The Scroll connects the two battles through a specific place and a weapon carried across the intervening years.

When the vision ends, Alduin arrives at the summit and confronts the Dragonborn. The newly learned Shout can now be used against him, with Paarthurnax joining the fight. Dragonrend pulls Alduin into a battle where the player's weapons and other abilities can reach him. Without its effect, his protection prevents the confrontation from proceeding in the same way. The lesson has an immediate purpose. What the Dragonborn has just watched the Tongues attempt becomes something the player must put into practice against the same dragon.

Alduin's defeat on the mountain doesn't end his movements through Skyrim and beyond. He withdraws, and pursuing him eventually takes the Dragonborn to Sovngarde, the realm where Nord dead gather. There, his presence has consequences for people who have already left the living world. He hunts souls in the mist outside the Hall of Valor. The road toward the hall has become dangerous even for the dead, and the Dragonborn encounters souls whose passage is obstructed by the dragon waiting within that mist.

Inside the Hall of Valor, the ancient struggle acquires familiar faces again. Gormlaith, Hakon, and Felldir are there, and they can accompany the Dragonborn into the confrontation. The people seen through the Scroll haven't disappeared from the story after passing on their knowledge. In Sovngarde, they fight alongside the person who learned from them. Their presence gives the final encounter a direct connection to the battle at the Time-Wound. They once drove Alduin away from their own age. Now they have another opportunity to face him.

Before they can fight him, the companions have to clear his mist. The Dragonborn uses Clear Skies, and the heroes lend their Voices to the effort. Alduin restores the obstruction, so they repeat the Shout until the way is open. It's a different use of the Voice from Dragonrend, with a practical task of its own. They need to remove the concealment around him before they can bring him down. The final battle depends on both kinds of learning: clearing the ground and making the dragon vulnerable.

With the mist dispersed, Dragonrend again allows Alduin to be confronted. The Dragonborn and the three heroes defeat him together in Sovngarde. His body breaks apart, but the familiar conclusion of a dragon battle doesn't follow. The Dragonborn doesn't absorb his soul. Throughout Skyrim, that absorption has accompanied the deaths of dragons the player defeats, giving a visible sign of the Dragonborn's unusual nature. Its absence here leaves the final encounter with a different ending, even though Alduin has been overcome and his hunting in Sovngarde has stopped.

Back at the Throat of the World, Paarthurnax can speak about the outcome if he's alive. He acknowledges Alduin's defeat while also mourning him as a fellow dragon. Arngeir raises the possibility that Alduin may still have a role at the end of time. He offers no certain answer, and the absence of the usual soul absorption doesn't establish where Alduin has gone. The Dragonborn returns with an accomplished task and no complete explanation of what follows for him.


Dawnstar's residents are afraid to sleep. Their conversations at the Windpeak Inn return to the nightmares that have left them exhausted, and the trouble extends beyond a single household. Erandur, a priest of Mara staying at the inn, believes he knows where the dreams begin. Above the town stands Nightcaller Temple, an old sanctuary of Vaermina, the Daedric Prince associated with dreams and nightmares. He offers to lead the Dragonborn there and end the disturbance. For the people below, his offer gives a reachable destination to something they can otherwise encounter only in sleep.

Erandur attributes the nightmares to the Skull of Corruption, an artifact still inside the temple. His explanation connects the suffering in Dawnstar to the activity of an abandoned religious sanctuary. The building hasn't ceased to affect its neighbors just because worshippers no longer come and go through its entrance. Erandur intends to destroy the Skull, and he needs help reaching it. He presents himself as a priest undertaking dangerous work on behalf of the town, though his familiarity with the temple will soon require an explanation.

Inside Nightcaller Temple, Erandur opens a concealed way beyond the outer chamber. The route leads into a place where a struggle has been suspended for years. Orc invaders once attacked the sanctuary, and Vaermina's priests released a substance called the Miasma to overcome them. It put defenders and attackers into an enchanted sleep. As Erandur and the Dragonborn move through the interior, sleepers awaken and become threats again. The temple contains people for whom the old attack is still close enough to resume.

A magical barrier prevents a straightforward approach to the Skull. Erandur knows that a passage exists beyond it, but knowing the layout doesn't give him a way through. His knowledge also prompts the admission he's avoided making. Before becoming a priest of Mara, he served Vaermina here under the name Casimir. During the attack, he fled while others remained. Returning to Nightcaller Temple means walking back among the people and obligations he left behind. His present task brings him into direct conflict with the worship he once practiced.

That admission changes the journey without making it impossible to continue. Erandur's knowledge has already helped the Dragonborn enter the temple, and his former allegiance explains how he acquired it. He speaks of regret and of the life he's pursued since leaving. Those statements belong to him, while his actions in the temple give the player something further to consider. He keeps working toward the Skull's destruction, even as the route forces him to disclose a past that makes him a more troubling companion.

The barrier sends them searching the temple's library. Erandur directs the Dragonborn toward a book called The Dreamstride, which describes the unusual effects of Vaermina's Torpor. Someone who drinks it can experience another person's memories through a dream journey. Within Nightcaller Temple, that offers a way to reach the space beyond the obstruction. The library therefore contains a practical answer to their immediate difficulty. They must find the potion in the laboratory and use a form of dream travel associated with the very Prince whose artifact they're trying to destroy.

After drinking the Torpor, the Dragonborn experiences the earlier attack through Casimir. The priests are facing the orc assault, and Casimir is sent to release the Miasma. The player moves through the temple as it was during that emergency, following the task that helped leave its occupants asleep. Erandur's account now has a place and an action attached to it. Casimir belonged among these priests, and he participated in their defense before abandoning the sanctuary. The dream gives the Dragonborn access to that particular incident.

When the experience ends, the Dragonborn awakens beyond the barrier. Removing the soul gem that sustains it opens the way for Erandur to follow. The journey through a remembered event has changed what they can reach in the present temple. It hasn't required them to undo the invasion or rescue everyone caught in the Miasma. Around them, the consequences of the attack remain. They can now continue toward the Skull together, with the obstruction removed and Erandur's former identity known.

Closer to the artifact, they encounter Veren and Thorek, priests who recognize their old companion. To them, Erandur is still Casimir, the man who deserted them. His changed name and service to Mara don't persuade them to accept his return. The confrontation becomes a fight, and the two priests are defeated before Erandur can begin his work at the Skull. For him, the sanctuary contains former associates with their own recollection of his departure. Their anger gives his regret a personal setting beyond the explanation he offered the Dragonborn.

At the Skull, Erandur begins the ritual intended to destroy it. Vaermina then speaks to the Dragonborn and urges an immediate intervention. She claims that Erandur is deceiving his companion and will turn against them when his work is complete. Killing him would preserve the artifact and allow the Dragonborn to take it. Her warning arrives while he is occupied with the ritual, after the player has already learned that he concealed his former identity.

Vaermina's accusation remains unverified, and she has an immediate interest in stopping the destruction of her artifact. The remembered attack gives her warning something to draw upon: Erandur once fled this temple and left his fellow priests behind. But that past desertion doesn't establish that he intends another betrayal now. The player has also accompanied him through the sanctuary, watched him face recognition, and helped him reach the object he promised to destroy.

The player can kill Erandur and claim the Skull of Corruption, or allow him to finish. If he's spared, he completes the ritual, the Skull is destroyed, and Dawnstar's nightmares end. He survives and can accompany the Dragonborn afterward as a follower. For the town, the quest resolves the source of its troubled sleep. Within Nightcaller Temple, Erandur is able to finish the return he began at the inn, carrying out his promise despite the voice that asks the Dragonborn to stop him.


Azura's shrine stands high in the mountains south of Winterhold, with a statue large enough to be seen against the surrounding peaks. Beneath it waits Aranea Ienith, a priestess who believes the Dragonborn's arrival has already been foretold. She asks the Dragonborn to recover Azura's Star, a Daedric artifact that can hold a soul and survive its use in enchanting. An ordinary soul gem breaks when its stored soul is spent. The Star can be filled again, which makes its disappearance a practical concern as well as a religious one.

Aranea directs the Dragonborn toward an elven mage associated with a vision of a fortress threatened by water. The search leads to Nelacar at the Frozen Hearth in Winterhold. He's a former College mage, living away from the institution where the trouble began. Aranea understands the search through Azura's guidance. Nelacar approaches it through his acquaintance with Malyn Varen, the mage who took the Star into his own research. Between them, they provide a destination and an explanation of what happened there.

Nelacar describes Malyn's attempt to overcome his approaching death. Malyn believed he could place his soul inside Azura's Star and continue to exist there. That ambition depended on altering an artifact whose ordinary purpose was already concerned with souls, though holding a soul for enchantment is very different from sustaining a person indefinitely. Nelacar had been Malyn's student. His account comes from someone who knew the work and its consequences, and who now warns the Dragonborn against returning the artifact to Azura.

Malyn's research led him away from the College and into Ilinalta's Deep, a ruined fort on Lake Ilinalta.  Parts of the structure lie beneath the water, and the surviving passages contain necromancers who resist the Dragonborn's approach. The journey moves through rooms occupied by people continuing their magical work in an isolated ruin. Reaching the Star requires passing through that community before descending to the chamber where Malyn's own body remains.

There, the Dragonborn finds the damaged artifact beside Malyn's remains. His physical death hasn't ended the problem. Malyn persists inside the Star, where his presence prevents it from being restored to use. The discovery answers the question of where the artifact went while creating a more difficult task. Carrying it out of Ilinalta's Deep removes it from the necromancers' refuge. Restoring the Star requires someone to enter it and confront Malyn.

At this point, the Dragonborn can return to Aranea or take the Star to Nelacar. Both routes address Malyn's presence, and both require the Dragonborn to enter the artifact. Their disagreement concerns what the Star should become afterward. Aranea wants it restored to Azura. Nelacar proposes finishing an alteration that will put it beyond the Prince's influence. Choosing a helper therefore determines the form of the reward as well as the circumstances of the journey into it.

Returning to the shrine brings the Dragonborn into direct contact with Azura. Through the restored connection to her artifact, she can send the player inside to confront Malyn. Aranea's faith now has an answering voice, and the task she began as an act of devotion becomes a direct service to her Prince. Azura supplies the means of entry and a way back. The Dragonborn must still undertake the dangerous part personally, entering a place where Malyn has been able to survive after his body failed.

Nelacar offers a different procedure at the Frozen Hearth. He uses soul trapping to send the Dragonborn into the Star and undertakes to bring them back when Malyn is defeated. His plan rests on magical knowledge acquired through the same field of study that produced the damage. He's opposed to Azura's claim, but he also needs someone capable of confronting his former teacher. His assistance makes the expedition possible without requiring the Dragonborn to return to the shrine or accept Azura's direction.

Inside, the Star is an enclosed magical space of crystalline surfaces and narrow paths. Malyn is present there with hostile Dremora, and he treats the arriving Dragonborn as another soul available to sustain him. The promise of prolonged existence has left him dependent on souls brought into his refuge. His body at Ilinalta's Deep and his active presence within the artifact belong to the same failed undertaking. He has continued beyond bodily death, although the result demands a supply of others rather than giving him a secure, independent life.

The Dragonborn defeats Malyn within that space and is then returned by the helper chosen outside. This ends his occupation of the Star and allows the repair to be completed.

On Aranea's path, the reward is Azura's Star restored to its familiar purpose, a reusable vessel for the souls of creatures. Aranea can also become available as a follower after her service at the shrine changes. Following Nelacar's path produces the Black Star, capable of holding the black souls of people. It retains the practical advantage of being reusable after its charge is spent. That difference matters whenever the Dragonborn chooses how to fill it and which souls to use for enchanting.

Neither repair makes the Star a sanctuary for everyone drawn into it. It remains an artifact used to capture souls, with its future use left in the player's hands. Malyn's personal attempt at survival is over, and the necromancers' damaged prize has become a working object again. At the shrine, Aranea receives the fulfillment of the task Azura set before her. At the Frozen Hearth, Nelacar completes his alternative. Whichever route the Dragonborn follows, the recovered Star can now be carried away from the people who made its restoration possible.


Kesh the Clean tends a shrine to Peryite in the hills of the Reach, where an audience with a Daedric Prince requires an unusual preparation. The Khajiit can explain whom he serves and help the Dragonborn approach him, but speaking with Peryite means accepting the effects of the incense Kesh prepares. The shrine has no hall of petitioners or priest offering ordinary treatment. Its attendant provides a way to hear the being associated with the sickness, and that being has a task waiting for someone able to carry it out.

The name Kesh uses for his master is the Taskmaster. Peryite's concern with order gives that title a particular meaning once he begins describing his followers. The people involved are called the Afflicted, and their illness belongs to their service to him. A traveler suffering from the disease can provide an earlier lead toward the shrine. That encounter puts a person on the road before the Dragonborn ever hears the Prince's account. The sickness has already disrupted lives outside the remote place where Kesh maintains his worship.

After the preparation, Peryite addresses the Dragonborn and identifies the person he wants killed: Orchendor, a Wood Elf who has abandoned his assigned responsibility. According to Peryite, Orchendor was supposed to shepherd the Afflicted. Instead, he's gathered them in Bthardamz and turned away from his patron's purpose. Peryite presents this as a failure requiring correction, and he offers a reward for dealing with it. There's no instruction to bring Orchendor back to the shrine for questioning. The errand begins with the intended outcome already decided.

For someone arriving because of the illness, that request changes the practical direction of the journey. Kesh doesn't send the Dragonborn to collect medicine for the people inside Bthardamz. Peryite wants his disobedient overseer removed. His description also makes the Afflicted's position uncomfortable. The disease is part of the work he assigned them, so their suffering doesn't prompt him to reconsider the undertaking. Orchendor's refusal to continue his role is the problem the Prince chooses to address through the player.

Bthardamz is a Dwemer ruin, with an extensive interior that gives Orchendor and his followers room to withdraw. Its stone passages and machinery remain recognizable beneath the presence of the people now occupying it. The Dragonborn passes through inhabited sections as well as ancient structures, following the route farther into the complex. Here, the ruin matters as a refuge with depth and barriers. Reaching Orchendor requires entering the place where the Afflicted have established themselves, far from Kesh and the open hillside around the shrine.

The Afflicted encountered within are hostile, and their sickness affects the fighting. They can project a poisonous attack that makes keeping a distance less protective than it might appear. A corridor that seems to offer space for an archer or a spellcaster can still leave the Dragonborn exposed. The disease Peryite discussed at the shrine is now visible through what his followers can do. They're able to defend the stronghold, and the journey through it demands attention to them as opponents with a dangerous ability.

There are also Dwemer constructs within Bthardamz. Their presence complicates the passage through the ruin, since the people occupying it haven't made its ancient defenses harmless. The Dragonborn has to deal with both the current inhabitants and machinery that still operates around them. That combination gives the search its pace. A room occupied by Afflicted belongs to the same expedition as a passage guarded by a construct, with Orchendor somewhere beyond both. The task remains personal even as the route passes through a much older place.

Conversations overheard among the Afflicted give their retreat a human scale. They speak about Orchendor and their circumstances, rather than existing only as the servants described by Peryite. They've followed someone into this ruin, and their expectations now depend on what that leader tells them. Those exchanges make his role easier to understand. Orchendor has people relying on him while he refuses the authority that originally placed him over them. The Dragonborn arrives carrying orders from the patron whose demands he has stopped obeying.

Orchendor himself is a capable mage, and his defenses make the final confrontation different from the encounters along the way. He can teleport during combat, forcing the Dragonborn to locate him again, and he's immune to magic. A player who has relied on destructive spells must adapt to that protection. Peryite has sent someone after a servant who can resist a considerable range of attacks. Defeating him takes more than reaching the deepest occupied part of Bthardamz and delivering the Prince's displeasure.

Once Orchendor is dead, the Dragonborn can return to Kesh's shrine and report the result. The local question has an answer: the leader sheltering within Bthardamz has been found and removed. Peryite accepts the service and awards Spellbreaker. The quest doesn't end with the Afflicted assembled outside for treatment, nor does Peryite announce their recovery. The completed errand concerns Orchendor's break with him. Whatever brought the Dragonborn to the shrine, the reward follows the completion of that particular order.

Spellbreaker is a shield with a magical defense of its own. When raised, it produces a ward that can absorb incoming spells until the ward's limit is exceeded. Its usefulness is easy to recognize after a journey through poison attacks, hostile magic, and working Dwemer defenses. The Dragonborn leaves with protection that can be carried into other dangerous places. Peryite has paid for the task in a form suited to someone who survived it, and receiving the artifact doesn't require resolving the future of everyone encountered underground.


Knight-Paladin Gelebor still tends a shrine of Auriel in Darkfall Cave. He's a Snow Elf, recognizable as a member of the people whose ruined sanctuaries the Dragonborn is about to enter. He can speak about the Chantry as someone who served there, and he understands what its abandoned passages were meant to accomplish. For someone seeking Auriel's Bow, that makes him an extraordinary guide. The route ahead belongs to a religious life he remembers, even though the worshippers who once followed it are gone.

Gelebor recognizes the purpose of the visit. Auriel's Bow lies within the Chantry, but reaching it requires passing through the old pilgrimage. He also asks the Dragonborn to deal with his brother, Arch-Curate Vyrthur, who occupies the Inner Sanctum. Gelebor believes the Falmer have corrupted him. He speaks with the sorrow of someone who has lost both a community and a brother, although Vyrthur's actual condition will turn out to differ from his explanation.

Before the journey begins, Gelebor gives the Dragonborn an initiate's ewer. The vessel has a particular use at the wayshrines along the route, where initiates once collected water as they advanced toward the Inner Sanctum. Reaching the bow therefore involves completing actions that belonged to the Chantry's worship. Gelebor opens the way, and the Dragonborn and Serana carry a piece of that ceremony into passages where the institution itself can no longer receive them as it once did.

The first stretch leads through Darkfall Passage. Its caves separate Gelebor's shrine from the open reaches of the Forgotten Vale, so the journey begins without revealing the sanctuary's full extent. Falmer occupy the route. Their presence gives Gelebor's grief a physical setting: the descendants he calls the Betrayed now inhabit places connected with his people's devotion. He's aware of the connection between them, and his language allows that shared history to remain present even when the Dragonborn encounters them as enemies.

Emerging into the Forgotten Vale changes the scale of the pilgrimage. The Chantry extends across a mountain valley, with wayshrines placed far enough apart that visiting them requires a substantial journey. There are frozen stretches, waterways, and Falmer settlements between its sacred sites. The Dragonborn can't reach the Inner Sanctum by walking directly to its entrance and demanding admission. The ewer has to be filled along the appointed route, and the valley makes that requirement into an exploration of the surviving sanctuary.

At the wayshrines, spectral prelates continue to attend their stations. They recognize the form of a pilgrimage and allow the initiate to proceed. Water is collected, and the opened shrines provide portals connecting parts of the journey. These encounters preserve a different side of the Snow Elves from the ruined buildings alone. Their priests had duties, their worshippers followed a prescribed course, and the sanctuary had a means of receiving people who came seeking enlightenment. Some of that ceremony still works.

Gelebor's account gives the Falmer encountered along the way a history beyond their immediate hostility. He mourns what happened to them, yet he also speaks about signs of increasing intelligence among them. That observation leaves him with a cautious hope that understanding might someday become possible. He doesn't offer the Dragonborn a way to accomplish it during this visit. Within the Vale, the practical task remains dangerous, while the living knight-paladin continues to think about what the Betrayed might still become.

The route eventually passes through the Glacial Crevice and continues toward the great temple. By then, the ewer has been carried through caves and across the Vale to the required wayshrines. Pouring its water into the basin at the Inner Sanctum completes the ceremonial requirement and opens the entrance. An ancient religious practice has brought a mortal visitor to the place Harkon's search requires. Serana has followed that practice too, although neither she nor the Dragonborn came as an ordinary worshipper.

Inside, Vyrthur waits among frozen Falmer and chaurus. The stillness doesn't last. He brings the creatures into the confrontation, and the battle damages the sanctuary around him. As the encounter continues, the space that seemed to preserve the old Chantry becomes the setting for its further destruction. Vyrthur's position as Arch-Curate gives him the appearance of a surviving religious authority, but his actions leave no peaceful meeting available. Serana and the Dragonborn have to survive before they can reach him.

When Serana confronts Vyrthur, his explanation overturns Gelebor's account of his brother's fall. Vyrthur is a vampire. He says an initiate infected him, and he blames Auriel for failing to protect him despite his devotion. His anger has become a plan to wound the god through the sun. Vyrthur reveals that he created the prophecy Harkon has been pursuing, arranging a path by which Auriel's Bow and the blood of a Daughter of Coldharbour could be brought together.

For Serana, that revelation makes the meeting painfully immediate. Vyrthur's scheme depends on someone like her, and her father's pursuit of the prophecy has helped bring her within his reach. The supposed promise of a future for vampires has its origin in this priest's resentment toward Auriel. Serana answers him herself and challenges the position from which he has spoken. Vyrthur then has to face the Dragonborn in the final confrontation, and his defeat ends his attempt to obtain what the prophecy requires.

Gelebor appears afterward and learns what became of his brother. With Vyrthur defeated, Auriel's Bow can be taken from the sanctuary. Gelebor can also bless elven arrows for use with it, so his service to Auriel continues in a practical form after the pilgrimage is complete. The Forgotten Vale has yielded both the weapon and an answer to the prophecy's origin. Its living witness remains at the Chantry, able to speak for a people whose surviving buildings can tell only part of their history. Serana leaves having confronted the man who turned his grievance into a danger to her life.


The cultists who approach the Dragonborn in Skyrim already have a name for the person they serve. They insist that Miraak is the true Dragonborn, and their challenge ends in an attack. Orders carried by the cultists point back to Solstheim, across the water from Skyrim. Someone on that island has taken an interest in the traveler who can absorb dragon souls. Before the Dragonborn has seen Miraak's temple or heard his voice, his followers have made the journey to find them.

Passage from Windhelm brings the investigation to Raven Rock aboard the Northern Maiden. Its captain, Gjalund Salt-Sage, has reason to be uneasy about the return voyage. He remembers carrying the cultists, though parts of the experience are unclear to him. On arrival, asking about Miraak produces another kind of uncertainty. The name seems familiar to people who can't readily explain where they've heard it. The trail continues beyond the harbor, toward the Earth Stone, where the work underway gives that uncertainty a visible shape.

People gather around the stone and labor on a structure dedicated to Miraak. Their repeated words concern his coming return, and ordinary conversation struggles to interrupt their work. Neloth, observing nearby, isn't caught in the same condition. He directs attention toward Miraak's temple elsewhere on the island. The contrast is unsettling in a practical way. Raven Rock still has its shops, disputes and daily business, yet some of its people spend time building something they don't consciously remember agreeing to build.

Sleeping on Solstheim can bring the Dragonborn into that work as well. Instead of waking where they rested, they may find themselves beside an All-Maker Stone, taking part in the construction. Walking away is possible, but the displaced awakening makes the island's missing hours more immediate. The people at the stones aren't merely repeating a story they've chosen to believe. Miraak's influence reaches into their sleep and brings their bodies to places where his monuments are rising. A visitor can experience the same interruption.

At the Temple of Miraak, Frea has come looking for members of her own community. She's a Skaal warrior, and the workers outside include people she knows. Her attempts to reach them don't break the compulsion. She remains able to act for herself and joins the Dragonborn in searching the temple. That gives the descent a purpose beyond finding the man named in the cultists' orders. Frea wants to understand what's happening to her people and find a way to bring them home.

The temple extends beneath the construction site into chambers occupied by cultists and draugr. Frea accompanies the Dragonborn through its passages, responding to the dangers they encounter rather than waiting outside for an answer. The building connects Miraak's present followers with an older place of worship. His name belongs here already. As the pair travel deeper, the excavation becomes easier to understand as a return to an existing center of devotion. The workers above are adding to a temple whose underground halls have survived his absence.

Within those halls, they find a Black Book. Reading it carries the Dragonborn into Apocrypha, the realm of Hermaeus Mora, where Miraak appears in person. Frea doesn't share the encounter. For the Dragonborn, the search suddenly reaches the figure whose influence has spread across Solstheim. Miraak recognizes what the visitor is and measures their strength through the dragon souls they've absorbed. His own command of the Voice makes the meeting unequal, and he sends the Dragonborn away after demonstrating how little resistance the newcomer can offer him there.

Miraak is the first Dragonborn, an ancient dragon priest who turned against the dragons he once served. His continued existence in Apocrypha explains why no journey across Solstheim's roads can bring the investigator directly to his present home. He seeks a way out of Hermaeus Mora's realm, and his activity on the island is part of that effort. The cultists, the temple and the compelled workers give him a presence in a place he hasn't yet returned to bodily. Frea sees the Dragonborn recover beside the book and hears what happened beyond her sight.

At Skaal Village, Frea's father, Storn, helps turn that encounter into a task the Dragonborn can attempt. He directs the search toward a Word of Power at Saering's Watch. There, the Dragonborn learns the first word of Bend Will. Its immediate use concerns the All-Maker Stones. Miraak has imposed his influence on places sacred to the Skaal, so reaching the people caught around them requires a way to break the enchantment that holds the stone itself.

Using Bend Will on the Wind Stone breaks the structure built around it and releases the workers. A lurker emerges, leaving a creature to defeat before the danger has passed. Afterward, the Skaal who had been working there can return to their village. The same Shout can cleanse other affected stones across Solstheim. Each cleansing changes an identifiable place and frees the people gathered there. Frea's search produces a result she can see in the return of her neighbors, even while Miraak remains beyond reach.

Miraak can also intrude when the Dragonborn defeats a dragon. He appears at the fallen creature and takes its soul, depriving the player of the reward they would ordinarily absorb. His distant residence doesn't prevent this interference. Dragon souls are the means by which the Dragonborn unlocks learned words, so his theft can obstruct the growth of the very power needed to oppose him. The rivalry introduced by his cultists now follows the Dragonborn into encounters that would otherwise have little connection to Solstheim's temple.

Bend Will eventually reaches beyond cleansing stones and allows the Dragonborn to command a dragon. That development makes Miraak's own use of dragons easier to recognize as a power the player can approach. Miraak still waits among the books of Apocrypha, beyond the reach of the people whose labor he commanded.


Ralis Sedarys has reached Kolbjorn Barrow, but the ash has left him with very little tomb to explore. Outside Raven Rock, the buried entrance presents a practical problem before it presents an ancient mystery. Someone has to pay workers to clear it. Ralis needs a backer, and the Dragonborn can supply the money. His proposal begins with the familiar promise of an excavation: clear away what's accumulated above a ruin, reach the chambers below, and recover something worth the expense of getting there.

Ralis talks about the difficulty of doing this work alone. Ash keeps frustrating his efforts, and an expedition needs hired hands. The first payment allows him to recruit miners and begin a larger operation. The player then leaves while the work proceeds. That separation matters to the way Unearthed unfolds. The Dragonborn isn't standing beside every worker as a passage opens. Progress arrives through messages from Ralis, followed by a return to a camp that has changed during the absence.

On the first return, the excavation has reached far enough to disturb the dead. Draugr have attacked the miners, and Ralis needs the underground chambers cleared before work can continue. The people hired to uncover the barrow have become casualties of entering it. Inside, the Dragonborn deals with enemies that ordinary excavation workers couldn't safely face. A route that looked like a matter of labor and money now requires someone equipped to fight through a Nordic tomb.

The exposed chambers also begin to deliver what Ralis had hoped to find. Ahzidal's Boots of Waterwalking are among the discoveries available as the excavation advances. Their enchantment gives the ancient name an immediate, usable presence. The Dragonborn can take an object out of the barrow and experience its magic beyond the ruin. For someone deciding whether to fund another stage, a discovery like that gives the promise of further treasure a concrete basis, even after the deaths below.

Ralis asks for more money, hires another group, and continues. Later visits expose additional passages, with further draugr attacks interrupting the work. The camp grows into an undertaking with miners and hired protection, yet the danger keeps returning as the excavation reaches deeper. Each payment answers an immediate difficulty. More workers can clear the ash. Guards can help protect them. The player can remove the draugr already awake. None of those measures makes the unopened chambers safe.

The repeated journeys give the barrow a different pace from a ruin explored in a single visit. A corridor that ended in debris can become a route into another room. New areas bring puzzles and enchanted objects associated with Ahzidal, including rings with unusual magical effects. The player learns the building in sections, returning to familiar ground before passing beyond it. Meanwhile, Ralis stays at the dig. His written account continues during the intervals when the Dragonborn is elsewhere, and it begins to disclose trouble that his requests for funding don't fully express.

His journals initially belong to the recognizable concerns of an excavator trying to make a venture succeed. As the work progresses, dreams and a voice intrude upon those concerns. Ahzidal occupies more of his attention. The volumes give that change room to develop across successive stages, rather than introducing it only when the final chamber opens. Read alongside the changing camp, they suggest that Ralis's growing commitment to the excavation is being shaped by something beneath it. His persistence has acquired a source the player couldn't see in the original proposal.

Ralis still has practical explanations to offer when the Dragonborn returns. Workers have died, the draugr need clearing, and another investment could allow the expedition to proceed. The player has already spent money and recovered evidence that valuable artifacts really are present. That combination makes continuing understandable without making it harmless. An additional payment means sending more people into the same undertaking. The journals leave an increasingly uncomfortable companion to the ordinary business of hiring miners and arranging the next stage.

The final descent finds Ralis summoning Ahzidal, the dragon priest whose influence has grown through the excavation. His later journals implicate him in killing workers for Ahzidal's return, so the deaths cannot all be attributed to awakened draugr. The Dragonborn must defeat Ahzidal before confronting Ralis about his part in what happened.

Afterward, Ralis speaks as someone emerging from an experience he can't fully account for. He says that Ahzidal was in his head and describes his recollection of what happened as confused. His journals and the summoning give reason to take the priest's influence seriously, though they don't settle exactly how much awareness Ralis retained throughout the excavation. His explanation comes after the Dragonborn has seen him at the center of it. The player now has to respond to both his account and the consequences spread through the barrow.

Ralis can be killed for his part in those deaths, or he can be spared. Sparing him allows him to leave the excavation, and he can later be found at the Retching Netch in Raven Rock, available as a follower. That's a substantial change in how the Dragonborn deals with him. The person who repeatedly asked for funding can become a companion on the road. The quest permits that future without undoing what happened to the people who worked for him at Kolbjorn.

The recovered artifacts remain useful, and the chambers remain open after Ahzidal's defeat. Ralis's original promise has therefore come true in a grimly limited way: the barrow did contain remarkable things. Reaching them also exposed workers to repeated danger and eventually helped bring an ancient priest into a confrontation with the living. When the Dragonborn leaves, there's no further payment waiting to turn the next stretch of ash into a safe passage. The excavation has reached the presence that kept drawing Ralis farther down.


Neloth approaches the Black Books with the habits of someone who expects dangerous magic to yield useful information. At Tel Mithryn, he's studying objects that other people would have good reason to leave alone. He knows enough about the books to help the Dragonborn locate another, and he accompanies the search into Nchardak. His confidence gives the expedition a practical rhythm. There's a book inside a sealed chamber, and reaching it means understanding the machinery around it before anyone can read what's waiting within.

Inside Nchardak, Neloth's confidence has something concrete behind it. He explains the control cubes and machinery, helping the Dragonborn work through flooded chambers toward the steam supply needed to release the Black Book. The book can be seen in the reading room before it can be reached. For Neloth, its barrier belongs to the ruin's working systems, and his explanations help make those systems usable. That gives the Dragonborn a knowledgeable companion through this part of the search. Releasing the book completes their work with the machinery; opening it will carry its reader somewhere Neloth's guidance through Nchardak can't accompany them.

That manner carries into his interest in Hermaeus Mora. Neloth understands that the books are connected to the Daedric Prince, yet the danger hasn't discouraged his research. 

Within Epistolary Acumen, progress leads through a library inhabited by seekers and lurkers. Moving platforms and passages carry the Dragonborn between sections, and the book's chapters open further routes. This is a library whose arrangement has to be navigated physically. Knowledge waits beyond creatures that defend the place and mechanisms that determine where the reader can go. At the end, Mora supplies another word of Bend Will. He also names the price of the final word: the knowledge preserved by the Skaal.

Mora describes the Skaal's secrets as something withheld from him. His demand brings the Dragonborn back to people whose traditions have survived through teaching and observance in their own village. Storn Crag-Strider is their shaman, and the decision reaches him in that role. Miraak threatens the Skaal, while the power needed to confront him remains incomplete. Mora has made access to that power depend upon Storn surrendering something the Prince wants. The Dragonborn can carry the demand, but Storn must decide whether he'll open the book.

Storn agrees because he sees a way to help his people escape Miraak's influence. Frea objects to the danger facing her father, and her presence keeps the exchange from becoming a distant transaction between a hero and a Daedric Prince. Storn reads the Black Book in the village. Mora reaches through it, takes the knowledge he sought, and Storn dies during the transfer. The final word of Bend Will becomes available to the Dragonborn. Frea is left beside her father after the bargain has delivered its promised power.

Waking Dreams provides the way back into Apocrypha for the confrontation. Its chapters lead through another sequence of rooms and passages, with books themselves used to solve a puzzle that opens the onward route. Reaching Miraak takes further movement through the realm rather than a single step from the Skaal village into battle. Eventually, the Dragonborn encounters the dragon Sahrotaar. The completed Bend Will can bring him under the Dragonborn's command, and he carries the rider toward Miraak at the summit of Apocrypha.

Miraak fights with powers familiar to another Dragonborn. He uses Shouts and restores himself by taking the souls of dragons during the battle. The souls sustain him as the confrontation continues, so overcoming him means surviving those renewals as well as his attacks. Sahrotaar, who carried the Dragonborn to the summit, is among the dragons Miraak consumes. The journey has brought the player close enough to challenge him, while the fight displays how readily he spends the lives around him to preserve his own.

When Miraak is defeated, Mora intervenes and kills him. The Prince speaks of replacing his former servant with the Dragonborn. That's Mora's claim upon the outcome, delivered in his realm after he has supplied the knowledge that helped make victory possible. Miraak's attempt to escape ends there. The Dragonborn survives and receives his souls, but the scene gives no equally final settlement of the survivor's future dealings with Mora. Leaving Apocrypha remains possible, even as its ruler describes the victory as useful to him.

Frea's warning belongs beside that claim. She urges the Dragonborn to remain alert to Mora's influence after Miraak has been overcome. She's watched a bargain take her father, and she has reason to treat the Prince's assistance with caution. Her advice leaves room for the Dragonborn to act without accepting Mora's description of what they have become. Miraak's defeat has ended a particular threat to Solstheim. It hasn't made every offer of knowledge safe, or given Frea any reason to forget the cost paid in her village.

The book allows the Dragonborn to return to Solstheim. After Apocrypha's moving passages and the battle above them, there are again paths across the island that can be traveled without turning a page. Frea remains with the Skaal, who must continue without Storn. The Black Book also remains among the things the Dragonborn carries. Its cover can be closed, and the journey across Solstheim can continue with it still in their possession.

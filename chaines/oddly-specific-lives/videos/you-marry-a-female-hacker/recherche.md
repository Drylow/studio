# Recherche et consignes — POV: You Marry a Female Hacker

Faits vérifiés et règles données au script.

VERIFIED FACTS about her world (use several, rounded, each inside a scene, with the source named in the sentence; never invent other statistics):
- ISC2's 2024 women in cybersecurity research found women make up about 22 percent of security teams on average (around 26 percent among workers under 30).
- DEF CON, held every August in Las Vegas since 1993, is one of the world's largest hacker conventions, with around 30,000 attendees.
- In 1947 engineers working on the Harvard Mark II computer found a moth stuck in a relay and taped it into their logbook as the "first actual case of bug being found"; Grace Hopper loved telling the story (the logbook page is at the Smithsonian).
- Companies and governments legally pay "penetration testers" and bug bounty hunters to break into their own systems and report the holes before criminals find them.
- "White hat" hackers work with permission, "black hat" without; "grey hat" is everything in between.
STORY SHAPE: Portland, Oregon. "You" are a baker, 32, who owns a tiny bakery, still uses a flip phone and writes orders on paper; your password for everything is the name of your sourdough starter. She is FICTIONAL: Juno Park, 30, Korean-American, known online only by a handle (invent one), a legendary former grey hat who now gets paid by big companies to break into them (legally); dry, sharp, sleeps four hours, funny, paranoid in very specific ways. First meeting: she comes every morning at 6:05, pays cash, sits facing the door, tape over her laptop camera; one day she quietly tells you your card reader is "a crime scene waiting to happen" and fixes it. Small tells: she owns many phones, never posts photos, unplugs your smart speaker at her first dinner, reads every terms-and-conditions page, knows things about your life you never told her (then admits it and apologizes). The reveal ~35%: an old part of her past (a group she exposed years ago, fictional) still wants her name; she tells you everything at the bakery counter at 2 a.m. Wedding by ~55%: a Las Vegas chapel during DEF CON week in August, guests in hoodies, her friends only use handles, the officiant is confused; honeymoon in a cabin with no internet on the Oregon coast and she is the happiest you have ever seen her. Married life: your smart fridge is banned, she changes your sourdough password, she quietly stops a phone scammer targeting your grandmother (off screen, no methods), a tense week when someone tries to expose her online and she disappears into her screens. Ending: she goes fully white hat and starts a free weekend class teaching teenage girls cybersecurity in the back of the bakery; quiet ending at 6:05 a.m., she still tapes the camera, and now so do you.
RULES: she is fictional; never name or depict a real hacker, hacking group, company, product, app or real cyber attack (brands never named: "a big bank", "a phone maker"); never explain hacking methods, tools, commands, exploits, scams or how to avoid getting caught (her skill is shown by results and character tells only); crime is never glamorized: the tone is dry, warm, funny and a little tense; she is competent and chooses you; never sexualize her; no fake on-screen code.

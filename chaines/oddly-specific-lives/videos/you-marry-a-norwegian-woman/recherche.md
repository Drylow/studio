# Recherche et consignes — POV: You Marry a Norwegian Woman

Faits vérifiés et règles données au script.

VERIFIED FACTS about her world (use several, rounded, each inside a scene, with the source named in the sentence; never invent other statistics):
- "Janteloven", the Law of Jante, comes from Aksel Sandemose's 1933 novel "A Fugitive Crosses His Tracks": the unwritten Scandinavian rule "don't think you're better than anyone".
- "Friluftsliv" (open-air life) was popularised by Henrik Ibsen in his 1859 poem "On the Heights"; Norwegians go outside in every weather ("there's no bad weather, only bad clothes").
- "Allemannsretten", the right to roam (Outdoor Recreation Act, 1957): anyone may walk on uncultivated land and pitch a tent for up to two nights in one spot, at least 150 metres from the nearest house, without asking.
- Norway has more than 400,000 holiday cabins ("hytte") according to Statistics Norway; many have no running water.
- "Matpakke": the packed lunch of open sandwiches wrapped in paper that most Norwegians take to work, brown cheese (brunost) on top. Friday is "taco Friday" in many homes.
- May 17 is Constitution Day (the 1814 constitution): children's parades, flags everywhere, people in their "bunad", the regional folk costume.
- The traditional Norwegian wedding cake is the "kransekake", a tower of almond rings.
- In Tromsø, during the polar night, the sun does not rise above the horizon from late November to mid-January.
- Norwegian parents share about a year of paid parental leave, with 15 weeks reserved for each parent (NAV).

Married life: matpakke every morning, taco Friday, camping anywhere (right to roam), you finally swim in the winter sea once; a baby and shared leave; quiet ending: the first sunrise after the polar night, the whole town outside, she squeezes your hand.

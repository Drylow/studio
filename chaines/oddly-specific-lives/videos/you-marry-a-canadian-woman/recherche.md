# Recherche et consignes — POV: You Marry a Canadian Woman

Faits vérifiés et règles données au script.

VERIFIED FACTS (name the source in the sentence; never invent figures):
- Canadian Thanksgiving is on the second Monday of October; a proclamation of 31 January 1957 fixed that date (The Canadian Encyclopedia).
- Poutine (fries, cheese curds, gravy) comes from rural Quebec in the late 1950s; the most cited story is Fernand Lachance's snack bar in Warwick, 1957, but Drummondville also claims it, and nobody agrees (The Canadian Encyclopedia). Fresh curds squeak against your teeth.
- Tim Hortons was founded in 1964 in Hamilton, Ontario, by the hockey player Tim Horton; a "double-double" is a coffee with two creams and two sugars.
- Quebec produces roughly 70 per cent of the world's maple syrup, and its producers keep a strategic reserve of syrup, set up in 2000 (CTV News, Quebec Maple Syrup Producers). Spring sugar shacks (cabanes à sucre) serve maple taffy poured on snow.
- Canada has two official languages, English and French (Official Languages Act, 1969); cereal boxes and road signs are bilingual.
- Almost half of Toronto's people were born outside Canada; the City of Toronto calls it one of the most multicultural cities in the world.
- At minus forty, Celsius and Fahrenheit read the same; parts of Canada fall below that in winter. Many drivers on the Prairies plug their car's block heater into an outlet overnight.
- Canada is often said to have about two million lakes (estimate; say "around").
STORY SHAPE: modern day. "You" are unnamed, an American (from Arizona) who moves to Winnipeg in November for work. She: Claire Tremblay-Okafor, 31, a hockey-mad paramedic, half Québécoise (her mother's family in a village near Drummondville), fictional. Meet: your car will not start at minus 32 because you never plugged in the block heater; she jump-starts it and lectures you. Beats: Tim Hortons order taught like a language lesson; first winter, layers, the cold that makes your nostrils freeze; outdoor rink and her beer-league team; meeting her family at Thanksgiving in October (her mother's tourtière, the poutine argument Warwick vs Drummondville, her dad's French you can't follow); spring sugar shack, taffy on snow; summer at the lake cottage, loons, mosquitoes, the dock; proposal; wedding in Quebec, bilingual speeches; the first winter you plug the car in yourself; ending on your own double-double order.
RULES: fictional characters only, no real people, no brands or logos on images (Tim Hortons named in narration only, never drawn), no stereotypes played as jokes about Canadians being dumb or weak, warm and funny, proper romance, advertiser-friendly. 25 minutes minimum.

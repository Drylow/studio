# Recherche et consignes — POV: You Marry a Female Assassin

Faits vérifiés et règles données au script.

VERIFIED FACTS about her world (use several, rounded, each inside a scene, with the source named in the sentence; never invent other statistics):
- In 2014, criminologists at Birmingham City University (Professor David Wilson and colleagues, published in The Howard Journal of Criminal Justice) published the first study of British contract killers: 27 contract killings carried out on the British mainland between 1974 and 2013, by 35 hitmen and one hitwoman.
- In that study the average fee was about 15,000 pounds (15,180); the cheapest was 200 pounds, the most expensive 100,000 pounds.
- The average British contract killer in the study was 38 years old (the youngest 15, the oldest 63); the average victim was 36.
- Most of those killings happened in ordinary suburban streets, often while the victim was walking the dog or going shopping.
- The study sorted contract killers into four types: the novice, the dilettante, the journeyman (capable, experienced, reliable) and the master: the least likely ever to be caught, sometimes with a military background.
STORY SHAPE: "You" are an ordinary man in London, early thirties, a primary-school music teacher in north London with a slightly anxious beagle called Biscuit. She is FICTIONAL: calm, warm, funny in a very dry way, 34, says she is a freelance "risk consultant" who travels for work; in the study's words she is a "master", the kind that never gets caught, ex-army. First meeting: Hampstead Heath on a Sunday morning, Biscuit runs off and she catches him without looking up from her coffee. Small tells you only understand later: she always sits facing the door, knows every exit of every restaurant, never appears in photos, pays cash, owns three phones, changes the dog-walk route every single day (dark irony: the study says most hits happened on dog walks), sleeps lightly, can't stand fireworks. Her world shown sideways and off screen: early-morning "work trips" with a small grey suitcase, a locked drawer, a second passport you find by accident, a phone that rings at 3 a.m., a man in a car outside who leaves when she looks at him. The reveal around 40%: she tells you the truth at the kitchen table, calmly, and that she is trying to stop: "one more year". Wedding by ~60% at Old Marylebone Town Hall (a real London register office), a small Sunday roast at a pub after, she insists nobody takes pictures; honeymoon on the Isle of Skye (no crowds, no cameras). Married life: you learn which questions not to ask, she teaches you to notice exits, Biscuit adores her, someone from her past shows up and she handles it off screen, she finally retires and takes up a ridiculously ordinary hobby (pottery evening class), quiet ending: 6 a.m., same dog walk, she still changes the route.
RULES: she is fictional, no real criminal, case, gang or victim is named or depicted (the study is only quoted for its numbers); violence is always implied and off screen, never graphic, no blood; never describe methods, weapons handling, planning or how to avoid being caught (no tradecraft that could work as instructions: just her habits as character tells); never glamorize killing: the tone is dry, calm, a little dark and funny, with real unease; she is competent and chooses you; never sexualize her; London places used correctly (Hampstead Heath, the Northern line, Borough Market, Columbia Road flower market on Sundays, a pub Sunday roast, Old Marylebone Town Hall, the Isle of Skye).

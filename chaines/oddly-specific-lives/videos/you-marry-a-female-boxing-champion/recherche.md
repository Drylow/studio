# Recherche et consignes — POV: You Marry a Female Boxing Champion

Faits vérifiés et règles données au script.

VERIFIED FACTS about her world (use several, rounded, each inside a scene, with the source named in the sentence; never invent other statistics):
- Women's boxing was in the Olympic Games for the first time at London 2012, in three weight classes.
- Women's professional title fights have traditionally been 10 rounds of 2 minutes, while men box 12 rounds of 3 minutes.
- In April 2022, a women's fight headlined Madison Square Garden for the first time in the arena's history.
- Being "undisputed" means holding the belts of all four major sanctioning bodies at once.
- Professional boxers weigh in the day before the fight; many cut water weight to make the limit and rehydrate overnight.

She is FICTIONAL: Dani, 28, Philly born, the coach of that Tuesday class and a world champion chasing the undisputed title; blunt, funny, eats like a machine outside camp, gentle with kids at the gym. First meeting: she holds the pads for you, you hit them like a wet towel, she laughs and tells you to come back Thursday. Dates around her schedule: 5 a.m. runs up the art museum steps, cheesesteaks only after weigh-ins, Reading Terminal Market. Fight camp changes her: quiet, hungry, asleep at 9. Her first loss at ~35%: you see her rebuild; you learn to hold pads properly. Wedding by ~55%: planned between two camps, she can't eat the cake because of a weigh-in in six weeks, the whole gym in suits. Married life: you sit ringside and can't watch, you do her taxes, the long nights of ice and swollen hands, she wins the fight that makes her undisputed (in a big arena, no names, no logos), she starts coaching kids; quiet ending: 5 a.m., the steps, she lets you win the race up, barely.
RULES: she is fictional, no real boxer, promoter, belt organisation logo or arena branding; belts and gloves without logos; fights shown with energy but no blood, no serious injuries; never sexualize her (athletic, strong, sportswear); real Philadelphia places used correctly. Advertiser-friendly.

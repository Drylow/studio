# Recherche et consignes — POV: You Fall in Love With a Female Billionaire

Faits vérifiés et règles données au script.

VERIFIED FACTS about her world (use several, rounded, each inside a scene, with the source named in the sentence; never invent other statistics):
- Forbes' 2024 World's Billionaires list counted a record 2,781 billionaires; only 369 of them were women, about 13 percent (Forbes, April 2024). Most women on the list inherited their fortune; self-made women are a small minority of that group (Forbes).
- A Harris Poll for Axios in 2022 found that 15 percent of married Americans had signed a prenuptial agreement, up from 3 percent in a 2010 survey (Axios/Harris Poll).
- Ernst & Young has estimated that there are at least 10,000 single-family offices worldwide, private companies that manage one rich family's money, staff and affairs (EY family office guide).
- The superyacht industry's rule of thumb is that a yacht costs roughly 10 percent of its purchase price every year just to run: crew, fuel, insurance, berths (yacht brokers' standard estimate; present as a rule of thumb).
- A new large-cabin business jet such as a Gulfstream G650 was listed at around 65 million dollars (aviation press; present as "around").
- Billionaires' net worth is mostly shares of their company, not cash; Forbes values them from stock prices, so a fortune can fall by billions in one day of trading (Forbes methodology).
STORY SHAPE: today, a big American city (San Francisco, then New York). "You" are FICTIONAL and UNNAMED ("you", never a name): 29, you repair bicycles in a tiny rented shop, broke, funny, honest, good with your hands, terrible with money talk. She is FICTIONAL: Victoria Hale, 33, founder of a logistics software company, self-made, on the billionaire list for two years; sharp, dry, punctual to the minute, never carries cash, secretly eats instant noodles, cannot ride a bike. HOOK: she walks into your shop with a broken vintage bicycle, her assistant tries to pay with a corporate card your reader refuses, she has no cash at all; you fix it for free; she sends a "thank you" that turns out to be a cheque you are scared to cash. 0-20%: you learn her world: security, the assistant, her calendar booked in 15-minute blocks, the family office, the board. 20-45%: secret dates that she schedules like meetings; you teach her to ride a bike in an empty parking lot at dawn; she takes you on her jet and you ask where the seatbelt for the sofa is; you insist on paying for one dinner and it costs a week of your income; the press notices you. ~45%: her lawyers bring a prenup thicker than a phone book; you sign everything without reading the money part and add one handwritten clause: "she rides a bike once a week"; small wedding, not the gala everyone expected. 45-55%: married life: two apartments, her security learning your shop, you still fixing bikes, her mother loving you, her board hating you. 55-85% (at least 9 scenes): (1) the yacht she bought and the running costs you calculate on a napkin; (2) her company's stock falls and she loses billions on paper in one day while you lose your bike pump; (3) paparazzi and a tabloid calling you a gold digger; (4) your friends treating you differently; (5) she works through your anniversary, you bring noodles to her office at 2 a.m.; (6) a fake "friend" tries to sell your story; (7) her board tries to push her out as CEO; (8) you nearly leave, she finds you in your old shop; (9) she sells the yacht. CRISIS ~80%: the board vote; she loses the CEO title but keeps her shares; she shows up at your shop in jeans with nothing scheduled. 85-100%: she builds something small again, on her own terms; you open a second shop together, a free repair day for kids every Saturday. ENDING: dawn, the same parking lot, she rides the vintage bike without wobbling, then stops and asks you to hold the seat anyway. Last image: two bikes leaning on each other outside the little shop.
RULES: you, Victoria, her assistant, mother and board are fictional; never name or depict real billionaires, real companies, real brands or logos (no Gulfstream logo, no Forbes cover with a real person); Forbes, Axios, EY only as sources of findings; adult romance stays proper (dates, one kiss at most, off screen), nothing sexual; no financial advice; modern setting 2020s: offices, glass towers, a bike shop, private jet cabin, a yacht deck, city streets; advertiser-friendly.

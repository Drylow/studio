# POV: You Have to Survive a Zombie Apocalypse (Locked in a Hospital)

Someone won't stop pushing Room 112's door, and you can't reach it. Your appendix came out yesterday, and the pull across your stomach puts you back in bed. At seven, someone in hospital slippers leans against the window in your door. Nadia calls. Her ringtone turns their head toward your phone. You silence it. Beyond the window by your bed, the park's paths are empty in the morning light. Your pitcher is empty. The juice box on your tray is all you can reach, and the sealed water is at the nurses' station, outside that door.

## Day 1: The door to the water
The figure follows the last vibration of your phone against the tray. You turn the screen face down and leave Nadia's call unanswered. The figure stays at the door.

Room 112 has one bed against the inner wall, a bathroom on your left, and the corridor door opposite the bed. The window on your right looks over a sunny park, a pond, and the skyline beyond it. Nadia's get-well card sits beside the juice box on your tray. A plastic wristband still has your name on it. There's no nurse here to read it.

You work nights at a warehouse. Nadia lives in another state, and she's used to calling when your shift ends. You put the straw between your lips and drink without sitting up. It helps. Your pitcher is still empty.

Across the corridor, you can see sealed water on a cart behind the nurses' station. Getting there means opening the door with someone standing against it.

An alarm starts somewhere near the east end of the hall. The figure turns its head. You wait as its slippers drag toward the sound, then ease yourself upright with one hand pressed to your stomach. By the time you reach the door, the alarm is still sounding.

You check through the little window.

Room 112 is on the north side of the ground-floor corridor, with the nurses' station opposite. The cafeteria passage runs south from beside the station. The pharmacy is farther north. Stairwell B is at the west end, and radiology has double doors to the east. You cross the few steps to the cart. You take two bottles. Your wristband taps one of them, and you hold still until you're sure the sound hasn't carried.

Back inside, you close your door gently. The alarm stops. You drink from one bottle and sit by the window until your breathing settles.

Several more figures are near the pond now. A car alarm starts across the park, and one of them slowly turns toward it.

## Day 2: The wrong kind of breakfast
Morning light falls on the second bottle. You leave its seal alone. The bathroom tap still runs, so you use it to wash your hands, but you can't tell whether the water will stay safe to drink. The bottle goes beside your pillow.

A television is on in an empty room across the corridor. A reporter says the infected follow noise and light, then asks where the emergency crews are going. The answer changes twice before you catch it.

Nadia calls. You let it ring while footsteps pass your door. When she calls again, you answer with the phone against your ear and your voice low.

"Are you still there?"

"I'm inside."

"I can try to get to you."

You look at the park. More figures have gathered along its noisy street edge.

"Stay where you are," you say.

She asks when you can leave. You tell her you don't know. For a few seconds, she talks about the weather back home, and you can almost picture your kitchen after a shift. Then the call ends, and you can hear the television again.

You need breakfast. At the nurses' station, the counter faces Room 112. The water cart is behind it, a small staff refrigerator is on the left, and the cafeteria hall is visible to the right. You find sealed crackers in a patient-snack drawer and take a few packets, holding them against your chest so you don't twist.

A metal tray falls somewhere beyond radiology. Slow footsteps turn toward it. You crouch behind the counter. Your stomach pulls sharply, and you stay there while slippers cross the tile on the other side.

When the sound fades, you make it back to Room 112. You set the crackers beside Nadia's card and tuck the sealed bottle into your pillowcase, where it can't roll off the tray. You eat one packet slowly.

Across the hall, the television loses its picture. Its voice breaks up, then stops. You count the packets you've brought back. There are enough for today, but you can see the cafeteria passage from your door.

## Day 3: The cafeteria
Before sunrise, you leave the sealed bottle in Room 112 and take an empty backpack from the closet. The corridor is quiet enough that you can hear your shoes lift from the tile. You reach the cafeteria passage without hurrying.

Inside, the serving counter is on the left. Vending machines and a pantry door are on the right, with tables between them. Behind the counter is a cold-room door. The exterior window looks onto the service road, so you can't see the park from here.

You try the pantry handle. It opens.

There are shelf-stable crackers, jars of peanut butter, and packaged drinks. You put a little of each in the backpack. Through the cold-room window, you can see food stacked on shelves, but the hospital's lights have flickered twice this morning. You leave the door shut.

From the cafeteria passage, the vending machine's bright panel looks like a small lit window. You hear slow steps beyond the tables. A figure comes into view and moves toward its hum.

The switch beside the machine is a few feet from you. You reach it, turn the machine off, and get behind the serving counter. The figure stops where the light was. Its slippers scrape once across the floor. You wait until it moves toward the service-road window before you take the backpack and leave.

The bag feels manageable until you lift it onto your shoulder. Your stitches pull, and you have to stand still beside the pantry door. Next time, you take less.

Back in Room 112, your phone rings under your pillow. You silence it before answering.

"I found food," you whisper.

"Good," Nadia says. "Please sit down."

You do. By dusk, a few blocks of the skyline have gone dark beyond your window. The hospital lights are still on. You look at the backpack, then at the lamp above your bed.

That night, an unfamiliar key turns in a door near stairwell B.

## Day 4: The man with the keys
The key turns again at the west end of the corridor. You ease out of Room 112 and wait behind the nurses' station counter. A man in gray work clothes steps out of a supply room. He catches the latch with his thumb so it closes without a sound.

He sees you anyway.

"Don't carry that much," he says, looking at the backpack against your stomach.

"I won't."

His name is Mo. He's the night janitor, about sixty, and he has a ring of utility keys clipped inside his pocket. He asks how much water you have. You tell him about the bottles in your room and the empty pitcher.

"Come downstairs," he says. "Slowly."

Stairwell B is at the west end. The handrail runs down its west wall, with a door at each landing. Mo lets the heavy ground-floor fire door close behind you instead of propping it open. You stop twice on the stairs. He waits both times without asking if you're all right.

In the basement hall, the laundry room is on your left. Mo points out its chute near the back. Across from it, the generator room is locked. A fire door at the far end leads toward the parking garage.

"The generator's got limited fuel," Mo says. "We don't get to choose when it runs out."

Beside the laundry, he unlocks a maintenance storeroom. Forty sealed one-gallon drinking-water jugs stand against the wall. You reach for one, and the pull across your stomach settles the question. Mo has a wheeled laundry cart. You'll need that.

On the way back, Mo stops at the ground-floor landing and points up the stairwell. The upper access leads to the roof, but he leaves it locked. You look up at the flights you'll have to climb when you need that exit.

At the ground-floor window, you look across the park. There's open grass to the right of the pond. The slow figures are closer to the street.

Mo follows your gaze, then checks the hallway before opening the door. Back in Room 112, your last bottle holds a mouthful.

"I'll cart two jugs up," Mo says. "You hold doors."

## Day 6: The last call
Two mornings later, Mo carts two sealed jugs from the basement storeroom to laundry, then carries each up stairwell B. He sets them in a closet near Room 112. The other jugs stay sealed below. You carry small food packets and hold doors for him.

The pharmacy is farther north along the corridor. Its locked door is on the left, opposite a dark waiting alcove. Mo opens it with his utility key, keeping a hand on the latch. Inside, a supply cabinet stands on the right.

He takes an emergency flashlight, batteries, a battery-powered radio, and basic dressings. You leave the medications where they are. Neither of you knows what the next person through this door might need.

Back in Room 112, Mo finds a broadcast between bursts of static. A recorded voice tells people to shelter inside and await further instructions. It says nothing about where to go.

You turn the volume down until you have to sit beside the radio to hear it.

Your phone rings while the signal still holds. Nadia sounds tired.

"Tell me where you are."

"Mercy Ridge General. I'm with Mo. He works here."

"Okay," she says. "I can try to..."

Her voice breaks apart. You hold the phone close, waiting for the rest. The call drops. When you try her back, the screen shows no service.

You charge the phone while the power works, then turn it off and put it in your pocket. Mo sets the radio beside your bed. He doesn't say the phone will work again.

That night, more slow figures stand under the park streetlights. You can still see the open grass beside the pond. On the back of Nadia's get-well card, you sketch the route past the pond to the road. You leave her writing on the front untouched.

A hallway light flickers. Mo looks up from the radio.

"Listen," he says.

You hear it after he does. Beneath the building's usual hum, the generator's pitch rises, catches, and falls.

## Day 8: The light you carry
Two days later, you wake in a darker Room 112. Through the little window in your door, only parts of the corridor have emergency lighting.

Mo listens near the stairwell before he comes in. "It's burning through what's left," he says.

You put the flashlight in your backpack and go with him to the cafeteria. The serving counter is on the left, the vending machines and pantry on the right. Tables fill the space between them. You take unopened crackers, peanut butter, and packaged drinks in small loads. Behind the counter, the cold-room door stays shut. You can't count on what's inside once the power goes.

The vending machine stays off. Through the cafeteria window, you can see the service road. A vehicle light blinks beside the hospital entrance, drawing slow figures across the pavement. Mo waits behind the serving counter with you until they drift away from the doors. Only then do you cross the passage back.

At the nurses' station, you split the food. A small supply goes into Room 112. The rest goes beside stairwell B, where you can reach it without crossing the whole corridor. You write both locations on the back of Nadia's card, beneath your rough sketch. You can still read every word she wrote on the front.

Mo takes the laundry cart downstairs and leaves it there, near the jugs. Carrying food is enough work for you. When you get back to your bed, you sit until the ache across your stomach eases.

You leave the overhead lamp off. You move the switched-off flashlight to your backpack's outer pocket, where you can find it by touch. Mo doesn't bring a candle. Through the window in your door, you can see the corridor wall. A flame would put your shadow on it. Through the radio's low hiss, the same voice tells you to remain sheltered. It still gives you nowhere to go.

At dusk, whole sections of the skyline disappear beyond the park. You check the card, the food by your bed, and the flashlight in your bag. You know where each one is.

Then the hospital lights blink once more, and slow footsteps begin to turn down the corridor.

## Day 11: The quiet after the generator
Three days later, on a rainy afternoon, the generator stops, and you hear someone moving outside Room 112. The footsteps have been there for a while. With the motor gone, you can hear each slipper drag across the corridor.

Mo comes up from the basement a few minutes later. He has closed the generator-room door and locked it.

"That's the fuel," he says.

You wait for the lights to come back anyway. Rain runs down the window, and the park disappears behind its reflection. When you need to cross the dim room, you aim the flashlight at your feet and switch it off as soon as you reach the door. The radio stays off between brief checks. Its batteries have a longer job now.

Mo takes a drink from one of the sealed jugs upstairs.

"We've got more below," he says.

The bathroom tap still runs clear. Mo shakes his head at it.

"Nobody's treating that water anymore," he says.

Neither of you knows what's feeding that line now. Mo fills your cup from a sealed jug.

Mo wants the next jugs closer than the maintenance storeroom, so you go down stairwell B together. Your hand stays on the west-wall rail. He takes the wheeled laundry cart into the storeroom and brings it back loaded while you hold the basement hall door. In the laundry room, washing machines line the left wall. Folding tables fill the right side, and the chute hatch sits near the back. Its only door opens onto the basement hall. Mo parks the jugs beside the tables, clear of that door.

You carry one light bag upstairs. Mo leaves the cart beside the tables downstairs, follows you into stairwell B, and pulls the fire door shut behind you. The route still works, even in the dark. You sit on your bed afterward until your stomach settles.

By evening, a distant fire glows above the skyline. Emergency lights flash near the park street, and slow shapes drift toward them. The grass beside the pond stays empty. Below you, somewhere past the laundry, the basement fire door leads toward the garage. You wonder how long that side will stay quiet.

## Day 15: The wrong exit
Four days on, at midday, you hear metal tapping somewhere beyond the basement laundry. Mo hears it too. He takes the keys from his pocket, and you follow him past the jugs to the fire door at the far end of the hall.

He opens it only far enough for you both to look through. The parking garage stretches beyond it, with cars in marked rows and a ramp at the east end. Daylight reaches partway down the ramp. Near it, a locked service gate opens onto the road beside the park.

The tapping comes from a loose sign beside that gate. Each time it shifts, several slow figures between the cars turn toward it. One wanders into the strip of daylight. Mo eases the fire door closed before its latch can click.

"We could get out that way," you whisper.

"We could get to the road."

You look back at the closed door. Mo has the gate key, but the sign would announce your arrival, and the road gives you no safe place to go. Upstairs, you write its location on the back of Nadia's card: Mo's ring, clipped inside his pocket. He checks the clip with his thumb when you tell him.

In Room 112, he comes to the window beside you.

"Tell me what you can see."

You point out the pond, with the open grass to its right. The nearest road runs between the garage ramp and the park. A few slow figures stand near the street edge. The grass is clear from here.

"Keep checking," Mo says.

The radio still repeats the shelter message. You put in a fresh pair of batteries from the pharmacy supply, then wrap the used pair in a food wrapper and keep them in another pocket. You don't want to reach for them in the dark.

Smoke rises beyond the skyline that evening. You watch the grass until the light goes. The gate is close enough that Mo can put a key in it, but nobody is waiting on the other side to tell you it's time.

## Day 19: A voice beyond the loop
Four days after that, rain taps against the window of Room 112 while Mo turns the radio dial. A voice comes through clearly enough that he takes his hand off it.

The speaker identifies a military-run evacuation broadcast. People sheltering in your district are told to stay inside and listen for further instructions. The message repeats, but it gives no pickup point.

Mo turns the volume down. You wait beside him until the voice returns and says the same thing again.

"That's our district," you say.

"That's what they're saying."

You can't reach the speaker to tell them you and Mo are inside Mercy Ridge General. How will they know to look for you? Mo switches it off to save the working batteries. You agree to check again later.

A draft moves through the corridor that afternoon. From the nurses' station, you hear the pharmacy door knock against its frame. A slow figure appears beside the dark waiting alcove and turns toward the sound.

Mo is closer to the pharmacy. He catches the loose door before it knocks again and draws it closed. You back toward the station counter together. The figure stays by the alcove, facing the door, so neither of you tries to pass it.

Back in your room, you lie down for an hour. When you get up, you walk to stairwell B and down one landing, with a hand on the west-wall rail. Coming back takes less out of you than it would have on your first day here. You still pause before opening the ground-floor fire door. A full jug would slow you badly, and running would hurt.

Late that night, the broadcast changes. Crews are assessing open areas for future pickups. Mo listens through a second repetition, then shuts the radio off.

You stand by the window. The grass to the right of the pond is visible between patches of rain, while figures gather along the noisy street edge. A crew might choose that grass. For now, you have a view from one room, and you need to see what lies beyond it.

## Day 23: The roof is not a pickup
Four days later, by late morning, the rain has cleared enough for you to see the park from Room 112. You can see the grass beside the pond, but the hospital wall hides the road that leads to it.

Mo unlocks the upper door of stairwell B. You carry the flashlight and radio in your backpack. He carries a bottle of water. The climb takes longer than either of you says it should. At each landing, you stop beside the west-wall handrail, catch your breath, and check that the fire door behind you has closed.

On the roof, the helipad takes up the center. The stairwell enclosure stands to its west, and a low barrier runs along the edge facing the park. A utility structure blocks part of the eastern skyline. You walk toward the barrier, keeping well back from its edge.

From here, you can trace the garage ramp to the service road. The road runs past the park, close to the open grass right of the pond. Your sketch on Nadia's card had the turn in roughly the right place. You feel pleased about that for perhaps three seconds.

Below you, the hospital's emergency entrance is brighter than the rest of the building. Slow figures have gathered near its doors. More stand along the street.

A helicopter passes beyond the skyline. You turn toward the helipad, and Mo watches you.

"Do they land here?" you ask.

"They haven't told us to come up."

There is no crew on the roof. The aircraft keeps going. You leave the flashlight off.

Mo points across the road. "Down there, they can move people away from it."

You look at the grass again. Getting there means using the basement hall and the garage gate, neither of which you can see from this height.

On the way down, Mo locks the roof door. You take the same stairs and make sure each door closes behind you. At the next radio check, a voice promises district-specific instructions soon.

Your backpack rests against the stairwell wall while you listen. The radio still hasn't told you when to move. You picture the basement hall, the garage gate, and the road to the park.

## Day 26: The basement bottleneck
Three days later, at the morning radio check, the broadcast lists several possible landing areas across the district. Final locations and times will follow.

Mo turns the radio off before the message repeats.

"We can't listen all day," he says. "Those batteries have to last."

You agree to check at the top of each hour. Between checks, the radio stays in your backpack by stairwell B.

You bring the rest of your supplies from Room 112 in two small trips. Two sealed water bottles go in first, followed by food packets, the flashlight, spare batteries, Nadia's card, and your powered-off phone. Mo keeps the key ring clipped inside his pocket. The sealed jugs remain in the basement laundry room. If the call comes, you can carry what you've packed.

The route still has to be clear.

At noon, you look through the window in the basement stairwell door. A slow figure stands near the laundry-room entrance, between you and the far fire door to the garage. You go back upstairs. A little later, something clatters deeper in the basement. Through the same window, Mo watches the figure turn and move out of sight. He waits another minute before opening the door.

You follow the handrail down. The washing machines are behind the door on your left, the locked generator room on your right. You pass the laundry and walk as far as the garage fire door. Mo leaves it shut.

On the way back, you stop twice. Mo checks his watch while you lean against the stairwell wall.

"Enough time?" you ask.

"If they give us warning."

He says it plainly. You climb the last flight without answering.

That night, you return to Room 112 for the window view. The card is gone from the tray; you tucked it into the backpack's inner pocket before leaving. The empty juice box still stands beside the bed.

At the hourly check, the broadcast names your district again. You listen for the park and hear only a promise of another update.

## Day 28: The park is named
Two days later, at the first hourly check after breakfast, the radio gives a location.

"Mercy Ridge General. Park adjacent to the hospital. Planned pickup at dawn, the day after tomorrow."

You move closer as the message repeats. It's the park outside your window. The broadcast says crews must secure it first. Everyone sheltering nearby is to remain inside until a final go-ahead.

Mo switches the radio off. You go to Room 112 and look out.

Temporary lights are approaching the far side of the park. You can't tell who's carrying them from this distance, but you can see what follows. Slow figures turn away from the street and drift toward the light. Two cross the grass to the right of the pond, where you had hoped to walk.

You stand there long enough for the sun to shift across the empty juice box on the tray. You stop counting on a walk across the grass. You'll wait for the crews to clear a route before you open the service gate.

Mo goes downstairs to check the route. You stay by the window with the radio off. When he returns, the garage fire door is closed, the service gate is locked, and its key is still on the ring inside his pocket.

"The locks are good," he says.

You meet him by stairwell B to sort the bags. Mo takes most of the water. You keep the radio, flashlight, food packets, powered-off phone, and Nadia's card. You put fresh batteries in the flashlight and save its old pair separately. The radio keeps the pair that's working.

You lift your backpack and walk the length of the corridor. Your stomach pulls when you turn toward the stairs. It eases when you stop.

"I can walk it," you tell Mo. "I can't sprint."

"Then we don't sprint."

At the next check, the pickup message is unchanged. You switch the radio off and return to the window. More lights move along the far edge of the park. You watch for someone to clear the road beside the pond, but darkness comes before you can see a crossing.

## Day 29: The last night in Room 112
The next day, by dusk, the pond has gone dark enough to lose its edges. You stand at the right-hand window of Room 112 and watch soldiers place lights along the far side of the park. Slow figures turn toward them from the street. The grass beside the pond is still there, but from here you can't see a way across from the garage ramp.

Mo turns on the radio for the evening check. The voice tells everyone in your district to stay sheltered overnight. A dawn message will identify the safe approach.

"Dawn," you say.

"I heard," Mo says.

He switches the radio off. Neither of you reaches for your bag.

You eat a packet of crackers beside the bed, slowly enough to notice how stale they are. The food at the nurses' station stays there. So does the small cache near stairwell B. Down in the laundry room, sealed water jugs wait for whoever finds the building after you leave. You hope somebody does.

Mo takes his key ring downstairs to test the service gate. You stay by the window, where you can keep an eye on the park without putting your stomach through another trip. For a while, you hear nothing from below. Then his steps return up stairwell B.

"The key works," he says. "Gate's locked again."

He has closed the garage fire door behind him. The route is intact. Outside, the soldiers' lights still draw movement toward the street edge, and you leave your backpack where it is.

Before you lie down, you check its inner pocket. Nadia's card is dry. Your phone is off. The empty juice box stays on the tray beside the bed, its straw bent toward the place your hand could reach on the first morning.

You sit with Mo through the last hourly radio check. The shelter message repeats. Mo leaves the radio on low. You watch the lights beyond the pond and wait for the next voice.

## Day 30: Across the park
At dawn, the voice says the park pickup area is secured. People leaving Mercy Ridge General should use the hospital-side service road and wait there for a soldier to direct them across.

You look through Room 112's window before Mo can ask. A soldier guides a small group onto the grass right of the pond. The radio said to use the hospital-side service road, so you turn from the window.

Mo picks up his bag. You take yours and follow him to stairwell B. Your hand stays on the west-wall rail as you descend. At the ground floor, Mo pulls the heavy stairwell door shut behind you. In the basement hall, you pass the laundry room on the left and the locked generator room on the right. Mo opens the far fire door. It closes behind both of you.

Daylight reaches the east end of the garage through the ramp. At the ramp, you can see the service road and the whole crossing. Soldiers stand along the road. One holds people back. You keep beside the parked cars, away from the loose sign rattling near the service gate. A slow figure stands among the far rows, its head turned toward the sound. Mo fits his key into the gate. It opens, and you step onto the service road. He closes and locks it behind you.

You don't run. The soldier by the road raises a hand, so you stop. Mo stops with you.

"Wait there," the soldier says.

You wait. When he points across, you follow the marked approach into the park. The pond stays on your left. Mo matches your pace, even when the helicopter comes into view beyond the grass.

A crew member keeps you back until there's room to board. Then she calls you forward and shows you where to step. Mo goes ahead. You follow, one hand against your stomach. As the aircraft rises, Room 112 becomes one window in a long row of them.

At the safe-zone reception area, a volunteer puts a working phone in your hand. You give her Nadia's number. Nadia answers before you decide what else to say.

"I'm out."

"Are you safe?"

Mo stands by the reception window, drinking from a bottle of water. You look at him.

"We're safe."

The volunteer offers you a tray with a fresh juice box. You set Nadia's card beside it. Her voice is still on the phone. Morning light reaches the card, the juice box, and your hand resting close enough to pick it up.

# Recherche et consignes — POV: You Marry a Korean Woman

Faits vérifiés et règles données au script.

VERIFIED FACTS about her world (use several, rounded, each inside a scene, with the source named in the sentence; never invent other statistics):
- In South Korea a woman traditionally keeps her own family name after marriage; children usually take the father's surname.
- In June 2023 South Korea officially dropped the traditional "Korean age" (everyone one year old at birth and a year older every January 1) for international age in legal and administrative use.
- After the wedding, many couples do the pyebaek: the bride bows to the groom's family in traditional hanbok, and the elders throw dates and chestnuts that the couple catch in a cloth; the number caught is said to predict how many children they will have.
- Kimjang, the late-autumn tradition of families making large batches of kimchi together, was added to UNESCO's Intangible Cultural Heritage list in 2013.
- At Seollal (Lunar New Year) families gather and children do a deep bow (sebae) to their elders and receive money in envelopes; Chuseok is the autumn harvest holiday.
- Statistics Korea: South Korea's fertility rate was 0.72 children per woman in 2023, the lowest in the world.
- Korean couples often celebrate the 100th day of dating, and the 200th, and every anniversary that ends in a hundred.

# Recherche et consignes — POV: You Marry a WNBA Star

Faits vérifiés et règles données au script.

VERIFIED FACTS about her world (use several, rounded, each inside a scene; never invent other statistics):
- The WNBA season runs roughly from May to September, playoffs into October. The league added teams in Toronto (the Tempo) and Portland (the Fire) for 2026.
- A new labor deal (term sheet signed in March 2026, seven years, through 2032) changed everything: the 2026 salary cap is about $7 million per team, the maximum salary about $1.4 million, the average salary expected around $583,000, the minimum between about $270,000 and $300,000. In 2025 the maximum was about $249,000 and the minimum about $66,000.
- For almost thirty years, WNBA players flew overseas every fall for a second paycheck (Turkey, China, Europe), often paid more than at home, sometimes paid late, far from family through the holidays. Unrivaled, a 3-on-3 winter league in Miami founded by WNBA stars, launched in January 2025 and paid an average of more than $200,000 for a ten-week season, so many players can now stay home.
- WNBA teams fly charter flights league-wide since 2024; before that they flew commercial, folded into economy seats.
- The WNBA ball is a size 6 (28.5 inches around), a little smaller than the men's size 7.
- Women athletes tear their ACL at a rate several times higher than men; the rehab after ACL surgery usually takes nine months to a year.
STORY SHAPE: "You" are an ordinary guy (late twenties, a physical therapist at a sports rehab clinic, 5'10"). She is a fictional WNBA guard, 6'2", fierce, funny, private, who tears her ACL; you meet as her physical therapist during rehab (keep it professional until she is discharged, then she asks you out). Her world: the rehab grind, the comeback game, the off-season choice between a big contract overseas (a winter in Istanbul) and staying home, airports and hotel rooms, fans and trolls online, the new labor deal turning her salary from about $100,000 into more than a million while you still drive a used Honda, her 6'2" in every doorway and photo, the family dinners where your uncle asks if she could beat LeBron. Marriage by ~60%, then married life: her travel schedule, your clinic, a second injury scare, a quiet ending on an outdoor court at night shooting free throws.
RULES: fictional player and fictional team (no real players or real team names); never sexualize her; humor aims at your ordinariness and the size of her world, never at her body, gender or sport.

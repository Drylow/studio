# Recherche et consignes — POV: You Marry a Singaporean Woman

Faits vérifiés et règles données au script.

VERIFIED FACTS (name the source in the sentence; never invent figures):
- About 77 per cent of Singapore's resident households live in HDB public flats (Singapore Department of Statistics, 2024).
- Couples can apply for a Build-To-Order HDB flat before they marry, under the Fiancé/Fiancée Scheme; they must be legally married when they collect the keys. Many Singaporeans "propose" by asking "Want to BTO together?". Waiting several years for the flat to be built is common (say "several years", no exact figure).
- Singapore's hawker culture was added to UNESCO's list of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in December 2020.
- "Chope": leaving a packet of tissues on a hawker-centre table to reserve the seat while you queue for food.
- Lo hei: at Chinese New Year, everyone tosses yusheng (raw fish salad) with chopsticks, as high as possible, shouting wishes; the colourful version was created in Singapore in 1964 by four chefs nicknamed the "Four Heavenly Kings" (National Heritage Board, Roots.gov.sg). Accounts differ on its exact origin.
- Singapore has four official languages: English, Malay, Mandarin and Tamil. Singlish mixes them ("can lah", "shiok", "paiseh").
- At Chinese weddings in Singapore, the groom and his brothers "gatecrash" the bride's home and must pass her bridesmaids' games before the tea ceremony with both families; guests give red packets (ang bao). The legal marriage is the ROM solemnisation.
- Singapore is close to the equator: it is hot and humid all year, with sudden afternoon thunderstorms.

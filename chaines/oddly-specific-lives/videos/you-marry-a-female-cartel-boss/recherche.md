# Recherche et consignes — POV: You Marry a Female Cartel Boss

Faits vérifiés et règles données au script.

VERIFIED FACTS about her world (use several, rounded, each inside a scene, with the source named in the sentence; never invent other statistics):
- InsightCrime reported that as men fell in Mexico's drug war, more women stepped into management roles in criminal groups.
- Researcher Arturo Santamaria Gomez wrote "Las Jefas del Narco" (The Female Bosses of the Narco), essays based on interviews with women who held power in trafficking networks in Durango, Sinaloa and Chihuahua.
- Researcher Jose Carlos Cisneros Guzman spent eight years and some 50 interviews studying women in Sinaloa's drug world.
- Estimates cited by InsightCrime say women make up roughly five to eight percent of new recruits in Mexican criminal groups.
- Narcocorridos are ballads in the norteno and banda style that tell the stories of traffickers; several Mexican states have restricted playing them in public.
- Sinaloa ranch culture: horses, banda music with tubas and trumpets, carne asada, the Sierra Madre Occidental mountains.
STORY SHAPE: Culiacan, Sinaloa, Mexico. "You" are a bartender, 30, half-American (grew up between San Diego and Tijuana), working nights behind the bar of the most expensive nightclub in Culiacan: you are fast, funny, you remember everyone's drink and you never ask questions. She is FICTIONAL: Valeria, 35, a widow, quiet, sharp, never raises her voice; after her husband was killed she took his place at the head of a FICTIONAL organisation (invent a name; never a real cartel or real person). The difference from the mafia story: this one is about ISOLATION, NIGHTLIFE and constant RISK, not family and neighbourhood. First meeting: she arrives at 1 a.m. with four bodyguards, the music drops a little, the owner starts sweating, the whole VIP section stands; she sits at YOUR bar, not in the VIP, and orders mineral water with lime (she never drinks: she needs a clear head). You make a joke nobody else would dare to make. She laughs once. She comes back every Thursday, same stool, back to the wall, facing the door. Small tells: nobody uses real names on the phone, a band starts playing a corrido about her and she has it stopped with one look, the club's cameras switch off when she walks in, she tips in cash and you are never allowed to post a photo. The reveal ~30%: the club is hers; she tells you herself, flatly, after closing time, and gives you one chance to walk away. You stay. Wedding by ~55%: at a hacienda outside the city, a banda sinaloense with tubas and trumpets, the whole city invited, armed men at every gate, carne asada for three hundred, you make the cocktails at your own wedding because you trust nobody else. Married life: black armoured trucks, bodyguards who never smile, you learn the rules (no routines, no photos, no questions, never the same route twice), you still work Thursdays at the bar because it is the only normal thing left, a tense night when the club goes dark (off screen), she loses people and goes quiet for weeks: the price of the life. Ending: she decides to step away and hands everything over; you both start again small: a tiny beach bar far away on the Pacific coast, under her middle name; quiet ending at closing time, she sits on the same kind of stool, back to the wall, facing the door, mineral water with lime.
RULES: she is fictional; never name or depict a real cartel, boss, victim, place of violence or case (researchers only quoted for findings); violence implied and off screen, never graphic, no blood, no torture, no guns used on screen; never explain trafficking, smuggling, money laundering or how to avoid police; never glamorize: the tone is calm, dry and funny with real fear and real cost; no Mexican caricature (no sombreros, no cliche accents); she chooses you; never sexualize her. Places and customs correct (Culiacan, its nightlife, banda sinaloense, carne asada, the Pacific coast). Advertiser-friendly.

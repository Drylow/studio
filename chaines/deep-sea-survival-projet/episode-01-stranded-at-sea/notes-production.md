# First episode — completed production

Working title: **Sea Monster Survival: 30 Days Stranded at Sea**. Fiction, English second-person narration. User accepted the simple marine drawing direction; Full production was explicitly authorized by the user; completed and inspected by Codex. No user approval of the final montage is claimed.

Narrative spine: a small boat initially appears safer than the water. Repeated attacks remove that shelter. The survivor learns that the fictional leviathan tracks vibration, then uses an established bell buoy to redirect it during a rescue. The rule belongs to this invented story, not actual animal behavior or survival advice.

## Causal progression

- Day 1: net pulls off railing; propeller fouled; nearby boat disappears. Food, water, emergency raft, repair supplies, chart, binoculars, spare oar, handheld radio and signal light established aboard.
- Days 2–6: vibrations near radio attract knocks; loose metal briefly distracts leviathan; giant shark damages hull/cooler; rain refreshes water; sealed food kept, spoiled food discarded; hull repair slows leak.
- Days 8–10: approaching rescue boat attracts leviathan with engine; purple tentacle damages cabin and window; metal pan distracts it. Rescue cannot simply motor alongside.
- Days 12–16: chart and visible red bell buoy establish destination; supplies packed; hull lifted, repair fails; orange emergency raft deployed before full sinking.
- Day 18: actual flooding and sinking; hero falls in, reaches deployed raft; releases connecting line before boat sinks; red boat disappears permanently.
- Days 19–23: drifting/paddling toward buoy; bell draws leviathan away; climbs buoy with bag and oar; handheld contact confirms fictional outer-lane rescue route.
- Days 25–29: storm tears raft away; bell bracket jams; shore rescue unavailable; ship spotted via established signal light; rescue boat stays short; saved oar frees bell.
- Day 30: pickup line reaches buoy; bell distracts creature; life jacket and crew recover hero; boarded onto cargo ship, concrete successful rescue.

# Babylon 01 Research Review

Neo-Babylonian setting around the beginning of the sixth century BCE. Follow an
illustrative carrier and household, not an individual documented by an archive.

- [Metropolitan Museum, Babylon](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/babylon): city setting and Neo-Babylonian building context. No completed-project timeline is invented.
- [Oracc, Babylonian Administrative and Legal Texts](https://oracc.museum.upenn.edu/balt/): corpus periods and provenances, private and institutional economic records. Uruk and Sippar temple documents are regional evidence, not Babylon building records.
- [Metropolitan Museum, house purchase tablet](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/321920): Neo-Babylonian property transaction, provenance probably Babylon. No fictional price attributed to the artifact.
- [Oracc, Babylon City rental text](https://oracc.museum.upenn.edu/babcity//P531037/html): house rent in silver, sixth-fifth century corpus. Broad property relationship, not a diary dated precisely 600 BCE.
- [Metropolitan Museum, seals and clay transactions](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/327659): use of seals and closures. The showcased seal predates this day; do not use its exact design as the protagonist's documented seal.
- [Metropolitan Museum, Ancient Near Eastern Art Bulletin](https://resources.metmuseum.org/resources/metpublications/pdf/Ancient_Near_Eastern_Art_The_Metropolitan_Museum_of_Art_Bulletin_v_41_no_4_Spring_1984.pdf): varied first-millennium sealing practices. Do not assert cylinders were the only method.

## Boundaries

No modern coins, universal private home ownership, invented exact wages or
water-access arrangements, literal early-second-millennium recipes as a 600
BCE household menu, Hanging Gardens presented as verified architecture, or
all workers treated as free. Building, delivery, food and household actions are
illustrative, not translations of a single clay tablet. Keep a modest skyline;
do not insert speculative towers into every lane.

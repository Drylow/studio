# Pirate Pages — episode 09 research notes

**Title:** Why Being a Pirate Cook Was Worse Than You Think
**Script:** `chaines/pirate-pages/episode-09/script-en.txt`: 2,723 words (`wc -w`), 59 paragraphs, about 19:40 at episode 1's measured voice pace (2,744 words = 1,185 s). Measure the real duration on the final voice.
**Setting:** The imagined cook ("you") of Tom's modest one-mast sloop, Caribbean, around 1717 to 1718, the same crew as episodes 1 to 8. He is a NEW character, described in §3: round and red-faced, about fifty-five, bushy white mustache, thick white eyebrows, no beard, faded red knitted cap, blue-and-white checked shirt, stained cream canvas apron with a wooden ladle, stiff left leg from a fall in "the last war", walks with a stick; a volunteer and former navy seaman. His navy past, the share vote, the brick-and-sand hearth, the stew, the captain's lost pork, Tom's biscuit (the cook's), the sandy key, Tom's turtle chase, the iguana stew, the water ladle, the eight o'clock fire, the rum night, the young captive as cook's mate, the pardon vote, the last meal and the clerk are fictional devices; §3 says the crew and voyage are imagined. Real events (Snelgrave 1719, the Mary Anne 1717, Cape Lopez and Cape Coast 1722, the Queen Anne's Revenge finds, Anstis's and Roberts's crews in Johnson) are narrator asides.

## How sources were weighed

- **Navy cooks**: Royal Museums Greenwich's catalogue note on Rowlandson's 1799 print *Cook* (RMG-CK) and B. Pappalardo's History Hit article (HH) agree that cooks were disabled seamen, not chosen for cooking. The "until 1806 the only qualification was a pension" rule is HH's wording; the script calls the pension fund "the navy's fund for injured seamen" rather than naming the Chatham/Greenwich Chest, whose name and administration changed over the century.
- **Johnson (JOH)**: pen name, mixes fact and invention (§8 says so). Johnson-only details (Roberts's tea, the water disaster, Anstis's turtle diet, the iguanas at Blanco) are narrated as "Johnson says / tells / describes". Roberts's articles are also reproduced by Leeson (LEE).
- **The 1723 trial pamphlet (TRY)**: the cook's-mate testimony, Roger Shelton's testimony and Edward Evans's role are TRY (one court record, named witnesses); Evans and the Porcupine also appear in Johnson's list of Roberts's prisoners.
- **The Mary Anne**: the sworn statements of Thomas FitzGerald and Alexander Mackconachy and the October 1717 trial are in Jameson (JAM docs. 109 and 112); History Is Now (HIN16) gives the cargo figure, Mackconachy's role at Cole's house and the outcome.
- **Queen Anne's Revenge**: the conservation lab's monthly report (QAR-LAB, read with `pdftotext`) is first-hand; the "last meal" and "cannon scrap" readings are both the report's own questions, narrated as questions. M. Hoots (HOO) confirms the iron cauldron fragments and the research question.

## Sensitivity checks done

- Violence stays off screen. The water disaster says "men died" without detail; the bird heads and the sow are one kitchen sentence, no butchery images; the Porcupine is one grave sentence, no burning shown.
- Slavery: the furnace built to feed captives and the Porcupine's captives are presented gravely, with no joke in the same paragraph, and the ending returns to "people held as cargo".
- No real person is a character. "You", Tom, the captain, the quartermaster, the gunner, the young captive, the dealer and the clerk are fictional. Roberts, Johnson, Leeson, Pappalardo, Hoots, Anstis, Lowther, Cocklyn, Snelgrave, Bellamy, Mackconachy, Cole, Shelton, Evans and Blackbeard appear only in narration.
- No parrot, peg leg (the cook walks with a plain stick), eye patch or treasure map. Every paragraph can be drawn as a flat 2D scene (a kettle on bricks, casks and biscuit, a conservation lab with a concretion, a queue at a cask, a moonlit beach with turtles, an iguana on a rock, a dry deck with a ladle, a slave ship's furnace seen from outside, a drunk crew asleep, a grounded pink in fog, a cottage on Cape Cod, a courtroom at Cape Coast Castle, a harbor clerk's table).

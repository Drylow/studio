# Pirate Pages — episode 02 research notes

**Title:** Why Being a Pirate Captain Was Worse Than You Think
**Script:** `chaines/pirate-pages/episode-02/script-en.txt`: 2,774 words (`wc -w`), 85 paragraphs. That is about 20:00 at episode 1's measured voice pace (2,744 words = 1,185 s). Measure the real duration on the final voice.
**Setting:** An imagined, newly elected captain ("you") of Tom's modest one-mast sloop, Caribbean, around 1717 to 1718. The captain, crew, dealer, coat auction, three-day careening and final vote are fictional devices. The narration states this in paragraph 3. Later real events (Vane 1718, Bonnet 1718, Blackbeard 1718, Roberts 1722) appear as narrator asides, not as events in the fictional present.

## How sources were weighed

- **Johnson, *A General History of the Pyrates* (1724)** was read in the full Project Gutenberg text and searched locally. It is near-contemporary and widely used, but "Captain Charles Johnson" is a pen name. Parts are exaggerated, and some chapters are apocryphal (Libertalia). Every Johnson-derived fact below is paired with a second source, or attributed to Johnson in the sentence and flagged.
- **Snelgrave, *A New Account of Some Parts of Guinea and the Slave-Trade* (1734)** was read in the archive.org scan text. He is an eyewitness who was held by pirates at Sierra Leone in 1719. He was also a slave-trading captain, with that bias, and the script says what he was.
- **Leeson, "An-arrgh-chy" (*Journal of Political Economy* 115:6, 2007)** is a peer-reviewed economics paper that quotes Johnson and Snelgrave. It confirms the primary texts and gives the "nearly the same pay" conclusion. Its rational-choice reading flatters pirate "democracy". The script balances this with the captives, forced men and enslaved people who had no vote.
- Quotations from Johnson and Snelgrave are spoken with **modernized spelling**: "uncontroulable" becomes "uncontrollable" and "controuls" becomes "controls". The wording is otherwise unchanged.

## Cut as uncertain or single-source

- **400 to 600 pirates executed between 1716 and 1726** (often attributed to Rediker): I could not confirm it on two accessible serious sources. Not used.
- **72 men sentenced at Cape Coast Castle, the largest Admiralty sentence ever** (RMG only): only the two-sourced figure of 52 hanged is used.
- **Ogle knighted, the only naval officer knighted for anti-piracy work** (RMG only). Cut.
- **Numbers who surrendered**: Gazette 114 with Hornigold, Johnson about 150 at Bermuda, Johnson about 400 pardoned men at Nassau. The figures do not cross-check. Cut.
- **Number hanged at Nassau in December 1718**: eight, nine or ten, depending on the source. The script says "some pardoned men".
- **Exact date of Rogers's arrival**: Johnson says "May or June", Manwaring 25 July, other summaries 22 July. The script says "summer of 1718".
- **La Concorde capture day** (17 Nov in Smithsonian, 28 Nov in DNCR) and **captive numbers** (516 embarked, 455 alive, 61 kept): the date is given as "November 1717" and the numbers are not used.
- **Captain's share "one and a half to two"**: Phillips's articles in Johnson give 1½. The Rediker interview (University of Pittsburgh *University Times*, "a share and a half or maybe two shares at most") now returns 404 and was seen only through a search index. Only Roberts's two shares are narrated.
- **A navy captain earning 60 to 100 times a sailor** (same Rediker interview, inaccessible). Cut.
- **Roberts: "better being a Commander than a common Man"** and his "thin Commons, low Wages, and hard Labour" speech (Johnson only). Cut.
- **Cocklyn elected "on account of his Brutality and Ignorance"** (Snelgrave only). Cut.
- **Edward England deposed and marooned at Mauritius; Howell Davis elected "over a large Bowl of Punch"** (Johnson only). Cut.
- **Vane sent away in a small captured sloop; Vane's letter to Rogers demanding terms** (one source each). Cut.
- **Rackham's hanging date (November 1720)**: Johnson's date has no second source retrieved. Cut.
- **Virginia's reward schedule for Blackbeard's men** (Johnson only). Cut.
- **Number of Bonnet's crew hanged** (29 per NCpedia; other counts differ). Cut.
- **Share of pirate crews of African descent** (Kinkor via Leeson, 25 to 30 percent; "over 40% of Blackbeard's crew" in a non-scholarly review). Numbers cut, only the qualitative statement kept.
- **No jury in colonial piracy trials under the 1700 Act** (one law-review source). Only "Admiralty courts sat in the colonies" is narrated.
- **"No prey, no pay"** and similar slogans: not found in the 1715 to 1726 articles. Not used.

## Sensitivity checks done

- Violence stays off screen. Blackbeard and Roberts "did not survive" or were "killed in the battle", with no wounds described. Hangings are named without description.
- Slavery is treated gravely: Snelgrave is identified as a slave-trading captain, La Concorde's captives were sold rather than freed, and the narration asks who had no vote. There is no loot humour near these lines.
- No real person is used as a character. "You", Tom, the quartermaster, crewmates, captured sailors and the dealer are fictional. Real people appear only in narrated history.

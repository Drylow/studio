# Pirate Pages — episode 05 research notes

**Title:** Why Being a Pirate Carpenter Was Worse Than You Think
**Script:** `chaines/pirate-pages/episode-05/script-en.txt`: 2,731 words (`wc -w`), 65 paragraphs, about 19:40 at episode 1's measured voice pace (2,744 words = 1,185 s). Measure the real duration on the final voice.
**Setting:** The imagined carpenter ("you") of Tom's modest one-mast sloop, Caribbean, around 1717 to 1718, the same crew as episodes 1 to 4. He is a NEW character, described in §3 so the images can draw him: a broad, bald man of about fifty, short gray beard, rolled sleeves, brown canvas apron full of tools. He joined willingly, before Tom. The pump and oakum routine, the refusal to keep the second sloop's carpenter, the boarding for stores, the merchant carpenter, the fitting-out of the second sloop, the squall and the fished mast, the chase spent below, the pardon vote and the work ashore are fictional devices; §3 says the crew and voyage are imagined. Real events (Bellamy and Davis 1716–17, La Concorde 1717, Cape Coast Castle 1722, Phillips's crew and the Boston trial of 1724) are narrator asides.

## How sources were weighed

- **Jameson (JAM)** reprints manuscript records of the Boston Admiralty courts (Suffolk Court Files; Massachusetts Archives vol. 63): the trial of Thomas Davis (doc. 113, October 1717), his memorial (doc. 114), the trials of Fillmore and Cheeseman and of William Phillips and others (docs. 119–120, May 1724), with Jameson's notes quoting the *Boston News-Letter*. It is independent of Johnson.
- **Johnson (JOH)**, *A General History of the Pyrates* (1724): pen name, mixes fact and invention (episodes 2–3: LEE fn. 6, MHS). His Phillips chapter is the only source for several details and is narrated with "Johnson's book alone", "Only Johnson's book prints" or "Johnson says". Johnson's Phillips chapter is muddled on one point: it says Taylor was wounded in the leg, then that "Phillips's leg" was cut off; the Boston depositions confirm that William Phillips had lost a leg.
- **The 1723 trial pamphlet (TRY)** and Johnson's Roberts chapter are two printings of the Cape Coast Castle record; flagged where it matters. Testimony found only in TRY is narrated as what "a witness" or "a shipmate" said, and listed below as one record with several witnesses.
- **Snelgrave (SNE)**: eyewitness, slave-trading captain held by pirates in 1719; identified as such in the script.
- **Exquemelin (EXQ)**: 1684 English translation (Gutenberg 26690), checked against Leeson's quotation of the Dover translation.
- **Popular secondary sources** used only as second confirmation: History Is Now (L. Nelson, 2017), the *Provincetown Independent* (P. Mumford, 2025), *Product of Newfoundland* (R. Hiscock), Divernet (2020, quoting the ECU archaeologist Jeremy Borrelli), Historic Ipswich.
- Quotations are spoken with modernized spelling. The wording is otherwise unchanged.

### Fictional elements (not historical claims)

The carpenter himself, his voluntary joining, the pump and well-sounding routine, the vote not to keep the second sloop's carpenter, the stores he takes and leaves, the merchant carpenter, the refit of the second sloop, the squall and the mast fished with spare timber, the chase spent below with plugs, the soft plank, the pardon vote and the work ashore with Tom's bucket. Oakum, pitch, plugs and fishing a mast are generic seamanship described as what "you" do, not as a sourced historical claim about a named crew.

## Cut as uncertain or single-source

- **Roberts's carpenter stopping a great shot hole "with hundreds of bullets flying round him" off Barbados (1720)**: one *Boston News-Letter* report (JAM doc. 117); not found in Johnson's text. Cut.
- **Spriggs's crew careening "with much Difficulty, for they had no Carpenter in their Company"** (Johnson only). Cut.
- **Davis's age (22), birthplace (Carmarthenshire) and the denied notarial affidavit** (Jameson's note quoting the 1718 pamphlet only). Cut.
- **Davis's father's petition** (one court-file document). Cut.
- **Davis falling to his knees at the verdict** (PI only). Cut.
- **A captain's testimony that Bellamy's men at first forced no prisoners** (JAM doc. 113 only). Cut.
- **Six Whydah men hanged in November 1717** (PI only among opened sources; the MHS blog returned 503). Not needed; cut.
- **Phillips's head preserved in a pickle barrel** (popular accounts; graphic). Cut.
- **Cheeseman's mallet and axe blows during the rising** (Johnson; graphic). Cut.

- **Blackbeard's carpenter Owen Roberts killed at Ocracoke** (Johnson's list only). Cut.

## Sensitivity checks done

- Violence stays off screen. The amputation is named without any description of the act; Fern's death and the rising are stated in one sentence each, without wounds or weapons. The squall causes a cracked mast, not injuries.
- Slavery: La Concorde's captives are mentioned only as the reason her guns were left in France (SMI's sentence), consistent with episode 4's longer aside; Snelgrave is again identified as a slave-trading captain.
- Forced men (Davis, Griffin, Cheeseman, the Loyd-galley carpenter) are presented as victims of the pirates as well as defendants.
- No real person is a character. "You", the captain, the quartermaster, Tom, the surgeon, the merchant's carpenter and the second sloop's carpenter are fictional. Bellamy, Lebous, Davis, South, Griffin, Roberts, Snelgrave, Blackbeard, Ogle, Low, John and William Phillips, Fern, Archer, Cheeseman, Fillmore, Nutt and Jameson appear only in narration.
- No parrot, peg leg, treasure map or buried-treasure gag. Every paragraph can be drawn as a flat 2D scene (lantern-lit hold with a pump and leaking seams, merchant deck with a pump and tool bags, a cabin with a candle, a sloop refit on deck, a fished mast, careening on a beach, a Boston courtroom seen plainly, a proclamation).

**Title (fixed):** Why Being a Pirate Carpenter Was Worse Than You Think

## Description (English)

You kept the sloop afloat, and nobody ever thanked you. Step into the canvas apron of an imagined pirate carpenter in the Caribbean around 1717, sailing with Tom, the captain, the quartermaster and the surgeon from our earlier episodes, and discover what the most needed man aboard really did: fighting leaks with oakum and pitch, robbing other ships of their nails and pumps, turning captured ships into pirate ships, standing in for a surgeon who was not there, and reading a royal price list that finally named his job. This illustrated story draws on the Boston court records of 1717 and 1724, the 1722 Cape Coast Castle trial record, William Snelgrave's eyewitness account, Alexander Exquemelin's account of the buccaneers, Captain Charles Johnson's General History of the Pyrates (used critically), and research from Royal Museums Greenwich, the Smithsonian and the economist Peter Leeson. The carpenter and crew are fictional, but piracy harmed real people, including the many carpenters pirates forced aboard and never let go.

They did not want you. They wanted your medicine chest. Step into the black coat and leather apron of an imagined ship's surgeon taken off a merchant sloop in the Caribbean around 1717, by the same crew as Tom, the captain and the quartermaster from our earlier episodes, and discover what the job really involved: being kept by force, refusing to sign the articles, a medicine chest the crew valued more than you, Blackbeard's medicine ransom at Charleston, the mercury syringe found on the Queen Anne's Revenge wreck, a price list for every lost limb, and a court that cared less about how you came aboard than about what you did there. This illustrated story draws on the 1722 Cape Coast Castle trial record, William Snelgrave's eyewitness account, Alexander Exquemelin's account of the buccaneers, archaeology from the Queen Anne's Revenge, Captain Charles Johnson's General History of the Pyrates (used critically), and research from Royal Museums Greenwich, the Smithsonian and the economist Peter Leeson. The surgeon and crew are fictional, but piracy harmed real people, including the enslaved Africans whom pirates kept, sold, or even listed as payment.

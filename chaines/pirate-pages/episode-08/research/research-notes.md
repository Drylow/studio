**Title:** Why Being a Pirate Captive Was Worse Than You Think
**Script:** `chaines/pirate-pages/episode-08/script-en.txt`: 2,723 words (`wc -w`), 64 paragraphs, about 19:40 at episode 1's measured voice pace (2,744 words = 1,185 s). Measure the real duration on the final voice.
**Setting:** The imagined young merchant sailor ("you") who handed the quartermaster the ship's papers in episode 3, taken by the same crew a few weeks later, Caribbean, around 1717 to 1718. He is drawn for the first time and described in §3: thin, nineteen (an adult), straight black hair, a first wispy mustache, faded red-and-white striped shirt, canvas trousers rolled to the knee, barefoot. The second capture, the marriage question, the refusal to sign, the word "forced" in the quartermaster's book, the shout to the merchant master, Tom's biscuit and confession, the navigator's warning, the boarding with a cutlass, the passenger betrayed by a shipmate, the watering party, the pardon vote without a vote, the clerk's table, the three witnesses and the return home are fictional devices; §3 says the crew and voyage are imagined. Real events (Bellamy and Davis 1716–17, Snelgrave 1719, Ashton 1722–25, Cape Coast 1722, Phillips's crew 1724) are narrator asides.

## How sources were weighed

- **Philip Ashton**: his own memoir (*Ashton's Memorial*, Boston, 1725, written up by his minister John Barnard) was not opened directly. Its facts are taken from two modern summaries that agree: History of Massachusetts (HOM, quoting the memoir) and Wikipedia (WIK). Details found in only one of them are attributed to Ashton's account ("by Ashton's account") or cut. Gregory Flemming's 2014 book is the fullest modern study but was not opened.
- **The 1723 trial pamphlet (TRY)** and Johnson's Roberts chapter (JOH) are two printings of the Cape Coast record. The court's suspicion of staged forcing is in Johnson's printing; narrated as "Johnson's version of that trial" with the pen-name warning.
- **Jameson (JAM)**: Boston court records of 1717 (Davis) and 1724 (Phillips's crew).
- **Leeson (LEE10)**: peer-reviewed economics paper; used for the reputation argument and the role of newspapers, attributed to him.
- **Slavery**: D. Wilson (WIL) and the Canadian Encyclopedia (CE) for the Black captives sold without trial after Cape Coast; History Is Now (HIN) and the *Provincetown Independent* (PI) for John Julian (as in episodes 3 and 6).

## Sensitivity checks done

- "You" is an adult (nineteen). Nothing romantic or sexual; the marriage question is about whom pirates chose to keep.
- Violence stays off screen: threats with a pistol are named, the misfire is a non-event, the rising is one sentence, executions are counts. The cutlass scene shows a frightened young man who does not raise it.
- Slavery: the Black captives sold after Cape Coast and John Julian are presented gravely as people the law treated as property, with no joke nearby, and the ending returns to them.
- No real person is a character. "You", Tom, the captain, the quartermaster, the surgeon, the navigator, the carpenter, the gunner, the merchant master, the clerk and the mother are fictional. Ashton, Low, Russell, Barnard, Davis, Bellamy, South, Phillips, Fillmore, Cheeseman, Glasby, Archer, Scudamore, Rolls, Snelgrave, Blackbeard, Julian, Leeson and Wilson appear only in narration.
- No parrot, peg leg, treasure map or chains in the images. Every paragraph can be drawn as a flat 2D scene (two sloops alongside, the quartermaster's ledger, a deck at night with Tom and a biscuit, a merchant sloop sailing away, a boarding with a lowered cutlass, a beach with water barrels and jungle, a harbor with a fort and a clerk's table, a village path and a doorway).

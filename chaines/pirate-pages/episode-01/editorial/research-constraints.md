# Pirate Pages — factual guardrails for this episode

The protagonist Tom, the crew, vessel and individual voyage are fictional narrative devices, set around1715 in the Caribbean. They are not a named historical voyage or a universal biography of every pirate. The narration uses a second-person everyday-life story to explain historically grounded conditions.

- Treat the early-eighteenth-century Golden Age setting as context, not a claim that all piracy began or ended in one year.
- Do not make pirates universally democratic, free, wealthy, egalitarian or harmless. Articles, authority and shares differed by crew; officers, sailors and forced/captive workers did not have identical lives.
- The single-masted fore-and-aft gaff-main/jib sloop is a plausible designed period vessel, not a precise reconstruction or the universal ship of pirates. Ship chores, food storage, rope/sail handling and uncertainty drive the visual narrative.
- Hard biscuit, preserved food and lack of fresh produce illustrate prolonged nutritional risk. Scurvy is connected to vitaminC deficiency; do not show instantaneous shipwide disease or diagnose one illustration.
- Merchant crews and people deprived of freedom appear as sober educational context. Do not glamorize coercion, invent a rescue by Tom or create demeaning auction/caricature imagery.
- Johnson's1724 A General History is a near-period account with attribution/reliability limitations. Individual sensational tales and purported rules cannot be presented as universal established fact.
- Future royal pardons are expressly a later possibility, not an event happening during the fictional1715 moment. The marooning panel is a contextual aside, not Tom's actual fate.
- The locked final English script contains the actual spoken qualifications; preserve those qualifications when writing the next episode.

The machine-readable source catalog retains actual URLs, successes and failures. The short excerpt ledger binds supporting text to original source hashes. Full museum articles/HTML, raw web responses and credentials are intentionally excluded from the public repository.

**Title (fixed):** Why Being a Pirate Quartermaster Was Worse Than You Think

## Description (English)

You held the crew's money, the boarding list and every complaint aboard, and you went first into every boat. Step into the waistcoat of an imagined pirate quartermaster in the Caribbean around 1717, sailing with Tom and the captain from our earlier episodes, and discover what the crew's elected officer really did: articles sworn on oath, boarding first, deciding what to keep and what to throw into the sea, judging quarrels between friends, commanding a captured sloop, and reading a royal price list that never mentioned his job. This illustrated story draws on trial records from 1722 and 1724, William Snelgrave's eyewitness account, the London Gazette, Captain Charles Johnson's General History of the Pyrates (used critically), and research from Royal Museums Greenwich and the economist Peter Leeson. The quartermaster and crew are fictional, but piracy harmed real people, including enslaved Africans who were never anyone's share. Subscribe to Pirate Pages for another life behind the legends.

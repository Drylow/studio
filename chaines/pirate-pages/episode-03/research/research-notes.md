# Pirate Pages — episode 03 research notes

**Title:** Why Being a Pirate Quartermaster Was Worse Than You Think
**Script:** `chaines/pirate-pages/episode-03/script-en.txt`: 2,838 words (`wc -w`), 79 paragraphs, about 20:25 at episode 1's measured voice pace (2,744 words = 1,185 s). Measure the real duration on the final voice.
**Setting:** The imagined quartermaster ("you") of Tom's modest one-mast sloop, Caribbean, around 1717 to 1718, the same crew as episodes 1 and 2. The re-election, the crew's articles, the Bible vote, the boarding of the merchant sloop, the buckle quarrel, the second sloop, the one-week command and the final pardon count are fictional devices. The narration says so in paragraph 3. Real events (Snelgrave 1719, Whydah and Cape Lopez 1722, Cape Coast Castle 1722, Boston 1724) are narrator asides, not events in the fictional present.

## How sources were weighed

- **Johnson, *A General History of the Pyrates* (1724)** was read in the full Project Gutenberg text and searched locally. "Captain Charles Johnson" is a pen name and the book mixes fact with invention (see episode 2 notes: LEE fn. 6, MHS). Johnson's copies of official documents (the 1717 proclamation, the Cape Coast trial) were checked against the original printings (London Gazette, 1723 trial pamphlet). Where a claim rests on Johnson alone, the narration says so ("only that one book from seventeen twenty-four", "Johnson's book alone", "Only Johnson's book"). The digits "1724" are spelled out for the voice. Where Leeson only quotes Johnson, the narration attributes the claim to Johnson ("Johnson says"), as in episode 2.
- **The 1723 trial pamphlet (TRY)** is the printed record of the Cape Coast Castle Admiralty court, read in the archive.org OCR text. It also reprints Captain Chaloner Ogle's report from the London Gazette of 4 September 1722. Johnson's Roberts chapter abridges this same record. They are two printings, not two independent investigations, and this is flagged where it matters.
- **Jameson (JAM)** reprints manuscript records of the Boston Admiralty court of May 1724 (Massachusetts Archives vol. 63), provincial bills, and newspaper extracts. It is independent of Johnson.
- **Snelgrave (SNE)** is an eyewitness held by pirates at the Sierra Leone River in 1719. He was a slave-trading captain, and the script says so.
- **Leeson (LEE, LEE10)** are peer-reviewed economics papers. They rely heavily on Johnson and Snelgrave, and their rational-choice reading flatters pirate "democracy". The script balances them with victims, forced men and enslaved people.
- Quotations are spoken with modernized spelling ("chusing" becomes "choosing", "Quarter-master" becomes "quartermaster"). The wording is otherwise unchanged.

### Fictional elements (not historical claims)

The re-election, the Bible-or-hatchet vote, the list kept "on your sloop", the merchant sloop's papers, the goods taken and left, the dealer's prices, the buckle quarrel and its resolution, the second sloop, the eight-man command and Tom as watcher, the narrow pardon vote and the final division of the chest. None is presented as a real event. The two-share split for "you" follows the Roberts articles (#10), which the imagined crew is shown to have borrowed from.

## Cut as uncertain or single-source

- **Magnes's defence** that his post as quartermaster obliged him to board prizes and "always went against his Conscience" (TRY only; OCR partly garbled). Cut.
- **Magnes leaving the Dutch ship Gertruyt "only enough to carry him to the Mine"** (TRY only). Cut, although the fictional bread in §31 echoes the practice described by Johnson (#22).
- **Bellamy's Whydah: money in open chests between decks, none to be taken "without the Quarter Masters leave"** (Peter Hoof's testimony). Seen only through LEE citing Rediker (2004) and a *History Is Now* blog. The Jameson fragment of the 1717 trial lacks the passage. Cut.
- **Blackbeard's quartermaster William Howard** arrested in Virginia and saved by the extended pardon. The Colonial Williamsburg page returned 403. The QAR crew-trial post names only Israel Hands as pardoned, and other results conflate Howard of Ocracoke. Cut.

## Sensitivity checks done

- Violence stays off screen. Boarding is shown as surrender and inventory; threats and beatings are named by witnesses, without wounds. The duel is described as a procedure that the fictional quartermaster avoids. Hangings and the gibbet are named without description.
- Slavery is treated gravely: Snelgrave is identified as a slave-trading captain; the Whydah burning is narrated soberly with Ogle's words and a clear flag on the Johnson-only number; the roughly seventy Black men sold without trial are stated plainly; the conclusion says the enslaved "were never anyone's share". There is no loot humour near these lines. ENSLAVED_ASIDE should appear only with dignity, never in an inventory or auction frame.
- The captured sailors (CAPTURED_SAILORS) are shown as frightened victims; the narration says they are right to be afraid.
- No real person is a character. "You", the captain, Tom, the crewmate and the dealer are fictional. Johnson, Snelgrave, Magnes, Simpson, Archer, Spriggs, Low, Rackham, Vane, Roberts, Blackbeard, Phillips, Lowther and Ogle appear only in narration.
- No parrot, peg leg, treasure map or buried-treasure gag. Every paragraph can be drawn as a flat 2D scene (deck, boat, chest, ledger, beach, harbour, proclamation, castle walls seen from the sea, Boston harbour at a distance).

# Pirate Pages — episode 06 research notes

**Title:** Why Being a Pirate Navigator Was Worse Than You Think
**Script:** `chaines/pirate-pages/episode-06/script-en.txt`: 2,744 words (`wc -w`), 66 paragraphs, about 19:45 at episode 1's measured voice pace (2,744 words = 1,185 s). Measure the real duration on the final voice.
**Setting:** The imagined sailing master and navigator ("you") of Tom's modest one-mast sloop, Caribbean, around 1717 to 1718, the same crew as episodes 1 to 5. He is a NEW character, described in §3: tall and thin, about thirty-five, curly black hair to the shoulders, thin mustache, long gray coat, brass dividers. He was forced from a merchant ship two years earlier and later voted master. The noon sights, the compass lesson, the captain's morning position, the boarding for instruments, the vote on the route, the fog and the reef, the island found by latitude, the temptation to escape, the list of people who might speak for him, the pardon vote and the last course are fictional devices; §3 says the crew and voyage are imagined. Real events (Sharp 1681, the Whydah 1717, La Concorde and the Queen Anne's Revenge 1717–18, Cape Coast Castle 1722, Phillips's crew 1724) are narrator asides.

## How sources were weighed

- **The 1723 trial pamphlet (TRY)** and Johnson's Roberts chapter (JOH) are two printings of the Cape Coast Castle record. Testimony found only in Johnson's printing is narrated as "Johnson's version" (Trengrove's evidence, the musician James White, "chief among brutes", the instruments left aboard robbed ships).
- **Johnson (JOH)**: pen name, mixes fact and invention (§6 says so). Johnson-only stories (Glasby's pirate trial and Ashplant's pistols, Israel Hands shot in the knee, Blackbeard grounding his ship on purpose) are narrated with "Johnson's book alone" or "Johnson claims", and the grounding is set against a modern archaeologist's preliminary view.
- **Jameson (JAM)** prints the Boston depositions of May 1724 (Henry Giles).
- **Archaeology of the Queen Anne's Revenge**: N. L. Baker's 2024 ECU thesis (abstract), the QAR Conservation Lab blog, National Geographic (2009), Divernet reporting J. Borrelli (2020).
- **Navigation background**: Royal Museums Greenwich (latitude and longitude; the 1714 Longitude Act) and the History of Parliament blog (A. Baker, 2014). The backstaff, quadrant, log line, sandglass, compass points, slate reckoning, sounding and running down a latitude are described as what "you" do (period seamanship), not as claims about a named crew.
- **John Julian**: History Is Now (2017) and the *Provincetown Independent* (2025). They differ on certainty ("was sold" / "likely sold"); both are narrated.

### Fictional elements (not historical claims)

The navigator himself, his capture two years earlier and his election as master, the noon and night sights, Tom's compass lesson, the captain repeating the position, the spare compass and charts taken and the quadrant left, the vote on the route, the fog, the reef and the drink, the island with a stream found along a latitude, the night he does not escape, the short list of people who might speak for him, the pardon vote and the last course to the governor.

## Sensitivity checks done

- Violence stays off screen: the two men shot after Glasby's escape, the shot in Israel Hands's knee and Nutt thrown overboard are each named in one clause, without wounds. The fog scene ends with a reef avoided.
- John Julian's fate is narrated soberly ("He was treated as property"), with no joke nearby, and with both sources' degrees of certainty.
- La Concorde is again called a slave ship; Snelgrave does not appear.
- No real person is a character. "You", the captain, Tom, the surgeon, the carpenter, the quartermaster and the dealer are fictional. Glasby, Roberts, Blackbeard, Israel Hands, Ashplant, Sharp, Ringrose, Julian, Davis, Bellamy, Phillips, Nutt, Cheeseman, Giles, Borrelli, Moore and Johnson appear only in narration.
- No parrot, peg leg, treasure map or "X marks the spot": charts are plain coastlines. Every paragraph can be drawn as a flat 2D scene (noon sight with a backstaff, log line and sandglass at the stern, compass lesson, pole star at night, sounding lead on deck, artefacts on a lab table, fog and surf over a reef, an island with a stream seen from the rail, a proclamation, the sloop heading for a harbor).

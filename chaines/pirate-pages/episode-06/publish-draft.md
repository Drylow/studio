# Pirate Pages — episode 06 publishing draft

**Title (fixed):** Why Being a Pirate Navigator Was Worse Than You Think

## Description (English)

You were the only man aboard who knew where the ship was, so they never let you leave it. Step into the long gray coat of an imagined pirate navigator in the Caribbean around 1717, sailing with Tom, the captain, the quartermaster, the surgeon and the carpenter from our earlier episodes, and discover what the sailing master really faced: taken for his skills, guessing longitude before anyone could measure it, reading the seabed from a lump of tallow, deciding which instruments to steal, losing votes on his own course, and watching every island he could never set foot on. This illustrated story draws on the 1722 Cape Coast Castle trial record, the Boston court records of 1724, archaeology from the Queen Anne's Revenge, Royal Museums Greenwich, Captain Charles Johnson's General History of the Pyrates (used critically), and research by the economist Peter Leeson. The navigator and crew are fictional, but piracy harmed real people, including the forced men who could not leave and the Native American pilot John Julian, who was never given a trial.

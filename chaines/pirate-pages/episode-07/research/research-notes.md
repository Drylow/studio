# Pirate Pages — episode 07 research notes

**Title:** Why Being a Pirate Gunner Was Worse Than You Think
**Script:** `chaines/pirate-pages/episode-07/script-en.txt`: 2,742 words (`wc -w`), 62 paragraphs, about 19:50 at episode 1's measured voice pace (2,744 words = 1,185 s). Measure the real duration on the final voice.
**Setting:** The imagined gunner ("you") of Tom's modest one-mast sloop with its TWO small cannons (episode 1 visual contract), Caribbean, around 1717 to 1718, the same crew as episodes 1 to 6. He is a NEW character, described in §3: short and stocky, about forty-five, shaved head, thick black beard going gray, sleeveless leather jerkin over a gray shirt, powder horn, slow match; a volunteer and former merchant gunner. The loading drill, the vote against practice, the warning shot, the merchant's answering shot and her gunner, the stores taken and left, the powder stowed in the hold, the week of rain, the spark Tom stamps out, the pardon vote and the drawn charges are fictional devices; §3 says the crew and voyage are imagined. Real events (Ocracoke 1718, Cape Lopez and Cape Coast 1722, Phillips's crew 1724, the Queen Anne's Revenge finds) are narrator asides.

## How sources were weighed

- **The 1723 trial pamphlet (TRY)** and Johnson's Roberts chapter (JOH) are two printings of the Cape Coast record. Several gunner testimonies (the Liverpool gunner, the Mercy's gunner, Hugh Menzies, the forced gunner's mate) are in TRY only: one court record with named witnesses, narrated as what "a witness said" or "the court heard". Sutton's "not taken half" is Johnson's printing only, narrated as "In Johnson's version".
- **Johnson (JOH)**: pen name, mixes fact and invention (§5 says so). Johnson-only details (Philip Morton killed at Ocracoke; Sutton's joke about thirteen guns) are narrated as "Johnson's book alone".
- **Queen Anne's Revenge**: W. R. Lusardi's 1999 project report (LUS) is a first-hand conservation report; the AP (2018) and Maritime Executive (2013, 2018), WRAL/AP (2011), Royal Museums Greenwich and the Smithsonian give independent confirmation where available. The drift-pin load and the unloading method are LUS only and are attributed to "the nineteen ninety-nine report".
- **Ocracoke**: Johnson and the Smithsonian (C. Woodard) differ slightly on casualties (20 or 21); both figures are narrated.
- The gun-loading drill (sponge, charge, wad, shot, wad, prime, match) is described as what "you" do, as generic period gunnery, not as a sourced claim about a named crew.

## Sensitivity checks done

- Violence stays off screen. The warning shot and the merchant's shot hurt nobody. Ocracoke and Cape Lopez are told through casualty counts and outcomes, without wounds; Roberts's death is "killed at once". Sparks's death is one sentence. Hugh Menzies's beating is named without detail.
- No explosion with victims is shown or suggested for the images; the grenades and the loaded gun are objects on a conservation table.
- No real person is a character. "You", the captain, the quartermaster, Tom, the merchant's gunner and the dealer are fictional. Sutton, Kreft, de Haen, Menzies, Glasby, Roberts, Blackbeard, Maynard, Morton, Ogle, Sparks, Cooke, Woodes Rogers, Watkins-Kenney and Lusardi appear only in narration.
- No parrot, peg leg, eye patch or treasure map. Every paragraph can be drawn as a flat 2D scene (two small guns on deck, loading drill, a warning shot and splash, a merchant hold with powder barrels, the gunner sleeping on barrels, conservators unloading a cannon in a lab, paper fragments, Ocracoke and Cape Lopez seen as distant ships and smoke, powder drying on sailcloth, a proclamation, guns being unloaded at dusk).

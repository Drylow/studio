You were in charge of the most frightening thing aboard, and your best day was the day you never fired. Step into the leather jerkin of an imagined pirate gunner in the Caribbean around 1717, sailing with Tom, the captain, the quartermaster, the surgeon, the carpenter and the navigator from our earlier episodes, and discover what the man beside the powder really did: firing warnings instead of broadsides, robbing other ships of their gunner's stores, sleeping on the barrels, drying gunpowder in the rain, and learning what happened when pirate guns met a real fight at Ocracoke and Cape Lopez. This illustrated story draws on the 1722 Cape Coast Castle trial record, the Boston court records of 1724, the conservation reports and finds from Blackbeard's Queen Anne's Revenge (including a loaded cannon and a book used as wadding), Royal Museums Greenwich, the Smithsonian, Captain Charles Johnson's General History of the Pyrates (used critically), and research by the economist Peter Leeson. The gunner and crew are fictional, but piracy harmed real people, including the sailors on the ships those guns were aimed at. Subscribe to Pirate Pages for another life behind the legends.

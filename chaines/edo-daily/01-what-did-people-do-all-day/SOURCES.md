## Source Ledger
- I: authored illustrative action, physical consequence or explicitly qualified interpretation; not documentary evidence for a named person's day.
- S01: [Edo-Tokyo Museum, permanent exhibition](https://www.edo-tokyo-museum.or.jp/en/p-exhibition/). Divided row houses, varied occupations, mutual assistance, bells indicating approximate hours. Model dimensions are NOT assigned to our pictured home. Some museum reconstructions are late-period; no automatic dating of all displayed props to 1700.
- S02: [Seiko Museum, Seasonal Time System](https://museum.seiko.co.jp/en/knowledge/relation_16/). Daylight and night each divided into six units, varying seasonally. No exact modern time labels used.
- S03: [Tokyo Waterworks Bureau, history](https://www.waterworks.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/suidojigyo/gaiyou/rekishi), [Tokyo Waterworks Historical Museum](https://www.suidorekishi.jp/en/). Stone/wood conduits supplied waterworks wells; Tamagawa completed in 1654. Bridge pictures illustrate urban connections, NOT the Tamagawa aqueduct itself.
- S04: [Tokyo Metropolitan Government, Edo Sashimono](https://www.dento-tokyo.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/english/items/16.html). Wooden joinery, development across 17th-19th centuries, attention to hidden workmanship. Our household is a plausible fictional joiner, not an identified workshop or proof that all woodworking lacked nails.
- S05: [Tokyo Metropolitan Library, food service industries](https://www.library.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/portals/0/edo/tokyo_library/english/gourmet/page1-1.html). Male retainers/employees, itinerant sellers, stalls, growth of prepared food after the 1657 reconstruction labor influx.
- S06: [Tokyo Metropolitan Library, food supply](https://www.library.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/portals/0/edo/tokyo_library/english/gourmet/page2-1.html). Agricultural/fishing production and land/sea distribution. No claim that later famous sushi or tempura already dominates around 1700. Breakfast is a qualified household illustration, not an exact dated menu.
- S07: [Asian Art Museum, Edo-period society](https://education.asianart.org/resources/edo-period-society-in-japan/). Civilian samurai roles in peace, artisans/merchants meeting demand, merchant wealth and consumption restrictions. Do not repeat the page's over-simple universal claims about taxes or hereditary immobility.
- S08: [Bank of Japan Currency Museum, monetary history](https://www.imes.boj.or.jp/cm/english/history/content/). Gold denominations, weighed silver, copper currency. No asserted cost for an invented purchase, no fixed exchange rate, no modern yen conversion.
- S09: [Tokyo Fire Department, Edo fire service](https://www.tfd.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/learning/elib/times/times01.html), [major Edo fires](https://www.tfd.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/learning/elib/times/times02.html). Wooden housing/open flame risks. Town fire brigades date to 1718: excluded from our circa-1700 street imagery. No death toll asserted.
- S10: [Tokyo Metropolitan Library, rebuilding after Meireki](https://www.library.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/portals/0/edo/tokyo_library/english/machi/page2-1.html). Post-1657 relocation and changed routes/development. No precise population, area or casualty figures.
- S11: [Tokyo bathhouse association, sento history](https://www.1010.or.jp/english/sento-history/). Edo public baths and private-bath restrictions linked to fire prevention. Exteriors only; no universal mixed/separate-bathing claim or twentieth-century mountain-wall imagery.
- S12: [Tokyo Metropolitan Library, Kabuki theaters](https://www.library.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/portals/0/edo/tokyo_library/english/kabuki/page1-1.html). Greater acceptance by late 17th century. Not an assertion of nightly attendance, no 19th-century Saruwakamachi location, no identified real actor as a depicted character.
- S13: Same museum source as S01: growth of publishing and print culture. Do not use late-period circulating-library statistics or universal literacy figures.
- S14: [The Met, Art of the Edo Period](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/art-of-the-edo-period-1615-1868). Kabuki and ukiyo-e as parts of urban cultural life.
- S15: [The Met, River of Omue and Bridge of Oda](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/56502). Ink-on-paper woodblock print dated 1700. No copied image; illustrative sheets use generic monochrome architecture. No later full-color print presented as circa-1700 evidence.
- S16: [Tokyo Metropolitan Library, Great-Edo Fashion](https://www.library.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/portals/0/edo/tokyo_library/english/fashion/index.html). Dress evolved across the period. Character clothing is a consistent stylization, not an exact catalogued garment; no claim of universal female occupation.

# Edo 01 Research Review

Period: approximately 1800, not 1700. Household and route are illustrative,
not the diary of a documented individual. Narration stays away from exact
prices, clock schedules, and a universal menu. Title scope includes other
occupations encountered through the assistant's errands.

- [Edo-Tokyo Museum, permanent exhibition](https://www.edo-tokyo-museum.or.jp/en/p-exhibition/): material anchors for row-house interiors, time bells, publication, commerce, and entertainment. The museum mixes dates; do not import its 1840s entertainment district model as an exact 1800 street plan or its 1832 shop facade as a specific protagonist's shop.
- [Tokyo Metropolitan Library, illustrated street sellers](https://www.library.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/portals/0/edo/tokyo_library/english/modal/index.html?d=6128): mobile selling and carrying. Artistic sources are not a minute-by-minute documentary.
- [Tokyo Metropolitan Government, public bathing](https://www.tokyoupdates.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/en/post-1607/): Edo-period spread of public bathhouses. Only the clothed entrance is staged; no copied modern bathhouse facade or modern bathing timetable.

## Boundaries

No paid-work-free city, invented wages, daily theatrical attendance, universal
literacy, fixed modern working hours, or exact household floor measurement.
The narrative's particular purchases, delivery, repair and shared tea are
illustrative actions, not claims recovered from a single historical diary.
All visual changes of time, place and object state need authored shot plans.

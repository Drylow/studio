# Ep01: BABYLON – 3D TIMELAPSE – 1900 BC to AD 200

About 305 s at 1920×1080, 60 fps. One unbroken drone flight with no voiceover and its own score. The style is the approved pilot's: low-poly toy city, tilt-shift, buildings that grow out of the ground, and the fixed HUD.

## Setting (world metres; x east, z south; the camera mostly looks north)
- A flat Mesopotamian plain (y≈2.4) of dry clay and sand. A green irrigated belt of fields and date-palm groves runs along the Euphrates and its canals.
- The Euphrates flows N→S through the city at x≈-350, about 150 m wide, with a slight meander.
- Inner city (Neo-Babylonian): a rectangle x∈[-1000, 1000], z∈[-800, 800], with a double wall (Imgur-Enlil + Nimitti-Enlil) and a moat. The west half is the smaller "new city"; the east half holds the sacred precinct.
- Old Babylonian city: the east bank only, with a mud wall x∈[-270, 650], z∈[-600, 600].
- Sacred precinct: Etemenanki (base 91 m) at (-150, -170); Esagila south of it at (-150, 20).
- Southern Palace at the NW of the east half, by the river. The Ishtar Gate is on the north wall at x≈-20. The Processional Way runs south from the gate to the precinct, then west to the Euphrates bridge at z≈-80.
- The outer wall (Nebuchadnezzar) is a triangle on the east bank, with the Summer Palace at its north tip.

## Captions (2 lines, serif). Every claim is checked against Wikipedia (Babylon, Etemenanki, Ishtar Gate)
1. Hammurabi made Babylon the capital of an empire / and issued one of history's best-known law codes.
2. In 1595 BC the Hittite king Mursili I / sacked Babylon.
3. The Kassites renamed the city Karduniash / and ruled it for over four centuries.
4. In 689 BC the Assyrian king Sennacherib razed Babylon / and threw its rubble into the canals.
5. Etemenanki, "Temple of the Foundation of Heaven and Earth", / stood on a base about 91 m square, a likely model for the Tower of Babel.
6. Faced with blue glazed bricks, the Ishtar Gate and the Processional Way / showed lions, bulls and dragons.
7. Later writers credited Nebuchadnezzar with the Hanging Gardens, / but no Babylonian text mentions them.
8. In 539 BC Cyrus the Great took Babylon / with little or no fighting.
9. Alexander the Great died here in 323 BC, / in the palace of Nebuchadnezzar.
10. Alexander had the ziggurat torn down to rebuild it, / but he died before the work began.
11. Seleucus I founded Seleucia on the Tigris, / and Babylon's people slowly moved away.

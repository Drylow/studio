## 1. Founding and political eras

- 1191 — Emperor Henry VI grants suzerainty over the area to Genoa; the Rock becomes a Genoese fortress site. [WP-HIST]; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince%27s_Palace_of_Monaco
- 10 June 1215 — Genoese Ghibellines led by Fulco del Cassello begin the fortress on the Rock (four towers + ramparts = core of today's palace). [WP-HIST]; [FR-HIST]; Prince's Palace page above
- 8 Jan 1297 (night of 8-9 Jan per French sources) — François (Francesco) Grimaldi takes the fortress, reportedly disguised as a (Franciscan) monk; the disguise is legend, disputed by some historians. [FM]; [FR-MC]; [FR-HIST]; Prince's Palace page. [WP-HIST] gives only "1297".
- 1346 / 1355 — Grimaldis acquire Menton (1346) and Roquebrune (1355); territory then reaches far beyond the Rock. [WP-HIST]
- 7 June 1524 (Treaty of Burgos) / 15 Nov 1524 (Tordesillas) — Monaco under Spanish/imperial protection of Charles V. **CONFLICT:** brief said 1524/1525; every source read says 1524. Use "1524". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Tordesillas_(1524); [WP-HIST]
- 1633 — Spain recognises Honoré II as sovereign ("Prince"). [WP-HIST]
- 14 Sept 1641 — Treaty of Péronne: French protectorate replaces Spain. [FR-HIST]; [WP-HIST]
- 1793-1814 — Annexed by revolutionary France (renamed "Fort d'Hercule"); palace looted, used as military hospital and poorhouse. [FR-HIST]; Prince's Palace page. **CONFLICT:** [JSSP-1878] gives 1792-1814. Use "1793".
- 17 May 1814 / 30 May 1814 — end of French rule; Treaty of Paris restores the principality. [WP-HIST]; [FR-HIST]
- 20 Nov 1815 — Second Treaty of Paris (Congress of Vienna settlement): Sardinian protectorate, reaffirmed by Stupinigi 1817. [FR-HIST]; [WP-HIST]
- 21 March 1848 — Menton and Roquebrune declare themselves free cities under Sardinian protection (lead of the article says May 1848). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Cities_of_Menton_and_Roquebrune
- 1847 — Monaco 1,250 inh., Roquebrune 850, Menton 4,900; principality about 24.5 km². [FR-MC]
- 2 Feb 1861 — Franco-Monegasque Treaty: Monaco recognised sovereign, cedes Menton + Roquebrune to France for 4 million gold francs; territory reduced "by more than 90%". [WP-HIST]; [FR-HIST]; [FR-MC]
- 5 Jan 1911 — first constitution (after the 1910 Monegasque Revolution). [FR-HIST]; [WP-HIST] (year only)
- July 1918 — treaty of limited French protection. [WP-HIST]
- 11 Nov 1942 — Italian army occupies Monaco; Sept 1943 German occupation, deportations of Jews begin (per [WP-HIST]); 3 Sept 1944 liberation. [WP-HIST]; [FR-HIST]
- July 1944 — Allied bombing destroys Fort Antoine (rebuilt by Rainier III in 1953). [FM]
- 9 May 1949 — Rainier III accedes on death of Louis II. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainier_III,_Prince_of_Monaco
- 18-19 April 1956 — civil (Palace) and religious (Saint Nicholas Cathedral) wedding of Rainier III and Grace Kelly. Rainier III page above
- 17 Dec 1962 — revised constitution (female suffrage, death penalty abolished). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Monaco; [WP-HIST]
- 28 May 1993 — Monaco admitted to the UN as 183rd member (Security Council res. 829 of 26 May 1993). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_829 (seen via search result, not opened)
- 6 April 2005 — Rainier III dies; Albert II succeeds (enthronement 12 July and 19 Nov 2005). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_II,_Prince_of_Monaco

## 2. Landmarks (year — fact — quarter — source)

- 1215 onward — Prince's Palace, Monaco-Ville (the Rock, about 60 m above the sea per [FM]). Phases: 1331-57 two buildings; 15th c. east wing; 1506-07 state-apartments wing; Honoré I (1523-81) arcaded courtyard; Honoré II (1597-1662) softened into a palace; Louis I baroque entrance + horseshoe staircase; 1793 looted; 1856-89 Charles III rebuilds St Mary's Tower; from 1949 Rainier III rebuilds the south wing. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince%27s_Palace_of_Monaco
- 1533 and 1714 — the two gates of the Rampe Major; 1700-1715 Fort Antoine (tip of the Rock); 1835-39 Avenue de la Porte Neuve. [FM]
- 1252 — first parish church of Saint Nicholas; demolished 1874. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Nicholas_Cathedral,_Monaco; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathédrale_Notre-Dame-Immaculée_de_Monaco
- 6 Jan 1875 – 12 Nov 1903 — Cathedral (architect Charles Lenormand), Monaco-Ville; consecrated 11 June 1911 (French WP: "1911"). Same two sources.
- 14 Dec 1856 — first casino, Villa Bellevue, La Condamine. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Carlo_Casino. **CONFLICT:** Monte Carlo page says first casino opened in La Condamine in 1862. Safe: "first casino, 1856 (La Condamine)".
- 13 May 1858 – 1863 — casino built on Les Spélugues plateau (Monte-Carlo), architect Gobineau de la Bretonnerie. Casino page. **CONFLICT:** its infobox says "July 1865". Safe caption: "1863 — Casino opens on the Spélugues".
- 2 April 1863 — Société des Bains de Mer founded (François Blanc, 50-year gambling concession). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soci%C3%A9t%C3%A9_des_bains_de_mer_de_Monaco
- 1864 — Hôtel de Paris opens, west side of Place du Casino, Monte-Carlo (extended 1865, remodelled 1908; reopened March 2019 after 4-year renovation). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%B4tel_de_Paris_Monte-Carlo
- 1 June 1866 — Spélugues renamed Monte-Carlo ("Mount Charles", for Charles III). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Carlo (infobox "Established"); [FR-HIST] (year)
- 1868 — railway reaches Monaco (Nice–Ventimiglia line); first station in La Condamine; underground station opens 7 Dec 1999. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monaco-Monte-Carlo_station; [FR-HIST]; Monte Carlo page
- 1878-79 — casino enlarged by Charles Garnier and Jules Dutrou; Salle Garnier opera inaugurated 25 Jan 1879 (Sarah Bernhardt), built in about 8.5 months, Monte-Carlo. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Op%C3%A9ra_de_Monte-Carlo; Casino page
- 25 April 1899 – 29 March 1910 — Oceanographic Museum (Prince Albert I, architect Paul Delefortrie), on the cliff of the Rock, Monaco-Ville; façade about 85 m high; 100,000 t of La Turbie stone; "eleven years to build". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanographic_Museum_of_Monaco (year 1910 only); exact dates via search of the Journal de Monaco 1899/1910 (not opened). One museum page reportedly gives 25 March 1910.
- 1901/1902 – 1926 — Port Hercule jetties built, La Condamine (first tender 2 July 1901, first rocks 1 March 1902); improved in the 1970s; extension with counter-jetty about 2001-2002; port about 16 ha. https://structurae.net/en/structures/port-hercule (1902-1926); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Hercule (completed 1926)
- 14 April 1929 — first Monaco Grand Prix, 100 laps of a 3.180 km street circuit, won by William Grover-Williams (Bugatti T35B); set up by Antony Noghès (Automobile Club de Monaco). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1929_Monaco_Grand_Prix
- 1939 — original Stade Louis II opens (AS Monaco). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Louis_II
- 1954-1961 — Larvotto / Portier reclamation (+54,000 m² east end, +35,000 m² at Portier), then the artificial Larvotto beach. [ML-1]; [ML-2]. [FR-MC]: Larvotto "built in the 1960s".
- 1954-1964 — railway put into a tunnel (frees surface land). [ML-1]
- 1965/1966 – 1971/1973 — Fontvieille reclaimed (about 22 ha / "220,000 m²" + 55,000 m² marina, sea up to 40 m deep, 1 km seawall), designed by Manfredi Nicoletti, works led by Gianfranco Gilardini, district built out 1970s-1990s. **CONFLICT:** start 1965 ([ML-1], [FR-MC] "initiative 1965") vs 1966 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fontvieille,_Monaco); end 1971 ([ML-1]) vs 1973 (search snippet). Safe: "about 1966-1973: Fontvieille won from the sea". 1981 is reportedly a cornerstone ceremony, not the end.
- May 1981 – 1984, inaugurated 25 Jan 1985 — new Stade Louis II on reclaimed land, Fontvieille (16,360 seats now; 18,500 per French WP). Stade Louis II page; [FR-MC]
- 20 July 2000 — Grimaldi Forum inaugurated, Larvotto seafront. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grimaldi_Forum
- 4 Nov 2009 – April 2015 — Tour Odéon, 170 m, 49 floors, La Rousse/Saint-Roman, architect Alexandre Giraldi; tallest in Monaco. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odeon_Tower (height); https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principato_di_Monaco ("tallest skyscraper of the principality", built 2009-2015)
- 2009 → 2015 → 4 Dec 2024 — Le Portier extension shelved (2009), relaunched (works from about 2015-2017), 18 caissons (last placed 18 July 2019), Mareterra inaugurated 4 Dec 2024: about 6 ha, +3% territory, €2 bn private, between Port Hercule and Grimaldi Forum, attached to Larvotto ward; buildings by Renzo Piano, Tadao Ando, Norman Foster; landscape Michel Desvigne. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mareterra; https://monaco-tribune.com/en/2024/12/princely-family-inaugurates-mareterra-eco-district. Paris Match reportedly says 5 Dec.

## 4. Area

- 1847: about 24.5 km² with Menton and Roquebrune; 1861: reduced by more than 90%. [FR-MC]
- Before modern reclamation: about 1.5 km² (150 ha). [ML-2]
- By 1880: about 5.5 ha already reclaimed (railway fill from 1865, Fontvieille, La Rousse, Condamine). https://www.tomorrow.city/monaco-land-reclamation; [FR-MC]
- 2016: 202.7 ha (2.03 km²). [IMSEE-2016]
- Since Dec 2024: about 208 ha (2.08 km²; ward table 208.4 ha). [WP-MC]; Monaco Tribune (above)
- Share reclaimed: "up to 20%" of today's land is artificial (tomorrow.city); French WP: about 40 ha gained; [ML-2] implies 150 → 202 ha (about 25%). **CONFLICT** — safe: "about a fifth of Monaco was won from the sea".

## 5. Colours and materials (for art direction)

- Old town (Monaco-Ville): "pastel-coloured façades", "typically Mediterranean colour scheme", old stonework, medieval lanes. https://www.visitmonaco.com/en/explore/the-most-beautiful-instagram-spots/narrow-streets-of-monaco-ville. No source read names the exact hues: ochre/pink/yellow is a reasonable reading of "pastel", not a sourced fact.
- Terracotta roofs and whitewashed façades along the coast; Belle Époque Monte-Carlo (late 19th - early 20th c.) = grand façades + wrought-iron balconies; modern towers = glass-clad. https://www.monacoproperties.mc/en/monaco-architecture-exploring-the-charms-that-define-real-estate.html
- Ramparts and Palace of Justice: grey porous marine tuff; cathedral: white La Turbie limestone (+ red/blue Esterel porphyry details). [FM]; French cathedral page
- Palace: rusticated ground floor, white marble balustrades, white La Turbie stone clock tower, crenellated towers. Prince's Palace page
- Oceanographic Museum: La Turbie stone, cliff-top façade. Oceanographic Museum page
- Salle Garnier interior: red and gold. Monte Carlo page

**Description :**

Tel Aviv, 1880 to now. From the orange groves and sand dunes north of the old port of Jaffa to the tallest skyline in Israel: 146 years rebuilt in 3D, year by year.

It starts with Ottoman Jaffa and Neve Tzedek, the first neighbourhood built outside the old town. Then come the 66 families who drew lots on the dunes in 1909, the Herzliya Hebrew Gymnasium, the expulsion of 1917 and the British Mandate. Patrick Geddes draws the master plan, thousands of Bauhaus buildings rise in the White City, and Tel Aviv Port opens. On 14 May 1948 the State of Israel is declared on Rothschild Boulevard, and in 1950 Tel Aviv and Jaffa become one city. The Shalom Meir Tower rises on the site of the old Gymnasium, Kings of Israel Square becomes Rabin Square, and the Azrieli and Sarona towers reshape the skyline. The video ends with the Red Line and the city in 2026.

📌 ABOUT THIS RECONSTRUCTION
This is an illustrative 3D reconstruction, not archival footage. Building footprints and heights come from OpenStreetMap. Towers appear in their real completion years; the dates of ordinary buildings are estimated from satellite data (GHSL, 1975–2020) and the documented founding dates of each neighbourhood. Lost buildings such as the original Herzliya Gymnasium are reconstructed from published histories, and landmark models are simplified. Population figures come from British Mandate censuses, the Tel Aviv-Yafo municipality and Israel's Central Bureau of Statistics; extents and populations are approximate.

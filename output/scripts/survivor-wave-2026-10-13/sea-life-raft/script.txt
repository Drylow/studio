You drag yourself into the life raft while the cargo ship tilts behind you. A teal creature strikes its stern, and containers slide into the sea. Your supply bag is still attached beside the emergency ladder. But a deckhand is climbing down that same ladder as the ship sinks. You have to get her aboard before you cut the raft loose, without leaving the supplies that may keep both of you alive.

The rope jerks tight. The raft bumps the hull. The red supply bag hangs above the water near the emergency station, close enough to reach only when the raft lifts on a swell.

You wait for the lift. Your hand closes around the strap. The clip will not open.

The deckhand is Tess. She clings to the ladder and calls for you to release the raft line before the sinking hull pulls it under. Her laminated crew card catches against a rung as she lowers herself. She tears it free and puts it inside her vest.

You take the knife from the raft pouch and free the supply bag's webbing. Tess moves another rung down while you pull the bag aboard. The hull drops and tightens the line between ship and raft. You cut that line before the next wave lifts you against the ladder.

The teal creature surfaces beside the stern. Its pale-yellow eye remains turned toward the cargo ship as it drives against another section of hull. Tess loses her footing on the ladder. You keep hold of the loose raft line as she falls toward the water.

The raft shoots backward on the next wave. Tess lets go of the ladder and lands in the water between you and the ship. You throw the loose line toward her. She misses once. The second time, it crosses her wrist, and she clamps down with both hands.

Tess gets one arm over the raft tube, then a knee. You lean the other way while she rolls aboard. The red bag slides toward the low edge, and you stop it with your foot. She lies still long enough to get her breath before asking you to move the bag clear of the water.

By the second morning, the cargo ship has disappeared beneath the sea. You have a folded canopy, two short paddles, a hand pump and the supply bag. Tess checks its sealed food and drinks while you open the raft's repair pouch. She puts the knife back where both of you can reach it. Your navy cap is still on your head, wet along the brim.

On day three, you hook a floating cooler lid with a paddle. Tess brings it aboard and leaves it flat beneath the supplies. A blue tarp drifts close enough to recover later. She secures it above the canopy frame so you can sit out of the strongest sun. Neither piece came from a rescue vessel. You still have to move through the cargo wreckage around you.

That afternoon, you slap the water with a paddle to move a crate away from the raft. A long teal back rises thirty paces off your left side.

The creature turns toward the splash, not toward the raft. Its pale-yellow eye breaks the surface, then sinks again where the crate knocks against a plank. The plank bumps the crate twice. The creature follows those knocks until the debris spreads out and quiets down.

After the creature follows those repeated knocks, Tess asks you to stop slapping the paddle against debris. She does not know how far it can detect a disturbance. You guide a loose plank past the raft without hitting it, then wait to see whether the teal body turns back. It stays beside the noisy crate until the current separates the pieces.

By day five, a wooden packing frame and a broken crate drift beside the raft. One frame edge is padded with cargo foam. Tess wants its yellow strapping to hold the canopy steady. You ask whether the padded frame could keep the supplies from sliding against the soft floor. She studies it before agreeing to bring it aboard.

You draw the padded frame close with a paddle while Tess controls its loose line. She secures it flat beneath the supply bag. The crate contains more foam and a thin rod from its latch. You keep the rod away from the tubes while she searches for a way to support the sagging tarp.

Tess uses the rod to prop up the tarp, fastening it to the frame instead of pressing its end into the raft. You sit beneath the makeshift shade and watch the loose gear whenever a wave tips the floor. The cooler lid remains flat under the supplies.

A rain squall crosses the cargo debris on day seven. You see the leviathan circle there, farther from your raft than it was yesterday. Tess points out a crate repeatedly striking another piece of wreckage beneath the rain. You cannot tell which disturbance has drawn it, but it has moved away from your drifting shelter. You secure the canopy without knocking the rod against the frame. Tess catches rain in the empty drink containers while you hold them steady. You move the refilled containers beneath the supply bag before the squall passes. Both of you have been avoiding the almost-empty last bottle since morning.

The gray shark reaches you on day nine. It bites at a loose strip trailing beside the raft and pulls the edge down. Tess releases that strip before the raft turns farther. You keep the recovered cooler lid inside while she gets both hands back onto the raft.

The shark circles again, its gray back and dark eye distinct from the teal leviathan farther out. You lower a paddle to keep its head away from the tube. Tess asks you to hold the raft steady instead of chasing it through the water.

The shark catches the paddle blade and twists. You release it before the broken shaft drags your hand against the raft edge. Tess pulls you back by the vest. The shark takes the blade underwater and leaves you with one usable paddle and a short broken piece.

The shark drops away with the paddle in its mouth. You have one paddle now, and the raft starts turning broadside to the swell.

The missing paddle makes the next days harder. One push turns the raft instead of holding its heading. Tess tests a corner of the emergency cloth against the canopy line. When the wind catches it, you use the remaining paddle to check the turn. You stop the test when the line pulls against the tube. She folds the cloth beside the supplies rather than keep a strained sail overhead.

On day eleven, the shark returns toward another strip dangling from the frame. Tess pulls it inside. You move the crate away from your feet, but its corner strikes the frame twice. The teal body rises farther out. You let the crate settle instead of kicking it again.

The shark moves off as the leviathan reaches the knocking debris. Tess asks you to remember where each creature came from. The shark approached the raft directly; the teal one turned after the impacts. That difference may matter later, but you keep the loose gear quiet rather than test it with both animals nearby.

On day fourteen, you see an island ridge beyond a ring of breaking surf. Neither of you has a chart showing a passage through those reefs. From the raft, you can see calmer water behind a hooked rock, but the current carries you along the outer edge. Tess watches the surf instead of promising a route.

You try pushing toward the island. The raft turns across the swell before it gets closer. Tess asks you to stop and watch where the floating seaweed is moving. It passes sideways along the reef. She wants to see a real opening before spending your remaining paddle on another approach.

On day fifteen, the storm comes from behind you.

The storm reaches you on day fifteen. Wind flattens the tarp and bends its supporting rod. Tess frees the strained canopy line before it pulls harder against the tube. You push the folded emergency cloth beneath the supplies while water fills the raft floor.

A wave lifts the supply bag toward the side. You catch its strap while Tess keeps hold of the canopy frame. The loose crate hits your hip, then strikes the tube. You cannot steady the crate and protect the bag at once. Tess cuts the crate's remaining line, and you push it clear.

The crate rolls away with most of the yellow strapping. Tess saves a short strip beside the repair pouch. The blue tarp tears down the middle and the support rod bends flat. You fold what remains out of the way before the next wave drives it against your knees.

By morning, one tube has softened. The broken paddle struck that part of the raft when the shark took it, and the storm worked the damaged spot harder. Tess presses the tube and asks you for the repair pouch. You take the heavy supplies off that side while she checks the damage.

You use the raft's repair kit together without pretending the damage has disappeared. Tess holds the material steady while you keep the patch corner clear of the water. After it is in place, you take turns using the hand pump. The tube firms enough to hold your weight, but you keep checking the repaired edge whenever the floor shifts.

On day twenty-one, you reach the outer reef current. The island is close enough to see a beach behind the hooked rock. Tess points to a narrow break where the surf looks weaker. You have watched that section from farther out, but the current has changed the angle of your approach.

The current slides you toward exposed rocks. Tess uses the single paddle while you lower the emergency cloth to stop it pulling sideways. The raft turns too late. A dark rock rises ahead with water draining from its edge.

You brace the paddle against the rock as you pass. Its blade holds just long enough to move the tube clear of the sharpest edge. The repaired side brushes stone. Tess shifts back and the raft swings into outer chop, away from the opening. You watch the patch while another wave lifts you off the reef.

A corner of the remaining paddle blade breaks away beside you. You bring the worn paddle inside while Tess checks the repaired tube. Its patch remains attached after the scrape against the rock.

From farther along the reef, you watch the narrow inlet between two rock heads. Some waves close it with surf. After a larger break, a darker strip remains for a moment before the next swell arrives. Tess watches several sets and refuses to treat them as a precise clock. She asks you to keep looking at the water inside the opening as well.

You watch the passage until dark. Tess points out where its current differs from the one that damaged your paddle.

On day twenty-three, Tess takes the cooler lid from beneath the supply bundle. Its broad surface is still intact because you kept it aboard when the shark pulled at the trailing strip. She asks you to hold it beside the broken paddle piece while she studies how it turns in the water.

You test the lid while Tess steadies your vest. It pulls harder than you expect. You bring it aboard before adjusting its line so you can release it if it snags.

The lid becomes a crude steering surface held from the raft's rear. You use the remaining paddle for forward movement while Tess controls the line. It takes both of you to keep the heading, and the lid catches seaweed. Each time that happens, you stop and clear it rather than pull until something breaks.

By day twenty-five, the leviathan spends more time outside the reef where waves strike a hollow in the rock. It passes the inlet, then turns toward a louder impact farther north. You watch that same turn again the following evening. Tess asks whether you can move while it is away without letting the lid slap against the raft.

You spend the next days practicing together outside the broken reef edge. Tess holds the lid while you make short paddle strokes, then you trade places so neither person is exhausted before the final attempt. She lowers the cloth when the wind turns the raft along the reef. You wait while it settles and begin again. The patched tube remains soft enough that you still take turns with the pump.

On day twenty-eight, the shark returns toward weed caught on the lid's line. Tess releases the line rather than let it drag her arm over the tube. You draw the lid back inside with the remaining paddle. The shark passes beneath the raft, then turns away as the leviathan reaches the reef hollow. You wait before placing anything back in the water.

The next day, wind pushes along the reef instead of toward the opening. You postpone the attempt. Tess rechecks the line that lets her release the lid if it catches. You watch the hooked rock and help her pump the softened tube again. Going now would repeat the sideways approach that already damaged your last paddle.

That evening, Tess takes the salt-curled crew card from her vest. She kept it because the alarm stopped before she could tell anyone she had left the ship. You remember it catching on the ladder. She puts it in the dry supply bag, and you agree to report both names once you reach land.

On day thirty, the wind changes after sunrise. The cloth now helps point the raft toward the inlet. Tess tests the lid once before placing it behind you. The patch holds its air long enough for you to stop pumping and take the paddle. You wait outside the reef while the creature moves toward the hollow farther north.

The leviathan moves near the north hollow. Its teal back rises, sinks, and rises again. The pale-yellow eye turns toward the crash of water inside the rocks.

A heavy wave strikes the hollow rock. The leviathan turns away from the inlet. Tess lifts the cloth partway and you begin the approach before the next swell closes the passage.

You use the remaining paddle while Tess steadies the lid. The raft enters the darker strip between the two reef heads. White water rises on both sides. A pull on the lid makes it wobble; Tess releases some line before it can spin you across the passage.

A wave lifts you before the gap fully opens. For a second, you see the beach over the reef, pale sand behind calm green water. Then the raft drops into the passage.

Rock passes close on your right. Too close. Tess shoves the folded blue tarp between the tube and the reef as the rubber brushes stone. The tarp tears, but the tube slides instead of catching.

The leviathan reaches the outside of the shallow passage after the wave passes. Its body meets the reef before its eye can come alongside you. It turns along the outer edge. You keep paddling toward the sheltered water rather than wait to see whether it will try another angle.

Inside the hooked rock, the surf is weaker. Tess lowers the cloth while you guide toward the beach. The lid catches on a submerged branch, and she releases its line before it can turn the raft back. You use the paddle alone for the last short approach. The sand touches the raft floor.

Tess steps into shallow water and steadies the raft while you lift the supply bag. You move it above the wash, then return to help her bring the raft onto the beach. The repaired tube has softened again, but the sea is no longer directly beneath the floor where you sit.

The raft tries to float back on the wash. You and Tess drag it higher together. The damaged tube leaves a wet line across the beach. You pull until the raft's base is above the reach of the small waves, then tie its line around a low, twisted root at the edge of the palms.

You inspect the shade above the beach before bringing the bag there. Tess checks the canopy scraps and places her crew card beside the drinks. You set the paddle down within reach of the secured raft. Outside the reef, the teal back passes through the surf without entering the inlet. Tess says your name, Marin, and asks you to help move the food into the shade. You lift one side of the bag while she takes the other. Neither of you has to balance against a sinking deck to carry it.

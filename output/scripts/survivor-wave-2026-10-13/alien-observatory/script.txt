The radio repeats your mountain coordinates while a light climbs the closed access road. Eve leans toward the speaker, hoping someone has answered her distress call. Malik locks the visitor entrance. You recognize the position because Eve read it into the microphone earlier. But the voice playing it back is hers, down to the cough between the numbers. Nobody is answering her yet.

Eve is the observatory technician. Malik maintains the building and its steep service paths. You stayed when the telescope visit was canceled, helping Eve move a battery unit into the radio room. Now she turns the receiver volume down while Malik brings the entrance keys back to the desk.

Eve listens to her own recorded call repeating beneath a clipped sequence of unfamiliar pulses. She says a rescue station might be relaying it. Malik asks why the light coming up the road moves without engine noise. You crouch by the window to see where it stops.

The paved road is blocked below the service shed. Malik lowered its gate after stones fell across the bend. Whatever is approaching has passed that barrier and is moving toward the building from the slope beside it.

A Surveyor enters the yard carrying its black panel. Behind it, a broad Loader in a heavy harness directs a lifting machine toward the shed. The machine carries a section of railing taken from the access road. You pull Eve below the window before the Surveyor reaches the radio-room wall.

Eve turns the room lights off. A relay clicks at the desk, and the Surveyor immediately turns toward the roof antenna.

You watch it raise its panel toward the mast. Eve looks at the radio controls. The station beacon is still on, automatically sending the observatory's identification between her attempts to call for help. She switches that transmission off. The receiver stays quiet enough for you to hear the machine moving outside.

By morning, the aliens have left the yard. Eve listens without transmitting, and nothing returns. When she sends another short call, the Surveyor turns back up the access road. Malik tells her to release the switch. It stops at the shed and raises its panel toward the antenna.

During the next test, you watch from the telescope platform while Eve works the radio. Listening alone does not draw a response. Sending does. You tell her what you saw after she closes the desk cover. She looks at the microphone for a long time before putting it down.

You move your sleeping places into the caretaker kitchen. Its small window faces the rock wall, away from the yard. Eve wants to keep listening for people in the valley. Malik agrees, provided she leaves the beacon and transmit switch off. You keep the radio desk clear so nobody leans onto those controls by accident.

On the visitor map, Eve marks the public dome, radio room, kitchen and rear service yard. Malik adds the supply shed and the trench beneath the yard. A low hatch in the kitchen service cupboard gives access to that trench. Beyond the shed, a narrow path reaches an old weather mast, then a maintenance trail descending to the lower road. Malik points to the ski lodge marked there, below the road bridge.

A Surveyor revisits the quiet roof mast on day four. It has not forgotten a place because you switched its signal off. From behind the kitchen curtain, you see it examining the antenna mount, then leaving a mark on the wall. Eve stays beside the covered radio controls until it moves away.

The Loader follows that mark later and checks the mast's support brackets with its lifting machine. Malik closes the cupboard hatch when the machine passes above the trench. You hear its weight through the floor and wait until the yard becomes quiet again.

Eve wants to send one more call each evening. She thinks rescue teams may be searching for a place with power. Malik asks her to watch what happens whenever the antenna transmits. You sit with her during a listening check. There is static, then the same recording of her earlier distress call. She stops reaching for the microphone.

Over the next days, you watch the Surveyor inspecting quiet equipment already marked around the dome. It does not turn toward whispered conversations inside the kitchen. When Eve transmits during a scheduled test, it leaves the dome track and faces the roof mast. You put that observation on the map without trying to translate the pulses on its panel. Malik brings you a blanket when the telescope platform becomes cold. He asks you to switch places before you are too tired to notice the next patrol. Eve takes the map to the kitchen rather than sit alone beside the radio. You describe the mark you saw on the dome bracket, and she adds it beside Malik's blocked-road sketch.

On day seven, Eve tries receiving with the beacon disabled. You watch the yard while she searches the channels. The Surveyor continues inspecting a dome support. She presses transmit for a brief call, and it changes direction immediately. You signal for her to stop. The test shows which action exposes the building, but offers no way to ask humans for help safely.

Eve reads the station's emergency card, made for injured hikers and storms. She asks what silence looks like to someone searching the valley. Malik tells her he cannot promise anyone is searching. You leave the map beside her and go with him to inspect the route below the shed.

In the supply shed, you find blankets, an orange trail rope and a portable radio case. Eve checks its battery against the spare unit you carried in on the first night. She can power the portable set away from the observatory. A map attached to the weather equipment shows the old mast beside the foot trail. Malik remembers the trail continuing beneath the broken road bend. He takes you as far as the fallen signpost that afternoon. Beyond it, a loose stone step shifts under your weight. You return while the path is still visible and mark that step on the map. Eve asks whether Malik could carry a case across it. He says he could, if someone watched the ridge instead of carrying another load. You leave the rope beside the portable set for that trip.

Below the first bend, the access road crosses a bridge over a drainage cut. Malik's service gate stands on its far side. If you reach it, the lower road leads to the ski lodge. He wants to try during fog, when you can move out of the yard without the Surveyor watching every step.

Before that attempt, the Loader reaches the bridge with its lifting machine. The Surveyor marks a support while the machine braces against the railing. Malik watches from the kitchen window, keeping one hand on the curtain so it cannot swing into the glass.

The machine pulls a guardrail free and lifts a long support beam into the fog. Part of the pavement drops into the drainage cut. By evening, the bridge is a gap. The gate beyond it is intact, but you cannot reach it from the upper road.

You spread the map on the kitchen table. The service path behind the shed climbs to the weather mast, then joins the descending foot trail below the ruined bridge. Malik has walked it before. Snow and loose stones will make carrying supplies difficult. The Surveyor now spends most of its patrol around the yard you must cross to reach that path. You ask why he did not suggest the trail when the bridge was intact. He points to the narrow section beside the mast, where the ground falls away toward the drainage cut. The road could have taken supplies and tired people out together. Now he has to decide what the three of you can move by hand. Eve begins separating the equipment she wants to keep from the pieces needed for the portable set. She puts a heavy instrument back in its drawer and keeps the map.

Malik checks the trail through binoculars from the telescope platform. He can see its lower switchback through the trees. He cannot see the section beside the weather mast. Eve asks him to mark that uncertainty rather than draw a route they have not inspected. You leave that part of the map open.

By day eighteen, the Surveyor has marked the dome track and shed entrance. The Loader removes a panel from the outer wall. You carry the sleeping supplies into the service cupboard behind the pantry. Its low trench hatch becomes a way to reach the shed without crossing the exposed yard.

On day twenty, a broken voice comes through the radio. Eve calls back before you can reach her. She gives the observatory's location and describes the ruined bridge. Outside, the Surveyor turns toward the antenna. The voice in the speaker repeats the first words she just said, including the hesitation before bridge.

Malik releases the transmit switch. You bring Eve back into the kitchen while the Surveyor inspects the cable conduit outside. She asks to hear the reply again. Malik lets her use the recording, but keeps the desk cover closed.

The Surveyor remains at the wall, checking the active equipment it traced. It does not look straight through the stone into the cupboard where you wait. When the beacon remains off, it eventually returns to the shed marks. You watch through the kitchen vent until the rear doorway clears. Eve takes the emergency card off the desk. On its back, she writes when she sent the call and when the Surveyor turned. She hands it to Malik without asking him to listen again. He reads it, then gives her the map so she can mark the route she now wants to use. You sit beside them until the light outside moves away from the kitchen window.

In the service cupboard, Malik opens the trench hatch marked earlier. It runs beneath the yard to a grate inside the shed. You inspect that route with him while Eve watches the kitchen. The portable set is still in the shed, beside the spare battery and trail rope.

Snow hits the dome after sunset. You take turns carrying food from the pantry into the cupboard because the Loader is opening more of the outer wall. Eve gives up her next radio check to help Malik move the water containers. For the first evening since the bridge fell, he asks her to plan the next trip with him. When the supply switches to its remaining emergency unit, the beacon relay starts another automatic cycle. Eve stops it before the Surveyor returns. She checks the desk instructions and finds that the beacon resets when its supply restarts. You agree that someone must remain at the controls while the portable set is being positioned. Switching the whole station off too soon would leave you unable to receive the agreed check-in from the mast.

From beneath the shed grate, you see the Surveyor pass the outer door. It raises its panel to a portable set that Eve switches on for a brief transmission from inside. She stops before it can reach the threshold. You retreat through the trench with her. The alien has followed a transmitting set away from the roof antenna.

Eve proposes moving that same set to the weather mast. Repeating a recorded transmission there could pull the Surveyor off the yard patrol while you leave along the foot trail. She does not claim to understand its language. The test showed it locating a transmitter. Malik asks who will stop the main beacon if the roof equipment starts calling again. You offer to remain by its controls until their portable signal is running.

On day twenty-three, you reach the shed through the trench while Malik watches the yard. Eve inspects the radio case and puts the spare battery beside it. You leave your bag by the grate so you can pass the equipment down with both hands. The Surveyor's light reaches the shed window before Malik gives the next signal.

You stop moving. Eve crouches beneath the workbench while you wait behind the battery boxes. Malik stays below the grate, ready to receive the case. Through the window, you see the Surveyor pause at the mark outside the door.

It enters and raises its panel toward the workbench. Eve has left the transmitter off. The alien studies the removed equipment's outline in the dust. You keep still while it moves between the shelves, close enough that Eve cannot reach the grate without passing it.

Eve rolls an empty cable spool toward the far wall. The Surveyor turns toward the movement and impact. You pass the radio case down to Malik while it faces away. Eve follows you into the trench. Back in the cupboard, Malik tests the case's handle and asks whether she can repeat the recorded call without staying beside it.

Over the next days, Malik scouts the path behind the shed while Eve tests the portable set inside the cupboard with its transmitter off. On day twenty-nine, they carry the case and rope toward the weather mast. You watch from the telescope platform as they pass a fallen signpost and drop out of sight. Eve's planned check-in is the next signal you need to hear.

The weather mast stands on the mountain shoulder above the descending trail. Malik checks its footing and the first turn below it. Eve places the portable set behind the mast base, sheltered from snow, and secures it with the rope before starting the recorded call.

At the observatory, the receiver picks up that call. You recognize Eve's recording. Through the platform window, you see the Surveyor leave the dome track and turn toward the ridge. Malik and Eve are already starting down the trail. The signal is the agreed confirmation that their set is running.

You leave the platform and return to the radio controls. The main beacon relay has begun another cycle on the remaining emergency power. Its click makes the Surveyor hesitate in the yard. You pull the beacon's breaker down. The receiver goes quiet with the roof equipment. The portable set on the ridge keeps transmitting.

The Surveyor turns back toward the weather mast. You wait until it passes the shed, then collect your bag from the cupboard. The trench gives you a route under the open yard. At the shed grate, you stop and listen before climbing out. The Loader's machine is still working at the public entrance.

Before dawn on day thirty, you reach the path behind the shed. The dome remains above you, partly dismantled. You follow Malik's marked route past the fallen signpost and toward the trees below the mast. A light moves on the ridge, so you stay behind the rock face until it turns toward the transmitting case.

Malik and Eve wait at the first sheltered trail turn. You reach them with the remaining food bundle. Eve asks whether the roof beacon is off. You tell her what the Surveyor did when you shut it down. Malik takes the heavier bag for the steep section, and you follow him below the mast.

Snow hides loose stones along the descent. Malik slips and tears his sleeve before Eve catches his coat. Later, Eve twists her ankle beside a buried root. You stop instead of trying to hurry her. Malik takes your bag while you help her over the next drop. At the flatter turn, she tests her weight before agreeing to continue.

By afternoon, the trees thin and the lower road appears beside the drainage cut. You are below the gap left by the demolished bridge. Malik opens the service gate from this side and leads you onto the ski-lodge road. Eve rests on a low wall while you share a drink and look back toward the ridge.

The weather set still draws the Surveyor toward the mast. It will not keep doing that forever. Malik can see the lodge roof beyond the next bend, so you help Eve stand and continue. At its service entrance, he checks the dark hallway before opening the door for her. You bring the supplies inside and close it behind you. Eve sets the folded map on the reception counter. This time, she sits down without reaching for a microphone.

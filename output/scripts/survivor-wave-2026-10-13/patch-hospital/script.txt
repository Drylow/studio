The emergency-room doors crash open while Jo is wrapping your injured hand, and the elevator behind you stops with its doors open.

You pull back before the tape is finished. Jo yanks the curtain around the outpatient bed, and the metal rings scrape across the rail. The noise at the entrance comes again. People in the waiting area stop shouting all at once.

You take your bag with your good hand. You came in through the ambulance yard and watched a transport driver hang his keys in the bay office. Now the staff door beside the curtains is your only clear route back toward that yard. Jo opens it before another body reaches the window.

Jo whispers, “Service hall.”

The staff door opens into a narrow corridor lined with linen carts. Jo pushes you through first, then pulls the door shut. A face hits the little window from the other side. Jo freezes with one hand still on the handle.

At the first corner, a man in a hospital gown is trying to move a wheeled chair away from the wall. One leg is braced. He uses the other foot and both hands to shift himself a few inches at a time.

Len looks past him at the staff door. “That was the front?”

Jo steadies the chair while Len moves away from the wall. Len asks you to give him room rather than lift him. You set down the bag and hold the chair brake while he adjusts his braced leg. Behind the door, a second body strikes the glass.

The service corridor turns behind the emergency rooms. Jo knows the way without checking signs. Len knows it differently. He tells you where the floor dips, where a door sticks, and where the hall widens enough to turn the chair without scraping the wall.

Jo leads you into an outpatient corner with three curtained beds and a desk behind a half wall. The windows face an inner courtyard, not the street. The door to the main hall has a lock on this side. Jo shuts it and wedges a rolling stool under the handle.

Jo finishes the wrap he started before the attack. Your injury came from a broken shelf at work, not from the people outside. He asks you to stop using that hand to brace doors. Len calls from the window that someone has found the staff corridor.

On day two, Jo opens the outpatient door a finger width. The hallway outside is empty, but the emergency department beyond the turn is not. You hear feet dragging over scattered plastic. Jo closes the door again.

On day two, Len describes a service corridor behind imaging. It reaches the pharmacy window, the staff stairs and the back of the building. He worked maintenance here before his leg injury and knows which doors connect. Jo checks his route through the window before opening the outpatient door.

At the pharmacy, Jo tries the staff door. It stays locked. An infected worker is moving behind the shutter, so you leave without trying to force it. Len points to the maintenance store farther along the service hall.

On day four, an infected woman in blue scrubs finds the outpatient door. It holds against her pushing, but the repeated impacts keep you awake. Jo watches its handle shift, then asks Len about another room farther from the emergency department.

Jo draws a map on the back of an intake form. Len adds a box at the far end of the service hall.

“Maintenance store,” he says. “Mops, batteries, old signs. No food, but the door doesn’t face the emergency room.”

You reach the store by reversing Len's chair through a tight turn. Your hand burns when you try lifting the front wheel. Jo takes that side while you check the corridor behind him. Len tells you where to angle the chair and when to stop.

Inside the store, Jo finds a portable delivery chime beside a toolbox and spare batteries. He presses its button. The alarm repeats until Len tells him how to stop it. A cart begins rattling in the corridor outside. All three of you stay still until it passes. Jo puts the chime and batteries in the supply bundle.

For two days, the store becomes your stopover. Jo knows which nearby rooms had water coolers and which nurses kept snack packets in drawers. You make careful trips only when the corridor is clear. Len stays by the store door and listens. When he hears a bad wheel squeak in the hall, he tells you not to open. A cart rolls past a moment later, pushed by nobody you want to meet.

On day six, Jo says the lower floors are getting worse. More infected have reached the service corridor. One keeps wandering between the pharmacy shutter and the elevator lobby.

Len marks the third-floor south ward and its storeroom beside the staff stairs on Jo's map.

The staff stairs are the remaining clear way upward. Jo exchanges Len's ordinary chair for the evacuation chair mounted beside the stairwell. Len adjusts his position himself, then lets you steady him. You leave the ordinary chair at the bottom and take the other one up a flight at a time.

An infected patient strikes the second-floor stair door's wired glass. Jo steadies the evacuation chair until you reach the next landing.

By the third floor, Jo's arms are shaking. You help roll the evacuation chair into the south ward and close the stair door. Len thanks Jo, then asks him to put the brakes on before he takes another breath. Your injured hand is damp beneath its wrap.

Empty beds line one side. The nurses’ station faces the corridor. A storeroom behind it has blankets, sealed cups, and ordinary supplies that help you stay put without solving everything. The roof stair door is at the far end, under a green exit sign that still glows.

From days seven to eleven, the upper ward becomes a routine. Jo moves blankets away from the windows so nothing outside catches a shadow. On day eighteen, the storm arrives.

A dark wet patch spreads near the roof stair. Another grows above the room where Jo stored blankets. Water runs down the wall and under the first bed in the barricade.

Water spreads through the ward ceiling. Len points out the sagging panels before Jo moves the stored blankets beneath them. You put the drinks beside the nurses' station. A panel falls where one container stood moments earlier.

On days nineteen through twenty-two, the damp ward gives you fewer dry places to sleep. A bed in the stair barricade slides when Jo moves it. You catch his sleeve with your injured hand, then release it as the pain strikes. He catches the counter. Len directs both of you away from the wet ceiling before another panel falls.

Jo scouts the far staircase and returns without finding infected people on its upper flights. He can see the ground-floor landing from above. Beyond it, the connecting imaging corridor is hidden behind a fire door. That last blind turn is where you will need to stop and look before rolling toward receiving.

On day twenty-three, you try the route.

On day twenty-three, you descend the far stairs. Len holds the bag while you and Jo manage the evacuation chair. You stop between flights when Jo needs to rest. At ground level, he opens the fire door toward imaging just enough to inspect the connecting corridor.

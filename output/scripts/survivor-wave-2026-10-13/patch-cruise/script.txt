A passenger slams into your cabin door with one green hand caught in the gap, and the ship keeps sailing away from shore.

You force the door shut and hold it until the latch catches. Across the ship, a lifeboat still hangs on the starboard side. But the corridor between you and it is filling with infected passengers. You have to reach that boat before this ship carries you beyond any chance of getting ashore.

You keep both hands on the door until the banging changes to scraping. The man outside was laughing with the table beside yours an hour ago. Now his nails drag down the paint, and then the sound moves farther along the corridor.

Your cabin is on the port side, one deck below the promenade. It has a bed, a bathroom, a small balcony, and no way to the lifeboats without crossing the public hall. Your phone shows one weak bar, then none. Through the balcony glass, the coast is already a dark line behind the ship.

You take a flashlight, a ticket map, and a pen from your orange backpack. The map shows guest corridors. You need a route the passengers outside are not already using.

Near midnight, someone taps three times on the wall from the next cabin.

A steward named Mara whispers from the next balcony. She watched the port lifeboats break loose during the first panic. The starboard boats are still attached, but infected passengers fill the promenade between you and them. She has service keys and knows the crew passages.

By morning, the hallway outside your cabin is quiet enough to check. You open the door a finger's width. A food tray lies upside down near the elevator. Farther along, a woman in a sequined dress stands facing a blank wall, her pale green head tilted toward the ceiling speaker. You close the door before she turns.

At dawn, you help Mara across the low divider between your balconies. She brings her keys and waits while you wedge the cabin door shut.

She points to your map and corrects it with your pen. Behind the cabin row, there is a laundry passage guests never see. It runs toward a service pantry near the galley. From there, a narrow hatch opens into the galley service lane. If that lane is clear, you can reach the starboard stairs without crossing the middle of the promenade.

You take your bag through Mara's cabin while she opens the service door behind a linen cart.

The laundry passage smells like soap and warm metal. Sheets spill from carts. A crew radio lies on the floor, clicking with static and half words. Mara tries it, gets nothing useful, and keeps it anyway.

On day two, you reach a service pantry stocked with sealed food and drinks. Mara wedges a cart under its door handle. An adult passenger named Ellis is crouching behind the soda bottles with a dinner knife. He lowers it when Mara speaks.

Ellis keeps looking toward the far pantry door. He asks Mara whether the route behind it reaches the promenade.

He says he was protecting his own cabin after the first alarm. His sister was on the promenade when people started running. He came down looking for a service route and got trapped in the pantry after Mara locked the opposite door.

On day four, Mara shows you a small service hatch in the galley wall. Through it, you can see a slice of the starboard promenade. One lifeboat hangs beyond the railing, covered and still. Between the hatch and the boat, three infected passengers wander past a fallen dessert cart.

Ellis says there will be others alive. Mara says maybe, but alive people make noise, and noise moves the infected. He looks toward the ceiling as if his sister might answer through it.

On day six, he stops waiting.

You wake in the cabin to a distant clank. Mara is already at the balcony divider. She looks toward the laundry passage and says Ellis took her spare key.

You reach the pantry as the forbidden galley door swings open.

Ellis is beyond it, calling his sister's name in a harsh whisper that carries too far. An infected crewman turns from the dish station. Then another rises behind the counter. Ellis backs toward you, empty handed, and for a second you think he has found her.

He has found her shoe.

Mara pulls him through the pantry doorway. You shove the cart back into place, but the first infected body hits before the wheel locks. Shelves rattle. Juice cartons fall and burst under your feet.

Mara drags Ellis toward the laundry passage. You grab your bag as the pantry barricade tips. At the junction, Mara wedges a second linen cart across the galley entrance and locks the laundry door. Infected passengers pour into the pantry behind that new barrier.

By day seven, the pantry is gone. You still have what you carried and what Mara stuffed into her jacket. Ellis has the shoe, one stolen key, and no answer except, "I heard someone."

Mara takes back her spare keys. Ellis says he heard his sister calling. Mara makes him look at the pantry door, still shaking under the bodies on the other side. Everything left on those shelves is out of reach now.

On day ten, Ellis leaves the shoe outside his cabin door and asks to stand watch. Mara says he can watch from inside your cabin, with the balcony door open, where both of you can see him.

On day eleven, the horn sounds and cuts off. The ship turns, bringing the coastline back into view. Mara wants to check the bridge. If someone is still operating the ship, they need to know why it has not returned to port.

You follow the forward crew stairs to the bridge. Ellis holds your flashlight while Mara checks the broken radio panel. The screen beside it flickers. Nobody answers her calls.

The ship is sailing on a damaged setting, slow but steady, with no one steering it properly. A marked course line should have ended near the coast already. Instead, the ship has been drifting along the shoreline at a distance, wasting every hour.

A note on the lifeboat board identifies the covered boat you saw earlier as starboard boat three. Its release handle was taken to the lower workshop for repair before departure. The other starboard boats were damaged during boarding. This one is intact because nobody could launch it.

The workshop is two decks below the galley, forward of the machinery spaces. Mara marks it with a circle. Ellis wants to go now. Mara looks at the dark stairwell and says going now means crossing lower decks nobody has checked since the outbreak began.

Over the next few days, you use the bridge windows to watch infected passengers moving between the promenade doors. Mara marks the hours when the forward stairs clear. Ellis brings sealed drinks and meal packs from an accessible crew emergency store. He leaves them for everyone and asks before opening its next door. You begin including him in the workshop plan.

On day sixteen, a storm rolls over the ship.

Rain hits the bridge windows so hard the sea disappears. The ship leans into waves and does not correct quickly. Somewhere below, something breaks with a heavy sound that travels through the floor. Emergency lights flicker, hold, then fail in half the corridor.

You return to the cabins after the storm because that is where your blanket and spare things are. Water is sliding under the cabin door.

Water runs down the cabin corridor and into the room where you have been sleeping. A ceiling panel hangs open above the stairs. Mara pulls you back as another wave shakes loose part of the railing. You collect what you can carry and abandon the cabins.

By day eighteen, the power stops staying on. Elevators die. Some automatic doors freeze open. Others freeze closed, which is worse when the closed side is yours. Mara uses a wrench from the bridge cabinet to secure the bridge door with a chain and a metal bar. Ellis helps without being asked.

On day twenty, you try for the workshop.

On the forward stairs, you leave the bag on a dry landing. Ellis holds the flashlight behind you. Mara descends toward the workshop door, bracing against the railing as the ship leans.

The corridor below is ankle deep in dark water. A floating chair bumps the wall. Mara reaches the watertight door and pulls twice. The handle turns partway, then stops, leaving you pressed against the landing rail.

Behind you, Ellis whispers your name.

An infected passenger climbs from the landing below. Ellis catches your sleeve before you step toward it.

You retreat too fast. Your foot slips. Mara catches your sleeve, and Ellis grabs your other arm. The flashlight drops, rolls down two steps, and goes out when the infected foot comes down on it.

You get back to the dry landing without the light and without the workshop. Ellis kicks the stairwell door shut. Mara bars it with the wrench through the handle, breathing hard through her nose.

Back at the bridge, Mara lays out the spare keys she recovered from Ellis. One has a locker number matching a square on the service map. Ellis remembers a chute symbol beside that locker. Mara knows the linen chute drops into a service alcove near the workshop. Its upper door has stayed locked throughout the outbreak.

That route requires returning toward the lost pantry. You spend the next days watching the galley entrance from the upper crew corridor. A group of infected passengers stays there whenever the loose kitchen door bangs. Ellis ties it back from behind the laundry barrier. With the noise stopped, the group gradually moves toward the promenade.

On day twenty-six, you return by the upper service passage. The pantry is still lost, but Mara's second cart holds across the galley junction. Beyond it, the kitchen is quiet enough to reach the numbered locker without opening the infected pantry door.

The key turns. Inside are folded uniforms, a sewing kit, and the square metal mouth of the linen chute. Mara ties sheets into a rough lowering line. Ellis insists on going first. Mara says no. He says he knows what owing looks like now.

You descend first into wet linen. Mara follows. Ellis lowers the bag to you before coming down himself. He catches his boot on the edge and lands on one knee. You help him stand, then check that he can still put weight on it.

The workshop corridor is half flooded but reachable from this side. The failed stairwell door is visible at the far end, blocked from behind by a twisted frame. You could have pulled all day and never opened it.

Ellis checks a lower cabinet and finds a repair tag tied to a cloth bag. Inside is the handle, heavy and red, wrapped with two bolts and a note from a mechanic who expected to reinstall it before passengers ever boarded.

Water pushes farther along the corridor as the list deepens. The chute is behind you, but climbing it will take time. Mara points to a maintenance ladder on the workshop wall. It leads up to a hatch near the starboard promenade, the same side as lifeboat three.

Ellis pushes the maintenance hatch a few inches upward. It catches against a deck chair outside. Dragging footsteps pass above him, so he stops pushing.

Mara passes him the wrench. He could force it and bring everything outside straight to the hatch. Instead, he stops. He looks down at Mara and says, very quietly, "Not like last time."

You wait until the dragging feet move away. Then all three of you push together, slow enough that the hatch rises without a bang. A deck chair has jammed across it. Ellis shifts it by inches. The opening clears.

Beyond the hatch, two infected passengers stand between you and the lifeboat access gate. Ellis holds the recovered handle against his chest. You crouch behind the hatch cover with Mara.

A loose cup rolls across the deck before anyone touches it. The ship tilts. The cup taps the railing. Both infected turn.

Mara leads you behind stacked loungers. Ellis freezes when an infected passenger steps into his path. You reach for his sleeve, expecting him to call out again.

Ellis does not call out. He throws the empty cloth bag toward the far end of the promenade. It flutters, lands, and slides. The infected follow the motion just long enough for Mara to open the lifeboat access gate.

Mara fits the recovered handle into its marked socket. Ellis holds the access gate while you brace it with a fallen lounge chair. By the time the infected return, all three of you are behind the barrier.

On day twenty-seven, waves strike high enough to throw spray across the boat. Mara refuses to lower it into that water. You stay behind the access gate, taking turns watching the brace and the waves below.

By morning, the ship is leaning more, but the water below has calmed. The coast is no longer a dark line. It is a broken strip of cliffs and trees off the starboard bow. The drifting ship has carried you near a sheltered inlet.

On day twenty-eight, calmer water lets Mara inspect the lowering lines. One runs across a bent deck chair and snags when Ellis moves the boat cover. You hold the loose chair while he frees the line. By evening, a new swell drives the boat against its stops. You have to secure it through another night.

The swell weakens during day twenty-nine. Mara watches it from the railing while you transfer the remaining supplies. Ellis retrieves his sister's shoe from the cabin passage, then leaves it on a bench. He tells Mara she would have wanted him to get off this ship.

At dawn on day thirty, the water below is calm enough to lower the lifeboat. Mara gets aboard with you and Ellis and takes the controls.

The boat drops unevenly at first, and your stomach goes with it. Ellis grabs the side rope when it snags. You reach over him and free the cover strap caught near the davit arm. Mara works the release handle you recovered from the workshop, and this time the mechanism moves.

The lifeboat hits the water with a slap that knocks you to one knee. Your backpack tips against the bench but stays in the boat. Ellis hauls you upright. Mara releases the last line.

The cruise ship slides away above you, huge and tilted, its promenade crowded with green headed figures that cannot follow across open water. For a few minutes, nobody speaks. You listen to the lifeboat creak and the small waves hitting its sides.

Mara steers toward the inlet you could see from the bridge. Ellis bails water with a container from under the seat. You watch for rocks as the cliffs separate into a narrow opening. A beach appears behind them, with a low boathouse above the tide line.

Before sunset, the lifeboat scrapes sand. Ellis climbs out and holds the bow line while you and Mara step ashore. He can still walk on his sore knee. You carry the supplies clear of the water and help pull the boat higher.

Behind you, the cruise ship is still visible beyond the inlet, leaning but afloat, sealed decks holding their dead in separate pockets. Ahead, the boathouse door opens onto a dry room with a bench, a coil of rope, and a window facing the beach.

Mara checks the boathouse through its open window before you enter. Ellis shuts the door and slides the bolt across. You sit against the wall while Mara lays the map on a dry bench. The ship is still visible beyond the inlet. Nobody has to go back to it.

For the first time in thirty days, the floor beneath you does not move.

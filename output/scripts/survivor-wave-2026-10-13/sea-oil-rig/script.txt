A blow comes up through the rig floor, and the coffee in your cup jumps onto your hand. You run to the rail in time to see the supply boat's wake stop beside a teal shadow larger than the boat itself. The stern lifts. The radio mast snaps. The access ladder rises out of the water bent around its base. You are high above the sea with Oren and Bea behind you, but the thing below has already started taking away the parts that let you leave.

Oren reaches the rail in his orange hard hat. Bea comes from the galley carrying a tray towel. She asks where the supply boat went. Oren pulls her back from the edge as the bent ladder strikes a platform leg.

The supply boat slides sideways beneath the surface. A pale-yellow eye opens beside its hull while the rig ladder scrapes a support.

The bent ladder scrapes against the rig leg. Metal shrieks up through the platform.

Oren grabs your yellow vest and pulls you back. The ladder drops, hits the water, and disappears in foam.

By sunset on the first day, the three of you are on the top deck under the helipad. Below you are the galley, the bunk room, the machinery bay, the grated walkway around the legs, and the ladder landing that used to connect to the sea.

Oren gets static on the emergency radio and shuts down the damaged machinery below the deck. Bea secures the galley supplies before joining you upstairs. With the pumps silent, you can hear water slapping the ladder landing. Nobody answers the next radio check.

From the rail, you can see the ladder landing hanging crooked below the main deck. Beyond it, on the far side, the small maintenance launch sits in its berth like a toy boat trapped in a cage.

A narrow walkway reaches the launch berth, but the supply boat damaged it as it went under. There is a gap between two sections of grating. Oren says the launch engine was working during his last inspection. The fuel they need is in a lower storage cage, reachable from the service ladder.

You draw the rig on a meal box while Oren names the routes between its decks. He marks the machinery-bay floor hatch and an older east maintenance path behind the pipe racks. A heavy rack has fallen against that path's entrance. All three of you try shifting it, but it is pinned under a bent support. You leave it marked as blocked.

On the third day, he opens that hatch and shows you a service ladder bolted inside one rig leg. It descends to a lower landing above the water.

Oren tests the service ladder and descends only far enough to see the lower landing. Bea watches from the hatch while you keep its cover from falling against the steel. He comes back to report that the storage cage is still attached and the landing can be reached.

On the fourth day, you test the damaged access walkway from the machinery bay side. You and Oren step onto the first grates while Bea waits at the door with a pan in her hand. Halfway across, a support bracket is twisted, leaving a gap where two floor grates no longer meet.

A gray shark passes beneath the launch berth. Its fin cuts across the water while the teal body remains farther out beside the supply-boat wreck.

By day six, you have moved sealed food and drinks to the top deck. Oren brings a portable generator from the machinery bay. Its loose frame knocks against the post beside it when he tests the engine. The teal shadow moves beneath that side of the rig. He stops the engine before the creature reaches the leg.

Bea asks him to try the same set away from the post. This time he steadies its frame while it runs. The motor sounds above the deck, but the creature stays farther out. Oren stops again and looks at the steel carrying those earlier knocks down into the water.

You move the generator back beneath its tarp. Bea leaves a fuel container beside it so nobody takes everything to the launch before you know what else may be needed.

That night, you listen from the bunk-room doorway. A broken ladder rung bangs a submerged brace below the landing. After a series of impacts, the teal body turns beneath that leg. When the wave eases and the rung stops hitting it, the creature slows. You tell Oren what you watched.

On the seventh day, you descend the service ladder for the first real supply run. Oren goes first. You follow. Bea stays above at the hatch with the rope around both hands. The inside of the rig leg smells like rust and trapped salt.

At the lower landing, the water rises and falls below the grating. Pieces of the broken main ladder are still caught between crossbeams. One rung knocks against a submerged brace with every swell.

Oren raises one hand. You freeze. The rung hits again, and the pale yellow eye rolls up through the water.

Oren lifts the dangling rung away from the brace. The knocking stops. The creature remains below, then drifts toward the supply-boat debris. You climb back to Bea and mark the observed response on the map.

On day nine, you return through the tested ladder route. Oren passes fuel cans upward from the storage cage while you and Bea receive them at the hatch. The fuel stays on the top deck, apart from the supply bins. Reaching the launch still requires crossing the broken walkway.

On the eleventh day, Oren risks the direct walkway with a rope tied around his waist. You hold the line from the machinery bay while Bea anchors it around a post. He reaches the gap and stretches one boot toward the next grate.

The loose walkway section twitches beneath Oren. You and Bea pull him back before he can step onto the far grate. He reaches the machinery-bay door with one boot scraping over the edge. Below him, the teal body turns toward the falling metal. Bea holds the rope until he is fully on the deck.

Oren proposes using the deck crane to place a spare grate over the gap. You can see that grate in its rack beside the machinery bay. Bea asks him to inspect the crane from the deck before anyone leaves the protected doorway again.

The storm arrives before the crane can help anyone. On the twelfth day, wind snaps at the helipad cables, and rain comes sideways across the deck. Oren wants to secure the crane before the wind gets worse. Bea says no. You go with him anyway because the crane is the only plan that reaches the broken walkway without putting someone over open water.

Halfway across the main deck, the generator tarp tears loose. The generator slides against the rail, and its metal frame rattles in the wind. The vibration travels down through the rig.

The water erupts under the far side. A teal back breaks the surface and slams into a support brace. The deck jumps under your feet.

You and Oren catch the sliding generator while Bea secures its frame with cable. Across the rig, the creature strikes a support below the repeated rattling. Once you pull the frame clear of the rail, the impacts stop. Bea checks the lashings again before letting either of you move toward the crane.

The crane jams during the storm. By morning, its guide wheel is bent and the access line is pinned beneath the arm. The lower storage cage has torn from its brackets. The fuel you already carried up remains on deck. Oren counts the tools still in his machinery-bay locker and admits they cannot restore the crane with what is left.

On day sixteen, Oren inspects the berth from a short frame beside the damaged walkway. You lower fuel to him while Bea watches the water. He crosses alone and refuels the launch. There is no place on that frame to stand and help the others across. He returns before the swell rises.

That evening, a wave drives part of the short service frame against the berth. It bends away from the boat. Oren watches from above and marks that crossing unusable. The launch now has fuel, but the only route he used to reach it is broken. You take the launch's key from his locker and keep it with the map.

On the eighteenth day, the rig tilts.

It is small at first. A cup slides an inch on the galley table. Bea catches it and looks at you. Outside, one sea-facing support brace has a fresh kink above the waterline. The paint is cracked around it, exposing dark metal.

Oren still wants to wait for another supply vessel. Bea asks what they can do if it arrives and loses its ladder approach as the first boat did. He looks down at the refueled launch. You spread the route map between them and ask which part of the rig they have not checked since the storm.

The old east entrance's blocking rack has shifted. You and Bea move it enough to see the floor behind it. Oren brings a lever from the surviving tool locker and helps open a narrow gap.

On day twenty, a submerged blow shakes the rig and throws Bea against the galley counter. Outside, the support you saw kink earlier hangs partly free of its joint. The teal leviathan presses beneath it. As that corner settles, the rack at the east entrance slides away from the doorway. You can now inspect the older route.

Over the following days, you clear the old maintenance walkway in sections. It begins behind the machinery bay, wraps around the east pipe racks and descends toward the launch berth from the opposite side. It remained blocked when the direct crossing failed. Oren tests the grates before Bea marks them with cloth strips.

At its final gate, you try moving a bent catch with a wrench. The tool slips and strikes the handrail twice. Water moves toward the leg beneath you. Oren puts the wrench down beside the gate and leads you back behind the pipe racks without another impact.

The gate stays closed. Oren thinks he can retrieve the wrench without stepping onto its exposed landing. Bea asks him to wait until the creature moves back toward the broken support. You watch with her while he brings a rope from the machinery bay.

On the twenty-fourth day, Oren tries to recover it with a rope loop from the machinery bay side. The loop catches the handle. He pulls. The wrench shifts, hits the rail once, and drops through a gap into the sea.

The dropped wrench hits submerged metal. The leviathan reaches that point and pushes the lower gate frame inward. Oren lets the rope go. When the water settles, Bea can see a lower section of the gate lying across the berth approach. Climbing over its remaining rail may work, but only while the creature is away from that leg.

You use the next days to prepare that crossing. The generator's loose frame produced repeated knocks when it touched the rail. Oren wants to secure it on the western post, opposite the launch route, with enough movement for the frame to strike that steel. Bea insists that nobody stay beside it after it starts.

The three of you move the generator across the top deck using the cable saved during the storm. You keep it off while it passes between posts. Oren secures it at the western rail and checks that its frame will remain on deck. Bea packs the food in a white galley bin and sets it beside the machinery-bay door.

On day twenty-eight, the already damaged support breaks above the water. The platform drops at that corner, and the bunk-room door swings open. Oren tries closing it, then stops when its frame will no longer line up. You move the last supplies to the escape route.

A wave strikes beneath the main deck. The crane arm shifts in its jammed guide, and part of the galley wall cracks. Bea goes to the food bin without asking whether they should try another radio check. Oren takes the launch key from beside your map.

Through clearing weather, you see a sheltered inlet beyond the rig field. You mark that notch in the coast on the meal-box map before Oren checks the launch route again.

As daylight fades on day twenty-nine, you start the generator. Its loose frame knocks against the western post. The teal body turns beneath that leg. You leave the running set and meet Bea and Oren at the machinery-bay door.

The teal shadow moves from beneath the broken support toward the sound. Its pale yellow eye rises near the western rail, exactly where the generator is shaking the post. The creature bumps the submerged steel once, then again, following the tremor.

Oren and Bea carry the bin while you lead along the east route. At the pipe racks, you check the water beneath the gate. The teal body remains below the opposite post.

At the pipe racks, the rig shifts. Bea stumbles, and the bin taps her knee. You catch her elbow. Oren steadies both of you, then points ahead.

The upper gate rail has bent farther toward the sea. You climb over it and lower yourself onto the berth landing. Oren passes the bin across before Bea follows. You steady it while she finds her footing. Oren crosses last, releasing the rail only when he can reach the landing below.

A gray shark passes beneath the berth as you step into the launch. Bea hands you the food bin and climbs aboard. Oren releases the lines and reaches the controls with the recovered key. Behind you, the generator still knocks against the western steel.

The launch starts as the berth shifts sideways. The shark passes under the bow and scrapes the hull. Bea grabs your vest when the boat jolts. Oren clears the nearest rig leg while you settle the food bin between the seats.

Oren pushes the launch away from the berth. The stern clears the last bumper as a section of the old access walkway tears loose behind you and drops into the sea.

The falling walkway hits steel beneath the berth. The leviathan turns from the western post toward that new series of impacts. Oren keeps the boat moving while the creature reaches the debris. The gap between two rig legs stays open long enough for the launch to pass.

Behind you, part of the western platform folds. The crane arm falls into the water, and the generator disappears beneath a tilted deck panel. Bea watches the galley wall split above the sea. Oren asks you to keep watching the coast so he does not steer back toward the collapsing rig.

Oren keeps the launch pointed toward the dark notch in the coast. Bea sits beside the bin with both hands on the lid. You stay at the stern and watch the water.

The leviathan does not follow at first. It circles the collapsing platform, drawn to the shaking, falling metal. The shark follows longer, a gray line under the wake, but it falls back when the water shallows and rocks begin to show beneath the surface.

Day thirty begins before you reach the inlet. The sky is pale, and the rig behind you is a slanted shape on the horizon. The sheltered coastline is real. The inlet curves between dark rocks into calmer water, and a small service beach sits at the back with an old concrete ramp.

Oren cuts the engine before the launch touches bottom. The boat glides the last few yards and bumps softly against the ramp.

Bea laughs once as the boat reaches the ramp, then presses her hands against her face. Oren climbs out and keeps the bow line steady while you step ashore. You help Bea over the side and wait until she is standing beside you on the concrete.

You carry the food bin above the tide line together. Oren secures the scraped launch to the ramp ring while Bea checks the water outside the inlet. The leviathan has remained beside the collapsing rig. The gray shark is no longer following the boat into the shallows.

A service road climbs inland from the ramp. Bea lifts one handle of the bin, Oren takes the other, and you check the first bend before they follow. There is a small maintenance shed above the inlet. You inspect it together, then bring the food inside. The launch remains below, tied where you can reach it. Oren sets its key beside the map. When Bea puts the bin down, nobody has to catch it sliding across a tilted floor.

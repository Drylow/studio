An infected guard is dragging a key ring past your cell, and the food is locked two blocks away.

You arrived for a visitor appointment. When someone attacked a guard in reception, another officer put you behind the holding-cell bars and went for help. He never came back. Now the visitor wing is sealed. But the infected guard outside still has the keys to your door.

On day one, you stay in the visitor holding cell because leaving it means stepping into the guard's reach.

The guard keeps moving between your cell and the visitor reception counter. His head has gone pale green. His hand opens and closes like he still remembers holding a clipboard. The key ring is clipped to his belt. Each time he turns, the keys tap the metal frame.

Near evening, a woman calls from behind the reception desk. She keeps her voice low and asks if you're bitten.

The woman is Nia, a kitchen worker sheltering in the staff passage. She can see your cell through the service-door window. The guard's ring opens reception and the visitor cells. Without it, she cannot reach you.

Nia tells you not to rattle the bars. The sound brings more of them from the public corridor.

Nia slides a wrapped roll and a drink through the reception gap. While you eat, she looks for something long enough to reach the guard's keys if he drops them. She finds a serving ladle in the tray trolley.

On day two, the guard finally gives you something useful.

He catches his belt on the chair behind the reception desk. The key ring snaps free and skids under the desk. It is out of his reach and still out of yours.

She disappears from the service window and returns with a long metal ladle. She pushes it under the reception counter, hooks the ring, and drags it toward her door. The guard follows the scraping noise and slams both hands against the counter.

She waits until the guard leans over the counter, retrieves the ring, then opens the service door. You scrape your shoe along the bars to turn his attention back toward you. While he approaches your cell, Nia moves behind him and unlocks its door.

You retreat toward the far bench with your bag. The guard pushes through the open cell doorway after you. Nia strikes the bars from outside, making him turn back toward her. You slip past his side before he reaches the doorway again. Nia pulls the bars shut. Both of you retreat into the staff passage while he strikes the gate behind you.

Nia knows the kitchen keys and the service route. She doesn't know the inmate blocks. The ring has colored tags on some keys, but not enough to make the prison simple.

From days two to five, you move only through doors Nia can name.

The service hall runs behind visitor reception, past a linen closet, to a serving counter with a metal shutter. Beyond the shutter is a cafeteria. The cafeteria connects to the kitchen through a swinging staff door. A locked security door on the far side leads toward the inmate blocks.

In the kitchen, you set the orange bag beside a prep table. Nia searches the cupboards while you check the rear door. That door opens onto a staff passage ending at a locked courtyard gate. Someone has tied it shut from the yard side.

The kitchen has enough sealed supplies to feed a block of inmates. You can reach those shelves, but most of the prison remains locked. Nia sets aside what you can carry and leaves the rest in storage. For now, you sleep behind the kitchen's bolted rear door.

On day four, you look through the serving shutter into the cafeteria.

Two infected inmates move between the tables. One still has a towel twisted in his hands. The other keeps pushing a chair until it hits a table, then starts again.

Beyond the cafeteria, a security door leads to Block B. Nia's keys do not open it. The rear staff passage avoids that room, but its courtyard gate is the barrier keeping you from the vehicle loading area.

On day five, an inmate calls through the rear courtyard gate. His name is Cal. He asks for food before he will untie the gate and let you into the yard.

Nia recognizes him and asks why he is keeping the gate shut when it could get all of you outside.

Cal says infected inmates reached the cafeteria before he secured the courtyard. He expects them to find the kitchen next. He kept the yard gate tied and locked so they could not follow him. The loading passage beyond his yard is blocked with equipment. He needs your food while he waits for another way out.

Nia asks what happens when her kitchen shelves run empty. Cal offers a trade: food for time in the yard, where you can inspect the loading route. She agrees, but tells you to keep the kitchen keys.

From days six to ten, you pass food through the courtyard gate before Cal opens it.

He lets you and Nia through the rear staff passage and into the yard. He keeps the courtyard key in his hand and counts how long you stay. Nia refuses to hand over more supplies while he is watching the clock.

Beyond the gate, the yard has an exercise shed, barred doors back to the inmate blocks, and a short loading passage. Cal waits beside the lock as you inspect it. Nia keeps looking back toward the kitchen.

You ask why he doesn't leave through the loading area.

He points beyond the passage. Equipment has been piled across the route: laundry bins, folded tables, broken carts, metal barriers. Past that, an old prison transport vehicle sits near a roll-up door.

By day ten, you have learned when Cal will open the yard and which equipment blocks the loading route. The trade keeps everyone fed, but it prevents you from working long enough to clear the exit. You ask for more time. Cal asks for more food. Nia puts his next portion back on the shelf.

On day eleven, smoke changes the agreement.

It comes as a gray ribbon under the separation door beyond the service hall. Then coughing starts behind it. Human coughing.

Nia knows the door. It separates your service route from an administrative wing. A red-tagged key might open it from your side.

Cal has come through the rear passage to argue about his portion. Smoke reaches the kitchen before he gets it. He wants you to keep the administrative separation door closed, even after a man begins coughing on the other side.

The voice behind the door is Officer Vale. He says the records room is burning. He is alone. He can't stay there.

That door separates your service route from the burning administrative wing. Opening it may admit whoever is following Vale. You look at Nia and tell her to try the red-tagged key.

The door opens into smoke and orange light far down the corridor. Vale stumbles through, coughing into his sleeve. Behind him, a pale green head appears at the bend.

Cal grabs the door edge with Nia and shoves it shut. You turn the lock with both hands around the key. Something hits the other side before the bolt lands.

Vale sinks to the floor. He is an older guard with ash on his uniform and no weapon. He thanks you, then starts apologizing for the smoke as if he brought it with him.

By day fifteen, the service hall is no longer safe to sleep near. Smoke leaks under the separation door whenever the air shifts. Infected gather on the other side, drawn by the fire and the voices. You move everything you still need into the kitchen and courtyard side.

Vale worked the front gate. He knows the old transport vehicle was kept for short yard moves. Beyond it, the laundry room has a staff exit through the perimeter wall. From there, a service road reaches fields outside the prison. He could use that route if the equipment piled in loading were cleared.

From days sixteen to twenty-one, the yard turns against you.

More infected reach the fences from the blocks. They press against the barred door from Block C. Others gather at the cafeteria windows. The courtyard still has open air, but the fences rattle all day. During one trip, a water container falls from your hand and rolls toward the Block C door. Cal stops you before you follow it into reach of the green hands. He lets the container empty through the bars. Back in the kitchen, Nia gives him the drink she had been keeping for herself. He divides it between two cups and leaves one beside her place. The next trip, you carry smaller containers that will fit in the bags.

On day eighteen, an infected inmate gets an arm through the cafeteria shutter gap while you and Nia are moving containers. Cal pulls you back by your sleeve. Nia slams the shutter down, trapping the arm until Vale helps secure it.

Nobody thanks him right then. The moment is too close. But the next food portion Nia sets aside for him is the same size as yours.

On day twenty, you reach the vehicle loading area for a full inspection.

Cal opens the courtyard gate. Nia unlocks the loading passage while Vale watches the cafeteria windows. You inspect the old transport vehicle and the narrow corridor beyond it.

The old transport vehicle sits under a dusty awning, boxy and white, with one flat tire and a bent front grille. Laundry bins and metal barriers are piled between it and the roll-up exit. Past the vehicle, you can see a corridor marked laundry.

You begin with the lighter barriers. Cal tries to lift a laundry bin alone and drops it with a crash. Infected inmates gather at the yard door. He waits while their pounding slows, then asks you to take the other end. Working together gets the bin out of the route.

On days twenty-two through twenty-six, Cal gives up his gate monopoly one piece at a time.

At first, Cal hands over his key only while he watches Nia use it. On day twenty-three, he leaves it with her for a whole trip because both his hands are lifting a bin with yours. She returns it without comment. The next morning, he puts it where everyone can reach it.

On day twenty-four, you clear a path to the driver's door. Vale retrieves the labeled vehicle key from the loading office. Nia points to the damaged front tire. Vale says he will only test the engine until they know whether the van can move safely through the cleared route.

The engine coughs, catches for a few seconds, and dies. The sound brings infected to the cafeteria windows. The barred door from Block C starts shaking so hard rust drops from the hinges.

On day twenty-six, the escape attempt fails for good.

Vale starts the engine while Cal and Nia watch the barriers. The van rolls forward, drops onto its damaged tire and hits a heavy laundry cart. Its front corner bends against the frame. Vale stops the engine and climbs out. Cal tries shifting the cart, but the van will not roll again.

The roll-up exit stays blocked. The vehicle is now crooked across the loading area, half in the path and half out of it.

That night, Cal puts the courtyard key on the prep table in front of Nia.

Nia picks it up. She tells him the food doesn't belong to her either. Then she divides the next meal into four even portions.

Vale draws the loading area on an intake form. The van has blocked the main roll-up exit, but you can still squeeze behind its rear bumper into the laundry corridor. He marks the staff exit through the perimeter wall. Reaching it means carrying everything by hand.

Nia fills your backpack with food that can travel. Cal leaves the extra blanket he wanted to bring. Vale trades his heavy uniform coat for a spare kitchen jacket. You share the remaining drinks across several containers so dropping one bag will not cost the whole group its supplies.

On day twenty-eight, Cal clears a basket wedged behind the van. Nia passes the supplies through the narrow gap before you squeeze after them. Vale follows last, stopping to catch his breath. You reach the laundry room without disturbing the inmates at the cafeteria windows.

Through the exit window, you see infected people moving along the service road outside. They pause wherever loose metal knocks against the perimeter fence. Vale tells you to keep the door shut until that group passes. You return to the kitchen with a clear route and a new reason to watch the weather.

Vale tries the key without opening the door. It turns.

On day twenty-nine, the cracked cafeteria pane breaks.

The broken window admits infected inmates into the cafeteria. You bolt its kitchen door while Cal moves the supplies into the rear passage. Outside, the wind has stopped and the group along the fence is moving away from the laundry exit. You wait for daylight so Vale can see the service road from the high window.

Day thirty arrives with gray light through the high windows.

The cafeteria door begins bending before breakfast. Nia checks that everyone has a food or water bundle. Vale folds the route map and takes the exit key. Cal looks once toward the kitchen shelves, then helps you lift the supplies.

Nia locks the rear kitchen door after you. Something strikes it before the key comes free. Cal holds the next gate open until Vale reaches him. You hear the kitchen door shake again, then the yard absorbs the sound.

In the courtyard, infected press against the Block C bars. Cal opens the gate and steps away so Nia can pass first. He does not take the key back.

Behind the van, your bag catches on the bumper. You stop before its fabric tears. Cal reaches back and frees the pocket while Nia steadies the water containers on the other side. Vale squeezes through after you and points toward the laundry door.

You reach the last door together, still carrying the supplies you packed.

In the laundry room, Vale unlocks the staff exit.

Nia looks through the high window. The infected group is farther along the fence, following a loose panel toward the front gate. Vale opens the staff exit a crack. The service road immediately outside is clear. He leads you onto it while Nia watches the bend.

Cal closes the door behind the group. He turns the key, then hands it to Nia instead of keeping it. You move off the road before the distant infected turn back toward the laundry wall.

Beyond the first field, Vale begins limping. Cal takes his water bundle and slows to match him. Nia helps you over a fallen fence. You follow the tree line until the prison walls disappear behind it. When someone needs a rest, all four of you stop.

Late that afternoon, you reach a church hall being used as a rural shelter. Two adults check the road behind you before opening the door. They ask whether anyone is hurt. You explain Vale's limp and lead him toward the space they clear beside a bench.

Cal sets the water down where everyone can reach it. Nia puts your orange bag beside the bench and checks its torn pocket. Vale lowers himself onto a mat. You rub your aching feet and look toward the open doorway. The adults outside are watching the road for the next arrivals.

Nia shares the food you brought with the adults who let you in. Cal sits beside Vale instead of beside the door. You can still hear people walking in the hall, but nobody is rattling bars. You close your eyes while someone brings another mat.

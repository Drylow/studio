Nassourdine Imavov has offered Sean Strickland half a million dollars of his own money, but there is one condition: Strickland has to beat him. This is not an anonymous report about a private conversation. Imavov put the challenge on his own X account, tagged Strickland, and called out the UFC. The post is direct. What it does not establish is that the fight, or the payment arrangement, has been agreed.

The original message was posted late on October fourth, shortly after midnight on October fifth in France. Imavov writes that if the issue really is money, he will put five hundred thousand dollars on the line if Strickland beats him in January. He adds that he is putting the offer on record. That gives the story a clear primary source: an identifiable, public challenge from the fighter himself.

That condition is the key to the offer. The post does not promise Strickland five hundred thousand dollars just for showing up, and it does not say Imavov is paying the UFC to arrange the fight. The money is tied to Strickland winning. The wording turns the challenge into a test of confidence: accept the matchup, beat Imavov, and take the amount he says he is willing to put at stake.

MMA News places the post against a discussion about pay and negotiations. In that account, Strickland has been talking about the money involved in a potential title defense. Imavov's opening line addresses precisely that explanation: if it is really about money. This is his response to the argument being made publicly. It does not reveal the terms of Strickland's contract, or establish what the promotion has actually offered either fighter.

Imavov does not stop at the financial challenge. In the same post, he says refusing would mean Strickland is scared, and he calls on the UFC to strip its champion. Those are Imavov's accusations and demands. They are not findings about Strickland's motives, and they are not a decision by the promotion. The language is designed to put pressure on both the opponent and the people who can make the matchup.

There is also a practical distinction between a public pledge and an agreed financial arrangement. The post gives an amount, a proposed month, and a condition. It does not provide jointly accepted terms or a published payment agreement. These sources also do not establish a confirmed bout announcement. Until that changes, the accurate description is that Imavov has offered the money, rather than that Strickland is guaranteed to receive it.

MMA News points to an important piece of background: this would not be their first meeting. The report recalls Strickland defeating Imavov by decision in January twenty twenty-three. That previous result helps explain why a new challenge carries more than a fresh callout's appeal. Imavov is inviting a fight against someone who has already beaten him, while the proposed financial condition places his own confidence at the center of the promotion.

January is another detail worth reading carefully. It comes from Imavov's own challenge, so it is fair to report that this is the month he names. It is not the same as reporting a booked January event. The message supplies no confirmed venue or exact date. A public callout can outline the fight a contender wants while negotiations, scheduling, and an opponent's willingness remain questions that still need answers.

A meaningful next development would be a clear response from Strickland to this specific offer, followed by an announcement from the UFC if the matchup is made. Accepted terms for the money would be a separate development. It is possible to agree to a fight without resolving this public pledge, and it is possible to keep exchanging messages without agreeing to a fight. Those outcomes should not be mixed together.

For now, Imavov has made a striking public offer: five hundred thousand dollars of his own money if Strickland beats him in January. The amount is real as a statement, the condition is explicit, and the demand for the champion to be stripped is his own. The unresolved part is whether the challenge becomes an agreed matchup.
